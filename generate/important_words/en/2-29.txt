Aron: Brother of Moses and the first high priest in Israel, chosen by God to serve in the Tabernacle.
High Priest: The supreme religious leader in Israel, responsible for performing sacrifices and ritual acts in the Tabernacle.
Tabernacle: A temporary holy place where the Israelites worshipped God after leaving Egypt, before Solomon's temple was built.
Sacrifice: A gift or act given to God as a form of worship, thanksgiving, or atonement for sins.
The Sons: Aaron's sons, who were also priests and took part in the religious ceremonies in the Tabernacle.
Consecration: A ceremony to set apart something or someone for a holy purpose.
Blood: Symbolizes life and is used in sacrifices and rituals to cleanse and sanctify people or objects.
Fat: Part of the animal that was sacrificed, regarded as especially valuable and reserved for God.
The Altar: A structure where sacrifices and burnt offerings were presented to God.
The Breastpiece: Part of the high priest's garment that contained the Urim and Thummim, two objects used to seek God's guidance.
