Preacher: A wise man and king in Jerusalem who shares his wisdom and reflections on life and its meaning.
Vanity: A feeling of meaninglessness or futile striving, which the Preacher often refers to when speaking about human efforts.
Man's Lot: Humanity's destiny or experiences in life, according to the Preacher, including both joys and sorrows.
Work: The effort and toil people put into their tasks and occupations, something the Preacher sees as an important part of life, but also as futile without God's guidance.
Time: A concept that the Preacher emphasizes as a crucial factor in human life, where everything has its time and season.
Wisdom: The ability to understand and use knowledge effectively and morally. The Preacher values wisdom, yet recognizes that it too has its limits.
Joy: The feeling of happiness and contentment that people can experience in life. The Preacher encourages people to enjoy life's pleasures as a gift from God.
Fear of God: Respect and reverence for God, which the Preacher believes is the key to a meaningful life and humanity's most important purpose.
Death: The inevitable end of every human life. The Preacher reflects on the significance of death and its impact on the meaning of life.
Judgment: God's righteous evaluation of people's actions and morality. The Preacher reminds us that God will judge everyone for their deeds, whether good or evil.
