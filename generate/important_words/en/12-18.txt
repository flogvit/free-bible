Hezekiah: King of Judah who reigned from approx. 715-686 BC, known for his piety and reforms.
Assyrians: An ancient empire and people in Mesopotamia, known for their military power and conquests.
Sennacherib: Assyrian king who attacked Judah and besieged Jerusalem during Hezekiah's reign.
Jerusalem: The capital city of the kingdom of Judah, a center for religious worship and political power.
Temple: The holy place in Jerusalem where the Jews worshipped God and offered sacrifices.
Baal: A Canaanite god, often worshipped by the Israelites despite the prohibition against idolatry.
Siloam Tunnel: A water channel built by Hezekiah to secure the water supply for Jerusalem during the Assyrian siege.
Judah: The southern kingdom after the division of Israel, consisting of twelve tribes with Jerusalem as its capital.
Repentance: An act of humility and sorrow, where one acknowledges their sins and seeks God's forgiveness.
Covenant: An agreement or promise between God and people, often linked to specific conditions and blessings.
