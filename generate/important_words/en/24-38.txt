Jeremiah: A prophet in the Old Testament who received God's messages and delivered them to the people of Israel.
Babylon: An ancient kingdom and city in Mesopotamia, known for its high tower and power. Babylon's king, Nebuchadnezzar, conquered Jerusalem and led the Jews into exile.
The Cistern of Mud: A cistern filled with mud where Jeremiah was thrown by his enemies as punishment for his prophecies about Jerusalem's destruction.
Ebed-Melech: An Ethiopian court official who rescued Jeremiah from the cistern of mud by obtaining the king's permission and help from other men.
King Zedekiah: The last king of Judah who ruled during the siege and destruction of Jerusalem by Babylon. He asked Jeremiah for advice but ignored it and was then punished by God.
The Fall of Jerusalem: The event where Babylon's army, led by Nebuchadnezzar, besieged and destroyed Jerusalem, including the temple, and led many Jews into exile.
God's Wrath: God's strong displeasure and judgment against the people of Israel because of their sins and disobedience to his commands.
Circumstance: A situation or event that occurs around a person or group of people, often used to describe the challenging conditions Jeremiah faced.
