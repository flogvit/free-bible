Herod: A king who ruled over Judea and was known for his brutality and paranoia.
Egypt: An ancient North African country where the Israelites were held as slaves under Pharaoh's rule.
Pharaoh: The supreme ruler of Egypt, often regarded as a divine figure.
Israelites: A people who, according to the Bible, are God's chosen people, descendants of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob.
Moses: A prophet and leader of the Israelites who helped them escape slavery in Egypt and led them through the wilderness toward the promised land.
Plagues: Ten disasters that God brought upon the Egyptians to force Pharaoh to let the Israelites go.
Angel of Death: A divine figure sent by God to kill all the firstborn in Egypt as punishment for their treatment of the Israelites.
Passover: A Jewish festival celebrating the Israelites' deliverance from slavery in Egypt. Passover gets its name from God's command that the Israelites should smear the blood of a sacrificial lamb on their doorposts, so that the Angel of Death would "pass over" their homes and not kill their firstborn.
Lamb: An animal sacrificed by the Israelites as part of the Passover ritual, whose blood was used to protect their firstborn.
Firstborn: The oldest child in a family, who held special status and rights in ancient society.
