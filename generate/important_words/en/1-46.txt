Jacob: An important patriarch in the Bible, son of Isaac and Rebekah, and father of the twelve tribes of Israel.
Egypt: An ancient land in Northeast Africa where Jacob's family settled under the leadership of his son Joseph.
Joseph: Jacob's beloved son who was sold into slavery by his brothers, but later became a powerful man in Egypt and was reunited with his family.
Goshen: A region in Egypt where Jacob's family settled after being invited by Joseph due to famine in Canaan.
Canaan: The ancient name for an area in the Middle East that includes parts of modern Israel, Palestine, Lebanon, and Syria. God promised this land to Abraham's descendants.
Isaac: Jacob's father and another important patriarch in the Bible. He was the son of Abraham and Sarah and the father of Jacob and Esau.
Pharaoh's house: The term for the Egyptian king's household or palace. Joseph held a high position here and could help his family.
Tribes: Groupings of Jacob's sons and their descendants that formed the twelve tribes of Israel.
Famine: A severe lack of food that led to Jacob's family having to move to Egypt to survive.
