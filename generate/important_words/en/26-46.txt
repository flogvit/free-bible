Temple: A holy building dedicated to God, where worship and religious ceremonies take place.
Sacrifice: An act in which something is given or offered to God as a symbolic gesture to honor and thank him.
High Priest: The supreme leader of the priesthood, responsible for performing important religious ceremonies and maintaining temple order.
Sabbath: The seventh day of the week, a day of rest and worship according to Jewish and Christian tradition.
New Moon: The first phase of the moon's cycle, marking the beginning of a new month and celebrated with special religious ceremonies.
Gate: The entrance to the temple, symbolizing the transition from the secular to the holy.
Prince: A leader figure, possibly a king or prophet, who has a close relationship with God and serves his purposes.
Burnt Offering: A sacrifice in which something, usually an animal, is burned on an altar as an act of devotion to God.
Peace Offering: A sacrifice that symbolizes reconciliation and peace between people and God.
Meal: A shared consumption of food, often used as part of a religious ceremony or celebration.
