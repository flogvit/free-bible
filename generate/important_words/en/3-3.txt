Offer: A religious act where gifts, usually food or animals, are given to God as a form of worship and gratitude.
Peace offering: A type of sacrifice in the Old Testament where part of the offering is burned on the altar, while the rest is shared between the priest and the giver, symbolizing fellowship with God.
Fat: The richest and most nourishing part of an animal, often seen as the best portion and therefore reserved for God in sacrificial acts.
Blood: A symbol of life and purity in the Old Testament, used in ritual actions as part of the cleansing process.
Internal organs: Inner parts of an animal, such as the liver and kidneys, which were also burned as part of the offering to God.
The altar: A sacred structure where sacrificial acts are performed as part of worship and communication with God.
Priest: A person appointed by God to carry out special religious duties and rituals, including receiving and managing offerings.
