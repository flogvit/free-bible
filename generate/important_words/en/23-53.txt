The Lord's Suffering Servant: A prophetic figure described in Isaiah 53, often interpreted as a reference to Jesus Christ, who suffers and dies for the sins of humanity.
Sin: An action or thought that breaks God's will and moral law, leading to spiritual separation from God.
Righteousness: A person who lives in harmony with God's will and moral standards, thereby maintaining a good relationship with God.
Punishment: The consequences of sin, including suffering and death, which the Suffering Servant takes upon himself to bring salvation to humanity.
Salvation: God's grace and mercy offered through the sacrifice of the Suffering Servant, giving people the opportunity to be reconciled with God and attain eternal life.
Faith: Trust and conviction in God's existence, promises, and plan of salvation, which is necessary to receive the salvation offered through the Suffering Servant.
