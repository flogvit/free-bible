Tabernacle: The temporary, portable sanctuary that the Israelites built and used to worship God while they wandered through the wilderness.
Place of Revelation: The place where God communicated with Moses and other leaders in Israel, often inside the Tabernacle.
Ark of the Covenant: A chest made of acacia wood, overlaid with gold, which contained the Ten Commandments, manna, and Aaron's staff. It was placed in the Most Holy Place within the Tabernacle.
Veil: A textile partition that separated the Holy Place from the Most Holy Place in the Tabernacle.
Altar: A structure where offerings were presented to God. In the Tabernacle there were two altars: the altar of burnt offering and the altar of incense.
Burnt Offering: A type of sacrifice in which an animal was burned on the altar as a gift to God. This offering symbolized surrender and devotion to God.
Incense Offering: Another offering in which incense was burned on a special altar to honor God and ask for his blessings.
Priesthood: A group of people, usually from the tribe of Levi, who were responsible for performing religious ceremonies and maintaining the Tabernacle.
