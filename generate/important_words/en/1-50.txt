Joseph: Youngest son of Jacob and Rachel, and an important figure in Genesis. He was sold into slavery by his brothers, but ended up as a powerful man in Egypt.
Brothers: Sons of Jacob and his wives, who became the ancestors of Israel's twelve tribes.
Egypt: An ancient kingdom in Northeast Africa where Joseph lived and worked after being sold as a slave.
Pharaoh: The supreme ruler of ancient Egypt, whom Joseph served under.
Jacob: Also known as Israel, Jacob was the son of Isaac and grandson of Abraham. He had twelve sons who became the ancestors of Israel's twelve tribes.
Canaan: The area that God promised to give to Abraham's descendants, and where Jacob's family originally came from.
Burial: A ceremony or act to honor and remember the dead, usually by placing the body in the ground or treating it with respect in another way.
Forgiveness: The act of accepting others' mistakes and letting go of anger, bitterness, or revenge.
God's will: The concept referring to God's plan and purpose for humanity and the world.
