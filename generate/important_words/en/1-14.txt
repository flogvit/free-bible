Abram: A biblical figure, later called Abraham, who is an important patriarch in Judaism, Christianity, and Islam.
Lot: Abraham's nephew who lived in Sodom and was taken captive during the war between the kings.
Melchizedek: King of Salem and priest of God Most High, who blessed Abram after his victory over the kings.
Kedorlaomer: The king of Elam who led a coalition of kings against Sodom and Gomorrah and took Lot captive.
Salem: An ancient city, identified with Jerusalem, where Melchizedek was both king and priest.
Gomorrah: One of the two biblical cities, along with Sodom, that were destroyed by God because of their wickedness and sin.
Alliance: An agreement or pact between two or more parties, often to cooperate toward a common goal or protect one another.
Captive: A person held in captivity, often as a result of war or conflict.
Victory: Success in battle or competition, often used in connection with war or military campaigns.
Tithe: A tax or levy, usually one-tenth of income or property, given to God or a priest as an expression of gratitude and reverence.
