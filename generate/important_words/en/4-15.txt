Sacrifices: Ritual actions involving giving gifts to God, often in the form of animals or food, to show gratitude, ask for forgiveness, or enter into a covenant.
Bread: A food made from flour and water, symbolizing nourishment and life, used as part of offerings to God.
Sin: A violation of God's laws or morality, which can lead to punishment or separation from God.
Forgiveness: God's grace and mercy that frees people from guilt and the consequences of their sins.
Foreigners: People who are not part of the Israelite community but live among them. Foreigners could participate in certain religious practices under specific conditions.
The Promised Land: The geographical area that God promised to give to the people of Israel, identified as Canaan in the Old Testament.
Tribes: The twelve groups of the people of Israel, each tribe descending from one of Jacob's sons.
Generation: A group of people born and living at about the same time, often used to describe eras or shifts in leadership and responsibility.
