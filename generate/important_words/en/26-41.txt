Temple: A holy place for worship and offerings dedicated to God, usually a building where religious ceremonies and rituals are performed.
Holy Place: The innermost room of the temple, also known as the Most Holy Place, where God's presence was strongest. In Solomon's temple, this room contained the Ark of the Covenant.
Cubit: A unit of length used in ancient Israel, corresponding to approximately 48 centimeters. Used in Ezekiel 41 to describe the dimensions of the temple and its structures.
Pillars: Large, vertical support elements in architecture, often made of stone or wood. Pillars were used in the temple to support the roof and decorate the building.
Doorway: The entrance to a room or a building. In Ezekiel 41 this refers to the entrances to the various rooms of the temple and the Holy Place.
Walls: Structures that surround and define a room or a building, often made of stone, wood, or brick. In Ezekiel 41 the thickness and height of the temple walls are described.
Altar: A structure where offerings and gifts are presented to God. In the temple the altar was the place for burnt offerings and other religious ceremonies.
Side Chambers: Smaller rooms located around the main room of the temple, used for various purposes, such as storing offerings and equipment.
Ezekiel: A prophet in the Old Testament who received visions from God, including detailed descriptions of a future temple in Jerusalem.
