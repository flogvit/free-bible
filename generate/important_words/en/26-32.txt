Ezekiel: A prophet in the Old Testament who conveys God's message to the people of Israel.
Book 32: A chapter in the book of Ezekiel containing a lament over Egypt and its king, as well as prophecies concerning judgment upon fallen nations.
Lament: A poetic text expressing grief, repentance, or regret, often used in biblical contexts to describe God's view of human sins.
Egypt: A powerful ancient nation in North Africa, frequently in conflict with the people of Israel.
Pharaoh: The title for the Egyptian king, regarded as a divine ruler and a symbol of the nation's power and prosperity.
The Deep: A term referring to the depths of the sea or the underworld, used here as an image of the death and destruction awaiting Egypt and its king.
Sword: A weapon representing God's judgment and punishment upon sinful nations and their leaders.
Allies: Nations or peoples who stand with Egypt and share their fate as a result of God's judgment.
The Lord: Another designation for God, emphasizing his sovereignty and authority over all creation.
