Moses: A central figure in Judaism, Christianity and Islam, who according to Exodus was born of a Hebrew woman and saved from death by Pharaoh's daughter.
Nile: The longest river in Africa and an important symbol in Egyptian culture and religion; Moses was found in a basket along the banks of the Nile.
Hebrews: An ancient Semitic people who were the ancestors of Israel and lived as slaves in Egypt under Pharaoh's rule.
Miriam: Moses' sister, who helped save him from death and later became a prophetess and leader of the women of Israel.
Pharaoh's daughter: The woman who rescued Moses from the Nile and adopted him as her own son, even though she knew he was a Hebrew.
Midian: A region in the northwestern part of the Arabian Peninsula where Moses fled after killing an Egyptian and married Jethro's daughter, Zipporah.
Jethro: A Midianite priest and Moses' father-in-law, who gave advice and support to Moses during his leadership of the people of Israel.
Burning bush: A miraculous phenomenon in which God spoke to Moses through a bush that burned without being consumed; this was the beginning of Moses' calling as God's servant and leader of the people of Israel.
