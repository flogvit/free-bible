Offer: A religious act where gifts, often animals or food, are given to God as a form of worship and gratitude.
Holy: Something dedicated or set apart for God and his service, regarded as pure and worthy.
Lamb: A young sheep often used as a sacrificial animal in the Old Testament.
Bread: Food made from flour and water, symbolizing God's presence and care for his people.
Wine: An alcoholic drink made from fermented grapes, used in religious ceremonies as a drink offering.
Incense: Fragrant substances burned during religious ceremonies, representing prayers and devotion rising up to God.
Month: A unit of time based on the moon's cycle, used to organize religious celebrations and rituals.
Sabbath: The seventh day of the week, the day of rest, when the Jewish people were to rest and worship God.
New Moon: The beginning of a new month in the Jewish calendar, marked with special offerings and celebrations.
Festival: A religious celebration involving community, worship, and often sacrificial gifts to God.
