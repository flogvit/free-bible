Solomon: The king of Israel and son of David, who built the temple in Jerusalem to honor God.
Temple: A holy place where people worship and offer sacrifices to God; in this context, the temple in Jerusalem.
Cedar: A type of tree, especially cedar trees, which were used in building the temple because of their durability and fragrance.
Lebanon: A country in the Middle East, known for its cedar trees, from which the timber for the temple was obtained.
Hiram's workers: The laborers from King Hiram of Tyre who helped Solomon build the temple by supplying materials and skilled craftsmanship.
Gold: A precious metal used abundantly in the temple's decorations and furnishings, symbolizing divine dignity and purity.
Almug wood: An exotic and costly timber, also known as sandalwood, used in the construction and furnishing of the temple.
Oracle: The innermost room of the temple where the Ark of the Covenant was kept, also called the Most Holy Place.
Ark of the Covenant: A gold-covered chest that held the stone tablets with the Ten Commandments, a central element in Jewish faith.
