Jacob: Grandfather of the twelve tribes of Israel and son of Isaac. He moved to Egypt with his family.
Egypt: A country in North Africa where Jacob's family settled during the famine.
Joseph: Jacob's eldest son by Rachel, who became vizier in Egypt and helped his family during the famine.
Pharaohs: The title for the Egyptian king who allowed Jacob's family to live in Egypt.
Goshen: The region in Egypt where Jacob's family settled, a fertile land suitable for sheep farming.
Famine: A prolonged shortage of food that affected both Egypt and Canaan, leading Jacob's family to move to Egypt.
Land: A fundamental resource that people in this time fought over and traded, especially agricultural land.
Buy: The act of acquiring something, often land, by paying a certain sum of money or through barter.
Necessities: Basic needs for survival, such as food, clothing, and shelter.
Servants: People who work for others, often without wages, performing various tasks and duties.
