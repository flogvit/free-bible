High Priest: The supreme religious leader in Israel, responsible for performing sacrifices and other ritual acts on behalf of the people.
Day of Atonement: An annual holy day in Judaism when the high priest makes atonement for the sins of Israel through special rituals and sacrifices.
Scapegoat: A goat that symbolically carries away the sins of Israel and is sent out into the wilderness as part of the Day of Atonement ritual.
Holy Place: The most sacred area in the tabernacle or temple, where God's presence was especially strong. The high priest could enter there only once a year, on the Day of Atonement.
Incense: A mixture of spices and resin burned to produce fragrant smoke, used in religious ceremonies as a symbol of prayer and devotion.
Blood: In biblical context, blood is associated with life and cleansing. On the Day of Atonement, animal blood was used in rituals to cleanse the sanctuary and the people from sin.
Sin Offering: A sacrifice offered to God to make atonement for sins. On the Day of Atonement, a bull and a goat were sacrificed as sin offerings for the high priest and the people.
