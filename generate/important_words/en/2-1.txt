Israelites: A people descended from the twelve sons of Israel (Jacob) and chosen by God as His special people.
Egypt: An ancient kingdom in Northeast Africa where the Israelites lived as slaves under Pharaoh's rule.
Pharaoh's daughter: The daughter of the Egyptian king, Pharaoh, who finds Moses in a basket on the river and raises him as her own son.
Moses: A central figure in Judaism, Christianity, and Islam. He was born an Israelite but grew up in the Egyptian royal household and later led the Israelites out of Egypt toward the Promised Land.
Hebrews: Another name for the Israelites, often used in Exodus 2.
Slavery: A condition in which people are treated as property and forced to work without pay or freedom.
Midian: An area in the Middle East where Moses fled after killing an Egyptian and later met God at the burning bush.
Burning bush: A bush that appears to burn without being consumed, where God reveals Himself to Moses and gives him the task of leading the Israelites out of Egypt.
