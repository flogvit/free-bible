Ruth: The main character in the Book of Ruth, a Moabite woman who becomes Naomi's daughter-in-law and later marries Boaz.
Naomi: An Israelite woman from Bethlehem who is the mother of two sons and the mother-in-law to Ruth and Orpah. She returns to Bethlehem after losing her husband and sons.
Boaz: A wealthy Israelite and relative of Naomi's deceased husband, Elimelech. He shows kindness to Ruth and marries her, thus becoming the kinsman-redeemer for Naomi and Ruth.
Kinsman-redeemer: A relative who has the duty and right to marry the widow of a deceased family member in order to carry on the dead man's name and property.
Harvest: The period when grain and other crops are gathered from the fields, an important time in the Book of Ruth when Ruth meets Boaz while gleaning grain in his field.
Loyalty: Faithfulness and devotion to someone or something, such as Ruth's loyalty to Naomi and her trust in God.
Sister-in-law: The sister of one's spouse or the spouse of one's sibling. In the Book of Ruth, Orpah is Ruth's sister-in-law and also Naomi's daughter-in-law.
