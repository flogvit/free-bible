Elijah: A prophet in the Old Testament who performed miracles and spoke God's word to the people of Israel.
Horeb: A mountain, also known as Mount Sinai, where Moses received the Ten Commandments from God.
Wilderness: A dry and inhospitable landscape with little vegetation and rainfall, where Elijah fled after killing the prophets of Baal.
Ravens: Black birds that God sent to feed Elijah while he was in the wilderness.
Angel: Spiritual beings created by God who serve and carry out His will.
Forty days and forty nights: A biblical time period symbolizing a long and significant duration.
Cave: A natural hollow in the rock where Elijah sought shelter and met with God.
Still small voice: A quiet and gentle sound through which God spoke to Elijah at Horeb.
Syrian: People from Syria, a land north of Israel, mentioned as enemies of Israel.
Hazael: A Syrian king whom God instructed Elijah to anoint.
Jehu: An Israelite general who became king over Israel after God instructed Elijah to anoint him.
Elisha: A prophet and disciple of Elijah, chosen by God to succeed Elijah as prophet.
