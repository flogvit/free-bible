David: A young shepherd and later king of Israel, known for his bravery and faith in God. He killed the giant Goliath in this story.
Goliath: A large Philistine warrior, described as a giant, who was defeated by David in a duel.
Philistines: A people who lived in Canaan, enemies of Israel during this period. Goliath was one of their foremost warriors.
Saul: The first king of Israel, who chose David to fight Goliath because of his faith and courage.
Harp: A musical instrument that David played to calm King Saul's mind.
Shepherd: The job David had before he became king; he tended his father's sheep.
Sling: The weapon David used to kill Goliath, a simple device that threw stones with great force and precision.
Israel's army: The army of the people of Israel, led by King Saul, which fought against the Philistine army.
Duel: A fight between two individuals, often representatives of each side in a conflict. David and Goliath met in such a contest.
Faith: Trust in and conviction about God's existence and power, something David demonstrated through his action against Goliath.
