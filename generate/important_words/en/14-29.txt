Hezekiah: King of Judah who reigned in Jerusalem and carried out religious reforms to restore the worship of God.
Temple: A holy place where the Israelites worshipped God; built by King Solomon in Jerusalem as a place for God's presence and sacrifices.
Cleansing: The process of removing impurity and sin from a person, an object, or a place to make it holy and acceptable to God.
Levites: Members of the tribe of Levi, chosen by God to serve as assistants to the priests and carry out tasks related to temple service.
Priests: Religious leaders from the line of Aaron, responsible for performing sacrifices and other religious ceremonies in the temple.
Sacrifice: A gift, often an animal or food, given to God as an act of worship, thanksgiving, or atonement for sin.
Covenant: An agreement between God and his people, involving promises and obligations from both sides.
Sin: Actions, thoughts, or attitudes that break God's law and lead to separation from God.
Atoning sacrifice: A sacrifice given to make amends for sin and restore the relationship between God and humanity.
Praise song: Religious singing or music expressing praise, thanksgiving, and honor to God.
