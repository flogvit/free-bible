Jehoshaphat: A king of Judah who is regarded as a righteous ruler and the son of Asa. He strengthened his nation through religious reform and military expansion.
Judah: An Old Testament kingdom in the southern part of Israel, named after the tribe of Judah. The kingdom consisted mainly of the tribes of Judah and Benjamin.
The law of the Lord: A reference to God's instructions, commands, and statutes given to the Israelites through Moses. This includes the Ten Commandments and other laws described in the books of Moses.
Teachers: Individuals sent by Jehoshaphat to teach the people the law of the Lord. Their role was to spread knowledge about God and His commands to promote justice and godliness.
Fear: In this context, fear refers to reverence and awe for God. It involves recognizing His authority and power, as well as a willingness to obey His commands.
Enemies: The nations and peoples who were in conflict with Judah. During Jehoshaphat's reign, the enemies were kept at bay because of his godliness and God's protection.
Wealth: Material resources and prosperity that Jehoshaphat and his people enjoyed during his reign. The wealth was a sign of God's blessing and support.
FORTRESSES: Fortified buildings or structures built to protect an area from hostile attacks. Jehoshaphat strengthened the fortresses in Judah as part of his military strategy.
