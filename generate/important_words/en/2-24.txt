Covenant: An agreement or bond between two parties, often involving promises and obligations. In this context, it is the covenant between God and the people of Israel.
Sinai: A mountain in the Middle East where Moses received the Ten Commandments from God.
Moses: The prophet and leader of the people of Israel who led them out of slavery in Egypt and received God's laws on Sinai.
Blood: Symbolizes life and is used in this context to confirm the covenant between God and the people of Israel.
Sacrifice: An act in which something is given up, usually as an offering to God, to show devotion, gratitude, or to establish a covenant.
Stone tablets: Physical objects on which God's laws, including the Ten Commandments, were written by God Himself and given to Moses on Sinai.
Glory: Respect and admiration for someone, in this case God, resulting from their greatness, power, or good deeds.
The glory of the Lord: God's presence and majestic manifestation, often described as radiant and overwhelming.
