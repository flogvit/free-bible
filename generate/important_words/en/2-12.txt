Lord: A title that shows respect and reverence for God, acknowledging his sovereignty and authority.
Israelites: A people descended from Jacob (also called Israel), who was the son of Isaac and grandson of Abraham. They are God's chosen people in the Old Testament.
Egypt: An ancient kingdom in Northeast Africa where the Israelites were held as slaves under Pharaoh's rule.
Pharaoh: The supreme leader and ruler of Egypt, regarded as a divine figure in Egyptian mythology.
Passover: A Jewish festival that commemorates the deliverance of the Israelites from slavery in Egypt under Moses' leadership.
Passover lamb: A lamb that was sacrificed and eaten during the Passover feast as a symbol of God's protection and deliverance.
Angel of death: A spiritual being sent by God to strike down the firstborn in Egypt as part of the ten plagues.
Blood: The blood of the Passover lamb, which was to be smeared on the doorframes of Israelite homes to protect them from the angel of death.
Exodus: The term used to describe the Israelites' escape from Egypt and their journey toward the promised land, Canaan.
Tenth plague: The final and most severe of the ten plagues that God sent upon Egypt to compel Pharaoh to release the Israelites.
