Ezekiel: A prophet in the Old Testament who received visions and messages from God.
Egypt: An ancient kingdom in Northeast Africa, often described as an opponent and rival of the people of Israel.
Pharaoh: The title for the supreme ruler of Egypt, equivalent to a king or emperor.
Nile: The longest river in Africa and a vital source of life and prosperity for ancient Egypt.
Dragon: A mythical creature used as a symbol of Pharaoh and his power; it also represents evil and destruction.
Lord: Another designation for God, emphasizing His sovereignty and authority over humanity and the world.
Judgment: God's righteous punishment or consequences for human sin and disobedience.
Babylon: An ancient city and empire in Mesopotamia, often associated with pride, idolatry, and hostility toward God's people.
Restoration: The process by which God heals, restores, and renews His people after periods of trial and suffering.
House of Israel: A collective term for the twelve tribes of Israel, God's chosen people.
