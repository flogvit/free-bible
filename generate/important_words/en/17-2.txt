Esther: A Jewish woman who becomes queen of Persia and saves her people from destruction.
Xerxes: The king of Persia who is married to Esther and plays a central role in the story.
Mordecai: Esther's adoptive father and advisor, who uncovers a conspiracy against the king and later receives a high position in the kingdom.
Haman: An evil advisor to King Xerxes who plans to exterminate the Jews, but is ultimately punished for his wickedness.
Purim: A Jewish festival that celebrates the deliverance of the Jews from Haman's evil plot, with Esther and Mordecai as heroes.
Destiny: The unchangeable pattern or plan for the future, often used to describe God's will and purpose for people.
Prayer: Communication with God, where people seek guidance, help, or give thanks for what they have received.
Fasting: A period of voluntary abstention from food and drink, often practiced as a religious act to show humility and seek God's grace.
Loyalty: Faithfulness and support toward a person, cause, or belief, such as Esther's loyalty to her people and God.
Justice: The principle of doing what is right and upholding moral and ethical standards, as demonstrated by Esther and Mordecai in their actions.
