Hosea: The prophet who wrote the book of Hosea, one of the twelve minor prophets in the Old Testament.
Israel: An ancient kingdom in the Middle East, also known as God's chosen people, whom Hosea criticized for their unfaithfulness to God.
Ephraim: One of the tribes of Israel, often used as a symbol for all Israel in the book of Hosea.
Priest: A religious leader who performs ceremonies and conveys God's message to the people.
Sin: An action or thought that breaks with God's will and commands, leading to spiritual separation from God.
Judgment: God's righteous punishment for sin and disobedience, which can manifest in various forms, including sickness, suffering, and destruction.
Ommah: An ancient city in Israel, mentioned in the book of Hosea as a place where the people turned away from God.
Repentance: A feeling of sorrow and regret over one's own sins and actions, along with a desire to change behavior and draw closer to God.
Reconciliation: The process of restoring a broken relationship between God and humanity through repentance and forgiveness.
