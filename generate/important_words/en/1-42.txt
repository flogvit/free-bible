Joseph: One of Jacob's twelve sons, and a central figure in Genesis. He is sold into slavery by his brothers, but ends up as a powerful man in Egypt.
Jacob: Also called Israel, he is the father of Joseph and his eleven brothers. He is an important patriarch in the Bible and the ancestor of the twelve tribes of Israel.
Egypt: An ancient kingdom in North Africa where Joseph was taken as a slave and later became governor under Pharaoh.
Grain: A basic food and resource that plays a key role in this story, since there is a shortage of grain in Canaan and Joseph is in charge of Egypt's grain stores.
Famine: A severe lack of food that leads to suffering and death. In Genesis 42 there is famine in Canaan, which causes Jacob to send his sons to Egypt to buy grain.
Brothers: The sons of Jacob, who make up the twelve tribes of Israel. They sell Joseph into slavery, but meet him again in Egypt without knowing who he is.
Pharaoh: The supreme ruler of ancient Egypt, who gives Joseph power to manage the country's grain stores and govern the land during the famine.
Benjamin: The youngest of Jacob's sons and Joseph's full brother. Joseph tests his brothers by keeping Benjamin behind in Egypt.
