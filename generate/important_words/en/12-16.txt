Ahas: A king of Judah who reigned from 735 to 715 BC and did many evil things in God's sight.
Jerusalem: The capital city of the kingdom of Judah, an important religious and political center for the Israelite people.
Damascus: The capital of Syria, a powerful city-state in ancient times and an enemy of the kingdom of Judah.
Rezin: The king of Syria who attacked Judah together with Pekah, the king of Israel, in an attempt to force Ahas to join their coalition against Assyria.
Pekah: The king of Israel who allied himself with Rezin, the king of Syria, to attack Judah and force Ahas to join their coalition against Assyria.
Tiglat-Pileser: The king of Assyria whom Ahas sought help from to protect his kingdom from attacks by Israel and Syria.
Assyria: A powerful empire in ancient Mesopotamia that dominated the region and was a threat to both Israel and Syria.
Altar: A structure where offerings and prayers were presented to God or gods as part of religious rituals.
Siloam's pool: A water source near Jerusalem that Ahas protected by building a tunnel to lead the water into the city.
Hezekiah: The son of Ahas and his successor as king of Judah, known for his piety and reforms to bring the people back to God.
