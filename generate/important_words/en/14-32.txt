Hezekiah: King of Judah who ruled from about 715 to 686 BC, known for his piety and reforms in the kingdom.
Sennacherib: Assyrian king who attacked Jerusalem during Hezekiah's reign but failed to capture the city.
Jerusalem: The capital of the kingdom of Judah, an important religious and political center for the Israelite people.
Assyria: A powerful empire in antiquity, located in Mesopotamia, known for its military strength and conquests.
The prophet Isaiah: One of the major prophets in the Old Testament, who gave counsel and divine guidance to King Hezekiah.
The tunnel: An underground water channel built by Hezekiah to secure Jerusalem's water supply during the siege.
Angel: Spiritual beings created by God, often sent as messengers or executors of God's will.
Siege: A military tactic in which an enemy army surrounds a city or fortress to force its surrender.
Prayer: Communication with God, where people express gratitude, praise, concerns, or requests for help.
