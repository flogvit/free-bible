Genesis 33: The chapter in the Hebrew Bible and Old Testament that describes the wanderings of the children of Israel in the wilderness.
Children of Israel: An expression referring to the descendants of the patriarch Jacob, also called Israel, which includes the twelve tribes of Israel.
Wilderness journey: The period of 40 years when the Israelites wandered through the wilderness after fleeing Egypt, before arriving in Canaan, the promised land.
Moses: The prophet and leader of the children of Israel, who received the Ten Commandments and led them out of slavery in Egypt and through the wilderness.
Stages: Describes the various places where the children of Israel camped during their wilderness journey. Genesis 33 lists a total of 42 stages.
Canaan: The promised land that God pledged to give to Abraham's descendants. Today's Israel, Palestine, and parts of Lebanon and Jordan make up historical Canaan.
Aaron: Moses' brother and the first high priest of Israel. He played an important role both in the exodus from Egypt and during the time in the wilderness.
