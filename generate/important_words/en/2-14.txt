Red Sea: A large sea located between Africa and the Arabian Peninsula, where the Israelites crossed under Moses' leadership.
Moses: The prophet and leader of the Israelites who brought them out of slavery in Egypt and received the Ten Commandments from God.
Egyptians: The inhabitants of ancient Egypt, who held the Israelites as slaves before their escape led by Moses.
Pharaoh: The ruler of ancient Egypt, who refused to let the Israelites go free until God sent ten plagues upon the land.
Miracle: A supernatural event that cannot be explained by natural or scientific laws, often attributed to God's intervention.
Column: A vertical structure that supports something; here used metaphorically to describe God's presence in the form of a pillar of cloud and fire that led the Israelites.
Parting: The act of dividing something into two parts; in this context, it refers to the parting of the Red Sea so that the Israelites could cross safely.
Dry land: The ground that became visible after the Red Sea was parted, allowing the Israelites to cross without getting wet.
Drowning: The process of dying due to submersion in water; here it refers to the fate of the Egyptians when the Red Sea closed over them.
