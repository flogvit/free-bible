Temple: A holy place where Jews performed religious ceremonies and sacrifices to God.
Levites: Members of the tribe of Levi who had special religious duties in the temple, including music, singing, and assisting the priests.
Priests: Religious leaders from the line of Aaron who were responsible for performing sacrifices and other ceremonies in the temple.
Nehemiah: A Jewish leader and governor in Jerusalem who helped rebuild the city's walls and reform religious life after the exile in Babylon.
Exile: The period when the Jews were taken captive and lived in Babylon after Jerusalem was destroyed by the Babylonians.
Rebuilding: The process of repairing and reconstructing the walls and temple of Jerusalem after the exile.
Dedication: The ceremony where the temple and walls of Jerusalem were consecrated and dedicated to God after the rebuilding.
Hymn: A religious song or hymn expressing gratitude, reverence, and praise to God.
