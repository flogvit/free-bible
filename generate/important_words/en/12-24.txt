Nebuchadnezzar: King of Babylon who invaded and conquered Jerusalem, and led the people into exile.
Jerusalem: The capital city of ancient Israel and Judah, a central place for Jewish faith and culture.
Judah: An ancient kingdom in the region of Canaan, inhabited by the Israelites with Jerusalem as its capital.
Jehoiakim: King of Judah who was installed by Pharaoh Neco II, but later submitted to Nebuchadnezzar.
Exile: A period when the Jews were forced to leave their homeland and live in other countries, especially Babylon.
The Lord's wrath: God's anger against the people of Israel because of their disobedience and idolatry.
Prophets: People who received messages from God and delivered them to the people, often with warnings about the consequences of their actions.
Babylon: An ancient city and empire in Mesopotamia, known for its greatness and cultural influence.
To serve: To work or carry out tasks for someone, often in a subordinate role or as a duty.
