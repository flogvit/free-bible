Cyrus: Persian king who allowed the Jews to return to Jerusalem and rebuild the temple.
Jerusalem: The holy city in Israel, center of Jewish faith and spirituality, where the temple was to be rebuilt.
Temple: Holy place for Jewish worship and sacrifice, which had previously been destroyed by the Babylonians and was to be rebuilt.
Babylon: An ancient city and empire in Mesopotamia that had earlier conquered Jerusalem and taken the Jews into exile.
Exile: The period when the Jews were taken from their homeland and lived under foreign rule, especially in Babylon.
Rebuilding: The process of restoring or repairing something that has been destroyed or damaged, such as the temple in Jerusalem.
Artaxerxes: Persian king who gave support to Ezra and Nehemiah to complete the rebuilding of the temple and the walls of Jerusalem.
Ezra: Jewish priest and scribe who led a group of Jews back to Jerusalem to rebuild the temple and teach God's laws.
Sacrifice: An act in which something is given to God as a sign of devotion, gratitude, or atonement for sins.
