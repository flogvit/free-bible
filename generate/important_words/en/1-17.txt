Covenant: An agreement or commitment between God and people, often with promises and conditions from both parties.
Circumcision: A religious ritual in which the foreskin of a man's penis is removed as a sign of belonging and submission to God's covenant.
Abraham: The first patriarch in the Jewish, Christian, and Islamic faiths, formerly known as Abram, who made a covenant with God and was promised many descendants.
Sarah: Abraham's wife, formerly known as Sarai, who was also part of the covenant and was promised to bear a son despite her old age.
Isaac: The son of Abraham and Sarah, born when they were old, and regarded as an important figure in Judaism, Christianity, and Islam.
Ishmael: The son of Abraham and Hagar, his Egyptian slave woman, considered the ancestor of the Arabs and an important figure in Islam.
Hagar: Abraham's Egyptian slave woman who bore him a son, Ishmael, before the covenant with God was made.
The Name of God: The holy name of God in Genesis, often translated as "Lord" or "Yahweh," used to emphasize God's presence and involvement in human life.
