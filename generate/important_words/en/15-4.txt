Ezra: A Jewish scribe and priest who helped rebuild the temple in Jerusalem after the exile in Babylon.
Book 4: The fourth chapter of the book of Ezra, which describes opposition to the rebuilding of the temple and the city walls in Jerusalem.
Rebuilding: The process of restoring or reconstructing something that has been destroyed, such as the temple and the city walls in Jerusalem.
Jerusalem: The holy city for Jews, Christians, and Muslims, and the capital of the ancient kingdoms of Israel and Judah.
Exile: A period when the Jews were forced to leave their homeland and live in other countries, especially in Babylon.
Babylon: An ancient Mesopotamian empire known for holding the Jews in exile and destroying the first temple in Jerusalem.
Artaxerxes: A Persian king who reigned during the time when the Jews were in exile in Babylon. He received letters from opponents of the rebuilding of Jerusalem and ordered that the work be stopped.
Opposition: In this context, it refers to individuals or groups who tried to prevent the rebuilding of the temple and the city walls in Jerusalem.
