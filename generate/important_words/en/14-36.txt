Cyrus: The king of Persia who allowed the Jews to return to Jerusalem and rebuild the temple.
Jerusalem: The capital city of ancient Israel and Judah, a central place for Jewish faith and culture.
Temple: A holy place in Jerusalem where the Jews worshipped God and performed religious ceremonies.
Nebuchadnezzar: The king of Babylon who conquered Jerusalem, destroyed the temple, and led the Jews into exile.
Exile: A period when the Jews were forced to leave their homeland and live in foreign lands, especially Babylon.
Josiah: A righteous king of Judah who sought to reform the kingdom and lead the people back to God.
Judah: The southern kingdom of the two Israelite kingdoms, consisting of the tribes of Judah and Benjamin.
Zedekiah: The last king of Judah, installed by Nebuchadnezzar but later rebelled against him.
Prophet: A person who received messages from God and delivered them to the people; often prophets warned about the consequences of disobedience to God.
