The Lord's goodness: God's love and mercy toward humanity, especially in the form of his protection and blessings.
To give thanks: To express gratitude and recognition for something, in this context, God's grace and goodness.
To redeem: To free or save someone from captivity, suffering, or danger. In the Book of Psalms, this refers to God's actions to deliver his people from distress.
Distress: A situation marked by suffering, hardship, or danger, which may be physical, spiritual, or emotional.
Wanderers: People who travel or move from place to place, often without a fixed home or destination.
Darkness: Symbolizes ignorance, despair, or evil. In Psalm 107 it is used to describe a state of spiritual or emotional blindness and confusion.
Shadow of death: A poetic expression describing a condition of great danger, suffering, or nearness to death.
Rebellion: The act of opposing or going against an authority, especially God and his will.
Captive: A person held imprisoned or under another's control, often as a result of war, rebellion, or sin.
To heal: To restore health, strength, or well-being, whether physical, spiritual, or emotional. In Psalm 107 it refers to God's power to heal and restore his people.
Storm: A powerful natural disaster that can symbolize chaos, destruction, or God's wrath. In Psalm 107 the storm is used as a metaphor for trials and challenges in life.
Sea: A large body of water that can represent the unknown, dangers, or God's creation.
Waves: Movements in the sea that can symbolize instability, danger, or God's power over nature.
To calm: To bring rest, peace, or an end to conflict, unrest, or suffering. In Psalm 107 it refers to God's ability to quiet life's storms and provide safety and peace.
