Jephthah: A judge in Israel who was the son of a prostitute and was rejected by his family, but later became a military leader and national hero.
Vow: A vow or oath that Jephthah made to God to sacrifice the first thing he met when he returned home after victory over the Ammonites, in exchange for God's help in war.
Ammonites: A people who lived east of the Jordan River and were enemies of Israel. Jephthah led Israel in battle against them.
Daughter: Jephthah's only child, whom he unintentionally sacrificed to God as a result of his vow. This is a tragic event in the story.
Judge: A leader in Israel who held both political and religious roles, and who served as an intermediary between God and the people.
Gilead: A region east of the Jordan River where Jephthah and his family lived.
Spirit: Refers to the Spirit of God that came upon Jephthah and gave him strength and wisdom to lead Israel in battle.
