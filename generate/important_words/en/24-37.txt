Jeremiah: A prophet in the Old Testament who received God's word and delivered it to the people.
Babylon: An ancient city and empire in Mesopotamia, known for its military and cultural strength. King Nebuchadnezzar was one of its most famous rulers.
Nebuchadnezzar: King of Babylon who conquered Jerusalem and led many Jews into exile in Babylon.
Jerusalem: The capital of ancient Israel and the kingdom of Judah, an important religious and political center for the Jews.
Exile: Involuntary banishment from one's homeland, often due to political or religious reasons. The Jews were taken into exile in Babylon after Jerusalem fell.
Prophecy: A message or revelation from God, usually concerning future events, delivered through a prophet.
The word of the Lord: An expression referring to the divine messages and instructions that God gives to his prophets.
Judgment: God's just punishment or reward for human actions, often related to their faithfulness toward him.
Repentance: To change one's heart, mind, and lifestyle in order to turn away from sin and toward God.
