Israel: A people and nation whom God chose as His special people, and about which the book of Hosea speaks.
Hosea: A prophet in the Old Testament who delivered God's message to Israel.
Sin: Evil actions or thoughts that break God's will and laws.
Punishment: The consequences of breaking God's laws, often described as suffering or destruction.
To turn back: To repent of one's sins and seek forgiveness from God in order to restore a good relationship with Him.
Calf: An idol that the Israelites worshipped, which was forbidden by God and led to punishment.
Egypt: A country in North Africa where the people of Israel had previously been slaves. Used in the book of Hosea as a symbol of oppression and sin.
Assyria: A powerful nation in ancient times that conquered and deported parts of the people of Israel.
Wind: In the book of Hosea used as an image of emptiness and worthlessness, especially in connection with false gods and idolatry.
Birds: A symbol of God's judgment and punishment over the people of Israel, where the birds represent hostile nations that will attack them.
