Esther: The main character in the book, a Jewish woman who becomes queen of Persia and saves her people from destruction.
Mordecai: Esther's adoptive father and a central figure in the story. He uncovers a conspiracy and helps Esther save the Jews.
Haman: A high-ranking Persian official and the main antagonist in the book. He plans to destroy the Jews, but his schemes are eventually exposed and thwarted.
Ahasuerus: The king of Persia, also known as Xerxes I, who marries Esther and allows her to save the Jews after Haman's plans are revealed.
Purim: A Jewish festival that celebrates the deliverance of the Jewish people from Haman's evil plot, as told in the book of Esther.
Decree: An official order or law issued by a king or other authority. In the book of Esther, Ahasuerus issues several decrees that shape the events.
Loyalty: Faithfulness and commitment to a person, cause, or group. In the book of Esther, both Esther and Mordecai show loyalty to God and the Jewish people.
