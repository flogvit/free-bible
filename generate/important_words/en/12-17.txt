Israel: A people and nation in the Middle East, chosen by God as his special possession and entered into a covenant with him.
Assyria: An ancient empire in Mesopotamia, known for its military strength and conquests, including the invasion and occupation of Israel.
Samaria: The capital city of the northern kingdom of Israel, conquered by the Assyrians in 2 Kings 17.
Sin: An action or condition that breaks God's law and morality, leading to separation from God.
Idolatry: To worship and honor other gods or divine beings besides the true God, which is forbidden according to the covenant between God and Israel.
Covenant: A binding agreement between two parties, in this case between God and Israel, containing promises and obligations from both sides.
Prophets: Men and women chosen by God to convey his message and instructions to the people and leaders of Israel.
Upheaval: A radical change or transformation, often used regarding political, social, or religious events.
Exile: Banishment or forced relocation of a people from their homeland, often as punishment or as a result of war and conquest.
