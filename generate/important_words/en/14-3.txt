Solomon: King Solomon, son of King David, who built the first temple in Jerusalem to house the Ark of the Covenant and honor God.
Temple: A holy place in Jerusalem where the Israelites worshiped God and performed religious ceremonies; built by King Solomon as a dwelling for God's presence.
Lebanon: A country in the Middle East, known for its cedar trees; Solomon obtained cedars from Lebanon to use in building the temple.
Gold: A precious metal symbolizing wealth and divinity; used abundantly in the construction of the temple to show respect and reverence for God.
Ark of the Covenant: A gold-covered chest containing the two stone tablets with the Ten Commandments, a symbolic representation of God's covenant with the people of Israel.
Cherub: Supernatural beings, often described as winged creatures, who served as guardians and attendants to God; two large cherubs were made of gold and placed in the Most Holy Place of the temple.
Most Holy Place: The innermost and most sacred room of the temple, where the Ark of the Covenant was kept and where the high priest entered once a year on the Day of Atonement to ask for forgiveness for the nation's sins.
