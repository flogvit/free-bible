Wisdom: The ability to think and act with understanding, insight, and sound judgment, regarded as a virtue in the book of Proverbs.
Understanding: The capacity to evaluate situations and people in a wise and intelligent manner, enabling one to make good decisions and avoid problems.
Righteousness: The principle of acting rightly and morally, both toward oneself and others. Righteous people follow God's laws and principles.
Unrighteousness: Actions or attitudes that contradict God's laws and principles, leading to injustice and harm for others.
Discipline: Training and instruction aimed at correcting and guiding a person to live a righteous and moral life.
Work: The effort and commitment required to achieve results and goals. Work is valued in the book of Proverbs, as it leads to success and prosperity.
Slothfulness: A lack of initiative and willingness to work, often leading to poverty and failure. Slothfulness is strongly criticized in the book of Proverbs.
Wealth: Material goods and resources that provide financial security and prosperity. Wealth can be a blessing, but also a source of temptation and problems.
Poverty: A lack of material resources and financial security, which can lead to hardship and suffering. Poverty is not necessarily a curse, but a test.
Tongue: A symbol of human speech and communication. The tongue holds great power, as it can be used both to bless and to curse, to encourage and to hurt.
