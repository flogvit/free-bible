Sin offering: An offering given to God to atone for a sin or impurity, usually in the form of an animal that is sacrificed.
Guilt offering: An offering given to God to make restitution for a specific guilt or transgression, often linked to compensation for damages or injustices.
Priest: A religious leader and mediator between God and people, responsible for performing rituals and conveying God's message.
Sacrificial animal: An animal sacrificed to God as part of a ritual, often to atone for sins or impurity.
Blood: An important part of the offering process, symbolizing life and cleansing. The blood from the sacrificial animal was used in ritual actions to cleanse and sanctify.
Fat: The portion of the sacrificial animal that was burned on the altar as a pleasing aroma to God; it represented the best part of the animal and humanity's devotion to God.
Impurity: A state of spiritual defilement that could be transmitted through contact with certain objects, animals, or people. Impurity had to be removed through ritual actions before one could approach God.
Day of Atonement: An annual festival where priests were to perform special rituals to cleanse the tabernacle, the people, and themselves from sins and impurity.
