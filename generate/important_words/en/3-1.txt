Burnt Offering: A sacrifice that was completely burned on the altar, often consisting of animals, to seek God's favor and forgiveness.
Altar: A sacred structure where sacrifices and rituals were performed according to Jewish law.
Sons of Israel: The term for the people of Israel, descendants of Jacob, who was also known as Israel.
Sacrificial Animal: An animal used in sacrifices, typically domesticated animals such as sheep, goats, and cattle.
Internal Organs: The inner organs of an animal, removed and washed before the burnt offering.
Fat Pieces: Special portions of fat from the sacrificial animal that were burned on the altar as a pleasing aroma to God.
Priest: A person responsible for performing religious ceremonies and sacrifices in Jewish society.
