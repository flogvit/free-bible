The Ten Commandments: Fundamental moral rules given by God to Moses on Mount Sinai.
Moses: The prophet and leader who led the Israelites out of slavery in Egypt and received the Ten Commandments from God.
Mount Sinai: The place where God gave Moses the Ten Commandments and made a covenant with the Israelites.
Israelites: A people descended from Jacob, also called the children of Israel, whom God freed from slavery in Egypt.
Idols: Represent false gods or idols, which are forbidden according to the first commandment.
Sabbath day: The seventh day of the week, on which God rested after creating the world. It is to be kept holy according to the fourth commandment.
Parents: Mother and father, whom one must honor and respect according to the fifth commandment.
Murder: Taking another person's life, which is forbidden according to the sixth commandment.
Adultery: Sexual immorality and unfaithfulness, forbidden according to the seventh commandment.
Stealing: Taking property that belongs to others, which is forbidden according to the eighth commandment.
False testimony: Lying, especially in legal contexts, which is forbidden according to the ninth commandment.
Coveting: A strong desire for another person's property, spouse, or other things, which is forbidden according to the tenth commandment.
