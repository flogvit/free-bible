Sacrifice: Ritual actions where gifts, often animals, are given to God as a form of worship or gratitude.
Peace offering: A type of sacrifice in the Old Testament where an animal was given to God in thanksgiving and its meat was shared with family and friends.
Sin offering: A sacrifice performed to atone for sin and receive forgiveness from God.
Bread: Food made from grain flour and water, used in religious ceremonies as a gift to God.
Fat: The most nutritious and valuable part of an animal, often burned as a gift to God.
Blood: Life-giving fluid in living beings, regarded as holy and used in certain sacrificial rituals.
High priest: The highest religious leader among Israel's priesthood, responsible for performing special rituals and maintaining contact between God and the people.
Priest: A religious leader who performed sacrifices and other ceremonies in service to God and the people.
Holy: Something dedicated to God and set apart from what is common or profane.
