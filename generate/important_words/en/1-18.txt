Abraham: The patriarch of the people of Israel and founder of the Hebrew nation, with whom God made a covenant.
The Lord: A title often used for God in the Old Testament, referring to his sovereignty and authority.
Mamre: A place near Hebron where Abraham settled and built an altar to the Lord. It was here that God visited him in chapter 18.
Hospitality: An important value in Middle Eastern cultures, including Abraham's time. Showing hospitality means welcoming others well and caring for their needs.
Angels: Heavenly beings who serve as God's messengers and carry out his will. In this story, three men visit Abraham, and they are later identified as angels.
Sodom and Gomorrah: Two cities that, according to the Bible, were destroyed by God because of their wickedness and immorality.
Righteousness: The concept refers to acting rightly and in accordance with God's will and moral standards. Abraham asks God to spare Sodom and Gomorrah if there are righteous people there.
Lot: Abraham's nephew, who lived in Sodom and was rescued by the angels before the city's destruction.
