Ruth: The main character in the Book of Ruth, a Moabite woman who converts to Judaism and becomes an ancestor of King David.
Naomi: Ruth's mother-in-law, an Israelite who returns to Bethlehem after losing her husband and two sons in Moab.
Boaz: A wealthy relative of Naomi's deceased husband, who marries Ruth and becomes her kinsman-redeemer.
Kinsman-redeemer: A close relative obligated to marry the widow of a deceased family member to continue his lineage and preserve his name.
Harvest: The season when grain and other crops are gathered from the fields. Ruth works in Boaz's field during the harvest.
Grace: God's unmerited favor or love. Ruth experiences grace through Boaz's care and protection.
Loyalty: Faithfulness and reliability in a relationship, especially between people and God. Ruth's loyalty to Naomi and to God is central to the story.
Marriage: A lifelong covenant between man and woman, where they become one in God's sight. The marriage of Ruth and Boaz leads to blessing and fruitfulness.
Inheritance: Material goods and possessions passed from one generation to another. Ruth's marriage to Boaz secures the inheritance for Naomi's family.
