Abijah: A king of Judah, son of Rehoboam and grandson of Solomon; he reigned for three years.
Asa: Abijah's son and king of Judah after his death; known for reforming the kingdom and removing idol worship.
Baasha: King of Israel who ruled at the same time as Asa; he attacked Judah and built a fortress at Ramah.
Ben-Hadad: King of Aram-Damascus who formed an alliance with Asa against Baasha.
Judah: One of the two Israelite kingdoms, located south of Israel, ruled by kings from David's line.
Israel: The northern kingdom consisting of ten tribes, separated from Judah after Solomon's death.
Ramah: A city near the border between Judah and Israel, where Baasha built a fortress to control traffic.
Prophet: A person who speaks on behalf of God and delivers His message to the people.
Idol worship: The practice of worshipping false gods or idols instead of the true God.
Alliance: An agreement between two or more parties to work together toward a common goal.
Reform: Improvement or change of something to make it better or more just.
