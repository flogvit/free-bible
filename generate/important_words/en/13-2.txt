Abraham: An important patriarch in Judaism, Christianity and Islam, who is regarded as the father of the people of Israel.
Isaac: Abraham's son and another central figure in the Bible, father of Jacob and Esau.
Jacob: Son of Isaac and grandson of Abraham, also known as Israel. He had twelve sons who became the founders of the twelve tribes of Israel.
Esau: Jacob's older twin brother and son of Isaac. He sold his birthright to Jacob for a bowl of lentil stew.
Judah: One of Jacob's twelve sons and founder of the tribe of Judah. He is an important ancestor of Jesus according to the Gospel of Matthew.
Caleb: A prominent leader among the children of Israel and one of the twelve spies that Moses sent to explore Canaan. He was one of the few who were allowed to enter the promised land.
David: King of Israel and the kingdom of Judah, and a central figure in the Old Testament. He is known for his skill as a warrior and poet, and as the author of many of the Psalms.
