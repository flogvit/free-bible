Nebuchadnezzar: King of Babylon, who conquered and destroyed Jerusalem and led the Jews into exile.
Egypt: An ancient kingdom in Northeast Africa, often in conflict with the people of Israel and other nations in the region.
The Nile: The longest river in Africa and a vital resource for Egyptian civilization.
Pharaohs: Rulers of ancient Egypt, often regarded as gods by their people.
Army: An organized military force, used here to refer to both Egyptian and Babylonian forces.
Sword: A symbol of war and destruction, frequently used in prophetic texts to describe God's judgment against nations.
The Lord: A title often used for God in the Old Testament, especially when He acts with justice and judgment.
Judgment: God's act of punishing nations for their sins and rebellion against His will.
God of Jacob: Another way to refer to God, emphasizing His close relationship with Israel and their ancestor Jacob.
Exile: A period during which the people of Israel were taken captive and lived in a foreign land as a consequence of their disobedience to God.
