Tyre: An ancient city in Phoenicia, known for its wealth and commercial power; described as a symbolic representation of worldly pride and luxury.
Lord: A title used to refer to God, the Almighty Creator, in religious texts.
Ships: Waterborne means of transport, often used for trade and navigation; symbolizing Tyre's commercial power and influence.
Merchants: People engaged in buying and selling goods, especially over long distances; representing Tyre's economic activity and prosperity.
Beauty: Aesthetic appeal or pleasing appearance; used to describe Tyre's splendor and greatness.
Sea: A large body of water covering much of the earth's surface; here used as a metaphor for the peoples and nations with whom Tyre had contact through trade.
Judgment: God's righteous punishment or consequence for human actions; in this case, judgment upon Tyre for their pride and idolatry.
Destruction: The process of being ruined or reduced to rubble; used to describe God's punishment against Tyre and their inevitable fall.
