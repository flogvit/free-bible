Saul: First king of Israel, chosen by God and anointed by the prophet Samuel.
Nahash: King of the Ammonites who threatened to attack Jabesh-Gilead and demanded humiliating terms for peace.
Jabesh-Gilead: A city east of the Jordan River that was under threat of attack from the Ammonites.
Ammonites: A people living east of the Jordan River who were enemies of Israel.
Samuel: Prophet and judge in Israel, who anointed both Saul and David as kings.
The People: The inhabitants of Israel, who gathered behind Saul to fight against the Ammonites.
Victory: The term used to describe the positive outcome of a conflict or battle, here Saul's victory over the Ammonites.
Gilead: A region east of the Jordan River where Jabesh-Gilead was located.
Offering: A gift to God, often in the form of an animal, food, or drink, as a way to show gratitude, reverence, or seek God's favor.
Gilgal: A holy place in Israel where Saul was officially installed as king after his victory over the Ammonites.
