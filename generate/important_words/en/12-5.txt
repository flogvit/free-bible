Naaman: A Syrian army commander who suffers from leprosy and seeks healing from the prophet Elisha.
Elisha: An Israelite prophet who succeeds the prophet Elijah and performs miracles by God's power.
Leprosy: A contagious skin disease that leads to deformity and disability, heavily stigmatized in biblical times.
The Jordan River: A river in the Middle East important for several biblical stories, including Naaman's healing.
Healing: The process of restoring health and well-being, often through divine intervention in biblical narratives.
Gehazi: Elisha's servant who becomes greedy and lies to obtain Naaman's gifts; as punishment, he and his descendants are struck with leprosy.
Syria: A country in the Middle East from which Naaman comes, and which had political and military conflicts with Israel during this period.
Faithfulness: Loyalty and trust in God and His prophets, essential for Naaman's healing and Gehazi's downfall.
