Josiah: A king of Judah known for his piety and reforms in the land.
Passover: A Jewish festival celebrating Israel's deliverance from slavery in Egypt, commanded by God in 2 Mosebok.
The Lord's house: Also known as the temple in Jerusalem, a holy place where the Israelites worshipped God.
Sacrifice: An act in which people offered animals or other gifts to God as a form of worship and gratitude.
Priests: Religious leaders who performed ceremonies and sacrifices in the Lord's house on behalf of the people.
Levites: Members of the tribe of Levi, who had special duties and responsibilities related to worship and service in the temple.
Burnt offering: A sacrifice in which an animal was burned on an altar as a symbolic act to honor and worship God.
Sin offering: An offering given to seek atonement and forgiveness for sins committed by individuals or the whole community.
Statute: A law or rule given by God to Moses and passed down to the Israelites to guide their lives and worship.
