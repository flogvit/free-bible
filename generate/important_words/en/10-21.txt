Gibeonites: A Canaanite tribe that made a covenant with the Israelites under Joshua and became servants in the temple.
Famine: A period of food shortage that can lead to hunger and death; in 2 Samuel 21, the famine is a result of God's anger toward Israel.
David: King over the kingdoms of Israel and Judah, the central figure in 2 Samuel. He seeks to restore the relationship between God and Israel by making amends for wrongs done against the Gibeonites.
Ishbosheth: Saul's son, also known as Eshbaal. One of his brothers is killed in chapter 21 as part of David's attempt to make restitution for the injustice toward the Gibeonites.
Rizpah: One of Saul's concubines who protects the bodies of her two sons and five other relatives after they are killed as a sacrifice to the Gibeonites.
Atonement offering: An offering of something or someone to atone for sins and restore the relationship with God.
Goliath: A giant from the Philistines, famous for his duel with David. In 2 Samuel 21, his brother is killed by one of David's men.
