Jacob: The main character in Genesis 37, son of Isaac and father of twelve sons who become the founders of Israel's twelve tribes.
Joseph: Jacob's beloved son, born to Rachel, who is sold into slavery by his brothers out of jealousy and ends up in Egypt.
Dreams: God's means of communicating with Joseph, giving him prophetic insights and leading to his later success in Egypt.
Brothers: Joseph's half-brothers who become jealous of his dreams and their father's love, selling him as a slave to a merchant caravan.
Rachel: Jacob's favorite wife and mother of Joseph and Benjamin, two of Jacob's twelve sons.
Benjamin: Rachel's second son and Jacob's youngest son, brother of Joseph.
Slave: The status Joseph receives after being sold by his brothers, before he later becomes a powerful man in Egypt.
Potiphar: An Egyptian officer who buys Joseph as a slave, and later has him imprisoned due to false accusations from his wife.
False Accusations: The charges Potiphar's wife brings against Joseph, which lead to his being thrown into prison.
Prison: The place where Joseph is put after being accused of attempting to seduce Potiphar's wife.
