Joshua: The main character in the book of Joshua and leader of the people of Israel after Moses' death.
The Ark of the Lord: A holy chest containing the stone tablets with the Ten Commandments, symbolizing God's presence among the people of Israel.
Jordan: A river in the Middle East that Joshua and the people of Israel cross on their way to the promised land of Canaan.
Canaan: The promised land that God gives to the people of Israel, described as a land flowing with milk and honey.
Twelve Tribes: The twelve groups of the people of Israel, each tribe being descendants of one of Jacob's sons.
Stones: Twelve stones taken from the Jordan River as a memorial to the miracle when God stopped the water so that the people could cross the river.
Gilgal: The place where the Israelites camped after crossing the Jordan River and where they set up a monument with the twelve stones.
Circumcision: A religious ritual in which the foreskin of the penis is removed as a sign of the covenant between God and the people of Israel.
Passover: A Jewish festival celebrating the deliverance of the Israelites from slavery in Egypt.
Jericho: A city in Canaan that was conquered by Joshua and the Israelites through God's help.
