Prophet: A person who conveys messages from God to people, often about future events or warnings.
Jeremiah: The prophet who is the main character in the book of Jeremiah and who delivers God's message to the people of Israel.
Hananiah: A false prophet who contradicts Jeremiah's prophecies and gives the people false hopes that they will soon be freed from the Babylonians.
Babylon: An ancient kingdom and city in Mesopotamia that conquered Jerusalem and led many Jews into exile.
Exile: The state of being forced away from one's homeland, as the Jews experienced after Babylon conquered Jerusalem.
Deceit: An act of deception or lying, such as Hananiah commits when he presents false prophecies.
Discipline: Punishment or correction that God uses to guide his people back when they stray from his ways.
Judgment: God's righteous evaluation and punishment of human actions, which Jeremiah conveys in his prophecies.
Temple: The holy place in Jerusalem where the Jews worshiped God and performed sacrificial rituals.
Yoke: A farming tool used for pulling loads; here it symbolizes oppression and slavery under the Babylonians.
