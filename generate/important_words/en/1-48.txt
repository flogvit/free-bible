Jacob: The grandfather of Ephraim and Manasseh, son of Isaac and father of Joseph. One of the patriarchs in the Bible.
Joseph: Jacob's eldest son with Rachel, brother to Benjamin and half-brother to his other siblings. An important figure in Genesis who becomes governor of Egypt.
Ephraim: The younger son of Joseph and Asenath, grandson of Jacob. He and his descendants become one of Israel's twelve tribes.
Manasseh: The older son of Joseph and Asenath, grandson of Jacob. He and his descendants also become one of Israel's twelve tribes.
Blessing: A positive statement or action performed by God or people, often to ask for prosperity, protection, or happiness for someone.
Israel: The name given to Jacob after his struggle with an angel. It also refers to the united people of Jacob's twelve sons and their descendants.
Adopt: To take in a child from another family and treat them as one's own child. In Genesis 48, Jacob adopts Joseph's two sons, Ephraim and Manasseh.
Right hand: Symbolizes strength, authority, and honor. In this context, Jacob places his right hand on Ephraim's head to bless him.
Crossing hands: An action performed by Jacob when he blesses his grandsons, Ephraim and Manasseh. This symbolizes that Ephraim, though younger, will become greater than his older brother Manasseh.
