Samuel: A prophet and judge in Israel who anoints Saul as king and guides him.
Saul: The first king of Israel, chosen by God through Samuel, but later rejected due to disobedience.
Anointing: A ritual act where oil is poured or rubbed on a person to mark them as chosen by God for a special task, such as being king.
Prophecy: A divinely inspired message delivered by a prophet, often concerning future events or God's will.
The Spirit: God's Spirit, which gives power, wisdom, and guidance to people, especially those chosen for special tasks.
The Ammonites: A people who lived east of the Jordan River and were enemies of Israel. In 1 Samuel 10, Saul's victory over the Ammonites is seen as a sign of his royal authority.
Nahash: The king of the Ammonites, who threatens to attack the city of Jabesh-Gilead. Saul defeats him and saves the city.
Gilead: A region east of the Jordan River, where the city of Jabesh-Gilead is located.
Mizpah: A place in Israel where the people gather to confirm Saul's kingship.
