Aron: Brother of Moses and the first high priest in Israel, chosen by God to serve as a priest.
Moses: Prophet and leader of the people of Israel, who led them out of Egypt and received the law from God on Mount Sinai.
High Priest: The highest religious leader in Israel, responsible for performing sacrifices and other ritual acts in the tabernacle or temple.
Consecration: The ceremony in which Aaron and his sons were consecrated as priests, including anointing with oil and being clothed in priestly garments.
Sacrifice: A gift to God, usually consisting of animals or food items, that was burned on an altar as a symbolic act to honor God and seek atonement for sins.
Anointing Oil: A special mixture of olive oil and spices used to anoint priests and holy objects, symbolizing God's blessing and presence.
Priestly Garments: Special clothing worn by priests during their service, including the ephod, breastplate, robe, turban, and sash, which symbolized their role as servants of God.
Blood: A symbol of life and cleansing in the Old Testament, used in sacrifices and ceremonies to cleanse and sanctify people and objects.
