David: The second king of Israel, who succeeded Saul and united the twelve tribes.
Saul: Israel's first king, who died in battle against the Philistines, after which David became his successor.
Hebron: The city where David was anointed king over Judah and later over all Israel.
Ish-Bosheth: Saul's son, who was appointed king over Israel by Abner but was later murdered.
Abner: Saul's army commander, who first supported Ish-Bosheth but later switched his loyalty to David.
Joab: David's army commander and nephew, who killed Abner in revenge for his brother's death.
Gibeon: The place where a battle was fought between the men of Abner and Joab, resulting in many deaths.
Anointing: A ceremony in which a person is appointed and blessed by God, as when David was anointed king.
Unity: The term describing the process by which David gathered and strengthened the kingdom of Israel under his leadership.
Loyalty: Faithfulness and support for a leader or cause, as shown by Abner and Joab in their actions.
