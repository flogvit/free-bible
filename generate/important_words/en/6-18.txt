Joshua: The main character in the book of Joshua, a leader of the people of Israel and Moses' successor.
Territories: The regions that God had promised to give to the tribes of Israel as their inheritance and homeland.
Inheritance: A gift or property passed from one generation to another, especially within the tribes of Israel.
Tribes: The twelve groups of Israelites descended from Jacob's twelve sons. In the book of Joshua, each tribe is assigned a territory.
Casting lots: An ancient method for deciding important matters or distributing resources, based on chance. In the book of Joshua, this was used to allocate territories among the tribes of Israel.
Sanctuary: A place dedicated to worship, often a temple or altar. In the book of Joshua, this refers to the Tabernacle, the temporary center of worship before the temple was built in Jerusalem.
Shiloh: A city in Canaan that served as an important religious center for the Israelites and where the Tabernacle was placed during Joshua's leadership.
Canaan: The ancient name for the region later known as Israel and Palestine, which God promised to give to the people of Israel as their homeland.
