Solomon: The king of Israel and son of King David, who built the temple in Jerusalem to house the Ark of the Covenant and honor God.
Temple: A sacred building dedicated to God, where people came to worship and offer sacrifices. Solomon's temple was the first temple in Jerusalem.
Ark of the Covenant: A holy object that contained the two stone tablets with the Ten Commandments, given by God to Moses on Mount Sinai. It represented God's presence among the people of Israel.
Dedication: A ceremony marking the official opening or consecration of a place or object to God. Solomon dedicated the temple with prayer, sacrifice, and celebration.
Prayer: Communication with God, usually in the form of thanksgiving, praise, repentance, or requests for help and guidance.
The glory of the Lord: A manifestation of God's presence and power, often described as a shining cloud. The glory of the Lord filled the temple after Solomon had offered his prayer of dedication.
Sacrifice: A gift, often in the form of animals, food, or incense, presented to God as an act of worship, gratitude, or atonement for sin.
Covenant: An agreement between God and people, where God promises blessings and protection in exchange for obedience and faithfulness.
