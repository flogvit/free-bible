Vanity: A feeling of meaninglessness and emptiness described in Ecclesiastes, where life's activities seem futile and without lasting value.
Futile: When something lacks meaning, purpose, or benefit, and does not lead to a desired outcome.
Time: A period or moment when events take place. Ecclesiastes emphasizes that there is a time for everything under heaven, both good and bad events.
Work: The effort and activities people undertake to achieve a goal, whether it is earning money, creating something, or reaching another form of success.
Wealth: Material goods and resources that people accumulate through work, inheritance, or other means. Ecclesiastes points out the emptiness of gathering wealth, as it does not bring lasting happiness or meaning.
Pleasure: Enjoyment and joy that can arise from various sources, such as food, drink, work, and relationships. Ecclesiastes argues that pleasure too can be futile and empty.
Gift: Something given or received, whether material or immaterial. Ecclesiastes claims that the ability to enjoy life is a gift from God.
Destiny: A predetermined course or plan for one's life. Ecclesiastes suggests that human destiny is in God's hands, and that what is determined cannot be changed.
Wisdom: Knowledge, understanding, and insight into life and its mysteries. Ecclesiastes acknowledges the value of wisdom but also recognizes its limitations.
