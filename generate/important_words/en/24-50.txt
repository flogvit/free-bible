Babylon: An ancient city and empire in Mesopotamia, often regarded as an enemy of Israel and Judah in the Bible.
The Lord: Another name for God, used to show respect and reverence.
Israel: The name of God's chosen people, also known as Hebrews, and the land they inhabited in the Old Testament.
Judah: One of the twelve tribes of Israel and a kingdom in the Old Testament, also used as a name for the people and the region.
Exile: The period when the Israelites were held captive and enslaved in Babylon.
Restoration: The process of restoring something to its original state; here it refers to the restoration of Israel and Judah after their time in exile.
Enemies: Individuals or nations who oppose God's people, often used to describe those who attack or oppress Israel and Judah.
Punishment: The consequences or retribution for an action, especially for sin and evil.
The Lord's sword: A symbol of God's power and justice, often used to describe his judgment against wicked nations and people.
Leaders: Rulers or high-ranking officials; here it refers to the leaders of the nations that oppose God's people.
