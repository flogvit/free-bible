Wisdom: The ability to understand and use knowledge in a good and effective way, often associated with sound judgment and moral behavior.
Understanding: The ability to think and learn from experiences, as well as the capacity to comprehend and evaluate situations and problems.
Discipline: Training or correction intended to guide, correct, and shape a person's character and conduct.
Knowledge: Information, facts, and skills acquired through experience, education, and observation.
Insight: The ability to perceive and interpret meaning or context in something, especially complex ideas and concepts.
Fear: A feeling of awe and reverence, particularly toward God, that leads to obedience and submission.
Righteousness: The quality of being right and just in actions, attitudes, and morality, in harmony with God's will and laws.
Faithfulness: Being loyal, steadfast, and trustworthy, especially in relation to God and other people.
Humility: A personal trait where one acknowledges their limitations and weaknesses, showing respect and submission toward God and others.
