Joshua: The main character in the book of Joshua, a leader of the people of Israel after Moses' death, who led them into the promised land of Canaan.
Canaan: The area that God had promised to give to the people of Israel, and which Joshua led them into to conquer and settle there.
Servant of the Lord: A title used to describe Joshua, showing his obedience and devotion to God and His will.
Conquer: To take control of a territory or nation by using military force, as Joshua and the Israelites did with Canaan.
Territories: The geographical areas that were divided among the twelve tribes of Israel, so that each tribe received its own land.
Tribes: The twelve groups of the people of Israel who were descendants of Jacob's twelve sons. Each tribe was assigned a territory in Canaan.
Alliances: The agreements between Joshua and the Israelites and the various Canaanite kings and cities they fought against and made treaties with.
Ark of the Covenant: A holy chest that contained the stone tablets with the Ten Commandments, and which symbolized God's presence among the people of Israel.
