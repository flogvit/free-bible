Assyrians: An ancient people and empire in Mesopotamia, known for their military power and conquests.
Ashdod: A city on the coast of the Mediterranean Sea, now part of Israel, which was attacked and besieged by the Assyrians.
Tartan: A high-ranking military officer in the Assyrian Empire who led the attack on Ashdod.
Sargon: King of Assyria (722–705 BC), who sent Tartan to conquer Ashdod.
Isaiah: The prophet to whom the book's message is attributed, and who delivered God's warnings and promises to the people of Israel.
Nakedness: Symbolizes vulnerability and humiliation, and in this case shows how God intends to punish Egypt and Cush for their pride and reliance on their own power.
Egypt: An ancient empire in Northeast Africa that was often in conflict with Israel and other nations in the region.
Cush: A region south of Egypt, in present-day Sudan, which had close political and cultural ties with Egypt.
