God: The almighty Creator who, according to Genesis, created the heavens, the earth, and all life.
Created: The term used to describe God's act of bringing the universe and everything in it into existence.
Heaven: The upper part of the universe, including the atmosphere and outer space, created by God in Genesis.
Earth: The planet we live on, which was created by God as a place for life and human existence.
Life: All living organisms, including plants, animals, and humans, which God created during the six days of creation.
Day: A period of time used in Genesis to describe the order and duration of God's creative acts.
Light: The first element God created, separating day from night and making sight and growth possible.
Water: A fundamental building block for all life, separated by God into two areas – the sea and the waters above.
Land: Dry ground, separated from the water by God, providing a place for plants, animals, and humans to live.
Plants: Living organisms that produce oxygen and nourishment, created by God to provide food and sustenance for animals and humans.
Fish: Aquatic animals that God created to populate the seas and serve as a food source for other creatures.
Birds: Flying animals that God created to fill the sky and serve as a food source for other creatures.
Animals: Living organisms that move and have senses, created by God to populate the earth and serve as food sources and helpers for humans.
Humans: The highest form of life, created by God in his own image, with responsibility to rule over and care for the earth and all its creatures.
Image: Likeness and representation of God, in which humanity was created, giving humans a unique position among all other creatures.
