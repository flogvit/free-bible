Jerusalem: The capital city of ancient Israel and the kingdom of Judah, central to Jeremiah's prophecies.
Nebuchadnezzar: King of Babylon who conquered Jerusalem and destroyed the temple, an event prophesied by Jeremiah.
Prophecy: A divine revelation or message delivered through a prophet, often concerning future events.
Judgment: God's righteous punishment for human sin and disobedience, a central theme in the book of Jeremiah.
Repentance: The process of turning away from sin and returning to God, which Jeremiah urges the people to do.
The Lord: The title used to refer to God in the Old Testament, highlighting His sovereignty and power.
David: The second king of Israel and ancestor of the messianic line, mentioned in Jeremiah's prophecies about a righteous descendant.
Zedekiah: The last king of Judah before Jerusalem fell; he sought Jeremiah's counsel but did not follow it.
Babylon: An ancient empire in Mesopotamia that conquered Jerusalem and exiled the Jews, as prophesied by Jeremiah.
