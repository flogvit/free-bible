Nehemiah: A leader in Israel who helped rebuild the walls of Jerusalem and reform the Jewish community.
Temple: A holy place where Jews worshiped God and offered sacrifices; in Jerusalem, the temple was the center for religious activity.
Tobiah: An Ammonite official who opposed Nehemiah and the rebuilding of Israel; he had unlawfully obtained a room in the temple.
Levites: Members of the tribe of Levi who had special religious duties, including service in the temple and teaching the people God's laws.
Sabbath: The seventh day of the week, a day of rest dedicated to God, where work was to cease and people were to focus on spiritual matters.
Tithe: A tenth part of income or harvest given to the temple and priests to support their work and help the poor.
God's law: The rules and regulations that God gave his people through Moses and other prophets, including the Ten Commandments and many other instructions.
Purity: Being free from sin and impurity, both spiritual and physical, which was important for being able to worship God and participate in his service.
Marriage: A holy covenant between a man and a woman, which in Nehemiah's time was complicated by mixed marriages with non-Jewish peoples.
