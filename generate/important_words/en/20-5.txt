Wisdom: The ability to understand and apply knowledge in an effective and ethical way, regarded as a virtue in the book of Proverbs.
Understanding: The ability to judge situations and people correctly and insightfully, often linked with wisdom and prudence.
Prudence: A combination of wisdom, understanding, and experience that leads to good decisions and actions.
Discipline: Self-control or training necessary to live a moral and ethical life.
Injustice: Unethical or wrongful acts that go against morality and justice.
Warning: An exhortation to be cautious or attentive in order to avoid dangers and bad consequences.
Loyalty: Faithfulness and trust toward others, especially in marriage and close relationships.
Temptation: A desire or urge to do something that may be harmful, immoral, or unethical.
Foolishness: Lack of caution, care, or judgment, which can lead to poor choices and consequences.
Righteousness: Acting in accordance with moral principles and ethical standards, characterized by honesty and integrity.
