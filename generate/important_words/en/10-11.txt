David: King of Israel and central figure in 2 Samuel 11, who commits both adultery and murder.
Batseba: A beautiful woman whom David is attracted to, and with whom he later has an affair.
Uria: Bathsheba's husband and a soldier in David's army, whom David arranges to be killed in battle to cover up his adultery.
Joab: David's military commander, who helps David arrange Uriah's death by placing him in a dangerous situation during combat.
The prophet Nathan: God's spokesperson who confronts David about his sins and delivers God's judgment against him.
Sin: An action or behavior that goes against God's will and moral law. In this context, it refers to David's adultery and murder.
Punishment: The consequences or response from God due to sin. In 2 Samuel 11, the punishment for David's sins is that his own household will experience violence and unrest.
Repentance: The feeling of sorrow for one's past actions and the desire to change. David deeply repented after being confronted by the prophet Nathan.
Forgiveness: God's grace and mercy despite human sin. Although David is punished for his actions, God also shows grace by forgiving him.
