# Micah

**About the book:** Micah was a prophet from Moresheth who prophesied during the reigns of kings Jotham, Ahaz, and Hezekiah in Judah (approximately 750–686 B.C.). The book contains prophecies concerning judgment against both Samaria and Jerusalem due to the people’s sin and the corruption of their leaders, but also powerful promises of restoration and a coming ruler from Bethlehem.

**Main theme:** God judges His people for social injustice, corrupt leadership, and idolatry, yet He promises restoration and forgiveness through His faithful love.

## Outline

- **Judgment against Samaria and Judah** (Chapter 1)
- **Israel’s sins and God’s judgment** (Chapter 2)
- **The leaders’ injustice and the judgment on Jerusalem** (Chapter 3)
- **Future vision: Peace and the gathering of Israel** (Chapter 4)
- **The ruler from Bethlehem and Israel’s victory** (Chapter 5)
- **God’s demand for justice and mercy** (Chapter 6)
- **Complaint, hope, and God’s forgiveness** (Chapter 7)

## Key Themes

- **Social justice:** God condemns those who oppress the weak, steal land, and exploit widows and orphans (2:1–2; 3:1–3).
- **Corrupt leaders:** The prophet accuses leaders, priests, and prophets of accepting bribes and misleading the people (3:5–11).
- **The coming ruler:** From small Bethlehem will come a ruler whose origins are from ancient times, who will be peace for His people (5:1–4).
- **God’s requirement:** What God requires is to act justly, love faithfulness, and walk humbly with your God (6:8).
- **God’s forgiveness:** God is a God who forgives sin and casts all sins into the depths of the sea, remaining faithful to His promises to Abraham and Jacob (7:18–20).
