# Isaiah

**About the book:** The book of Isaiah is the first and longest of the major prophetic books in the Old Testament, with 66 chapters. The prophet Isaiah ministered in Jerusalem under kings Uzziah, Jotham, Ahaz, and Hezekiah (approximately 740–686 B.C.), and his message ranges from judgment over sin to promises of salvation and restoration.

**Main theme:** God is the Holy One of Israel who judges sin, but who also brings salvation and restoration through His servant.

## Outline of Contents

- **Judgment against Judah and Jerusalem** (Chapters 1–5)
- **Isaiah’s call and vision** (Chapter 6)
- **Immanuel and hope for the people** (Chapters 7–12)
- **Oracles against the nations** (Chapters 13–23)
- **The great world judgment and the Lord’s victory** (Chapters 24–27)
- **Woe oracles and promises to Judah** (Chapters 28–35)
- **Historical interlude: The time of Hezekiah** (Chapters 36–39)
- **Comfort for Israel and the call of Cyrus** (Chapters 40–48)
- **The Lord’s servant and Zion’s redemption** (Chapters 49–55)
- **True worship and God’s final restoration** (Chapters 56–66)

## Key Themes

- **The Holy One of Israel:** God is repeatedly referred to as "the Holy One of Israel" – holy and exalted, yet near to His people.
- **Judgment and salvation:** Isaiah proclaims both God’s righteous judgment against sin and His gracious plan for salvation.
- **The Messiah and the Lord’s servant:** Prophecies about a coming Savior, from the Prince of Peace (chapters 9 and 11) to the suffering Servant (chapter 53).
- **Salvation for the nations:** God’s plan of salvation includes not only Israel but all peoples.
- **The faithful remnant:** In the midst of judgment, a holy remnant will be preserved and restored.
