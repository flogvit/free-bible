# Zephaniah

**About the book:** The book of Zephaniah is the ninth of the twelve Minor Prophets in the Old Testament. The prophet Zephaniah ministered during the reign of King Josiah in Judah (ca. 640–609 B.C.) and was of royal descent, a descendant of Hezekiah. The book consists of three chapters containing messages of judgment and hope.

**Main theme:** The day of the Lord brings judgment upon Judah and the nations, but also offers hope of salvation and restoration for a humble remnant.

**Content overview:**
- **The day of the Lord and judgment against Judah** (Chapter 1): Universal judgment, judgment against idolatry and syncretism, judgment against indifference and pride
- **Call to repentance and judgment against the nations** (Chapter 2): A call for the humble to seek the Lord; judgment against the Philistine cities, Moab, Ammon, Cush, and Assyria
- **Woe to Jerusalem and promises of restoration** (Chapter 3): Accusations against Jerusalem and its leaders; God’s purifying judgment; promises of a humble remnant and God’s saving presence

**Key themes:**
- **The day of the Lord:** A near and terrifying day marked by wrath, distress, darkness, and destruction upon those who sin against the Lord (1:14–18).
- **Judgment against idolatry:** God will eradicate the worship of Baal, the worship of the host of heaven, and those who mix the Lord with other gods (1:4–6).
- **Call to humility and repentance:** The humble in the land are urged to seek the Lord, righteousness, and humility so that they may find shelter on the day of wrath (2:3).
- **The humble remnant:** God will leave behind a poor and lowly people who take refuge in the name of the Lord and live righteously (3:12–13).
- **God’s saving presence:** The Lord Himself is in the midst of His people as a mighty Savior, rejoicing over them with gladness and love (3:17).
