# Habakkuk

**About the book:** The book of Habakkuk is the eighth of the twelve minor prophets and stands out because the prophet asks God direct questions about evil and injustice. The book is written as a dialogue between the prophet and God, and it concludes with a powerful psalm that expresses trust in God amid trials.

**Main theme:** God is sovereign over history and will judge evil, and the righteous shall live by their faith.

**Outline of contents:**
- **Habakkuk’s first complaint and God’s answer** (Chapter 1:1–11) – The prophet complains about violence and injustice in Judah, and God answers that He is raising up the Chaldeans as His instrument for judgment.
- **Habakkuk’s second complaint and God’s answer** (Chapter 1:12–2:20) – The prophet asks how God can use a more wicked nation to punish Judah. God responds by affirming that the righteous shall live by their faith, and pronounces five woes against the oppressor.
- **Habakkuk’s prayer and song of praise** (Chapter 3) – A psalm containing a powerful description of a theophany displaying God’s power, ending with the prophet’s confession of trust in God even in times of distress.

**Key themes:**
- **God’s justice:** The prophet struggles with how a holy God can allow evil, but comes to see that God will judge all wrongdoing in His time.
- **Faith amid suffering:** The key statement "the righteous shall live by their faith" (2:4) emphasizes the importance of trusting God even when circumstances are difficult.
- **God’s sovereignty over nations:** God uses even pagan nations like the Chaldeans to carry out His will and judgment.
- **Judgment on pride:** The five woes in chapter 2 warn against greed, violence, exploitation, and idolatry.
- **Trust and worship:** Despite destruction and lack, the prophet expresses joy in the Lord and confesses Him as his strength (3:17–19).
