# Joshua

**About the book:** The Book of Joshua is the sixth book in the Bible and tells of Israel's conquest and settlement in the promised land of Canaan under Joshua's leadership after Moses' death. The book covers the period from Israel's crossing of the Jordan to the distribution of the land among the twelve tribes and Joshua's death.

**Main theme:** God's faithfulness in fulfilling his promises regarding the land, and the importance of obedience to his commands in order to experience his blessings.

**Content overview:**
- Joshua's appointment and God's promise (Chapter 1)
- Spies in Jericho and Rahab's faith (Chapter 2)
- The people of Israel cross the Jordan (Chapters 3–4)
- Circumcision, Passover celebration, and meeting with the commander of the Lord's army (Chapter 5)
- The fall of Jericho (Chapter 6)
- Achan's sin and defeat at Ai (Chapter 7)
- Conquest of Ai and renewal of the covenant at Ebal (Chapter 8)
- The Gibeonites' deception (Chapter 9)
- Conquest of southern Canaan (Chapter 10)
- Conquest of northern Canaan (Chapter 11)
- List of defeated kings (Chapter 12)
- Distribution of the land (Chapters 13–21)
- Eastern tribes return home and the altar controversy (Chapter 22)
- Joshua's farewell speech and renewal of the covenant (Chapters 23–24)

**Key themes:**
- **God's faithfulness:** The Lord fulfills his promise to the fathers by giving Israel the promised land.
- **Obedience and blessing:** Success in the conquest depends on faithful obedience to God's law and commands.
- **Holy war (herem):** Everything belonging to God must be devoted to him, and disobedience brings serious consequences, as shown in Achan's sin.
- **Distribution of the land:** God gives each tribe its inheritance as a sign of his covenant with Israel.
- **Covenant loyalty:** Israel is called to choose whom they will serve and remain faithful to the Lord alone.
