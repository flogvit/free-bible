# Philippians

**About the book:** The letter to the Philippians is one of Paul’s prison letters, written to the church in Philippi—the first Christian congregation in Europe. Despite writing from imprisonment, the letter is marked by warmth, gratitude, and joy.

**Main theme:** Joy in Christ regardless of external circumstances, along with exhortations to unity and humility following Christ’s example.

**Outline:**
- **Greeting, thanks, and prayer** (Chapter 1:1–11)
- **Paul’s imprisonment and the advance of the gospel** (Chapter 1:12–26)
- **Exhortation to worthy living and unity** (Chapter 1:27–2:4)
- **The Christ hymn: Humility and exaltation** (Chapter 2:5–11)
- **Exhortation to obedience and a pure life** (Chapter 2:12–18)
- **Timothy and Epaphroditus** (Chapter 2:19–30)
- **Warning against false teachers** (Chapter 3:1–11)
- **Pressing toward the goal** (Chapter 3:12–21)
- **Exhortations to unity, joy, and prayer** (Chapter 4:1–9)
- **Thanks for the Philippians’ gift** (Chapter 4:10–23)

**Key themes:**
- **Joy:** Despite being in prison, Paul repeatedly urges believers to rejoice in the Lord (4:4).
- **Humility and Christ’s example:** The Christ hymn (2:5–11) shows how Jesus gave up his divine status and became obedient even to death on a cross.
- **Unity in the church:** Paul encourages believers to be of one mind and stand together in service to the gospel (2:2; 4:2–3).
- **Righteousness by faith:** Paul counts everything as loss so that he may gain Christ and be found in him, having the righteousness that comes from God (3:8–9).
- **Contentment and God’s care:** Paul has learned to be content in every situation, strengthened by Christ (4:11–13).
