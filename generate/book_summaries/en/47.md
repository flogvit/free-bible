# 2 Corinthians

**About the Book:** Second Corinthians is Paul’s most personal letter, written to the church in Corinth to defend his apostolic ministry and restore his relationship with them. The letter was composed after a painful conflict and contains both defense against false teachers and deep teaching on service, suffering, and reconciliation.

**Main Theme:** God’s power is made perfect in weakness, and the apostolic ministry is characterized by suffering, humility, and Christ’s reconciliation.

**Outline of Contents:**
- Comfort in afflictions and Paul’s travel plans (Chapters 1–2)
- The ministry of the new covenant and the glory of the Spirit (Chapter 3)
- The treasure of the gospel in jars of clay (Chapter 4)
- Heavenly hope and the ministry of reconciliation (Chapter 5)
- Life in holiness and separation (Chapter 6)
- Godly sorrow and joy over Titus’s report (Chapter 7)
- The collection for the saints in Jerusalem (Chapters 8–9)
- Paul defends his apostolic authority (Chapter 10)
- Warnings against false apostles and Paul’s sufferings (Chapter 11)
- Paul’s vision, the thorn in the flesh, and power in weakness (Chapter 12)
- Exhortation to self-examination and final greetings (Chapter 13)

**Key Themes:**
- **Comfort in suffering:** God comforts us in all our troubles so that we can comfort others with the comfort we ourselves have received from God (1:3–7).
- **The glory of the new covenant:** The ministry of the Spirit surpasses the ministry of the letter in glory and leads to freedom and transformation (3:6–18).
- **Power in weakness:** God’s power is made perfect in our weakness, and we carry the treasure of the gospel in jars of clay (4:7; 12:9–10).
- **Reconciliation with God:** God reconciled the world to himself in Christ and has given us the ministry of reconciliation (5:18–21).
- **Generous service:** Christian giving flows from grace and leads to thankfulness toward God (8:9; 9:7–15).
