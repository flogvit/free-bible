# Romans

**About the book:** The letter to the Romans is Paul’s most comprehensive and systematic presentation of the Christian gospel, written to the church in Rome which he had not founded himself. The letter was likely written from Corinth around 57 AD, and Paul expresses his desire to visit them on his way to Spain.

**Main theme:** Justification by faith alone – the gospel is God’s power for salvation to everyone who believes, both Jews and Gentiles.

**Content overview:**
- Introduction and Paul’s longing to visit Rome (1:1–17)
- The sin and guilt of all humanity before God (1:18–3:20)
- Justification by faith in Christ (3:21–4:25)
- The fruits of salvation: peace, hope, and reconciliation (5:1–21)
- Freedom from the power of sin through union with Christ (6:1–23)
- The struggle with the law and the flesh (7:1–25)
- Life in the Spirit and eternal security in Christ (8:1–39)
- God’s sovereign plan for Israel and the Gentiles (9:1–11:36)
- Exhortation to holy living and service in the church (12:1–21)
- Relationship with authorities and love as the fulfillment of the law (13:1–14)
- The strong and the weak – Christian freedom and consideration (14:1–15:13)
- Paul’s ministry, travel plans, and greetings (15:14–16:27)

**Key themes:**
- **Justification by faith:** People are declared righteous before God through faith in Jesus Christ, not by works of the law (3:21–28).
- **All have sinned:** Both Jews and Gentiles stand guilty before God and need salvation (3:23).
- **Life in the Spirit:** Believers are freed from the law of sin and death and now live by the power of the Holy Spirit (8:1–17).
- **God’s sovereignty and Israel’s future:** God has not rejected Israel, and all Israel will ultimately be saved (11:26).
- **Practical Christian living:** Faith is demonstrated in a transformed life marked by love for God and others (12:1–2).
