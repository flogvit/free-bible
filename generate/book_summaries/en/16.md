# Nehemiah

**About the book:** The book of Nehemiah tells the story of the Jewish cupbearer Nehemiah, who receives permission from the Persian king Artaxerxes to return to Jerusalem to rebuild the city's walls. The book describes the rebuilding work amid strong opposition, as well as the spiritual and social reforms that followed.

**Main theme:** The rebuilding of Jerusalem’s walls and the renewal of the covenant between God and his people through faithful leadership and obedience to God’s law.

**Outline:**
- Nehemiah’s prayer and the king’s permission (Chapters 1–2)
- Rebuilding the walls of Jerusalem (Chapters 3–4)
- Addressing social injustice (Chapter 5)
- Completion of the wall despite enemy plots (Chapter 6)
- Registration of the people and Ezra’s reading of the law (Chapters 7–8)
- Israel’s confession and renewal of the covenant (Chapters 9–10)
- Repopulation of Jerusalem and dedication of the wall (Chapters 11–12)
- Nehemiah’s reforms (Chapter 13)

**Key themes:**
- **Prayer and dependence on God:** Nehemiah repeatedly turns to God in prayer before making important decisions and when facing opposition.
- **Faithful leadership:** Nehemiah demonstrates selfless leadership by giving up his rights as governor and working alongside the people.
- **Opposition and perseverance:** The enemies Sanballat, Tobiah, and Geshem use mockery, threats, and plots, but the work continues with a sword in one hand.
- **Renewal of the covenant:** The people recommit themselves to follow God’s law, including keeping the Sabbath holy, giving tithes, and avoiding mixed marriages.
- **Social justice:** Nehemiah confronts economic exploitation among the people and ensures that the poor regain their property.
