# Amos

**About the book:** The book of Amos is one of the twelve minor prophets in the Old Testament, written by Amos, a shepherd from Tekoa in Judah. The prophecies were given during the reigns of Uzziah, king of Judah, and Jeroboam II, king of Israel, two years before an earthquake (around 760–750 B.C.).

**Main theme:** God demands social justice and true worship, and will judge Israel for oppressing the poor and practicing hypocritical religion, but ultimately promises restoration.

**Outline:**
- Amos’s call and background (1:1–2)
- Judgment against the nations and Judah (1:3–2:5)
- Judgment against Israel (2:6–16)
- Israel’s sin and coming punishment (3:1–15)
- Criticism of oppression and false worship (4:1–13)
- Lament and call to repentance (5:1–27)
- Woe to the complacent (6:1–14)
- Amos’s visions and confrontation with Amaziah (7:1–17)
- The basket of summer fruit and Israel’s end (8:1–14)
- Judgment and promise of restoration (9:1–15)

**Key themes:**
- **Social justice:** God condemns the oppression of the poor and corruption in the legal system (2:6–7, 5:11–12, 8:4–6).
- **True worship:** God rejects hypocritical religion and empty ritual, demanding that justice and righteousness flow like water (5:21–24).
- **The day of the Lord:** Warnings that the day of the Lord will be darkness, not light, for those who live in sin (5:18–20).
- **God’s sovereignty:** The Lord rules over all nations and holds Israel accountable for being chosen (3:2, 9:7).
- **Hope of restoration:** Despite judgment, God promises to restore the fallen tent of David and bless His people in the future (9:11–15).
