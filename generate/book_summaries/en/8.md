# Ruth

**About the book:** The Book of Ruth is one of the historical books in the Old Testament and tells the beautiful story of the Moabite woman Ruth, her loyalty to her mother-in-law Naomi, and how she became part of God's people. The book takes place during the time of the judges and culminates with Ruth becoming the great-great-grandmother of King David.

**Main theme:** God’s faithful care (hesed) is shown through human loyalty and the role of the kinsman-redeemer, where a foreign woman is incorporated into God's people and into David’s lineage.

## Summary of Contents

- **Naomi’s Tragedy and Return** (Chapter 1)
- **Ruth Gleans Grain in Boaz’s Field** (Chapter 2)
- **Ruth at the Threshing Floor and Boaz’s Promise** (Chapter 3)
- **Boaz Redeems Ruth and the Birth of Obed** (Chapter 4)

## Key Themes

- **Hesed (faithful love):** Both Ruth’s loyalty to Naomi and Boaz’s kindness toward Ruth demonstrate hesed—the faithful, covenantal love.
- **The Kinsman-Redeemer (go'el):** Boaz acts as the redeemer who restores Elimelech’s family line and secures the inheritance, serving as a picture of God’s saving action.
- **God’s Providence:** God guides events so that Ruth "by chance" comes to Boaz’s field, turning tragedy into blessing.
- **Inclusion of Foreigners:** A Moabite woman is incorporated into God’s people and becomes an ancestor in the lineage of David and the Messiah.
- **From Emptiness to Fullness:** Naomi’s bitterness and emptiness are transformed into joy through Ruth’s faithfulness and the birth of Obed.
