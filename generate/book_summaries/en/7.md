# Judges

**About the book:** Judges is the seventh book of the Old Testament and covers the period between Joshua’s death and the establishment of the monarchy in Israel—roughly 300 years of Israelite history. The book describes how God raised up judges (Heb. *shofetim*) to deliver Israel from oppression whenever the people turned away from him.

**Main theme:** Israel’s repeated cycle of sin, oppression, crying out for help, and God’s deliverance through judges—summarized in the words: "In those days there was no king in Israel; everyone did what was right in his own eyes".

**Outline:**
- Introduction: Incomplete conquest and its consequences (ch. 1–2)
- The first judges: Othniel, Ehud, and Shamgar (ch. 3)
- Deborah and Barak (ch. 4–5)
- Gideon (ch. 6–8)
- Abimelech and his downfall (ch. 9)
- Tola and Jair (10:1–5)
- Jephthah (10:6–12:7)
- Ibzan, Elon, and Abdon (12:8–15)
- Samson (ch. 13–16)
- Chaos in Israel: Micah’s idol and the tribe of Dan (ch. 17–18)
- The crime at Gibeah and the civil war against Benjamin (ch. 19–21)

**Key themes:**
- **Cyclical apostasy:** Israel goes through repeated cycles of sin, punishment, repentance, and deliverance—a pattern that reveals humanity’s tendency to forget God.
- **God’s faithful grace:** Despite Israel’s constant turning away, God responds to their cries and raises up deliverers when they return to him.
- **Charismatic leadership:** The judges are not hereditary rulers but individuals empowered by God’s Spirit for specific missions.
- **Consequences of disobedience:** Israel’s failure to completely drive out the Canaanites leads to spiritual and moral decay.
- **The need for righteous leadership:** The book’s closing words point forward to the need for a godly king who can lead the people in the right way.
