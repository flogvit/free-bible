# 3 John

**About the book:** The third letter of John is the shortest letter in the New Testament, written by "the elder" (the apostle John) to a believing man named Gaius. The letter offers insight into challenges in the early church related to hospitality, leadership, and service to the truth.

**Main theme:** An encouragement to faithful hospitality toward traveling preachers and a warning against divisive leadership within the congregation.

**Content overview:**
- Greeting and joy over Gaius's walk in the truth (vv. 1–4)
- Encouragement to show hospitality to traveling brothers (vv. 5–8)
- Warning against Diotrephes's self-exaltation (vv. 9–10)
- Exhortation to follow what is good, with Demetrius as an example (vv. 11–12)
- Closing and wish for a personal meeting (vv. 13–15)

**Key themes:**
- **Walking in the truth:** John expresses his greatest joy that his spiritual children are walking in the truth (vv. 3–4).
- **Christian hospitality:** Believers have a duty to support and welcome those who go out for the sake of the Name (vv. 5–8).
- **True and false leadership:** The contrast between Gaius's faithfulness, Diotrephes's abuse of power, and Demetrius's good testimony shows what right leadership entails (vv. 9–12).
- **Doing good:** Whoever does good is from God, while whoever does evil has not seen God (v. 11).
