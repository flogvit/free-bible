# Colossians

**About the book:** The letter to the Colossians is written by the apostle Paul and Timothy to the church in Colossae, a city in western Asia Minor. It was likely written while Paul was imprisoned, and the church had been founded by Epaphras, one of Paul’s coworkers.

**Main theme:** Christ is perfect and sufficient—in him dwells all the fullness of deity, and believers are complete in him.

**Outline:**
- Greeting, thanksgiving, and prayer (Chapter 1:1–14)
- The hymn to Christ: Christ as head of creation and church (Chapter 1:15–23)
- Paul’s ministry and the mystery revealed (Chapter 1:24–2:5)
- Warning against false teaching and human regulations (Chapter 2:6–23)
- The new life in Christ (Chapter 3:1–17)
- Household rules: Family and work relationships (Chapter 3:18–4:1)
- Exhortation to prayer, wisdom, and final greetings (Chapter 4:2–18)

**Key themes:**
- **Christ’s supreme position:** Christ is the image of the invisible God, the firstborn over all creation, and in him dwells all the fullness of deity bodily (1:15–19; 2:9).
- **Completeness in Christ:** Believers are made complete in Christ and do not need human additions such as philosophy, legalistic works, or ascetic rules (2:10; 2:16–23).
- **The new life:** Since believers have been raised with Christ, they should seek the things above and let the word of Christ dwell richly in them (3:1–4; 3:16).
- **Practical holiness:** The Christian life is shown by putting off sinful behavior, putting on virtues such as love, and living rightly in family and work (3:5–4:1).
