# Titus

**About the book:** The letter to Titus is one of Paul’s pastoral letters, written to his coworker Titus, who was left on Crete to complete the organization of the churches there. The letter gives practical instructions about church leadership, sound teaching, and Christian conduct in the face of false doctrine and cultural challenges.

**Main theme:** Sound teaching must be evident in a godly life marked by good works, because God’s grace has been revealed for the salvation of all people.

**Outline:**
- Introduction and greeting (1:1–4)
- Qualifications for elders and overseers (1:5–9)
- Warning against false teachers, especially those from circumcision (1:10–16)
- Instructions for various groups in the church (2:1–10)
- God’s saving grace and the blessed hope (2:11–15)
- Exhortation to obedience toward authorities and good works (3:1–8)
- Warning against useless arguments and divisive people (3:9–11)
- Personal messages and final greeting (3:12–15)

**Key themes:**
- **Sound teaching (ὑγιαίνουσα διδασκαλία):** Repeated emphasis that instruction must be healthy and lead to a godly life.
- **Good works:** Believers are to be eager for good works as the fruit of salvation, not as its basis.
- **God’s grace and salvation:** Salvation comes from God’s mercy, not our works, through the washing of rebirth and renewal by the Holy Spirit.
- **Church leadership:** Clear qualifications for elders/overseers who must be blameless and hold firmly to the trustworthy message.
- **Rejection of false doctrine:** Warning against Jewish myths, empty talk, and divisive people who must be strongly corrected.
