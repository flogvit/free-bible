# Psalms

**About the book:** The Book of Psalms is a collection of 150 poetic prayers, songs of praise, and laments that were used in Israel’s worship. The book is traditionally divided into five books, and the psalms are attributed to several authors, including David (73 psalms), Asaph, the sons of Korah, Solomon, and Moses.

**Main theme:** The Psalms express the full range of human experience in relation to God—from deep despair to joyful praise—and reveal God’s faithfulness, justice, and mercy.

**Overview of content:**
- **Book I: Davidic psalms on trust and prayer** (Psalms 1–41)
- **Book II: Psalms of the Korahites and David** (Psalms 42–72)
- **Book III: Psalms of Asaph and the Korahites** (Psalms 73–89)
- **Book IV: The Lord’s kingship** (Psalms 90–106)
- **Book V: Thanksgiving, songs of ascent, and praise** (Psalms 107–150)
  - Songs of Ascent (Psalms 120–134)
  - Concluding Hallel psalms (Psalms 146–150)

**Key themes:**
- **God’s faithfulness and grace:** God’s enduring love (hesed) is a recurring theme, especially in Psalm 136 where it is repeated in every verse.
- **Trust in suffering:** Many psalms express lament and distress but end with trust in God as the deliverer (for example, Psalms 22, 23, 42–43).
- **The Lord’s kingship:** God is portrayed as the King of the universe who rules with justice (Psalms 93, 96–99).
- **God’s word and law:** Psalm 119, the longest psalm, is a meditation on the beauty and guidance of God’s word.
- **Praise and worship:** The book culminates in the concluding Hallel psalms that call everything that breathes to praise the Lord (Psalm 150).
