# Jonah

**About the book:** Jonah is one of the twelve minor prophets in the Old Testament. The book tells the dramatic story of the prophet Jonah who flees from God’s call to proclaim judgment against Nineveh, but ultimately learns about God’s boundless mercy toward all nations.

**Main theme:** God’s grace and mercy extend to all peoples, and he desires repentance rather than judgment.

**Outline:**
- **God’s call and Jonah’s flight** (Chapter 1)
- **Jonah’s prayer from the belly of the fish** (Chapter 2)
- **Nineveh’s repentance** (Chapter 3)
- **Jonah’s anger and God’s lesson** (Chapter 4)

**Key themes:**
- **God’s sovereignty:** God rules over nature—the storm, the fish, the plant, and the worm—to accomplish his will.
- **God’s universal grace:** God’s mercy applies not only to Israel but also to the Gentile nations in Nineveh.
- **Repentance:** Both the sailors and the people of Nineveh turn to God when they hear his message.
- **Obedience to God:** Jonah’s disobedience leads to consequences, yet God gives him another chance.
- **God’s character:** Jonah describes God as "gracious and compassionate, slow to anger and rich in love" (4:2).
