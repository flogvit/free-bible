# Ecclesiastes

**About the book:** Ecclesiastes (Hebrew: Qohelet) is a wisdom book in which the author identifies himself as "the son of David, king in Jerusalem" (1:1). The book explores the meaning of life through philosophical reflection on work, wisdom, joy, and mortality, concluding that fearing God is humanity's highest calling.

**Main theme:** Everything under the sun is "hevel" (transient/vanity), but people can find meaning by fearing God and keeping His commandments.

**Content overview:**
- The transience of life and the limits of wisdom (Chapter 1)
- The search for meaning through pleasure, work, and wisdom (Chapter 2)
- Everything has its time – the seasons of life (Chapter 3)
- Oppression, community, and life's injustices (Chapter 4)
- Fearing God, vows, and the emptiness of wealth (Chapters 5–6)
- Words of wisdom and life's contradictions (Chapter 7)
- Authority, justice, and life's unpredictability (Chapter 8)
- Death as a common fate and an encouragement to live (Chapter 9)
- Practical wisdom about folly and leadership (Chapter 10)
- Live generously and rejoice in your youth (Chapter 11)
- The allegory of old age and the book's conclusion (Chapter 12)

**Key themes:**
- **Transience (hevel):** Repeated statements that everything is "vanity" or fleeting – work, wisdom, wealth, and pleasure yield no lasting gain "under the sun."
- **The cyclical nature of life:** Generations come and go, nature repeats itself, and there is "nothing new under the sun" (1:9).
- **God's sovereignty and human limitation:** God has set eternity in the human heart, yet people cannot fully grasp His work from beginning to end (3:11).
- **Joy as a gift:** Despite everything, it is good to eat, drink, and find joy in one's labor – this is a gift from God (2:24; 3:13; 5:18).
- **Fearing God as the conclusion:** The book ends by summarizing all: "Fear God and keep His commandments, for this is the whole duty of man" (12:13).
