# Galatians

**About the book:** The letter to the Galatians was written by the apostle Paul to the churches in Galatia. Paul defends his apostolic authority and warns against false teachers who demand circumcision and works of the law for salvation. The letter is a powerful defense of the gospel of justification by faith alone.

**Main theme:** Justification comes through faith in Christ alone, not by works of the law.

**Outline:**
- **Greeting and defense of Paul’s apostleship** (Chapter 1)
- **Paul defends his gospel and confronts Peter** (Chapter 2)
- **Justification by faith – the example of Abraham** (Chapter 3)
- **From slavery to sonship – the allegory of Hagar and Sarah** (Chapter 4)
- **Christian freedom and the fruit of the Spirit** (Chapter 5)
- **Carrying one another’s burdens and final exhortations** (Chapter 6)

**Key themes:**
- **Justification by faith:** A person is justified by faith in Christ, not by works of the law (2:16; 3:11).
- **Christian freedom:** Christ has set us free from slavery to the law, and we must stand firm in this freedom (5:1).
- **The fruit of the Spirit:** Those who live by the Spirit bear fruit such as love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, and self-control (5:22–23).
- **Unity in Christ:** In Christ there is no distinction between Jew and Greek, slave and free, male and female – all are one in Christ (3:28).
