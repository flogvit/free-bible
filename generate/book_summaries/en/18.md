# Job

**About the book:** The Book of Job is a wisdom book in the Old Testament that explores the question of why righteous people suffer. The story follows the godly and wealthy Job through extreme suffering, philosophical dialogues with friends, and a direct encounter with God.

**Main theme:** God's sovereignty and humanity's limited understanding of the mystery of suffering.

**Content overview:**
- **Prologue: Job's trial** (Chapters 1–2)
- **Job's lament** (Chapter 3)
- **First round of dialogues with friends** (Chapters 4–14)
- **Second round of dialogues with friends** (Chapters 15–21)
- **Third round of dialogues with friends** (Chapters 22–27)
- **Hymn to wisdom** (Chapter 28)
- **Job's final defense speech** (Chapters 29–31)
- **Elihu speaks** (Chapters 32–37)
- **God answers from the storm** (Chapters 38–41)
- **Epilogue: Job's humility and restoration** (Chapter 42)

**Key themes:**
- **The mystery of suffering:** The book rejects a simple connection between sin and suffering, showing that the righteous can suffer without any obvious reason.
- **God's sovereignty:** God is the Creator and Sustainer of all things, and His ways are beyond human understanding.
- **True godliness:** Satan challenges whether Job serves God for gain, but Job proves that genuine faith endures even in suffering.
- **Human limitation:** Through God's speech from the storm, Job learns that humans cannot fully comprehend God's plan.
- **Restoration and grace:** The book ends with God restoring Job and blessing him with twice as much as he had before.
