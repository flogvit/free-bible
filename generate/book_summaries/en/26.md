# Ezekiel

**About the book:** The book of Ezekiel was written by the priest and prophet Ezekiel, who was taken into exile in Babylon in 597 B.C. He prophesied by the Kebar River from about 593–571 B.C., both before and after Jerusalem’s fall in 586 B.C. The book contains dramatic visions, symbolic actions, and prophecies concerning judgment and restoration.

**Main theme:** God’s glory and sovereignty over all nations, with judgment against sin and the promise of future restoration for His people.

**Outline:**
- **Ezekiel’s call and vision of God’s glory** (Chapters 1–3)
- **God’s judgment on Jerusalem and Judah** (Chapters 4–24)
- **God’s judgment on pagan nations** (Chapters 25–32)
- **Israel’s restoration and hope** (Chapters 33–39)
- **The vision of the new temple and land** (Chapters 40–48)

**Key themes:**
- **God’s glory (kavod):** Ezekiel sees God’s glory depart from the temple because of the people’s sin, but also return to the new temple.
- **Individual responsibility:** Each person is accountable for their own sins—"the one who sins shall die" (chap. 18).
- **The good shepherd:** God Himself will be shepherd to His people and raise up a Davidic shepherd (chap. 34).
- **A new heart and a new spirit:** God promises to give the people a new heart of flesh instead of a heart of stone, and to put His Spirit within them (chap. 36:26–27).
- **The dry bones:** The vision of the dry bones coming to life symbolizes Israel’s national resurrection (chap. 37).
