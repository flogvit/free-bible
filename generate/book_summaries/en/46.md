# 1 Corinthians

**About the book:** First Corinthians was written by the apostle Paul to the church in Corinth, a city known for its moral decay and religious diversity. The letter addresses specific problems within the congregation, including divisions, sexual immorality, and misunderstandings about spiritual gifts and the resurrection.

**Main theme:** Paul urges the church toward unity in Christ, holy living, and a correct understanding of the gospel concerning the cross and the resurrection.

**Outline:**
- Introduction and thanksgiving (1:1–9)
- Divisions in the church and the wisdom of the cross (1:10–2:16)
- Servants of Christ and God’s temple (3–4)
- Sexual immorality and church discipline (5)
- Lawsuits and the body as God’s temple (6)
- Marriage, divorce, and the unmarried life (7)
- Food offered to idols and Christian freedom (8–10)
- Worship: head coverings and the Lord’s Supper (11)
- Spiritual gifts and the body of Christ (12)
- The way of love (13)
- Speaking in tongues and prophecy in the church (14)
- The resurrection from the dead (15)
- Collection, travel plans, and final greetings (16)

**Key themes:**
- **Unity in Christ:** The church is called to stand together and avoid divisions based on human leaders (1:10–13; 3:1–9).
- **The wisdom of the cross:** God’s power is revealed through the "foolishness" of the cross, not through worldly wisdom (1:18–25).
- **Holiness and the body:** Believers’ bodies are temples of the Holy Spirit and should be honored through moral purity (6:19–20).
- **Love as supreme:** All spiritual gifts are worthless without love, which is described as patient, kind, and enduring (13:1–13).
- **The hope of resurrection:** Christ’s resurrection is the foundation of the Christian faith and the guarantee of the future resurrection of believers (15:3–22).
