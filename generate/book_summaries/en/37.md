# Haggai

**About the book:** The book of Haggai is the tenth of the twelve minor prophets and consists of only two chapters containing four dated prophecies from the year 520 B.C. The prophet Haggai delivers God’s word to Zerubbabel, governor of Judah, and to Joshua the high priest concerning the rebuilding of the temple after the exile in Babylon.

**Main theme:** God calls His people to prioritize His house above their own, promising blessing and future glory when they obey.

## Outline

- **Call to rebuild the temple** (Chapter 1)
- **Encouragement and promise of the new temple’s glory** (Haggai 2:1–9)
- **The people’s uncleanness and promise of blessing** (Haggai 2:10–19)
- **Zerubbabel as God’s chosen signet ring** (Haggai 2:20–23)

## Key Themes

- **Prioritizing God’s house:** The people are rebuked for living in paneled houses while the Lord’s house lies in ruins.
- **Obedience and blessing:** When the people obey God’s call, the Lord stirs their spirits and promises blessing.
- **God’s presence and promises:** God assures His people: "I am with you" and promises that the glory of the new temple will surpass that of the first.
- **Messianic expectations:** Zerubbabel is appointed as God’s signet ring, a sign of God’s choice with messianic overtones.
