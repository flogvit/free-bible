# Song of Solomon

**About the book:** The Song of Solomon, also known as the Song of Songs (שִׁיר הַשִּׁירִים), is a collection of love poems attributed to King Solomon. The book portrays the love between a bridegroom and his bride through rich poetic symbolism using images from nature, gardens, and vineyards.

**Main theme:** The beauty, power, and invincibility of love between man and woman.

**Content overview:**
- **The beginning of love and falling in love** (Chapters 1–2:7)
- **Mutual admiration and longing between the lovers** (Chapters 2:8–3:5)
- **The bride’s beauty and the bridegroom’s praise** (Chapters 3:6–5:1)
- **Separation, longing, and searching** (Chapters 5:2–6:3)
- **Reunion and renewed love** (Chapters 6:4–8:4)
- **The seal of love and its enduring power** (Chapters 8:5–14)

**Key themes:**
- **Mutual love:** The bridegroom and the bride express deep devotion and admiration for each other throughout the book.
- **Symbolism of nature:** Gardens, vineyards, flowers, and animals (gazelles, doves) are used as images of love and beauty.
- **The strength of love:** Love is described as strong as death, with flames that water cannot quench (8:6–7).
- **Longing and searching:** Separation and reunion reveal the depth and endurance of love.
- **Daughters of Jerusalem:** A recurring chorus that bears witness to love and receives solemn warnings not to stir up love until it so desires.
