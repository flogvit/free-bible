# James

**About the book:** The letter of James was written by James, a servant of God and of the Lord Jesus Christ, to the twelve tribes scattered among the nations. This letter is practical guidance for Christian living, focusing on how faith should be demonstrated through actions in daily life.

**Main theme:** Genuine faith is shown through deeds, and Christians are called to live out their faith in practical obedience, self-control, and care for others.

**Outline of contents:**
- Greeting, trials, and temptations (Chapter 1:1–18)
- Hearers and doers of the word (Chapter 1:19–27)
- Warning against favoritism (Chapter 2:1–13)
- Faith and deeds (Chapter 2:14–26)
- The power of the tongue (Chapter 3:1–12)
- Wisdom from above (Chapter 3:13–18)
- Warning against worldliness and pride (Chapter 4:1–10)
- Against slander and arrogant planning (Chapter 4:11–17)
- Warning to the rich (Chapter 5:1–6)
- Patience and the coming of the Lord (Chapter 5:7–12)
- Prayer, confession, and restoration (Chapter 5:13–20)

**Key themes:**
- **Faith and deeds:** True faith is not merely intellectual belief but is demonstrated through actions—faith without deeds is dead (2:17, 26).
- **The power of the tongue:** The tongue is a small part of the body with great power to both bless and curse, requiring strict self-control (3:1–12).
- **Wisdom from God:** Heavenly wisdom is pure, peace-loving, and merciful, in contrast to earthly wisdom which creates strife (3:13–18).
- **Humility and submission:** Believers are called to humble themselves before God, resist the devil, and draw near to God (4:7–10).
- **Patience in trials:** Christians are encouraged to endure suffering with perseverance, using examples from the prophets and Job (5:7–11).
