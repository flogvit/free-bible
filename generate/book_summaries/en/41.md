# Mark

**About the book:** The Gospel of Mark is the second of the four gospels and is considered the earliest. It presents Jesus as the suffering Messiah and Son of God, emphasizing his actions and miracles. The gospel is characterized by pace and action, with the distinctive word "straks" (εὐθύς) driving the narrative forward.

**Main theme:** Jesus Christ, the Son of God, reveals his divine authority through teaching, miracles, and ultimately through his suffering, death, and resurrection.

**Content overview:**
- **Preparation for Jesus' ministry** (Chapter 1:1–13): John the Baptist's preaching, Jesus' baptism, and temptation in the wilderness
- **Jesus' activity in Galilee** (Chapter 1:14–3:6): Calling of disciples, teaching with authority, healings, and conflicts with the Pharisees
- **Selection and opposition** (Chapter 3:7–35): The twelve are chosen; accusations that he casts out demons by Beelzebul
- **Parables about the kingdom of God** (Chapter 4): The sower, the lamp, the mustard seed, and the calming of the storm
- **Miracles and missionary journeys** (Chapters 5–6): The demon-possessed man in the region of the Gerasenes, Jairus' daughter, the woman with the issue of blood, rejection in Nazareth, sending out the twelve, the death of John the Baptist, feeding of the five thousand
- **Conflict over purity and miracles** (Chapters 7–8:26): Clean and unclean, the Syrophoenician woman, healings, feeding of the four thousand, warning against the yeast of the Pharisees
- **The way to the cross** (Chapter 8:27–10:52): Peter's confession, predictions of suffering, the transfiguration on the mountain, teaching on discipleship, marriage, children, wealth, and service
- **Jesus' final week in Jerusalem** (Chapters 11–13): The triumphal entry into Jerusalem, cleansing of the temple, confrontations with religious leaders, the apocalyptic discourse
- **Suffering, death, and resurrection** (Chapters 14–16): Anointing at Bethany, the Last Supper, Gethsemane, arrest, trial, crucifixion, burial, and the empty tomb

**Key themes:**
- **Jesus' authority:** Jesus demonstrates divine power over sickness, demons, nature, and even sin (2:10: "The Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins")
- **The Messianic secret:** Jesus repeatedly commands people to remain silent about who he is, gradually revealing his identity (1:34; 3:12; 8:30)
- **The suffering servant:** The Son of Man came not to be served but to serve and to give his life as a ransom for many (10:45)
- **Discipleship and following:** To follow Jesus means self-denial, taking up one's cross, and being willing to lose one's life for the sake of the gospel (8:34–35)
- **The kingdom of God:** The time is fulfilled, and the kingdom of God is at hand—repent and believe in the good news (1:15)
