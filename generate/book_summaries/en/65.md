# Jude

**About the Book:** The Epistle of Jude was written by Jude, a servant of Jesus Christ and brother of James. This short letter, consisting of only one chapter, is a powerful warning against false teachers who have slipped into the church, and an exhortation to believers to contend for the faith that was once for all delivered to the saints.

**Main Theme:** A warning against false teachers and an encouragement to hold fast to the faith while waiting for the Lord's mercy.

**Outline of Contents:**
- Greeting and Purpose (Verses 1–4)
- Historical Warnings: Israel, the Angels, and Sodom (Verses 5–7)
- Evil Deeds and Destiny of False Teachers (Verses 8–11)
- Description of False Teachers: Dangers in the Church (Verses 12–13)
- Enoch's Prophecy Concerning God's Judgment (Verses 14–16)
- Exhortation to Watchfulness and Faithfulness (Verses 17–19)
- Building Up Your Faith and Keeping Yourself in God's Love (Verses 20–21)
- Mercy toward Those Who Doubt (Verses 22–23)
- Doxology to God (Verses 24–25)

**Key Themes:**
- **False teaching:** Ungodly people have slipped in and twisted the grace of God into licentiousness, denying our only Master and Lord, Jesus Christ.
- **God's judgment:** Historical examples (Israel in the wilderness, fallen angels, Sodom and Gomorrah) show that God judges disobedience and immorality.
- **The struggle for the faith:** Believers are urged to contend for the faith once delivered to the saints.
- **Spiritual preservation:** The church is to build itself up in the faith, pray in the Holy Spirit, and keep itself in God's love.
- **God's power to preserve:** A closing doxology to Him who is able to keep believers from stumbling and to present them faultless before His glory with great joy.
