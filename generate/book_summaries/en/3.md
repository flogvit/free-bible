# 3. Mosebok (Leviticus)

**About the book:** The third book of Moses, also known as Leviticus, is the third book in the Pentateuch and consists of 27 chapters. It contains God's instructions to the Israelites concerning sacrifices, the priesthood, purity, and holy living, given through Moses at Mount Sinai. It serves as a handbook for worship and holy life within the covenant community.

**Main theme:** God calls His people to holiness and gives them detailed regulations on how they may approach Him in worship and live as a holy nation.

**Overview of contents:**
- Laws concerning offerings and sacred rituals (Chapters 1–7)
- The consecration of the priesthood (Chapters 8–10)
- Laws of cleansing and uncleanness (Chapters 11–15)
- The Day of Atonement (Chapter 16)
- Holy laws and lifestyle (Chapters 17–20)
- Priestly regulations and duties (Chapters 21–22)
- Feasts and sacred times (Chapter 23)
- The showbread, the lampstand, and blasphemy (Chapter 24)
- The sabbatical year and the Year of Jubilee (Chapter 25)
- Blessings and curses (Chapter 26)
- Laws concerning vows and tithes (Chapter 27)

**Key themes:**
- **Holiness:** God calls Israel to be holy because He is holy, a principle that permeates all the regulations in the book.
- **Atonement and forgiveness:** The sacrificial system shows how sin can be atoned for and fellowship with God restored through blood sacrifices.
- **The priesthood:** Aaron and his sons are consecrated to serve as mediators between God and the people.
- **Purity and impurity:** Detailed laws distinguish between what is clean and unclean in order to preserve the holiness of the nation.
- **God's presence:** The tabernacle and the offerings make possible God's dwelling among His people.
