# Proverbs

**About the book:** Proverbs is a collection of wisdom literature attributed to King Solomon, with contributions from other wise men, Agur, and King Lemuel. The book contains practical life advice, moral exhortations, and poetic descriptions of the value of wisdom, gathered over several generations in Israel's history.

**Main theme:** The fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom and knowledge, and this reverence for God should shape all areas of life.

**Overview of contents:**
- The purpose and call of wisdom (Chapters 1–9)
- Proverbs of Solomon – first collection (Chapters 10–22:16)
- Words of the wise (Chapters 22:17–24:22)
- More words from the wise (Chapters 24:23–34)
- Proverbs of Solomon collected by Hezekiah's men (Chapters 25–29)
- Words of Agur (Chapter 30)
- Words of King Lemuel and the capable woman (Chapter 31)

**Key themes:**
- **Reverence for God as the foundation of wisdom:** The fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom and a source of life (Prov. 1:7; 9:10; 15:33).
- **Wisdom versus folly:** The contrast between the way of the wise, which leads to life, and the way of the fool, which leads to destruction.
- **Righteousness and wickedness:** The blessing of the righteous stands in opposition to the ruin of the wicked, and a righteous life is rewarded.
- **The power of the tongue:** The ability of words to heal or destroy, and the importance of truthful and careful speech.
- **Diligence and laziness:** The blessing of work and the consequences of slothfulness, illustrated through the ant and other examples.
