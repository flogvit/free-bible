# Nahum

**About the book:** The book of Nahum is one of the Minor Prophets in the Old Testament, written by the prophet Nahum from Elkosh. The book consists of three chapters containing a judgment oracle (massa) and a prophetic vision concerning the fall of Nineveh, the capital of the mighty Assyrian Empire that had oppressed Israel and Judah.

**Main theme:** God’s righteous judgment against Nineveh and the Assyrian Empire, and His protection of His people.

**Summary of contents:**
- **God’s nature and power** (Chapter 1): The Lord’s jealousy, wrath against enemies, and goodness toward those who take refuge in Him
- **The destruction of Nineveh** (Chapter 2): Description of the attack on the city, its plundering, and total collapse
- **Nineveh’s sin and inevitable fall** (Chapter 3): Accusations against the city and its fate like that of No-Amon

**Key themes:**
- **God’s righteous wrath and patience:** The Lord is a jealous and avenging God toward His adversaries, yet also patient and mighty in power—He does not leave the guilty unpunished (1:2–3)
- **God’s goodness and protection:** The Lord is good, a refuge on the day of trouble for those who take shelter in Him (1:7)
- **Freedom for God’s people:** The fall of Nineveh brings peace and freedom to Judah, which can now celebrate its festivals and fulfill its vows (2:1)
- **The wages of sin:** Nineveh is condemned for its bloodguilt, lies, violence, and sorcery—the city that sold nations through its immorality and magical arts (3:1–4)
- **Lord of history:** Even the mightiest empires fall under God’s judgment, just as No-Amon fell before Nineveh (3:8–10)
