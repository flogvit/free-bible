# Philemon

**About the Book:** Philemon is a short, personal letter written by the apostle Paul and Timothy to Philemon, a fellow believer, as well as to Apphia, Archippus, and the church that meets in his house. The letter was written while Paul was in prison and concerns Onesimus, a runaway slave who has become a Christian.

**Main Theme:** An appeal for forgiveness and reconciliation, where Paul urges Philemon to receive his runaway slave Onesimus as a beloved brother in Christ.

**Outline of Contents:**
- Greeting (vv. 1–3)
- Thanksgiving for Philemon’s love and faith (vv. 4–7)
- Paul’s appeal on behalf of Onesimus (vv. 8–16)
- Paul’s promise and expectation (vv. 17–21)
- Closing remarks and greetings (vv. 22–25)

**Key Themes:**
- **Brotherhood in Christ:** Onesimus is no longer to be seen as a slave, but as a beloved brother both humanly and in the Lord (v. 16).
- **Forgiveness and Reconciliation:** Paul appeals to Philemon to receive Onesimus with the same goodwill he would have received Paul himself (v. 17).
- **Transformation through the Gospel:** Onesimus, who once was "useless," has now become "useful" after becoming Paul’s spiritual child in prison (vv. 10–11).
- **Voluntary Goodness:** Paul does not wish to compel Philemon, but that his good deed should be done willingly (v. 14).
- **Christian Fellowship:** The letter shows how the gospel transforms social relationships within the Christian community.
