# 2 Kings

**About the book:** 2 Kings continues the history of the kingdoms of Israel and Judah from where 1 Kings ends. The book covers the period from the final days of the prophet Elijah to the fall of Jerusalem and the exile of Judah to Babylon in 586 B.C.E. Through the accounts of the kings and the prophets Elijah and Elisha, the book shows the consequences of obedience and disobedience toward the Lord.

**Main theme:** The consequences of faithfulness or unfaithfulness toward the Lord, demonstrated through the actions of the kings and the ultimate fate of the kingdoms.

**Content overview:**
- **Ahaziah’s illness and death** (Chapter 1)
- **Elijah’s ascension and Elisha’s beginning** (Chapter 2)
- **Jehoram of Israel and the war against Moab** (Chapter 3)
- **Elisha’s miracles and service** (Chapters 4–8:15)
- **Kings in Judah and Israel** (Chapters 8:16–29)
- **Jehu’s revolution and the destruction of Ahab’s house** (Chapters 9–10)
- **Athaliah and Joash in Judah** (Chapters 11–12)
- **Kings in Israel and Judah, Elisha’s death, and the Assyrian threat** (Chapters 13–16)
- **The fall of Israel and exile to Assyria** (Chapter 17)
- **Hezekiah: A righteous king in Judah** (Chapters 18–20)
- **Manasseh and Amon: Evil in Judah** (Chapter 21)
- **Josiah’s reformation and return to God** (Chapters 22–23)
- **The fall of Judah and the destruction of Jerusalem** (Chapters 24–25)

**Key themes:**
- **God’s judgment on sin:** Both Israel and Judah fall because of persistent idolatry and disobedience to the Lord’s covenant.
- **The role of the prophets:** Elijah and Elisha serve as God’s spokespeople and perform mighty miracles that testify to the Lord’s power.
- **Faithfulness is rewarded:** Kings such as Hezekiah and Josiah, who turn to the Lord, experience God’s blessing, even though final judgment is not always averted.
- **Covenant breaking has consequences:** Israel’s exile to Assyria and Judah’s exile to Babylon are direct results of breaking the covenant with the Lord.
- **God’s faithfulness:** Despite the people’s sin, God preserves a remnant and keeps His promises to David.
