**Main Theme:** James exhorts believers to face trials with joy, seek wisdom from God, and live out their faith through practical action.

**Content:** James, a servant of God and of the Lord Jesus Christ, writes to the twelve tribes in the dispersion and encourages them to view trials as opportunities for spiritual growth and endurance. He emphasizes that God gives wisdom generously to those who ask in faith without doubting, while temptations arise from human desires—not from God. Believers are urged to be quick to listen, slow to speak, and to be doers of the word, not merely hearers. True religion is demonstrated by caring for orphans and widows, and by keeping oneself unstained by the world.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Trials (πειρασμοί):** Challenges that test faith and develop endurance, with the promise of the crown of life for those who persevere.
- **Wisdom (σοφία):** A gift from God given generously to those who ask in faith without doubting.
- **The double-minded person as a sea wave (κλύδων θαλάσσης):** An image of instability, driven and tossed by the wind.
- **The mirror (ἔσοπτρον):** Illustrates the difference between merely hearing the word and acting on it.
- **Pure religion (θρησκεία καθαρά):** Practical care for the vulnerable and personal holiness.
