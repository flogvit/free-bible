**Main Theme:** Laws concerning offerings, atonement for sin, and reminders of obedience in the promised land.

**Content:** The chapter gives instructions for grain offerings and drink offerings that must accompany burnt offerings and slaughter offerings when Israel enters the land, as well as the command to give the first portion of dough (challah) to the Lord. The same law applies to both Israelites and foreigners. A distinction is made between unintentional sins (which can be atoned for) and deliberate sins ("with a high hand"), which lead to being cut off. A man who gathered wood on the Sabbath is stoned as an example of intentional lawbreaking. Finally, tassels (tzitzit) are commanded on garments as a reminder to keep God's commands.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Grain offering and drink offering (minchah/nesekh):** Flour, oil, and wine that accompany animal sacrifices – expressing wholeness in worship.
- **One law for all (torah achat):** Israelites and foreigners stand equal before the Lord – universal justice.
- **Unintentional sin (shegagah):** Sin committed in ignorance can be atoned for through sacrifice.
- **The wood gatherer:** A narrative showing the consequence of deliberate Sabbath breaking – the death penalty by stoning.
- **Tassels (tzitzit):** A visible reminder on garments to remember and keep God's commands.
