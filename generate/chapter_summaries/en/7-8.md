**Main Theme:** Gideon completes the victory over Midian, but the ephod he makes becomes a stumbling block for Israel.

**Content:** Gideon avoids conflict with the Ephraimites through wise speech. He pursues the Midianite kings Zebah and Zalmunna, punishes the cities of Succoth and Penuel for refusing to help him, and kills the kings in revenge for his brothers. Gideon refuses to become king and declares that the Lord shall rule, but he makes an ephod from the gold plundered, which leads Israel into idolatry. The land has peace for 40 years, but after Gideon's death, the people return to Baal worship.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Ephod:** A cultic object made by Gideon from gold, which became a stumbling block and led Israel into spiritual unfaithfulness.
- **"The Lord shall rule over you":** Gideon's rejection of kingship and confession of God's sovereignty over Israel.
- **Succoth and Penuel:** Israelite cities that refused to help Gideon and were punished – showing internal division within Israel.
- **Baal-Berith:** The idol the Israelites turned to after Gideon's death, demonstrating the people's recurring unfaithfulness.
