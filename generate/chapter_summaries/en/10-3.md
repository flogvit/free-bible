**Main Theme:** The chapter depicts how David's kingdom is strengthened while Saul's house weakens, culminating in Abner's death and David's mourning.

**Content:** The war between the house of David and the house of Saul continues, but David grows ever stronger. In Hebron, David has six sons. Abner, Saul's commander, breaks with Ish-Bosheth after an accusation concerning Saul's concubine Rizpah, and offers to transfer all Israel to David. David first demands the return of his wife Michal. Joab kills Abner in blood vengeance for his brother Asahel, but David distances himself from the murder and publicly mourns over Abner, convincing the people of his innocence.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **House of David vs. House of Saul** – The long-standing conflict between the two dynasties, in which David gradually gains strength.
- **Abner** – Saul's powerful commander who shifts loyalty but is killed by Joab.
- **Michal** – David's first wife, who is brought back as a symbol of his legitimate claim to the throne.
- **Blood vengeance** – Joab's killing of Abner in revenge for Asahel, which David condemns.
- **David's mourning** – David's public funeral rites demonstrate his integrity and clear him before the people.
