**Main Theme:** Jacob and his family settle in Egypt, where Joseph administers the famine and secures Pharaoh's authority over the land.

**Content:** Joseph presents five of his brothers to Pharaoh, followed by his father Jacob, who blesses Pharaoh twice. Pharaoh grants them permission to live in the fertile region of Goshen. During the ongoing famine, Joseph buys all the land in Egypt for Pharaoh in exchange for grain, except for the priests' land, and institutes a law requiring one-fifth of the harvest to go to Pharaoh. Jacob lives 17 years in Egypt, and before he dies, he makes Joseph swear to bury him in Canaan with his ancestors.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Goshen:** The fertile region in Egypt where Israel's family is allowed to live and thrive.
- **Jacob's Blessing:** Jacob blesses Pharaoh twice—the seemingly subordinate one blesses the world's most powerful ruler, showing that God's people possess spiritual authority that surpasses earthly power.
- **The Fifth:** The tax system Joseph establishes whereby the people give 20% of their harvest to Pharaoh.
- **Hand under the Thigh:** An oath-taking gesture in which Jacob asks Joseph to place his hand under his thigh and swear to bury him in Canaan, symbolizing the promise that God's people belong in the Promised Land.
