**Main Theme:** Ezra, a scribe-priest of the line of Aaron, receives authority from King Artaxerxes to lead Jews back to Jerusalem.

**Content:** The chapter begins with Ezra’s genealogy tracing back to Aaron. King Artaxerxes gives Ezra an extensive letter granting him authority to bring people to Jerusalem, carry gifts for the temple, appoint judges, and enforce God’s law. Ezra had prepared his heart to study the Lord’s law, live by it, and teach it to Israel. The chapter concludes with Ezra praising God for placing this in the king’s heart.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The good hand of the Lord:** This expression is repeated several times and shows that Ezra’s success was due to God’s guidance and favor.
- **Scribe (sofer):** Ezra was a skilled scribe in the Law of Moses, qualified to teach and interpret Scripture.
- **The king’s letter:** The official document that gave Ezra authority, resources, and legal power from the Persian king.
- **Prepared heart:** Ezra had prepared his heart to study, practice, and teach God’s law—a model for spiritual leadership.
