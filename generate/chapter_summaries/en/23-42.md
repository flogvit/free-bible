**Main Theme:** God presents his chosen servant who will bring justice to the nations, while confronting Israel’s blindness and disobedience.

**Content:** The chapter opens with God presenting his servant, who has received God’s Spirit and will lead justice to the nations with humility and perseverance (vv. 1–4). God calls this servant to be a covenant for the people and a light to the nations, to open blind eyes and free prisoners from darkness (vv. 5–9). All creation is called to sing a new song to the Lord, who breaks his long silence and emerges as a mighty warrior (vv. 10–17). Yet Israel itself is blind and deaf—they have seen much but not understood, and their disobedience has led to God’s judgment upon them (vv. 18–25).

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The Lord’s Servant:** A chosen figure who will bring justice to the nations with gentleness—he will not break a bruised reed.
- **A Light to the Nations:** The servant will open blind eyes and lead prisoners out of darkness into freedom.
- **God as Warrior:** The Lord breaks his long silence and goes forth as a mighty champion against his enemies.
- **Blind and Deaf Servant:** Israel is paradoxically described as God’s blind and deaf servant who has neither heard nor understood.
- **Judgment for Disobedience:** Israel’s sufferings are explained as God’s wrath because they did not walk in his ways or listen to his law.
