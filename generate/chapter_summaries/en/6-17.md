**Main Theme:** The tribe of Manasseh receives its inheritance, and Joshua challenges the sons of Joseph to take more land through their own efforts.

**Content:** Manasseh receives territories on both sides of the Jordan—Gilead and Bashan in the east, and ten portions in the west. The daughters of Zelophehad receive the right to inherit among their brothers, just as the Lord had commanded Moses. The sons of Joseph complain that they have been given too little land and that the Canaanites possess iron chariots. Joshua replies that they are a great people with great strength, and encourages them to clear the forested areas and drive out the Canaanites.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Daughters of Zelophehad:** Women who received inheritance rights when they had no brothers—an important precedent for inheritance law in Israel.
- **Iron Chariots:** The military superiority of the Canaanites in the valleys, which the Israelites feared.
- **The Forest (יַעַר):** Joshua's solution—clear new land in the mountainous regions rather than complaining about what was allotted.
- **Gilead and Bashan:** The areas east of the Jordan that Machir received because he was a warrior.
