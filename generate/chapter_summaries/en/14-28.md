**Main Theme:** King Ahaz's apostasy from the Lord through idolatry leads to military defeats and God's judgment upon Judah.

**Content:** Ahaz did evil in the sight of the Lord; he practiced idolatry and burned his sons as sacrifices. God allowed Judah to be defeated by the Arameans, Israelites, Edomites, and Philistines. When Israel took 200,000 captives, the prophet Oded warned them to send their brothers back, which they did. Instead of seeking help from the Lord, Ahaz turned to Assyria, but this brought no relief. He shut down the temple, set up altars to foreign gods throughout the land, and at his death was denied a burial place among the kings.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Valley of Hinnom:** The place where Ahaz burned his sons as sacrifices to idols, symbolizing the deepest form of apostasy.
- **The Prophet Oded:** God's voice reminding Israel to show mercy toward their brothers and warning against increasing sin.
- **Shutting of the Temple:** Ahaz's final rejection of the Lord by closing the temple doors and building altars to foreign gods.
- **Assyria's Help:** Illustrates Ahaz's misplaced trust in human power rather than God, which only led to greater distress.
