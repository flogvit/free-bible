**Main Theme:** God calls his people to judgment and demands genuine worship over empty ritualism.

**Content:** This Psalm of Asaph portrays God revealing himself in glory from Zion, surrounded by fire and storm, to judge his people. He declares that he does not need their sacrifices, for everything in creation already belongs to him. What God seeks is gratitude, faithfulness to promises, and trust in times of trouble. The wicked are sternly warned: those who confess God's law but live in sin will be held accountable if they do not repent.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Theophany (vv. 1–6):** God's majestic appearance from Zion with fire and storm underscores his holiness and authority as judge.
- **Thank Offering (תּוֹדָה, vv. 14, 23):** God desires gratitude and praise rather than mechanical sacrifices.
- **Covenant (בְּרִית, vv. 5, 16):** The relationship between God and his people is founded on covenant, which requires obedience, not merely outward confession.
- **Judgment (vv. 4, 21–22):** God is the judge who will hold both the faithful and the wicked accountable.
