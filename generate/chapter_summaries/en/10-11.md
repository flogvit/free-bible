**Main Theme:** King David’s fall through adultery and murder.

**Content:** While the army of Israel was besieging Rabbah, David remained in Jerusalem. From the roof of the palace he saw Bathsheba bathing, sent for her, and lay with her. When she became pregnant, David tried to hide his sin by recalling her husband Uriah from the war, but Uriah refused to go home. David then sent Uriah back with a letter ordering that he be placed in the front lines and left there—leading to his death. David took Bathsheba as his wife, but what he had done was evil in the Lord’s sight.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The Roof** – The place where David saw Bathsheba and temptation began; symbolizes how sin often starts with the eyes.
- **Uriah’s Loyalty** – His refusal to go home while the army was fighting stands in sharp contrast to David’s betrayal.
- **The Letter** – David used Uriah himself to carry his own death sentence; shows the depth of David’s deceit.
- **"Evil in the Lord’s sight"** – The chapter’s concluding judgment that prepares for God’s intervention in the next chapter.
