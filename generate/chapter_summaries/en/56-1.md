**Main Theme:** Paul instructs Titus to appoint qualified leaders on Crete and warns against false teachers.

**Content:** Paul introduces himself as a servant of God and an apostle of Jesus Christ, with a mission founded on the hope of eternal life that God promised before time began. He instructs Titus to appoint elders in every town and gives detailed requirements for their character and conduct. Paul warns against rebellious people, especially those from the circumcision group, who ruin households with false teaching for the sake of shameful gain. He quotes a Cretan prophet saying that Cretans are always liars, evil beasts, and lazy gluttons, and urges sharp rebuke. The chapter concludes with the principle that all things are pure to the pure, while the defiled and unbelieving have nothing pure; even their mind and conscience are defiled, and they deny God by what they do.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Elder/Overseer (πρεσβύτερος/ἐπίσκοπος):** Leadership roles in the church requiring an unblemished character, a believing family, and the ability to encourage others with sound teaching.
- **Sound Teaching (διδασκαλία ὑγιαίνουσα):** The true, life-giving Christian instruction that stands in contrast to false doctrine and Jewish myths.
- **Above Reproach (ἀνέγκλητος):** A fundamental requirement for church leaders—to be without accusation and have a good reputation.
- **False Teachers:** Rebellious individuals who spread empty words and deception, especially tied to Jewish traditions, and must be silenced.
