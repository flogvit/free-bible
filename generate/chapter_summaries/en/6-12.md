**Main Theme:** A summary of all the kings that Israel defeated during the conquest of the land, both east and west of the Jordan.

**Content:** The chapter first lists the two kings east of the Jordan—Sihon, king of the Amorites, and Og, king of Bashan—who were defeated by Moses and the Israelites. Their land was given to the tribes of Reuben, Gad, and half the tribe of Manasseh. Then 31 kings west of the Jordan are listed, whom Joshua and the Israelites defeated, from the Valley of Lebanon in the north to Mount Seir in the south. The land was distributed as an inheritance among the tribes of Israel.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **31 Kings:** The total number of kings defeated west of the Jordan, showing the extent of the conquest.
- **Sihon and Og:** The two powerful kings east of the Jordan, defeated under Moses' leadership.
- **Inheritance (יְרֻשָּׁה):** The land was given as an inheritance to the tribes, fulfilling God's promise.
- **Moses and Joshua:** Two leaders, one conquest—the continuity of God's plan for His people.
