**Main Theme:** Bildad's first speech defends God's justice and asserts that suffering results from sin.

**Content:** Bildad rejects Job's complaints and rhetorically asks whether God can pervert justice. He suggests that Job's children died because of their own sins, but urges Job to seek God if he himself is pure and righteous. Appealing to the wisdom of the ancestors, Bildad uses nature imagery to show that the wicked wither away like papyrus without water. He concludes with the promise that God will restore the innocent.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Papyrus without water (v.11–12):** A picture of the fate of the wicked—they quickly wither without God's life-giving presence.
- **The spider's web (v.14):** Symbolizes the false security of those who forget God—fragile and worthless.
- **Wisdom of the fathers (v.8–10):** Bildad appeals to traditional teaching as authority for his doctrine of retribution.
