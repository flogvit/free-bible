**Main Theme:** God gives the Israelites a calendar of holy assemblies (moadim) where they are to appear before Him throughout the year.

**Content:** The chapter presents the Lord's appointed times: the weekly Sabbath, Passover and the Feast of Unleavened Bread in spring, the firstfruits offering (omer) and the Feast of Weeks (shavuot) linked to the harvest, and in autumn the Day of Trumpets, the Great Day of Atonement (yom kippur) with fasting and atonement, and the Feast of Tabernacles (sukkot). At harvest time, the corners of the field are to be left for the poor and the foreigner. These festivals are eternal statutes that remind Israel of their deliverance from Egypt and their covenant relationship with the Lord.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Moed (appointed time/feast):** God-given times when Israel gathers for holy assemblies.
- **Miqra qodesh (holy convocation):** Days when God calls the people together; work is forbidden.
- **Yom hakippurim (Day of Atonement):** The most holy day of the year, with fasting and atonement for the sins of the entire nation.
- **Sukkot (Feast of Tabernacles):** Seven days spent in shelters to remember the wilderness journey after deliverance from Egypt.
- **Shabbaton (Sabbath rest):** Complete rest expressing trust in God and separation from daily routines.
