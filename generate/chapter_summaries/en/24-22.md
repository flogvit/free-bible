**Main Theme:** God's judgment on the kings of Judah for their injustice and the promise that the Davidic royal line will come to an end.

**Content:** Jeremiah conveys God's demand to the kings to practice justice and righteousness, protect the vulnerable (foreigners, fatherless children, widows), and not shed innocent blood. The chapter contains judgments against three named kings: Shallum (Jehoahaz), who is to die in exile; Jehoiakim, who will receive a donkey's burial for his injustice and exploitation; and Coniah (Jeconiah), who will be cast out along with his mother. The chapter concludes with the prophecy that none of Coniah's descendants shall sit on David's throne.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Justice and Righteousness (מִשְׁפָּט וּצְדָקָה):** The fundamental requirement for kings—to judge fairly and protect the weak in society.
- **Cedar Tree/Lebanon:** A symbol of the splendor and greatness of the royal palace, but also of the proud king who will be felled like a tree.
- **Donkey's Burial:** The image of Jehoiakim's shameful death—without mourning or a dignified burial.
- **Signet Ring:** Although Coniah was as valuable as a signet ring on God's right hand, he is to be torn off and cast away.
