**Main Theme:** Paul exhorts the church in Corinth to unity in Christ and shows that the message of the cross is God's wisdom and power.

**Content:** Paul greets the church and thanks God for the spiritual gifts they have received in Christ. He then addresses the divisions among them, where some say "I belong to Paul," others to Apollos, Cephas, or Christ. Paul asks rhetorically whether Christ is divided, and reminds them that he was sent to preach the gospel, not to baptize. He contrasts the wisdom of the world with the "foolishness" of the cross, which is a stumbling block to Jews and foolishness to Greeks, but God's power and wisdom to those who are called. God chose what is weak and despised in order to shame what is strong, so that no one may boast before Him, but only boast in the Lord.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Divisions (σχίσματα):** The church was divided into factions around various leaders, which Paul strongly warns against.
- **The message of the cross (ὁ λόγος τοῦ σταυροῦ):** The message about the crucified Christ appears as foolishness to the world, yet it is God's saving power.
- **God's wisdom vs. worldly wisdom:** God has made the wisdom of this world into foolishness and reveals His true wisdom through Christ.
- **The calling (κλῆσις):** Believers are called by the faithful God to fellowship with Christ—not many who are wise or powerful, but those who are weak and despised.
