**Main Theme:** God confirms Aaron's priesthood through signs and wonders following continued rebellion.

**Content:** After Korah's rebellion, the people complained against Moses and Aaron, accusing them of killing "the Lord's people." A plague broke out, but Aaron stopped it by running into the midst of the people with fire from the altar and incense—yet 14,700 still died. To end the complaints, God commanded that twelve staffs be placed in the tabernacle, one for each tribe. The next day, Aaron's staff had sprouted, blossomed, and produced ripe almonds, serving as proof of God's choice of Aaron for priestly service.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Incense Shovels:** The shovels from the dead rebels were hammered into plates for the altar as a warning memorial.
- **Between the Dead and the Living:** Aaron stood between the dead and the living with incense and stopped the plague—an illustration of the priest's role in intercession.
- **Aaron's Blossoming Staff:** The staff that sprouted buds, blossomed, and bore almonds in one night was God's visible confirmation of whom He had chosen.
- **Sign for the Rebels:** The staff was to be kept as a lasting sign to stop complaints and prevent death.
