**Main Theme:** Christ's one, perfect sacrifice surpasses the repeated offerings of the old covenant and opens the way into God's presence.

**Content:** The chapter describes the structure of the earthly tabernacle and the Levitical priesthood, in which the high priest entered the Most Holy Place only once a year with animal blood. These arrangements could not cleanse the conscience but were a shadow of something better. As high priest, Christ has entered heaven itself with his own blood and obtained eternal redemption. Without the shedding of blood there is no forgiveness, and the new covenant was inaugurated by Christ's death. He will come again—not to deal with sin, but to bring salvation to those who are waiting for him.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The Most Holy Place:** The innermost room of the tabernacle where God's presence dwelt, which only the high priest could enter once a year.
- **Blood:** A symbol of life given for atonement; Christ's blood cleanses the conscience from dead works.
- **Eternal redemption (αἰωνίαν λύτρωσιν):** The lasting deliverance that Christ secured, in contrast to the temporary effects of animal sacrifices.
- **Mediator of a new covenant:** Christ is the guarantor who, through his death, makes it possible for those called to receive the eternal inheritance.
