**Main Theme:** Amnon's rape of Tamar and Absalom's revenge killing of Amnon demonstrate the destructive consequences of sin within David's family.

**Content:** Amnon, David's son, becomes consumed with desire for his half-sister Tamar and rapes her through a cunning plan. Afterwards, he hates her and casts her out in disgrace. Absalom takes his sister in but secretly plans revenge. Two years later, Absalom's servants kill Amnon during a sheep-shearing festival, and Absalom flees to Geshur, where he remains for three years. David mourns, yet longs for Absalom.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The Coat of Many Colors** (כְּתֹנֶת פַּסִּים): Tamar's royal garment, which she tears in pieces, symbolizes her lost honor and innocence.
- **Nebalah (נְבָלָה)** – "folly/shame": Tamar's term for the act shows that this is a serious sin against God's people.
- **Hatred greater than love:** Amnon's sudden hatred toward Tamar reveals that his "love" was selfish desire, not genuine love.
- **Sheep-shearing festival:** The festive occasion becomes the setting for bloody revenge, illustrating how sin breeds more sin.
