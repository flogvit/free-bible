**Main Theme:** Joseph's dreams and his brothers' betrayal mark the beginning of God's plan to bring Jacob's family to Egypt.

**Content:** The 17-year-old Joseph, Jacob's favored son, receives a special tunic and has dreams suggesting that his family will bow down to him. Driven by jealousy, his brothers plot to kill him, but Reuben intervenes and suggests throwing him into a pit to save him. At Judah's suggestion, they sell him to Ishmaelite traders for twenty pieces of silver. They deceive Jacob by dipping Joseph's tunic in goat blood, and the grieving father refuses to be comforted.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The Tunic (כְּתֹנֶת פַּסִּים):** A symbol of Jacob's favoritism that sparks his brothers' hatred and jealousy.
- **The Dreams:** Prophetic images of sheaves of grain and heavenly bodies bowing to Joseph, pointing forward to his future exaltation.
- **The Pit:** The place of Joseph's humiliation, where his brothers cast him before selling him.
- **The Blood on the Tunic:** Deceptive evidence that convinces Jacob of Joseph's death and causes deep sorrow.
