**Main Theme:** The chapter describes the allocation of land areas to the tribe of Judah, with detailed boundary descriptions and city lists.

**Content:** The tribe of Judah receives its territory by lot, with boundaries extending from the wilderness of Edom in the south to the Mediterranean Sea in the west. Caleb, who received Hebron according to the Lord's word, drives out the three sons of Anak and offers his daughter Achsah to whoever conquers Debir—Othniel wins and receives her. Achsah wisely asks for water springs as a dowry. The chapter lists the cities in Judah's four regions: the Negev, the lowland, the hill country, and the wilderness. The Jebusites in Jerusalem could not be driven out and continued to live among the people of Judah.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Lot (goral):** God's method for the fair distribution of the land among the tribes.
- **Caleb and Hebron:** The faithful spy's reward—he receives Kiriath-Arba (Hebron), the city of the Anakites.
- **Water springs (gullot majim):** Achsah's wise request symbolizes blessing and a foundation for life in the dry land.
- **The Jebusites in Jerusalem:** A testimony to incomplete conquest that has consequences later in Israel's history.
