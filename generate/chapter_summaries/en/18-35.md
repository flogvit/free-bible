**Main Theme:** Elihu argues that God's greatness makes him exalted above human sin and righteousness.

**Content:** Elihu challenges Job’s claim to be more righteous than God. He points to the heavens to demonstrate God’s exaltation and explains that human sin or righteousness does not affect God—it concerns only humans themselves. Elihu asserts that God does not answer the oppressed because they cry out in pride without seeking him sincerely. He concludes by criticizing Job for speaking empty words without knowledge.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Heavens (שָׁמַיִם):** A symbol of God’s exaltation and distance from humanity.
- **Songs in the night (זְמִרוֹת בַּלָּיְלָה):** God’s gift of comfort and hope amid suffering.
- **Pride (גְּאוֹן):** The reason why the cries of the oppressed go unheard.
- **Empty words (הֶבֶל):** Elihu’s description of Job’s speech as devoid of substance.
