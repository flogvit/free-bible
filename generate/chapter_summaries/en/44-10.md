**Main Theme:** God opens salvation to the Gentiles through Peter’s preaching to Cornelius and his household.

**Content:** The Roman officer Cornelius, a devout man, receives an angelic vision instructing him to send for Peter. At the same time, Peter has a vision of clean and unclean animals lowered from heaven three times, with the message: "What God has made clean, do not call common." Peter understands that God shows no partiality among people, and he preaches the gospel in Cornelius’ house. The Holy Spirit falls upon all the listeners—to the astonishment of the Jewish believers—and they are baptized.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The Sheet Vision:** A sheet containing all kinds of animals lowered from heaven symbolizes that God has removed the distinction between clean and unclean—and between Jew and Gentile.
- **"God shows no partiality":** Peter’s realization that the gospel is for all nations, not only for the Jews.
- **The Outpouring of the Holy Spirit:** The Spirit falls upon the Gentiles before baptism, as proof that God has accepted them.
- **Cornelius:** A devout Gentile who represents the first non-Jewish Christians.
