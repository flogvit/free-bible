**Main Theme:** Psalm 107 is a hymn of thanksgiving that praises God’s faithful love (חֶסֶד) through his deliverance of people in distress.

**Content:** The psalm calls those redeemed by the Lord to testify to his goodness. Through four examples—travelers in the wilderness, prisoners in darkness, the sick near death, and sailors in a storm—the same pattern is shown: people in distress cry out to the Lord, he saves them, and they are to thank him. In conclusion, God’s sovereign power over nature and society is described, where he humbles the mighty and lifts up the needy.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **God’s faithful love (חֶסֶד):** The refrain that is repeated—God’s enduring and reliable goodness, which is the foundation of all deliverance.
- **The four distress situations:** Wilderness, captivity, sickness, and storm illustrate different forms of human helplessness where only God can save.
- **Crying out to the Lord:** In each situation, those in distress cry out to God, and he answers—a pattern that shows the power of prayer.
- **Thank offerings and testimony:** The response to deliverance is thanksgiving, sacrifice, and public proclamation of God’s deeds.
- **The wisdom exhortation (v. 43):** The psalm ends with an invitation to notice and understand the Lord’s faithful love.
