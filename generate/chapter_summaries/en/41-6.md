**Main Theme:** Mark 6 shows Jesus' growing influence and power through teaching, miracles, and the sending out of the disciples, contrasted with unbelief and opposition.

**Content:** Jesus meets rejection in his hometown where people take offense at him, and he can do few miracles because of their unbelief. He sends out the twelve disciples two by two with authority over unclean spirits. The chapter also tells about the execution of John the Baptist after Herod's foolish promise. Jesus feeds five thousand men with five loaves and two fish, and later walks on water to the disciples—but they do not understand, for their hearts were hardened.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **A prophet without honor:** Jesus compares himself to a prophet who is not recognized in his own hometown and among his own people.
- **Two by two:** The disciples are sent out in pairs, which provides witness and mutual support in the mission.
- **Sheep without a shepherd:** Jesus sees the crowd with compassion because they were like sheep without a shepherd—an image of spiritual need and leadership.
- **Hardened hearts:** Despite seeing the miracles, the disciples do not understand who Jesus is—a recurring theme in Mark.
