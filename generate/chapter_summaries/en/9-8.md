**Main Theme:** The people of Israel demand a king and thereby reject God as their true ruler.

**Content:** When Samuel grew old, he appointed his sons Joel and Abijah as judges, but they were corrupt and took bribes. Therefore, the elders of Israel demanded a king "like all the other nations." God revealed to Samuel that the people had not rejected him, but God himself as their king. Samuel warned about the rights of the king: he would take their sons for his army, their daughters as servants, and demand tithes from their crops and livestock. Despite this warning, the people insisted on their demand, and God commanded Samuel to give them a king.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Corrupt judges:** Samuel's sons perverted justice for gain, which triggered the demand for a king.
- **"Like all the other nations":** The people's desire to be like other nations reveals a rejection of Israel's unique calling.
- **The law of the king (mishpat hammelekh):** Samuel's warning describes how royal power will impose heavy burdens on the people.
- **Rejection of God:** The Lord interprets the demand for a king as the people rejecting his kingship over them.
