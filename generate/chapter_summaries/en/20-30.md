**Main Theme:** Agur's words of wisdom express humility before God's incomprehensible greatness and convey practical wisdom through numerical proverbs.

**Content:** Agur acknowledges his limited understanding and God's perfect word (vv. 1–6), and asks for neither poverty nor riches, but only the daily bread (vv. 7–9). The chapter contains several numerical lists: four insatiable things, four detestable generations, four amazing ways (eagle, snake, ship, man with a young woman), four things that shake the earth, and four small but wise creatures. The conclusion warns against pride and provocation.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Agur's humility:** He calls himself ignorant and asks rhetorically who has ascended to heaven—only God knows such things.
- **Numerical proverbs:** The "three... four" structure is a literary device that builds tension toward the final, most important element.
- **The four small wise ones:** Ants, rock badgers, locusts, and spiders show that wisdom does not depend on size or strength.
- **God's word as a shield:** Every word of God is pure and provides protection to those who take refuge in him (v. 5).
