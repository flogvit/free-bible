**Main Theme:** God’s judgment on Samaria and Judah because of their rebellion and idolatry.

**Content:** The prophet Micah from Moresheth delivers the Lord’s message during the reigns of kings Jotham, Ahaz, and Hezekiah. God Himself descends from His holy dwelling, and the mountains melt beneath His feet. Samaria will be laid in ruins because of idolatry, and judgment reaches all the way to Jerusalem’s gates. The prophet mourns deeply over the destruction and cries out laments over the towns of Judah through a series of wordplays.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Theophany (v.3–4):** The Lord’s coming is described with cosmic imagery—mountains melt like wax and valleys split open.
- **Fall of Samaria (v.6–7):** The city will become a heap of stones and a vineyard; idols and the wages of prostitution will be destroyed.
- **The Prophet’s Lament (v.8–9):** Micah goes naked and barefoot, howling like jackals and ostriches over the incurable wound.
- **Wordplays on City Names (v.10–15):** A series of wordplays link the names of the towns to their fate (for example, Beth-leaphrah – "house of dust" – roll yourself in dust).
