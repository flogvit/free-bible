**Main Theme:** God's judgment against oppressive world powers and His promise of restoration for Israel.

**Content:** The chapter opens with God's promise to show mercy to Jacob and bring the people back to their land (vv. 1–3). The main section is a taunt song against the fallen king of Babylon, who in his arrogance sought to ascend to heaven but was cast down to Sheol (vv. 4–23). The chapter concludes with brief words of judgment against Assyria (vv. 24–27) and Philistia (vv. 28–32), affirming that the Lord has established Zion as a refuge for His people.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Morning Star (הֵילֵל):** A poetic designation for the king of Babylon, who fell from his exalted position of power down to Sheol.
- **Sheol (שְׁאוֹל):** The place where the mighty king meets the dead kings and becomes as powerless as they are.
- **The Lord's Outstretched Hand:** A symbol of God's sovereign judgment that no one can stop (vv. 26–27).
- **Zion as a Refuge:** God's city where the poor among His people find safety (v. 32).
