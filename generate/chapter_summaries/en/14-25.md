**Main Theme:** Amaziah's reign is marked by partial obedience to God, military success against Edom, but also idolatry and a humiliating defeat at the hands of Israel.

**Content:** Amaziah became king at twenty-five years old and reigned for twenty-nine years. He did what was right, yet not wholeheartedly. He gathered the army and sent away the Israelite mercenaries on the advice of a prophet, even though this cost him money. He defeated Edom in the Valley of Salt but took their gods as trophies and began to worship them, provoking God's anger. His pride led to an unnecessary attack on Israel, where he suffered defeat, was taken captive, and Jerusalem was plundered. Finally, a conspiracy was formed against him, and he was killed in Lachish.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Not Wholeheartedly (v.2):** Amaziah's fundamental problem—outward obedience without complete devotion to God.
- **Man of God/Prophet (vv.7–9, 15–16):** God sends warnings, but Amaziah rejects them when they challenge his choices.
- **The Thistle and the Cedar (v.18):** Joash's parable that exposes Amaziah's pride following his victory over Edom.
- **Edomite Gods (vv.14–15):** Amaziah's incomprehensible decision to worship the gods of a people he himself had defeated.
