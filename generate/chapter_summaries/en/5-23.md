**Main Theme:** Laws concerning marriage, who may belong to the assembly of the Lord, purity in the camp, and ethical obligations in daily life.

**Content:** The chapter begins with a prohibition against marrying one's father's wife. It then defines those excluded from the assembly of the Lord: men with damaged genitalia, those born of forbidden unions (mamzer), as well as Ammonites and Moabites—while Edomites and Egyptians are treated more leniently. Rules for purity in the war camp follow, where God's presence requires holiness. The chapter also gives laws concerning protection for runaway slaves, a prohibition against temple prostitution, a ban on charging interest to fellow countrymen, the importance of keeping vows, and the right to eat from a neighbor's crop without taking any away.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Assembly of the Lord (קְהַל יְהוָה):** The cultic and social community that requires certain qualifications for membership.
- **Holiness of the Camp:** God's presence among the people demands physical and ritual purity, even in times of war.
- **Vows to the Lord:** What one promises God must be kept without delay—it is better not to vow than to break a vow.
- **Ban on Interest (נֶשֶׁךְ):** Israelites are not to charge interest from one another, as an expression of brotherhood and solidarity.
- **Protection for Slaves:** A runaway slave must not be handed over but allowed to live freely in Israel—an expression of human dignity.
