**Main Theme:** God’s faithfulness to Israel despite the people’s sin, demonstrated through deliverance from Aram and the prophet Elisha’s final acts.

**Content:** King Jehoahaz and his son Joash both rule over Israel in Samaria, yet they continue in the sins of Jeroboam. God allows Aram to oppress Israel but shows mercy when Jehoahaz cries out, granting them a deliverer—for the sake of His covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. The dying prophet Elisha gives Joash a promise of victory over Aram through a symbolic act involving arrows, but the king’s halfhearted response limits the victory to three blows. After Elisha’s death, his bones raise a dead man to life, and Joash recovers the cities that Aram had taken from his father.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The Lord’s anger and mercy:** God punishes Israel for sin but shows compassion when they cry out to Him, remaining faithful to His covenant.
- **The arrow and victory:** Elisha’s symbolic act with arrows shows that the intensity of faith affects the extent of God’s blessing.
- **Elisha’s bones:** Even in death, the prophet conveys God’s life-giving power—a sign that God’s word endures.
- **The covenant with the fathers:** God’s mercy toward Israel is rooted in the promises made to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob.
