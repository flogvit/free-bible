**Main Theme:** The Preacher asks what man gains from all his toil under the sun, and concludes that everything is fleeting.

**Content:** The Preacher, who presents himself as king in Jerusalem, declares that everything is "hevel" (fleeting/vanity). He observes nature's eternal cycles—the sun, wind, and rivers repeat their patterns, while generations come and go without lasting memory. The Preacher has sought wisdom to understand all that happens under heaven, but finds this a heavy task God has given mankind. His conclusion: much wisdom brings much sorrow.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Hevel (הֶבֶל):** "Vanity" or "fleeting"—describes the transitory nature of human striving and existence.
- **Under the sun:** An expression referring to life on earth, the earthly perspective on existence.
- **Nature's cycles:** Sun, wind, and rivers illustrate endless repetition where nothing new happens.
- **Chasing after wind (רְעוּת רוּחַ):** A picture of futile striving—as if trying to grasp the wind.
