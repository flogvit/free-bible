**Main Theme:** Jethro's visit to Moses and the establishment of a delegated judicial system in Israel.

**Content:** Jethro, priest of Midian and Moses' father-in-law, brings Zipporah and Moses' two sons, Gershom and Eliezer, to him in the wilderness after hearing about God's deliverance of Israel. He praises the Lord and confesses that He is greater than all gods, and offers burnt offerings and sacrifices together with Aaron and the elders of Israel. When Jethro sees that Moses judges the people alone from morning until evening, he advises him to appoint godly, trustworthy men who hate dishonest gain as leaders over thousands, hundreds, fifties, and tens. Moses follows this advice, and the new leaders handle smaller cases while the difficult ones are brought to Moses.

**Key Terms/Images:**
- **Jethro:** Moses' father-in-law and a Midianite priest who recognizes Israel's God and gives wise counsel on leadership.
- **Delegated Leadership:** A system of leaders at various levels that relieves Moses and ensures efficient administration of justice.
- **Godliness:** The qualification requirement for the leaders—they must be godly, trustworthy, and hate dishonest gain.
- **Burnt Offerings and Sacrifices:** Jethro's worship of God together with Aaron and the elders of Israel, confirming his faith.
