**Main Theme:** God accuses Israel of unfaithfulness and idolatry, bringing a legal case against His people who have abandoned Him.

**Content:** God reminds Israel of their early faithfulness in the wilderness but accuses them of forsaking Him—the source of living water—in favor of worthless idols (cracked cisterns). Both priests, teachers of the law, shepherds, and prophets have failed. Instead of trusting in God, Israel has sought political alliances with Egypt and Assyria. The chapter concludes with a warning that their own evil will bring punishment upon them.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The Source of Living Water vs. Cracked Cisterns (v.13):** God is the life-giving source, while idols are like broken cisterns that cannot hold water.
- **The Vine That Became Wild (v.21):** Israel was planted as a noble vine but has become foreign and wild.
- **Marriage Metaphor (vv.2, 20, 33):** The relationship between God and Israel is described as marriage, with Israel's unfaithfulness portrayed as adultery.
- **Legal Case (rîb) (vv.9, 29):** God brings a legal charge against the people and future generations.
