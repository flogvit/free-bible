**Main Theme:** The chapter explores the meaninglessness of life through oppression, loneliness, and fleeting success, but highlights the value of community.

**Content:** The Preacher observes the suffering of the oppressed who have no comforter, concluding that the dead and those never born are better off. He sees that all striving is driven by envy—this too is vanity. Community provides practical strength: two are better than one, and a three-stranded cord is not easily broken. Even a poor but wise youth is better than an old and foolish king, yet even popularity is fleeting.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Oppression (עֲשֻׁקִים):** The suffering who lack comforters, while the oppressors hold power.
- **Hebel (הֶבֶל):** "Vanity" or "vapor"—a repeated judgment on life's striving.
- **The three-stranded cord:** A symbol of community strength—it is not easily broken.
- **The wise youth and the foolish king:** Wisdom surpasses position and age, yet even popularity passes away.
