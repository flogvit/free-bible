**Main Theme:** The lineage from Adam to Noah demonstrates humanity's continuation through ten generations, characterized by the image of God, long lifespans, and the reality of death.

**Content:** The chapter opens with a reminder that God created mankind in His image, as male and female. This is followed by a genealogy from Adam through Seth, Enosh, Kenan, Mahalalel, Jared, Enoch, Methuselah, and Lamech to Noah. Each generation is described with their years of life and descendants, and the refrain "and he died" emphasizes the entry of death. Enoch stands out because he "walked with God" and was taken away without dying. Noah, whose name is linked to comfort from the curse upon the earth, concludes the list with his three sons: Shem, Ham, and Japheth.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Image of God (בִּדְמוּת אֱלֹהִים):** Human dignity and likeness to God, passed down from Adam to Seth.
- **"And he died" (וַיָּמֹת):** A repeated refrain showing the consequence of the fall—death as humanity's lot.
- **Walk with God (וַיִּתְהַלֵּךְ אֶת־הָאֱלֹהִים):** Enoch's unique fellowship with God, which led to him being taken away without dying.
- **Noah (נֹחַ):** The name means "rest/comfort" and points to hope amidst the curse upon the earth.
