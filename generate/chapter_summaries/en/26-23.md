**Main Theme:** God accuses Samaria (Oholah) and Jerusalem (Oholibamah) of spiritual adultery and announces judgment through their former allies.

**Content:** The chapter is an allegory in which two sisters, Oholah and Oholibamah, represent Samaria and Jerusalem. Both sisters practiced "adultery" from their youth in Egypt and continued with Assyria and Babylon—this describes idolatry and political alliances. They desecrated God's sanctuary and sacrificed their own children to idols. As punishment, God will bring their former "lovers" against them with armies, and Jerusalem must drink the same cup of destruction that Samaria drank.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Oholah and Oholibamah:** Allegorical names for Samaria ("her tent") and Jerusalem ("my tent is in her"), portrayed as God's unfaithful wives.
- **Adultery/Spiritual Unfaithfulness:** A sexual metaphor for idolatry and political alliances with pagan nations.
- **The Cup:** A symbol of the judgment that Jerusalem must drink—the same fateful cup that Samaria drank.
- **Child Sacrifice:** The most serious accusation—they sacrificed their children to idols and desecrated God's sanctuary on the very same day.
