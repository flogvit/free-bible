**Main Theme:** The chapter deals with purity in the camp and procedures for handling sin and suspicion of adultery.

**Content:** God commands that anyone with a skin disease, a discharge, or who has touched a dead body must be sent outside the camp to preserve its holiness where He dwells. Furthermore, rules are given for making restitution when someone sins against another: the guilty party must confess, pay full compensation plus one-fifth—and if no relative is available, the compensation goes to the priest along with a ram of atonement. The chapter concludes with the "trial of bitter water" for a woman suspected of adultery, where she is brought before the Lord and must drink "the bitter water"—holy water mixed with dust from the tabernacle floor and words of curse washed off a scroll—which will reveal guilt or innocence.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Purity in the Camp:** The unclean must be sent out because God dwells in the midst of the camp, and uncleanness is incompatible with His holiness.
- **Confession and Restitution:** Sin requires both open confession and full restoration plus one-fifth to the wronged party or to the priest.
- **The Bitter Water (מֵי הַמָּרִים):** A divine test in which the water reveals the truth when human witnesses are lacking.
- **Standing Before the Lord:** The woman is brought before the Lord with her hair loosened, emphasizing that God Himself is the judge in this matter.
