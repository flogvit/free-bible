**Main Theme:** God confronts Jerusalem with her unfaithfulness through an allegory of a forsaken girl who becomes queen but betrays her benefactor.

**Content:** God finds Jerusalem as a forsaken infant, rescues her, and makes her His bride with great beauty and wealth. But Jerusalem misuses these gifts for idolatry, child sacrifice, and political alliances with Egypt, Assyria, and Babylon. She is worse than her "sisters" Samaria and Sodom. God will punish her through her own lovers, but ultimately promises to remember His covenant and establish an everlasting covenant with her.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The Foundling (v.4-6):** Jerusalem as a forsaken newborn symbolizes Israel’s humble origins without any merit of its own.
- **The Marriage Covenant (v.8):** God’s relationship with Israel as a loving covenant with obligations.
- **Prostitution/Unfaithfulness (זנה):** Idolatry and political alliances portrayed as sexual unfaithfulness toward a marriage partner.
- **Samaria and Sodom (v.46-52):** Jerusalem’s "sisters" highlighting that her sin surpasses even these notorious cities.
- **Everlasting Covenant (v.60-63):** God’s promise of restoration despite Jerusalem’s unfaithfulness—grace that exceeds judgment.
