**Main Theme:** Twelve memorial stones are set up after Israel’s miraculous crossing of the Jordan.

**Content:** After all the people have crossed the Jordan, God commands Joshua to have twelve men—one from each tribe—take twelve stones from the riverbed where the priests stood with the ark of the covenant. The stones are erected at Gilgal as an everlasting memorial, and Joshua also sets up twelve stones in the middle of the Jordan. When the priests come out of the river, the water returns to its place. Joshua explains that these stones will remind future generations how God dried up the Jordan, just as He did with the Red Sea—so that all the peoples of the earth may know the Lord’s mighty hand.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Twelve Stones:** A memorial taken from the riverbed, one for each tribe, serving as a witness to future generations.
- **Ark of the Covenant:** Carried by the priests who stood in the middle of the Jordan while the people crossed, symbolizing God’s presence.
- **Gilgal:** The place where Israel first camped and the stones were set up, on the tenth day of the first month.
- **Red Sea Parallel:** Joshua connects the drying up of the Jordan to the deliverance from Egypt, demonstrating God’s enduring power.
