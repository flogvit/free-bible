**Main Theme:** The contrast between the security of the righteous and the downfall of the wicked, with emphasis on obedience to the Torah and righteous leadership.

**Content:** The chapter consists of proverbs that contrast righteous and wicked people across various areas of life: leadership, economics, confession of sin, and trust in God. Those who keep the Torah and seek the Lord gain insight and blessing, while those who abandon it are praised by the wicked. A central verse (v.13) promises mercy to the one who confesses and forsakes their sins. The chapter strongly warns against oppressive leaders and greed.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Lion/Lion cub (כְּפִיר):** The righteous have boldness like a lion, while a wicked ruler is compared to a roaring lion and bear that threaten the people.
- **Torah (תּוֹרָה):** Keeping or forsaking the law is decisive—it determines whether one gains insight and whether their prayer is heard.
- **Confession (מוֹדֶה):** The one who hides their sins will fail, but the one who confesses and forsakes them receives mercy.
- **Poor and rich (רָשׁ/עָשִׁיר):** A poor person who lives blamelessly is better than a rich person with crooked ways—integrity triumphs over wealth.
