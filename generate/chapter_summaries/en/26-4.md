**Main Theme:** God commands Ezekiel to perform symbolic acts that prophetically foretell the siege of Jerusalem and the people’s punishment for sin.

**Content:** Ezekiel is instructed to carve a depiction of Jerusalem on a brick and build siege works around it, with an iron pan as a wall between himself and the city. He must lie on his left side for 390 days to bear the guilt of Israel, then 40 days on his right side for the guilt of Judah—one day for each year. The prophet is required to eat strict rations of mixed grain baked over cow dung (changed from human excrement after his protest), as a sign of the unclean food the people will consume in exile.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The Brick with Jerusalem:** A prophetic model that vividly portrays the coming siege of the city.
- **The Iron Pan:** Symbolizes the impenetrable barrier between God and His people due to their sin.
- **Lying on One’s Side:** Each day represents one year of guilt—390 years for Israel and 40 years for Judah.
- **The Rations:** A mixture of six types of grain with meager portions illustrates the famine and uncleanness during the siege and exile.
