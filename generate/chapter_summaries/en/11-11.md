**Main Theme:** Solomon's apostasy from God through idolatry leads to the division of the kingdom.

**Content:** King Solomon loved many foreign women (700 wives and 300 concubines) who turned his heart to other gods, including Ashtoreth, Chemosh, and Milcom. God became angry and declared that the kingdom would be torn away from Solomon's descendants and given to his servant; yet for David's sake, this would not happen until after Solomon's death, and one tribe would remain with his son. God raised up adversaries against Solomon: Hadad from Edom and Rezon from Damascus. The prophet Ahijah gave Jeroboam the promise of ten tribes, and Solomon sought to kill him, but Jeroboam fled to Egypt. Solomon died after 40 years of reign, and his son Rehoboam became king.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Turning of the Heart:** Solomon's foreign wives turned his heart away from wholehearted loyalty to the Lord (v.3-4).
- **High Places:** Solomon built places of worship for foreign gods on the hill east of Jerusalem (v.7-8).
- **The Torn Robe:** The prophet Ahijah tore his new robe into twelve pieces as a sign of the kingdom's division (v.30-31).
- **For David's Sake:** God's grace toward David's line limits the judgment—one tribe is preserved for his descendants (v.12-13, 32).
