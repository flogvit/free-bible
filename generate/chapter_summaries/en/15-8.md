**Main Theme:** Ezra leads a group of Jewish exiles from Babylon to Jerusalem under God's protecting hand.

**Content:** Ezra gathers the family heads who are to travel with him but discovers that no Levites are present, so he sends messengers to recruit temple servants. At the Ahava River, he proclaims a fast and prayer to seek God's protection for the journey—he did not want to ask the king for a military escort after having testified to God's care. Silver, gold, and holy vessels are entrusted to priests and Levites. After arriving safely in Jerusalem, the gifts are delivered to the temple; the people offer burnt offerings and sin offerings, and the king's decree is handed over to the authorities.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Ahava River:** The gathering place where Ezra organized the group and proclaimed a fast before departure.
- **"God's good hand upon us":** A repeated expression (vv. 18, 31) emphasizing that God guided and protected the journey.
- **Fasting and prayer:** Ezra's choice to trust in God rather than military power demonstrated his faith in God's promises.
- **Holy vessels:** The silver, gold, and vessels were consecrated to the Lord and had to be carefully guarded until they reached the temple.
- **Burnt offerings and sin offerings:** Upon arrival, the people offered sacrifices to the God of Israel—12 bulls for all Israel symbolized reunion.
