**Main Theme:** Jesus demonstrates his authority to forgive sins and heal, showing compassion for people who are like sheep without a shepherd.

**Content:** Jesus heals a paralyzed man by forgiving his sins, which provokes accusations of blasphemy from the scribes. He calls the tax collector Matthew and eats with sinners, defending this by saying he has come to call sinners, not the righteous. In response to questions about fasting, he uses images of the bridegroom and new wine in new wineskins. Jesus performs several miracles: healing a woman who had been bleeding, raising the daughter of a synagogue leader from the dead, giving sight to two blind men, and casting out a demon from a mute man. The chapter concludes with Jesus' compassion for the crowds and his exhortation to pray for workers for the harvest.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Son of Man:** Jesus uses this title for himself when he claims authority on earth to forgive sins.
- **Bridegroom:** A picture of Jesus himself—as long as he is with his disciples, there is no time for fasting and mourning.
- **New wine in new wineskins:** Illustrates that Jesus' teaching cannot be forced into old religious forms.
- **Sheep without a shepherd:** Describes the spiritual condition of the people and stirs deep compassion in Jesus.
- **Harvest:** A picture of the many who need to be reached with the gospel, while the workers are few.
