**Main Theme:** The chapter shows the seriousness of approaching God in the right way, illustrated through the death of Nadab and Abihu and the subsequent instructions to the priests.

**Content:** Nadab and Abihu, Aaron's sons, bring "strange fire" before the Lord and are consumed by divine fire. Moses forbids Aaron and his remaining sons from showing signs of mourning (letting their hair hang loose or tearing their clothes) or leaving the entrance of the Tent, while all Israel must mourn. The Lord speaks directly to Aaron and forbids wine and strong drink before priestly service, so that the priests may distinguish between holy and common. The chapter concludes with a discussion about the sin offering, where Aaron explains why it was not eaten but burned, and Moses accepts his explanation.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Strange fire (אֵשׁ זָרָה):** Unauthorized worship that God had not commanded, with death as the consequence.
- **"By those who come near me I will show myself holy":** The Lord's words quoted by Moses to Aaron – closeness to God requires greater holiness and precision.
- **Wine and strong drink:** Prohibition against intoxication in service so that one may distinguish between holy and common, clean and unclean.
- **Aaron's silence (וַיִּדֹּם אַהֲרֹן):** Aaron's silence expresses submission to God's judgment amid personal tragedy.
