**Main Theme:** God opens salvation to all who keep his covenant, including foreigners and eunuchs, while criticizing Israel’s failing leaders.

**Content:** The chapter begins with an exhortation to uphold justice and righteousness because God’s salvation is near. Foreigners and eunuchs who join themselves to the Lord and keep the Sabbath are promised an eternal name and a place in God’s house. God promises that his house shall be called a house of prayer for all peoples. The chapter concludes with sharp criticism of Israel’s leaders (watchmen and shepherds) who are blind, mute, and self-centered.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Sabbath (שַׁבָּת):** A sign of covenant faithfulness that applies to all who wish to belong to God.
- **House of prayer:** God’s temple is to be open to all nations, not only Israel.
- **Mute dogs:** A picture of leaders who fail in their duty as watchmen and seek their own gain.
- **Eunuch (סָּרִיס):** A symbol of the marginalized who nevertheless receive an eternal name from God.
