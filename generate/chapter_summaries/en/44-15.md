**Main Theme:** The Apostolic Council in Jerusalem decides that Gentile Christians must not be circumcised but are saved by grace.

**Content:** Some from Judea claim that Gentiles must be circumcised to be saved, leading to dispute. Paul and Barnabas are sent to Jerusalem to resolve the question. Peter argues that God makes no distinction between Jews and Gentiles, and that all are saved through the Lord's grace. James proposes that Gentiles should only abstain from food offered to idols, blood, meat of strangled animals, and sexual immorality. The chapter concludes with Paul and Barnabas parting ways due to disagreement over John Mark.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Circumcision:** The central issue of dispute – must Gentiles become Jews to be Christians?
- **The Yoke (ζυγόν):** Peter's image of the Mosaic Law as a burden that neither the fathers nor they themselves could bear.
- **The Holy Spirit and we:** The wording in the letter (v. 28) shows that the decision was understood as guided by the Spirit.
- **The Apostolic Letter:** The official letter containing the four guidelines, sent to Gentile Christian congregations.
