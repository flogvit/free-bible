**Main Theme:** Regulations for the Peace Offering (שְׁלָמִים), a voluntary offering that expresses fellowship with God.

**Content:** The peace offering could be cattle, sheep, or goats—either male or female—but had to be without defect. The offerer laid his hand on the animal's head before slaughter, and the priests sprinkled the blood around the altar. The fat covering the entrails, the kidneys with their surrounding fat, and the lobe of the liver were burned as a burnt offering—a pleasing aroma to the Lord. For sheep, the entire fatty tail was also offered. The chapter concludes with an eternal command: The people of Israel must never eat fat or blood.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Peace Offering (שְׁלָמִים):** A voluntary offering expressing gratitude, peace, and fellowship between God and humanity.
- **Laying on of Hands:** The offerer identifies with the sacrificial animal by placing his hand on its head.
- **The Fat (חֵלֶב):** Belongs to the Lord alone and is burned as a burnt offering; people are not permitted to eat it.
- **Eternal Command:** The prohibition against eating fat and blood applies to all generations in all their dwellings.
