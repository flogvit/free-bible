**Main Theme:** Job curses the day he was born and expresses a deep longing for death as deliverance from suffering.

**Content:** After seven days of silence, Job breaks his silence with an intense lament in which he curses the day of his birth and wishes he had never been conceived. He asks why light and life are given to those who suffer, and describes death as a place of rest where all are equal—kings and slaves, prisoners and free people. The chapter ends with Job describing his condition: turmoil, fear, and lack of peace.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Darkness and Light:** Job wishes that the day of his birth be swallowed up by darkness and never see light—symbolizing his desire for obliteration.
- **The Realm of the Dead as Rest:** Death is portrayed as a place where the weary find peace and prisoners escape the voice of their oppressors (v. 13–19).
- **Leviathan:** A mythological sea monster that those who curse days might awaken—representing chaotic forces (v. 8).
- **Groans and Roars:** Job's suffering is so intense that his groans come before his food, and his cries flow like water (v. 24).
