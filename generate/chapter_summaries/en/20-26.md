**Main Theme:** Warnings against folly, laziness, and deceitful speech through vivid imagery and practical wisdom.

**Content:** The chapter begins with a series of proverbs about the fool (vv. 1–12), including the paradoxical advice to both answer and not answer a fool. This is followed by descriptions of the lazy person who always makes excuses (vv. 13–16). The chapter concludes with warnings against meddling in others' disputes, gossip, falsehood, and malicious speech that creates conflict (vv. 17–28).

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The Fool (kesil):** One who lacks wisdom and repeats his folly, like a dog returning to its vomit (v. 11).
- **The Lazy Person (atsel):** Described humorously as someone who turns in bed like a door on hinges, too tired even to bring his hand to his mouth (vv. 14–15).
- **Words of Gossip:** Compared to choice morsels that go deep into the innermost being (v. 22).
- **Fire and Strife:** Without wood, the fire goes out; without a gossip, strife ceases (vv. 20–21).
