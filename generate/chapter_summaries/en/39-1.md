**Main Theme:** God declares his love for Israel and accuses the priests of profaning his name through imperfect sacrifices.

**Content:** The chapter opens with God’s declaration of his love for Jacob (Israel) in contrast to Esau (Edom), whose land lies desolate. Then God sharply criticizes the priests who despise his name by offering blind, lame, and sick animals on the altar. God rejects their defiled offerings and declares that his name is great among the nations from east to west. Whoever has an unblemished animal but offers a defective one is cursed—for the Lord is a great King.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Jacob and Esau:** God’s sovereign election—he loved Jacob but hated Esau, as shown in Edom’s destruction.
- **Profaned Sacrifices:** The priests offered animals with defects (blind, lame, sick), which would have been unacceptable even to a human governor.
- **The Lord’s Table:** The altar that the priests treated with contempt by calling it "despicable".
- **God’s Name Among the Nations:** A universal vision where God’s name is honored from sunrise to sunset, in contrast to Israel’s dishonor.
