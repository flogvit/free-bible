**Main Theme:** The chapter shows human pride at the Tower of Babel and God's response through the confusion of languages, followed by the genealogy from Shem to Abram.

**Content:** The people gathered in the plain of Shinar to build a city and a tower that reached heaven, so they could make a name for themselves and avoid being scattered. God confused their language and scattered them over the earth—the place was called Babel. Then follows the genealogy from Shem (Noah's son) through several generations to Terah, who had sons Abram, Nahor, and Haran. Terah took Abram, Lot, and Sarai from Ur of the Chaldeans toward Canaan, but settled in Haran where he died.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Babel (בָּבֶל):** The name is connected with the Hebrew בָּלַל (balal), "to confuse"—the place where God confused human language as judgment on their arrogance.
- **The Tower:** A symbol of humanity's desire for self-exaltation and independence from God.
- **Confusion of Languages:** God's intervention to prevent united human rebellion and to scatter the peoples.
- **Genealogy from Shem:** Connects the flood with Abram and shows how God's plan of salvation continues through one chosen line.
- **Ur of the Chaldeans:** The starting point of Abram's journey, pointing forward to God's call and promises in chapter 12.
