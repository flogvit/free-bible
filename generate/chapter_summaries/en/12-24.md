**Main Theme:** God’s judgment on Judah through Babylonian invasions and exile as punishment for the people’s sins.

**Content:** King Jehoiakim became Nebuchadnezzar’s vassal for three years before he rebelled. The Lord sent enemies against Judah because of Manasseh’s sins and the innocent blood he had shed—sins God would not forgive. Jehoiakim’s son Jehoiachin reigned only three months before Nebuchadnezzar besieged Jerusalem, captured the king, and exiled the elite to Babylon along with the temple treasures. Nebuchadnezzar installed Zedekiah as a vassal king, but he too rebelled against Babylon.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The Exile (גּוֹלָה):** The first major deportation to Babylon, in which 10,000 of Judah’s leaders, soldiers, and craftsmen were taken away.
- **Manasseh’s Sins:** The reference to the former king’s wickedness shows that national sin has lasting consequences that ultimately lead to judgment.
- **Vassal King:** Both Jehoiakim and Zedekiah were installed as subordinate kings, demonstrating Judah’s loss of independence under God’s judgment.
