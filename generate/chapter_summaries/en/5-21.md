**Main Theme:** Laws that protect life, honor, and purity in Israel's community.

**Content:** The chapter provides regulations for various situations: In cases of unsolved murder, the elders must perform a ritual of atonement with a heifer to cleanse the community from bloodguilt. A captive woman taken as a wife is to be given time to mourn her parents and cannot be sold or treated as a slave if the husband later rejects her. The firstborn son is to receive his inheritance rights regardless of the father's feelings toward the mother. A stubborn and rebellious son who is a glutton and a drunkard is to be stoned. Anyone hanged on a tree after execution must be buried on the same day so as not to defile the land.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The Heifer with the Broken Neck** – Symbolizes atonement for the community when the murderer is unknown, freeing the people from bloodguilt.
- **The Captive Woman** – The rituals mark her transition from captive to wife and provide her with protection and dignity.
- **The Right of the Firstborn** – The firstborn son is to receive a double portion of the inheritance, regardless of which wife the father prefers.
- **The One Hanged on the Tree** – A curse rests upon the one hanged; prompt burial prevents the land from becoming defiled.
