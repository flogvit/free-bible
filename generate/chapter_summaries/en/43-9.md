**Main Theme:** Jesus reveals himself as the light of the world by healing a man born blind, exposing both physical and spiritual blindness.

**Content:** Jesus heals a man who has been blind from birth by applying clay to his eyes and sending him to the Pool of Siloam. The healing on the Sabbath creates conflict with the Pharisees, who interrogate the man repeatedly and eventually cast him out. Through the story, the man's faith grows—from calling Jesus "a man" to "a prophet," and finally worshiping him as the Son of Man. Jesus concludes by confronting the spiritual blindness of the Pharisees: those who admit their blindness will see, while those who claim to see remain in their sin.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Light of the world (φῶς τοῦ κόσμου):** Before the healing, Jesus declares himself the light of the world, linking physical sight with spiritual enlightenment.
- **Pool of Siloam (Σιλωάμ):** The name means "sent," symbolically pointing to Jesus as the one sent from God.
- **Sabbath violation:** The Pharisees' focus on Sabbath rules prevents them from seeing God's work in the healing.
- **Spiritual blindness:** Contrasts the growing insight of the man born blind with the increasing blindness of the Pharisees.
- **Excommunication (ἀποσυνάγωγος):** The threat of being cast out of the synagogue shows the social cost of following Jesus.
