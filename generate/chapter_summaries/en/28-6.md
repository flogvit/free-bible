**Main Theme:** God’s frustration over Israel’s fleeting faithfulness and superficial repentance.

**Content:** The chapter opens with the people’s call to return to the Lord, who will heal them (vv. 1–3). But God responds with frustration: their loyalty is like morning mist that quickly vanishes (v. 4). God desires steadfast love and knowledge of Him, not mere sacrifices (v. 6). The chapter concludes with harsh accusations against Gilead for bloodguilt, priests who murder, and Israel’s impurity through spiritual adultery (vv. 7–11).

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Morning mist and dew (v. 4):** A picture of the people’s fleeting and short-lived faithfulness to God.
- **Chesed/steadfast love (v. 6):** God’s desire for covenant loyalty rather than outward religiosity.
- **Covenant breaking (v. 7):** Israel’s fundamental betrayal of their covenant with God.
- **Gilead (v. 8):** An example of a city full of evildoers and bloodguilt.
