**Main Theme:** God contrasts His eternal care and sovereignty with the helpless Babylonian idols.

**Content:** The chapter opens with a sharp contrast: Babylon’s gods Bel and Nebo must themselves be carried as heavy burdens, while Israel’s God has carried His people from the womb to old age. God emphasizes His unique position as the only one who can predict the future and fulfill His plan. He calls a "bird of prey from the east" as His instrument and promises salvation in Zion for Israel.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Bel and Nebo:** Babylonian gods portrayed as powerless idol statues that must be carried away into captivity.
- **Carried from the womb:** A picture of God’s faithful care for Israel throughout their entire history, in contrast to idols that themselves must be carried.
- **Bird of prey from the east:** A warrior leader whom God calls to accomplish His plan—traditionally understood as Cyrus.
- **Salvation in Zion:** God’s promise of restoration and glory for Israel.
