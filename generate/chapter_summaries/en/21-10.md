**Main Theme:** Practical wisdom about speech, leadership, and action—and how small mistakes can ruin great things.

**Content:** The chapter opens with the idea that a little foolishness can spoil much wisdom, just as dead flies spoil fragrant ointment. The Preacher gives practical advice on maintaining composure before rulers, warns against poor leadership (an immature king versus an excellent king), and emphasizes that laziness leads to decay. The chapter concludes with a warning to be careful about what one says, even in private—for "the birds of the air will carry the message."

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Dead flies in ointment** (v.1): A little foolishness can ruin much wisdom and honor.
- **The snake that bites** (vv.8,11): Foolishness and lack of preparation lead to harm.
- **Dull iron** (v.10): Without wisdom, more strength is required—wisdom makes the work easier.
- **A leaking roof** (v.18): Laziness leads to decay and destruction.
- **The bird of heaven** (v.20): Even secret words can become known—be careful with your speech.
