**Main Theme:** Nehemiah confronts economic exploitation among the Jews and demonstrates selfless leadership.

**Content:** The people complain that they are forced to mortgage their fields and houses, borrow money to pay the king's tax, and sell their children into slavery due to debts owed to their own countrymen. Nehemiah becomes angry and confronts the wealthy and leaders who charge interest on loans to their brothers. He compels them to return the properties and cancel the interest, making them swear an oath before the priests. Nehemiah himself sets an example by refusing to claim the governor's food allowance during his twelve years as governor.

**Key Terms/Images:**
- **Mashsha (usury/interest):** The forbidden practice of charging interest on loans to fellow Israelites, which Nehemiah condemns.
- **Pledging collateral:** The people were forced to pledge their fields, vineyards, and houses to survive famine and pay taxes.
- **The governor's food allowance:** The support that Nehemiah voluntarily gave up so as not to burden the people further.
- **The oath and shaking of the garment:** As a symbolic act, Nehemiah shakes out the folds of his robe and makes the people swear to keep their agreement.
