**Main Theme:** Joash's reign shows how faithfulness to God under proper guidance can turn into apostasy and judgment when that guidance is removed.

**Content:** King Joash does what is right in the Lord's eyes as long as the priest Jehoiada lives, and leads a successful collection to restore the temple. After Jehoiada's death (at 130 years old, buried among the kings), Joash allows himself to be influenced by Judah's leaders into idol worship. The prophet Zechariah, son of Jehoiada, warns the people but is stoned at the king's command. As punishment, the Arameans attack Judah with a small force and defeat them, and Joash is eventually murdered by his own servants.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The chest at the temple gate** – Symbolizes the people's willing generosity and unity in rebuilding God's house.
- **Jehoiada's burial among the kings** – Shows his unique honor and influence, in contrast to Joash who did not receive a royal burial.
- **Zechariah's blood cries out** – His final words "May the Lord see and demand justice" are fulfilled through the Aramean attack and Joash's death.
- **The small Aramean army** – Illustrates that the Lord grants victory regardless of military strength when the people have abandoned Him.
