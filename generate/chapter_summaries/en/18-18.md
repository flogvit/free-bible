**Main Theme:** Bildad's second speech describes the inevitable destruction of the wicked as an expression of God's righteous order.

**Content:** Bildad begins by criticizing Job for his choice of words and for treating his friends as senseless animals (vv. 2-4). He then paints a detailed picture of the fate of the unrighteous: their light is extinguished, they are caught in snares and traps, and are led to "the king of terrors" (death). The destruction of the wicked is total—their roots dry up, their branches wither, their memory is erased, and no descendants survive. Such is the fate of those who do not know God.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Light and Darkness (vv. 5-6, 18):** The light of the wicked is snuffed out, and they are driven from light into darkness—a symbol of life's end and God's absence.
- **Snares and Traps (vv. 8-10):** Multiple terms for trapping devices show that the wicked are caught in their own evil with no escape.
- **King of Terrors (v. 14):** "Melech ballahot"—a personification of death as ruler over the wicked.
- **Roots and Branches (v. 16):** A plant metaphor for total destruction—neither foundation nor future remains.
- **Erasure of Memory and Lineage (vv. 17-19):** The wicked leave behind neither name, descendants, nor anyone to remember them.
