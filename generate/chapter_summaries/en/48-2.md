**Main Theme:** Paul defends his apostleship and the doctrine of justification by faith alone, not by works of the law.

**Content:** After fourteen years, Paul visits Jerusalem with Barnabas and Titus, where the pillar apostles (James, Peter, and John) recognize his calling to the Gentiles. The fact that Titus, a Greek, was not compelled to be circumcised confirms the freedom of the gospel. In Antioch, Paul publicly confronts Peter for hypocrisy when he withdrew from table fellowship with Gentile believers. Paul concludes that a person is justified by faith in Christ, not by works of the law, and declares: "I have been crucified with Christ; it is no longer I who live, but Christ lives in me".

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Justification by Faith:** A person is made right with God through faith in Christ, not through works of the law.
- **Crucified with Christ:** The believer has died to the law and now lives a new life where Christ lives in and through them.
- **Freedom in Christ:** The gospel frees us from the demands of the law, such as circumcision, and false brothers threaten this freedom.
- **Apostolic Unity:** The pillar apostles recognize Paul's calling and extend the right hand of fellowship to him and Barnabas.
