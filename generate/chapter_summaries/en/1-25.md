**Main Theme:** The transition from Abraham to the next generation, focusing on Isaac's family and the conflict between the twins Esau and Jacob.

**Content:** Abraham has several children with Keturah but gives everything to Isaac before he dies at 175 years old and is buried in the cave of Machpelah. Ishmael has twelve sons who become princes, and he dies at 137 years old. Isaac prays for his barren wife Rebekah, and God reveals that she carries two nations—the older shall serve the younger. The twins Esau and Jacob are born, and they develop very differently. Esau sells his birthright to Jacob for a bowl of lentil stew, thus showing contempt for his right.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Birthright (בְּכֹרָה):** The eldest son's right to a double inheritance and family leadership, which Esau despised.
- **"The older shall serve the younger":** God's prophetic word that overturns the natural order and points to God's sovereign choice.
- **Lentil stew (נְזִיד עֲדָשִׁים):** Symbolizes the earthly and immediate, which Esau prioritized over spiritual blessings.
- **Twelve princes:** Ishmael's descendants fulfill God's promise that he too would become a great nation.
