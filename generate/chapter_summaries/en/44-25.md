**Main Theme:** Paul appeals his case to the emperor after new legal hearings under Governor Festus.

**Content:** Festus takes over as governor and is immediately confronted with demands from Jewish leaders to have Paul transferred to Jerusalem, where they plan an ambush. Festus holds a trial in Caesarea, but when he suggests that Paul be tried in Jerusalem, Paul invokes his right as a Roman citizen and appeals to the emperor. King Agrippa and Bernice visit Festus, who presents the case to them because he lacks specific charges to send with him to Rome.

**Key Terms/Images:**
- **Appeal to the Emperor:** Paul's right as a Roman citizen to have his case heard by the emperor in Rome, which protects him from local injustice.
- **Βῆμα (the judgment seat):** The official judge's platform where Festus sits as representative of Roman law and authority.
- **Festus' Dilemma:** The governor finds no criminal act, yet must still send Paul to Rome with a formal indictment.
- **Agrippa as Advisor:** The Jewish king is involved to help Festus understand the religious questions in the case.
