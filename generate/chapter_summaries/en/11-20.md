**Main Theme:** God’s sovereignty is demonstrated through two victories over Aram, but Ahab loses the blessing by sparing Ben-Hadad, who was destined for destruction.

**Content:** King Ben-Hadad of Aram besieges Samaria with 32 allied kings and demands Ahab’s wealth, wives, and children. A prophet promises victory from the Lord, and Israel defeats the Arameans twice—first near Samaria, then at Aphek, where 100,000 Aramean soldiers fall. Ahab makes a covenant with the captured Ben-Hadad and lets him go free. A prophet announces God’s judgment: Because Ahab released a man devoted to destruction, his own life and people will pay the price.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **32 kings:** Ben-Hadad’s coalition shows the overwhelming strength of Israel’s enemies, making God’s victory all the more impressive.
- **"The God of the Hills":** The Arameans’ mistaken belief that Israel’s God had power only in the mountains, which God disproves on the plain.
- **Cherem (devoted to destruction):** Ben-Hadad was destined by God for destruction; sparing him was serious disobedience.
- **The prophet’s parable:** Through a staged story, Ahab is led to condemn himself for releasing the captive.
