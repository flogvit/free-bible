**Main Theme:** Jeremiah buys a field as a prophetic sign of God’s promise of restoration in the midst of Jerusalem’s fall.

**Content:** While Jerusalem is under siege and Jeremiah is imprisoned, God commands him to buy a field in Anathoth from his cousin Hanamel. The purchase, carried out with witnesses and sealed documents, becomes a sign that "houses, fields, and vineyards will again be bought in this land" (v.15). Jeremiah prays to God and confesses both God’s almighty power and the people’s sin. God responds with judgment over Jerusalem due to idolatry, but also with a promise to gather the people back and make an everlasting covenant with them.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The Field Purchase:** A prophetic act confirming hope for future restoration in the midst of crisis.
- **Sealed and Open Documents:** The deed was to be preserved in a clay jar for a long time as testimony to God’s promise.
- **Everlasting Covenant (berit olam):** God’s promise to give the people one heart and one way, so that they will never turn away from him.
- **"Is anything too hard for me?":** God’s rhetorical question (v.27) affirming his sovereign power over history.
