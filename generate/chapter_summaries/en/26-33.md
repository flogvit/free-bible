**Main Theme:** Ezekiel is confirmed as a watchman for Israel with the responsibility to warn the people, while God emphasizes that repentance is always possible.

**Content:** God explains the watchman's responsibility: both the one who warns and the one who hears the warning are accountable for their choices. The Lord declares that He does not desire the death of the wicked, but that they turn from their ways and live. After news of Jerusalem’s fall (v. 21), God rebukes those remaining in the land who claim Abraham as their ancestor yet continue to live in sin. The chapter concludes with criticism of the people who listen to Ezekiel’s words as entertainment without obeying them.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The Watchman (צֹפֶה):** The prophet’s role as a spiritual sentinel responsible for warning of coming danger and judgment.
- **Shofar (שׁוֹפָר):** The warning horn symbolizing the prophet’s proclamation and call to repentance.
- **Repentance (שׁוּב):** God’s heartfelt appeal for the wicked to turn from their ways and live.
- **The Fugitive (הַפָּלִיט):** The messenger who confirms Jerusalem’s fall, thereby validating Ezekiel’s prophetic credibility.
- **Love Song (שִׁיר עֲגָבִים):** A metaphor for how the people treat God’s word as mere entertainment without obedience.
