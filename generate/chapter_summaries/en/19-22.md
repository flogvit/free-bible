**Main Theme:** A lament psalm that moves from deep despair and a sense of God-forsakenness to praise and universal worship.

**Content:** David opens with the desperate cry, "My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?" and describes intense physical and emotional suffering. He is surrounded by enemies compared to bulls, lions, and dogs, while his body breaks down. In the midst of distress, he holds fast to trust in God who has been with him from birth. The psalm turns to praise: God has heard the sufferer, and all the ends of the earth shall turn and worship the Lord.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **"My God, why have you forsaken me"** – The opening cry expresses the experience of being abandoned by God, quoted by Jesus on the cross (Matt 27:46).
- **Bulls, lions, and dogs** – Predatory images describing the wickedness and threat of the enemies (vv.13–17).
- **Pierced hands and feet** – Description of the sufferer's condition, traditionally understood as messianic (v.17).
- **Casting lots for garments** – The enemies divide the sufferer's clothes, fulfilled at the crucifixion (v.19; cf. John 19:24).
- **All nations shall worship** – The climax of the psalm: God's kingdom extends to all nations (vv.28–32).
