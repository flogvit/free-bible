**Main Theme:** The chapter describes the final distribution of the restored land among Israel’s twelve tribes, with a central holy district and a city named "The Lord Is There."

**Content:** Each of the twelve tribes receives its own elongated portion of the land stretching from east to west, distributed from north (Dan) to south (Gad). Between Judah and Benjamin lies a holy area containing the temple, the territories of the priests and Levites, as well as the city. The prince receives land on both sides of this holy district. The city has twelve gates named after the tribes, and its name is "The Lord Is There" (JHVH Shammah).

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Land Distribution:** Each tribe receives an equal portion of the land, symbolizing God’s righteous restoration of Israel.
- **The Holy District (תְּרוּמָה):** A central area dedicated to God, set apart for the priests (the sons of Zadok), the Levites, and the city.
- **Zadokite Priests:** The priests from Zadok’s line who remained faithful receive the most sacred portion closest to the temple.
- **"The Lord Is There" (יְהוָה שָׁמָּה):** The name of the city expressing God’s permanent presence among His people—the climax and promise of the book.
