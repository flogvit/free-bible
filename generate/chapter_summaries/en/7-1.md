**Main Theme:** Israel's partial conquest of Canaan after Joshua's death, marked by both victories and disobedience.

**Content:** After Joshua's death, God chooses Judah to lead the conquest of Canaan. Together with Simeon, Judah achieves significant victories, including the capture of Jerusalem and Hebron. However, the chapter reveals a pattern of incomplete obedience: the tribes do not completely drive out the Canaanites but allow them to live among them or put them to forced labor. Particularly noteworthy is that Judah could not drive out the inhabitants of the valley because of their iron chariots.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Adoni-Bezek:** A Canaanite king whose thumbs and big toes were cut off – he acknowledges that God has repaid him for his own cruelty toward 70 kings.
- **Iron Chariots:** Symbolizing superior military technology that limited Israel's conquest, revealing the tension between God's promise and human resistance.
- **"Did not drive out":** A repeated expression (vv.21, 27–33) describing the tribes' disobedience and lack of trust in God.
- **Caleb and Achsah:** Examples of faithfulness and blessing – Caleb receives Hebron as promised, and Achsah wisely asks for water springs.
