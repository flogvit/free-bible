**Main Theme:** King Ahaz of Judah rejects the Lord and seeks help from Assyria, leading to religious syncretism in the temple.

**Content:** Ahaz did what was evil in the sight of the Lord: he followed the ways of the kings of Israel, made his son pass through the fire, and offered sacrifices on the high places. When Syria and Israel attacked, he sought help from Tiglath-Pileser king of Assyria and sent him gold and silver from the temple as tribute. In Damascus, Ahaz saw a pagan altar and had priest Uriah build a copy in Jerusalem, while moving the Lord's bronze altar aside and making changes to the temple furnishings to accommodate the king of Assyria.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Made his son pass through the fire:** Child sacrifice according to Canaanite custom, an abominable practice that the Lord had driven out the nations before (v.3).
- **Tribute to Assyria:** Ahaz emptied the temple and the treasury to buy military help, calling himself the king of Assyria's "servant and son" (vv.7-8).
- **The new altar:** A pagan altar copied from Damascus that replaced the Lord's bronze altar as the main altar in the temple (vv.10-15).
- **Priest Uriah:** Obeyed the king's command to build the foreign altar, an example of religious leadership failing (vv.11,16).
