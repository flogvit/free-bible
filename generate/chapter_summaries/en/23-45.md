**Main Theme:** God reveals his sovereignty by calling the Persian king Cyrus as his instrument and confirms that he alone is God and Savior for all peoples.

**Content:** God calls Cyrus his "anointed" and promises to go before him to defeat nations and open gates, all to free Israel from exile. Through the image of the potter and the clay, God emphasizes his right to deal with creation according to his will. The chapter proclaims God's unique divinity—he creates both light and darkness, peace and trouble. It concludes with a universal invitation to salvation: "Turn to me and be saved, all you ends of the earth."

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The Anointed (Cyrus):** A pagan king receives the messianic title "anointed" as God's chosen instrument for Israel's deliverance.
- **The Potter and the Clay:** The image illustrates God's sovereign right over his creation—humans cannot call the Creator to account.
- **"I am the Lord, and there is no other":** A repeated confession that emphasizes strict monotheism against idolatry.
- **Universal Salvation:** God's invitation to salvation extends to all ends of the earth, not only to Israel.
