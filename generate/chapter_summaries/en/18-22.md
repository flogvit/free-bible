**Main Theme:** Eliphaz's third speech, in which he accuses Job of hidden sins and urges him to repent.

**Content:** Eliphaz claims that Job's suffering must be due to serious sins, specifically accusing him of taking pledges unjustly, refusing food and water to those in need, and oppressing widows and orphans (vv. 6-9). He charges Job with believing that God is too exalted to see human deeds (vv. 12-14). Eliphaz concludes with a strong appeal for Job to turn back to God; if he does, he will be restored, blessed, and his prayers heard (vv. 21-30).

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Hidden Sin (vv. 5-9):** Eliphaz assumes that suffering proves guilt and constructs a list of social sins Job is alleged to have committed.
- **God's Exaltation (vv. 12-14):** Eliphaz distorts Job's position, accusing him of believing God is too distant to intervene.
- **Repentance and Restoration (vv. 21-30):** A conditional promise of blessing if Job repents – "Turn back to the Almighty, and you will be restored."
