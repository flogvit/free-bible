**Main Theme:** Ezekiel receives a detailed vision of a new temple with precise measurements, 25 years after the beginning of the exile.

**Content:** In the 25th year of the exile, Ezekiel is brought in a divine vision to a high mountain in Israel, where he sees a man whose appearance was like bronze, holding a measuring rod and a linen thread. The man systematically measures the outer wall of the temple, the three gates (east, north, and south) in both the outer and inner courts, as well as chambers and colonnades. The chapter also describes tables for sacrificial offerings at the north gate and rooms for the priests—the sons of Zadok—who are to serve at the altar.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Measuring Rod (קְנֵה הַמִּדָּה):** A rod six long cubits in length used to measure the temple's dimensions, symbolizing divine precision.
- **Gates (שְׁעָרִים):** Three identical gates in each court with guard chambers, marking the transition between the holy and the common.
- **Sons of Zadok (בְנֵי־צָדוֹק):** The priests entrusted with responsibility for the altar, highlighted as the only Levites who remained faithful.
- **Inner Court (הֶחָצֵר הַפְּנִימִי):** A square area measuring 100 by 100 cubits in front of the temple building itself, with the altar at its center.
