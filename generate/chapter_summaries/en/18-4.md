**Main Theme:** Eliphaz's first speech, where he confronts Job with the doctrine of retribution and a divine night vision concerning human frailty.

**Content:** Eliphaz begins by pointing out that Job, who has comforted many others, now falters when disaster strikes him (vv. 3–5). He appeals to Job's reverence for God as a basis for hope, asserting that the innocent do not perish (vv. 6–7). Then Eliphaz describes a frightening night vision in which a spirit revealed that no human can be righteous before God—even angels are not without fault (vv. 12–18). Humans, who dwell in "houses of clay", are crushed more easily than moths (vv. 19–21).

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Retribution (vv. 7–8):** The principle that whoever sows evil reaps disaster—the foundation of Eliphaz's theology.
- **The Night Vision (vv. 12–16):** A mysterious revelation that lends authority to Eliphaz's message about divine insight.
- **Houses of Clay (v. 19):** An image of human frailty and earthly existence compared with God's eternity.
- **Lions (vv. 10–11):** Even the mighty lion perished without prey—illustrating that no one escapes God's judgment.
