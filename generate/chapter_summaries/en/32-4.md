**Main Theme:** Jonah’s anger over God’s grace toward Nineveh and God’s lesson on compassion.

**Content:** Jonah becomes angry because God spares Nineveh, admitting that he fled precisely because he knew God is gracious and compassionate. He wishes to die and sits outside the city to see what will happen. God causes a plant to grow up to provide shade, but then sends a worm to destroy it. Through Jonah’s grief over the plant, God teaches him a lesson: If Jonah could grieve over a plant he did not create, how much more should God have compassion on the more than 120,000 people and many animals in Nineveh?

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The Plant:** A visual aid God uses to teach Jonah about compassion and the value of life.
- **God’s Grace (חַנּוּן וְרַחוּם):** Jonah quotes God’s character traits—gracious, compassionate, slow to anger, and abounding in love.
- **The People of Nineveh:** More than 120,000 people who cannot tell their right hand from their left, highlighting God’s care for the ignorant.
- **Jonah’s Anger:** Represents human resistance to God’s boundless grace toward enemies.
