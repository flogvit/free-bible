**Main Theme:** A prophetic parable about two eagles and a vine, illustrating Judah’s breach of covenant and God’s coming restoration.

**Content:** The first eagle (Babylon) took the king and leaders of Judah into captivity but planted a member of the royal line (Zedekiah) as a vassal king. Zedekiah broke his oath and covenant by turning to the second eagle (Egypt) for help. God condemns this as rebellion against Himself and announces judgment. The chapter concludes with a promise that God Himself will plant a branch from the cedar on the mountains of Israel—a messianic hope of restoration.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The Eagles:** Represent the great powers Babylon and Egypt, competing for influence over Judah.
- **The Vine:** Symbolizes Judah/Zedekiah turning away from his lord toward Egypt.
- **Oath and Covenant (אָלָה/בְּרִית):** Zedekiah’s breaking of the vassal oath is seen as rebellion against God Himself.
- **The Cedar on the Mountain:** A messianic picture of God’s future restoration of David’s kingdom.
