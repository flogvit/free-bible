**Main Theme:** God warns Jerusalem of judgment due to superficial piety but promises renewal for the humble.

**Content:** The prophet warns Ariel (Jerusalem) of an impending siege, yet assures that God will ultimately shatter the enemies like a vanishing dream. Isaiah criticizes the people’s hypocrisy—they honor God with their lips, but their hearts are far away, and their reverence consists only of learned human rules. As punishment, their wisdom will vanish, and their vision will become like a sealed book that no one can read. The chapter concludes with promises of renewal: the deaf will hear, the blind will see, and the humble will rejoice in the Lord.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Ariel:** A designation for Jerusalem, literally meaning "Lion of God" or "God’s altar"—the city where David camped.
- **Sealed Book:** A picture of the people’s spiritual blindness—vision is inaccessible to both the learned and the unlearned.
- **The Potter and the Clay:** Illustrates humanity’s rebellion against God—as if the created should say to the Creator, "You did not make me."
- **Lip Service:** The people honor God outwardly, but their hearts are distant—reverence has become empty, learned rituals.
- **The Humble and Poor:** Those who will rejoice when God intervenes and removes the oppressors.
