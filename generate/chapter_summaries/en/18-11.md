**Main Theme:** Zophar rebukes Job and asserts that God's unfathomable wisdom justifies His actions, and that repentance is the path to restoration.

**Content:** Zophar accuses Job of empty talk and pride, claiming that God is actually punishing him more gently than he deserves. He describes God's wisdom as incomprehensible—higher than heaven, deeper than Sheol. Zophar urges Job to turn to God with a pure heart and remove wrongdoing from his life. Then he will find security, hope, and honor, while the wicked move toward destruction.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **God's unfathomability (vv. 7–9):** God's wisdom surpasses all measurable dimensions—heaven, Sheol, earth, and sea.
- **The empty man (v. 12):** Zophar compares the foolish person to a wild donkey's colt—empty and untamed.
- **The lifted head (v. 15):** A picture of restored honor and confidence after repentance.
- **The hope of the wicked (v. 20):** Their hope ends like a final dying gasp—completely without outcome.
