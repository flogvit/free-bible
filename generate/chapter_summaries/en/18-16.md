**Main Theme:** Job defends his innocence against his friends' empty comfort and cries out in lament over God's harsh treatment.

**Content:** Job rejects his friends' comfort as worthless and calls them "miserable comforters" (v. 2). With vivid imagery, he describes how God has attacked him like a predator and a warrior—tearing him apart, using him as a target, and crushing him (vv. 9–14). Despite his suffering, Job maintains his innocence and appeals to a witness in heaven who will plead his case before God (vv. 17–21).

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Miserable comforters** (v. 2): Job's sarcastic label for his friends, whose words only increase his pain.
- **Target** (v. 12): An image of Job as God's target for suffering—God has set him up to be shot at.
- **Sackcloth and dust** (v. 15): Symbols of mourning and humiliation that Job has put on.
- **Witness in heaven** (v. 19): Job's hope for someone who can plead his case in the heavenly courtroom.
