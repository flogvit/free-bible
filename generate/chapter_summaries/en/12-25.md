**Main Theme:** The fall of Jerusalem, the destruction of the temple, and the exile of Judah to Babylon mark God's judgment upon an unfaithful people.

**Content:** In the ninth year of Zedekiah’s reign, Nebuchadnezzar lays siege to Jerusalem. After a severe famine, the city falls; Zedekiah is captured, his sons are killed before him, and he is blinded and taken to Babylon. The Babylonians burn the temple and the royal palace, plunder the temple treasures, and lead the people into exile. Gedaliah is appointed governor but is murdered, prompting the remaining survivors to flee to Egypt. The chapter concludes with Jehoiachin being released from prison in Babylon and given a place of honor at the court.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The Siege:** Babylon’s prolonged siege symbolizes God’s judgment on Jerusalem for breaking covenant.
- **Destruction of the Temple:** The burning of the Lord’s house marks the end of an era and God’s apparent absence.
- **Exile:** The deportation to Babylon fulfills the prophets’ warnings about the consequences of disobedience.
- **Jehoiachin’s Exaltation:** The former king’s release offers a glimpse of hope for David’s line amid judgment.
