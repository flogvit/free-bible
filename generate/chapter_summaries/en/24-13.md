**Main Theme:** God warns Judah and Jerusalem of coming judgment due to their pride, disobedience, and idolatry.

**Content:** Through the sign of the linen belt that rots by the Euphrates, God shows how He will destroy the pride of Judah and Jerusalem. The people have refused to hear God's word and have followed other gods. The parable of the wine jars illustrates that everyone—from kings to ordinary citizens—will be filled with drunkenness and crushed. The chapter concludes with a lament over Jerusalem as an unfaithful wife, posing the rhetorical question: Can an Ethiopian change his skin? Just so, Judah cannot do good when they are accustomed to evil.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Linen Belt (אֵזוֹר):** Symbolizes Israel's close relationship with God, now ruined by disobedience and pride.
- **Wine Jar (נֵבֶל):** Illustrates how the entire people will be filled with drunkenness and confusion before judgment.
- **The Ethiopian's Skin and the Leopard's Spots:** A rhetorical image showing that the people's sinful habits have become so deeply ingrained that change is impossible.
- **The Lord's Flock (עֵדֶר יְהוָה):** The people who will be led into captivity, causing the prophet to weep.
