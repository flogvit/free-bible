**Main Theme:** Isaiah warns against false security and proclaims judgment over the proud leaders of Ephraim and Jerusalem, yet also points to God’s faithful cornerstone in Zion.

**Content:** The prophet pronounces woe upon the drunken leaders of Ephraim, whose proud splendor will be trampled down. The priests and prophets of Jerusalem are also criticized for being so drunk that they cannot judge rightly. The leaders of Jerusalem have made a "covenant with death" in false security, but the Lord lays a tested cornerstone in Zion—the one who believes will not be put to shame. The chapter concludes with a parable about a farmer who treats different crops with wisdom, illustrating God’s wise and just judgment adapted to each situation.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The fading crown of Ephraim:** Symbolizes the passing splendor and pride of the Northern Kingdom that will be destroyed.
- **The cornerstone in Zion:** A tested, precious foundation stone representing God’s faithful promise and salvation for those who believe.
- **The covenant with death:** The false security of Jerusalem’s leaders through political alliances and lies, which will not endure.
- **The farmer and the crops:** A parable showing that God acts with wisdom and adapts His judgment according to the situation.
