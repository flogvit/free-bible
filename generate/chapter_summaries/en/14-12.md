**Main Theme:** Rehoboam’s apostasy leads to an Egyptian invasion, but humility softens God’s judgment.

**Content:** After Rehoboam had strengthened his kingdom, he and all Israel abandoned the law of the Lord. As punishment, God sent Shishak, king of Egypt, with a massive army—1,200 chariots and 60,000 horsemen. The prophet Shemaiah explained that this happened because they had forsaken the Lord. When the king and the leaders humbled themselves and acknowledged that the Lord is righteous, God spared them from total destruction. Nevertheless, Shishak plundered the temple and took the gold shields that Solomon had made; Rehoboam replaced them with bronze shields. Rehoboam reigned 17 years in Jerusalem before his son Abijah succeeded him.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Abandoning the law of the Lord** – Rehoboam’s apostasy when he felt secure shows how prosperity can lead to spiritual indifference.
- **Shishak’s invasion** – God’s instrument of discipline, a concrete example that unfaithfulness brings consequences.
- **From gold shields to bronze shields** – A symbol of the kingdom’s spiritual and material decline from Solomon’s era of greatness.
- **Humility** – The king’s and leaders’ recognition of God’s righteousness led to the mitigation of His wrath.
