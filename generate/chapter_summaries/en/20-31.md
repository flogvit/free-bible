**Main Theme:** King Lemuel’s exhortation on righteous leadership and the praise of the capable woman.

**Content:** The chapter has two parts: First, King Lemuel conveys his mother’s advice to avoid wasting his strength on women and alcohol, and instead to speak up for the cause of the weak and the poor (vv. 1–9). This is followed by an acrostic poem (alphabetical from aleph to tav) that praises the capable woman (אֵשֶׁת־חַיִל) for her diligence, care, wisdom, and reverence for God (vv. 10–31).

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Eshet chayil (דֶיקֶת חַיִל):** The capable or worthy woman—an ideal encompassing strength, wisdom, and reverence for God.
- **Reverence for God:** A woman who fears the Lord is to be praised, in contrast to fleeting beauty and charm (v. 30).
- **City gates:** Symbolize public recognition—both a man’s honor and a woman’s praise are given here (vv. 23, 31).
- **Justice for the weak:** The king is urged to open his mouth for those who cannot speak and to judge fairly on behalf of the poor and helpless.
