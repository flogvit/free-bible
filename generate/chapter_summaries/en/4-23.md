**Main Theme:** God transforms Balaam's planned curses into blessings over Israel through two prophetic oracles.

**Content:** King Balak takes Balaam to two different high places to curse Israel, but both times God puts words of blessing in Balaam's mouth. In the first oracle, Balaam declares that he cannot curse those whom God has not cursed and describes Israel as a people who dwell alone, countless as the dust. In the second oracle, he proclaims that God is not a human being who lies and that the Lord is with Israel as a king. The chapter concludes with Balak taking Balaam to a third place at Peor for yet another attempt.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Seven altars and sacrificial animals:** Balaam requests seven altars with bulls and rams—a pagan sacrificial practice that cannot bind or influence Israel's God.
- **God is not a human (v.19):** God's word stands firm and cannot be reversed—a contrast to human unreliability.
- **The trumpet blast of a king (v.21):** The Lord is present in Israel as king and warrior—an image of God's protective presence.
- **The lion that rises up (v.24):** Israel is compared to a lion that does not lie down until it has devoured its prey—an image of invincible strength.
