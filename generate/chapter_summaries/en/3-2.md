**Main Theme:** The chapter gives detailed regulations for the grain offering (*mincha*) as an unleavened offering of flour, oil, and frankincense to the Lord.

**Content:** The grain offering shall consist of fine flour with oil and frankincense, and may be prepared in various ways: baked in an oven, cooked on a griddle, or made in a pan. The offering must be unleavened—leaven and honey are not to be burned as a food offering, but may be brought as a firstfruits offering. The priest burns a "memorial portion" on the altar as a pleasing aroma to the Lord, while the remainder belongs to Aaron and his sons as most holy. All grain offerings must be seasoned with the salt of the covenant, and the chapter concludes with regulations for the firstfruits offering of roasted grain.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Mincha (grain offering):** A bloodless offering of flour, oil, and frankincense expressing gratitude and devotion.
- **Azkara (memorial portion):** The part of the offering burned on the altar as a reminder before God.
- **Salt of the covenant:** Salt symbolizing the enduring covenant between God and Israel—it must be included in all offerings.
- **Unleavened (*matzot*):** The absence of yeast symbolizes purity and haste; leaven and honey are forbidden in food offerings.
