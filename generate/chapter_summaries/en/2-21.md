**Main Theme:** The chapter provides detailed laws (מִשְׁפָּטִים) to regulate social relationships, justice, and compensation in Israel's community.

**Content:** The chapter opens with rules for Hebrew slaves, including release after six years and rights for female slaves. This is followed by laws concerning punishment for violence and killing, distinguishing between intentional and unintentional killing. The lex talionis principle ("eye for eye") is established as the basis for proportional justice. The chapter concludes with compensation rules for damage caused by animals and negligence.

**Key Terms/Images:**
- **Hebrew slave (עֶבֶד עִבְרִי):** An Israelite who serves another but has a legally guaranteed right to freedom after six years.
- **Lex talionis ("eye for eye"):** The principle of proportional retribution that ensures fair punishment corresponding to the harm done.
- **City of refuge (מָקוֹם):** A place appointed by God for someone who kills unintentionally, providing protection from blood vengeance.
- **Ransom (כֹּפֶר):** Financial compensation that may replace the death penalty in certain cases.
