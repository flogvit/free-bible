**Main Theme:** God judges Tyre and Sidon for their pride but promises Israel future restoration.

**Content:** The chapter begins with a judgment against the prince of Tyre, who in his arrogance has exalted himself to be like God (vv. 1–10). This is followed by a lament over the king of Tyre using cosmic imagery: he is compared to an anointed cherub in Eden, covered with precious stones, who fell because of pride and unjust trade (vv. 11–19). Sidon also receives its judgment (vv. 20–24), before the chapter concludes with a promise that God will gather Israel and let them dwell securely in their land (vv. 25–26).

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Eden, God's garden:** The king of Tyre is described as one who was in Eden, surrounded by precious stones and splendor, before his fall.
- **The anointed cherub:** The king is compared to a protective cherub on God's holy mountain, who was cast down because of sin.
- **Pride and fall:** Both the prince and the king fall because they exalted themselves and set their hearts as God's heart.
- **Israel's restoration:** God promises to gather His scattered people back to the land He gave Jacob, where they will dwell securely.
