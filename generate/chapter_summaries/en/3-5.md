**Main Theme:** The chapter provides regulations for sin offerings and guilt offerings in cases of various oversights and transgressions.

**Content:** The chapter opens with four situations requiring a sin offering: failing to speak up when one should have borne witness, touching something unclean, coming into contact with human uncleanness, and making a thoughtless oath. The guilty person must confess their sin and bring an offering suited to their financial means—from young livestock, to two doves, down to flour. It then describes the guilt offering (אָשָׁם) for neglect concerning holy things and for wrongfully taking another's property, where the sinner must make full restitution plus one-fifth.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Confession (וְהִתְוַדָּה):** The guilty person must acknowledge their sin before the offering can be brought.
- **Graduated Offerings:** The offering is adjusted to the giver's financial ability—from lambs to doves to flour—demonstrating God's grace toward the poor.
- **Guilt Offering (אָשׁם):** A special offering for unfaithfulness regarding holy things and fellow humans, linked with restitution.
- **One-Fifth (חֲמִישִׁית):** In cases of guilt against the sanctuary or a neighbor, one must repay the value plus 20 percent.
