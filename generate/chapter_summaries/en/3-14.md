**Main Theme:** The chapter provides detailed regulations for the cleansing of people healed from a skin disease (tzara'at) and for houses with similar afflictions.

**Content:** The cleansing of a healed person occurs in two phases: first, a ritual outside the camp involving two birds, cedar wood, scarlet yarn, and hyssop, where one bird is sacrificed and the other released alive. Then, on the eighth day, a guilt offering, a sin offering, and a burnt offering are brought, and the priest applies blood and oil to the person's right earlobe, thumb, and big toe. For those who are poor, simpler sacrificial provisions are given. Houses showing signs of tzara'at must be inspected by the priest; if the affliction spreads after treatment, the house is to be demolished; otherwise, it is cleansed using the same ritual as for persons.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Two birds:** One is sacrificed, the other released alive – symbolizing the death of the old and freedom in the new, clean life.
- **Blood and oil on ear, hand, and foot:** Applied to the right side – symbolizing that the whole person (hearing, action, walk) is dedicated to the Lord.
- **Living water:** Running water used in the ritual – symbolizing life and cleansing.
- **Guilt offering (asham):** The central offering in the cleansing process – emphasizing that impurity requires atonement.
