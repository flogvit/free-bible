**Main Theme:** Jesus reveals his authority over the Sabbath, chooses the twelve apostles, and teaches about radical love and genuine discipleship.

**Content:** The chapter opens with two Sabbath conflicts in which Jesus heals and declares himself Lord of the Sabbath. After a night in prayer, he chooses twelve apostles. On a level place, he teaches the crowds with beatitudes for the poor and suffering, contrasted with woes against the rich and self-satisfied. He calls for radical love toward enemies, mercy without judgment, and concludes with the parable of the two builders, illustrating the importance of putting his words into practice.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The Son of Man's lordship over the Sabbath:** Jesus claims divine authority over Sabbath laws and shows that human needs take precedence over ritual observance.
- **Beatitudes and Woes:** Four blessings for the poor, hungry, weeping, and persecuted are contrasted with four woes against the rich, full, laughing, and popular.
- **Love for enemies:** The command to love enemies, bless those who curse you, and pray for persecutors goes beyond ordinary human reciprocity.
- **The plank and the speck:** This image illustrates hypocrisy in judging others without self-examination.
- **The two builders:** The parable contrasts the one who hears and acts on Jesus' words (house built on rock) with the one who only hears (house without foundation).
