**Main Theme:** Jehoshaphat is rebuked by the prophet Jehu, yet reforms the judicial system in Judah with godly principles.

**Content:** After returning to Jerusalem, King Jehoshaphat is rebuked by the seer Jehu for having aided the wicked king Ahab; however, Jehu also acknowledges what was good in him—that he had removed the Asherah poles and sought God. Jehoshaphat himself travels among the people from Beersheba to the hill country of Ephraim to bring them back to the Lord. He appoints judges in all the fortified cities of Judah and establishes a central court in Jerusalem, staffed with Levites, priests, and heads of families, charging them to judge with reverence for God, faithfulness, and impartiality.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Godliness in the judicial system:** The judges are charged to judge for the Lord, not for men, without partiality or bribery.
- **Asherah poles:** Idolatrous symbols that Jehoshaphat had removed from the land, a sign of his faithfulness.
- **Amariah and Zebadiah:** The high priest and the leader of the house of Judah who preside over the court in religious and civil matters respectively.
- **Return to the Lord:** Jehoshaphat's active work to bring the people back to the God of their fathers.
