**Main Theme:** The terms of the covenant are presented through blessings for obedience and curses for disobedience to God's commands.

**Content:** Moses sets before Israel two paths: Obedience to God's commands leads to blessing in every area of life—fertility, prosperity, victory over enemies, and exaltation among the nations. Disobedience leads to opposite curses: disease, defeat, oppression, and ultimately exile and dispersion among the peoples. The curses are far more extensive (vv. 15–68) than the blessings (vv. 1–14), culminating in a return to Egypt as slaves. The chapter concludes by identifying this as the words of the covenant in Moab, in addition to the covenant at Sinai.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Blessed/Cursed (בָּרוּךְ/אָרוּר):** Parallel formulations showing the two possible outcomes of the covenant relationship.
- **Head and tail (רֹאשׁ/זָנָב):** A metaphor for Israel's position—either leading or subordinate among the nations.
- **Heaven like bronze, earth like iron:** A metaphor for drought and infertility as punishment for disobedience.
- **Scattered among the peoples (וֶהֱפִֽיצְךָ בְּכָל־הָעַמִּים):** The ultimate curse—exile and loss of the land.
