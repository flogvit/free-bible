**Main Theme:** The chapter describes the reigns of Amaziah of Judah and Jeroboam II of Israel, showing how pride leads to downfall, while God in His grace nevertheless preserves His people.

**Content:** Amaziah does what is right but fails to remove the high places. He executes his father's murderers according to the Law of Moses and defeats Edom, but becomes proud and challenges Joash king of Israel to battle. Amaziah loses, Jerusalem is plundered, and he is later killed in a conspiracy. Jeroboam II of Israel does what is evil, yet God uses him to restore Israel's borders because He sees the people's distress.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The high places (הַבָּמוֹת):** Symbolize incomplete faithfulness—Amaziah does right but fails to remove idolatry.
- **The thornbush and the cedar (v.9):** Joash's parable warns Amaziah against pride—a thornbush should not challenge a cedar tree.
- **God's grace (vv.26–27):** Despite Israel's sin, God saves them because He sees their distress and does not wish to destroy them.
