**Main Theme:** God's conditional promise to Solomon and an overview of the king's building projects and commercial activities.

**Content:** After the temple was completed, God appeared to Solomon and promised to bless his dynasty if he remained obedient, but warned that apostasy would lead to Israel's destruction and the rejection of the temple. The chapter describes Solomon's extensive building projects, his agreement with King Hiram of Tyre (who received 20 cities in Galilee), and the use of Canaanite peoples for forced labor. Solomon also built a fleet at the Red Sea that brought back 420 talents of gold from Ophir.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **God's eyes and heart:** God promises that His presence will be in the temple forever (v. 3).
- **Conditional covenant:** Blessing depends on obedience; disobedience leads to exile and the destruction of the temple (vv. 4–9).
- **Cabul:** The name Hiram gave to the 20 cities he was dissatisfied with, possibly meaning "worthless land" (v. 13).
- **Forced labor (mas):** The remaining Canaanite peoples were subjected to slave labor for the building projects (vv. 20–21).
