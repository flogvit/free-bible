**Main Theme:** The Jews' victory over their enemies and the establishment of the Purim festival.

**Content:** On the 13th of Adar, when the enemies had planned to destroy the Jews, the situation was reversed and the Jews triumphed over those who hated them. In Susa they killed 500 men on the first day and 300 on the second day, as well as Haman's ten sons, who were hanged. In the provinces the Jews killed 75,000 enemies but took no plunder. Mordecai and Esther sent letters to all the Jews to establish Purim as an annual festival on the 14th and 15th of Adar, with celebration, giving gifts to one another, and providing for the poor.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Purim (פּוּר):** The name of the festival, from the word "pur" (lot), because Haman cast lots to determine the day for the destruction of the Jews.
- **The Reversal (וְנַהֲפוֹךְ):** The situation was turned around—from sorrow to joy, from the enemies ruling over the Jews to the Jews ruling over them.
- **Mishloach manot (מִשְׁלוֹחַ מָנוֹת):** The custom of sending food gifts to one another as part of the Purim celebration.
- **No Plunder Taken:** The Jews refused to take war spoils, emphasizing that the struggle was about self-defense, not gain.
