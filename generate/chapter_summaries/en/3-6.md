**Main Theme:** The chapter gives detailed instructions to the priests on how the various offerings are to be handled and who may eat from them.

**Content:** The chapter begins with rules for the burnt offering, which is to remain on the altar all night while the fire is kept burning constantly—an eternal fire that must never go out. Next, the grain offering is described: the priest takes a handful of flour and oil along with all the incense and burns it, while the remainder is eaten unleavened by Aaron’s sons in a holy place. The priests’ own grain offering, which is presented on the day of their consecration and brought daily, must be burned completely and not eaten. Finally, rules are given for the sin offering, which is most holy and must be eaten by the priest in a holy place, while blood stained on garments must be washed, and vessels must either be cleansed or broken.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Eternal fire (אֵשׁ תָּמִיד):** The fire on the altar is never to go out, symbolizing God’s enduring presence and uninterrupted worship.
- **Most holy (קֹדֶשׁ קָדָשִׁים):** Both the grain offering and the sin offering are designated as most holy, requiring special handling and consumption in a holy place.
- **Holy place:** The priests must eat their portion of the offerings in the courtyard of the tent of meeting, marking the distinction between the holy and the common.
- **Cleansing of vessels:** Clay vessels must be broken, while bronze vessels are to be scoured and rinsed—this shows how seriously holiness must be preserved.
