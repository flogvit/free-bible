**Main Theme:** Paul and Barnabas preach the gospel amid opposition and establish churches in Asia Minor.

**Content:** In Iconium, Paul and Barnabas preach in the synagogue, and many Jews and Greeks come to faith, but opponents create division. They flee to Lystra and Derbe, where the healing of a lame man leads the people to want to worship them as gods—Barnabas as Zeus and Paul as Hermes. The apostles reject this and point to the living God who is the Creator. Jews from Antioch and Iconium stone Paul, but he survives. On their return journey, they strengthen the disciples, appoint elders in the churches, and teach that the way into God’s kingdom goes through many trials.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Zeus and Hermes:** Pagan gods whom the people mistakenly identified with the apostles, illustrating the cultural conflict in missionary work.
- **Stoning:** Paul was stoned and left for dead, but rose again—a testimony to the apostles’ endurance in suffering.
- **Elders (presbyteroi):** The apostles appointed leaders in each church, demonstrating early church organization.
- **Trials:** The apostles’ teaching that the way into God’s kingdom goes through many hardships.
