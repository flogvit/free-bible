**Main Theme:** King Hezekiah’s faithfulness to God is tested when Assyria threatens Judah.

**Content:** Hezekiah becomes king over Judah and does what is right in the Lord’s eyes—he removes the high places, breaks down the sacred pillars, and destroys the bronze serpent from Moses’ time. In his sixth year as king, Samaria falls, and Israel is taken into exile to Assyria because they did not obey the Lord. Later, the Assyrian king Sennacherib attacks Judah’s fortified cities, and Hezekiah pays a heavy tribute to buy peace. Nevertheless, Sennacherib sends his officials to Jerusalem, where the Rabshakeh mocks both Hezekiah and the Lord, trying to persuade the people to surrender. The people remain silent as ordered by the king, and Hezekiah’s servants bring the message to him with their clothes torn.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Nehushtan (the bronze serpent):** The serpent Moses made in the wilderness, which the people had begun to worship—Hezekiah breaks it as part of his religious reform.
- **High places:** Local worship sites that were removed to centralize worship in Jerusalem.
- **Rabshakeh:** Assyria’s official who uses psychological warfare by speaking Hebrew to the people on the wall.
- **The broken reed staff:** A symbol of Egypt as an unreliable ally—those who lean on it will be injured.
- **The fall of Samaria:** The downfall of the northern kingdom serves as a warning and contrast to Judah’s situation under faithful Hezekiah.
