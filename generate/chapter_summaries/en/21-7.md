**Main Theme:** Practical wisdom about life's realities, the value and limitations of wisdom, and the human condition.

**Content:** The chapter contains a series of wise sayings that contrast what is good with what is bad—a good name is better than good oil; sorrow is better than laughter. The Preacher emphasizes that God has created both good days and bad days, and that humans cannot fully understand His work. He warns against excessive righteousness or wickedness, acknowledging that no one is without sin. Finally, he concludes that God made humanity upright, but they have sought many schemes.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Good name (שֵׁם טוֹב):** A good reputation has lasting value, unlike fleeting pleasures.
- **Sorrow and laughter:** Sorrow and seriousness lead to deeper insight than superficial joy.
- **The limits of wisdom:** Wisdom is distant and deep—humans cannot fully comprehend existence.
- **God's sovereignty:** God has made everything, even the crooked parts, and humans cannot change it.
- **Human uprightness:** God created humanity straight, but they have chosen their own paths.
