**Main Theme:** Jerusalem's total degradation and destruction as a result of sin, with the promise that the punishment will one day end.

**Content:** The chapter opens with shock at how what was precious—gold, the holy stones, and Zion's children—has become worthless and despised. The famine is so extreme that mothers cook their own children, and those who once lived in abundance now lie on garbage heaps. The cause of the disaster is the sins of the prophets and the misdeeds of the priests, those who shed the blood of the righteous. Even the Lord's anointed, the king upon whom they placed their hope, was captured. The chapter concludes with a warning to Edom and a promise to Zion that the punishment will soon be over.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Gold that loses its luster (v.1-2):** Zion's precious children, once worth their weight in gold, are now regarded as worthless clay pots.
- **The famine (v.4-5, 9-10):** So extreme that mothers eat their own children—worse than the sudden destruction of Sodom.
- **The sin of the prophets and priests (v.13):** The leaders' misdeeds and bloodshed are the direct cause of Jerusalem's fall.
- **The Lord's anointed captured (v.20):** The king, the people's breath of life and hope, was taken captive—an expression of total political collapse.
- **Edom's cup (v.21-22):** A warning to Edom that they too must drink the cup of wrath, while Zion's punishment nears its end.
