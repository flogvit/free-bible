**Main Theme:** A prophecy of judgment against Jerusalem ("The Valley of Vision") and an oracle concerning the officials Shebna and Eliakim.

**Content:** Isaiah describes Jerusalem's panic under a looming siege, where leaders flee and the people prepare military defenses without turning to God. While the Lord calls for mourning and repentance, the people respond with feasting and fatalism: "Let us eat and drink, for tomorrow we die." God declares that this sin will not be forgiven until they die. The official Shebna, who arrogantly has carved out a splendid tomb for himself, is to be removed and replaced by Eliakim, who receives the keys to the house of David.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Valley of Vision (גֵּיא חִזָּיוֹן):** A play on words regarding "Valley of Vision" – Jerusalem, which should have been the valley of prophecy, has become a valley of sin.
- **The Key of David:** A symbol of authority and power over the royal house, given to Eliakim – he opens and no one shuts.
- **A Peg in a Sure Place:** Eliakim is to be a stable support for his entire family, but even this peg will ultimately fall.
- **"Tomorrow we die":** An expression of the people's desperate hedonism and lack of faith in God's salvation.
