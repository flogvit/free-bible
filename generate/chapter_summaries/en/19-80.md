**Main Theme:** A lament in which the people of Israel implore God to restore and save them from distress and destruction by their enemies.

**Content:** The psalm opens with an invocation to God as Israel's Shepherd and the One who is enthroned above the cherubim. The people complain that God has allowed them to suffer through tears and the mockery of neighbors. Using the image of a vine, the psalm describes how God brought Israel out of Egypt and planted them in the land, but now has let the walls fall so that enemies can destroy it. A refrain is repeated three times: "Restore us, O God; let your face shine, that we may be saved."

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Shepherd:** God is portrayed as Israel's Shepherd who leads Joseph like a flock (v. 2).
- **Vine:** Israel is compared to a vine that God planted from Egypt, which grew mighty but is now destroyed (vv. 9–16).
- **God's shining face:** The refrain's plea for God's face to shine upon the people, an expression of grace and salvation (vv. 4, 8, 20).
- **Restoration (השיבנו):** The central cry that God must turn toward His people and restore their condition.
