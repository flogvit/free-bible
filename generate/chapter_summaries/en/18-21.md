**Main Theme:** Job rejects his friends' simplistic doctrine of retribution by showing that the wicked often live happily and die in peace.

**Content:** Job urges his friends to listen carefully before answering (vv. 2-6). He describes how the ungodly often prosper with family, wealth, and security, even though they reject God and say, "Depart from us!" (vv. 7-16). Job asks rhetorically how often the wicked are truly struck by disaster (vv. 17-21), pointing out that both the righteous and the unrighteous end up in the same grave (vv. 23-26). He concludes by dismissing his friends' comfort as empty and deceitful (v. 34).

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The Prosperity of the Wicked** (vv. 7-13): The ungodly live long, become powerful, have secure homes, and children who play—directly contradicting the doctrine of retribution.
- **"Depart from us, God"** (vv. 14-15): The wicked openly reject God yet suffer no consequences.
- **Common Grave** (v. 26): Both the joyful and the bitter are laid in the dust—death levels all.
- **Empty Comfort** (v. 34): Job rejects his friends' answers as meaningless deceit (Heb. *ma'al*).
