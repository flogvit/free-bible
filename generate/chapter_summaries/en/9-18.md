**Main Theme:** David's growing popularity creates a friendship with Jonathan but arouses Saul's deadly jealousy.

**Content:** After the victory over Goliath, Jonathan's soul was bound to David's, and they made a covenant in which Jonathan gave him his robe, armor, sword, bow, and belt. The women's song saying, "Saul has slain his thousands, and David his ten thousands," stirred Saul's envy, and an evil spirit came upon him so that he threw his spear at David twice. Saul tried to use his daughters as a trap: first he promised Merab, but gave her to another man; then he offered Michal in exchange for a bride price of 100 Philistine foreskins—hoping that David would fall. David delivered 200 foreskins and received Michal as his wife, while Saul realized that the Lord was with David and became his enemy forever.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The Covenant between Jonathan and David:** Jonathan's soul was "bound" to David's, and the gifts symbolize the transfer of status and loyalty.
- **"Thousands and Ten Thousands":** The women's song expresses David's superiority and becomes the catalyst for Saul's jealousy.
- **Trap (mokesh):** Saul deliberately uses his daughters as a "snare" to get the Philistines to kill David.
- **The Lord was with David:** A repeated motif that explains David's success and Saul's fear.
