**Main Theme:** The furnishings of Solomon’s Temple – bronze and gold articles made by the craftsman Huram.

**Content:** The chapter describes the bronze altar (20x20x10 cubits) and the great "Sea" – a large round bronze basin for the priests’ ceremonial cleansing, holding 3,000 baths and standing on twelve oxen facing the four directions of the sky. Ten basins were made to cleanse the burnt offering equipment, placed five on each side. Further described are ten golden lampstands, ten tables, the two pillars with their capitals and pomegranates, as well as various utensils of polished bronze. All were cast in the Jordan Valley between Succoth and Zarethan.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The Bronze Sea:** A large round water basin (10 cubits in diameter) for the priests’ ritual cleansing, supported by twelve oxen.
- **The Twelve Oxen:** Bronze oxen beneath the Sea, three facing each direction – symbolizing God’s universal sovereignty.
- **The Basins:** Ten vessels for cleansing the burnt offering equipment, separate from the Sea which was reserved only for the priests.
- **The Gold Articles:** Lampstands, tables for the showbread, and a gold altar in the Holy Place – illustrating the degrees of holiness within the temple.
- **Huram:** A skilled craftsman who completed all the work in polished bronze and gold for King Solomon.
