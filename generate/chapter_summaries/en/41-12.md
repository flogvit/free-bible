**Main Theme:** Jesus confronts religious leaders through parables and teaching dialogues, revealing what true worship and genuine faith entail.

**Content:** Jesus tells the parable of the vineyard in which the tenants mistreat the owner's servants and kill his son—a picture of Israel’s treatment of the prophets and the coming Messiah. The Pharisees and Herodians try to trap Jesus with a question about taxes, but he answers wisely regarding duties to Caesar and to God. The Sadducees are challenged on their belief in the resurrection, and a scribe is told that love for God and neighbor is more important than all sacrifices. Jesus himself questions the identity of the Messiah and warns against hypocritical scribes, before highlighting the widow’s offering as exemplary.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Vineyard:** Symbolizes Israel, where God is the owner and the religious leaders are the unfaithful tenants who reject God’s messengers.
- **Cornerstone:** The rejected stone that becomes the chief cornerstone—a picture of Jesus, rejected by the leaders but exalted by God.
- **Caesar’s coin:** Illustrates the balance between earthly obligations and what belongs to God.
- **The Great Commandment:** Love for God and love for neighbor are the foundation for everything else in the law.
- **The Widow’s Mite:** True willingness to give is measured not by amount, but by the heart’s devotion and trust in God.
