**Main Theme:** Solomon's building projects, administration, and organization of temple service after the completion of the Lord's house.

**Content:** After twenty years of construction work, Solomon expands his kingdom by developing cities that Hiram gave him, conquering Hamath-Zobah, and establishing supply cities and fortresses. He organizes forced labor among the remaining Canaanite peoples, while the Israelites serve as soldiers and officers. Solomon moves Pharaoh's daughter from the City of David because the ark of the covenant has made that place holy. He organizes temple service with priests, Levites, and gatekeepers according to David's arrangement, and observes the three annual festivals as required by the Law of Moses. The chapter concludes with trading expeditions to Ophir that bring 450 talents of gold.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Tadmor and Supply Cities:** Solomon builds strategic cities in the wilderness and in Hamath to secure trade and defense.
- **Forced Labor (mas):** The remaining Canaanite peoples are put to forced labor, while Israelites receive military and administrative roles.
- **David's Arrangement:** Temple service is organized according to the arrangement that David, "the man of God," had established for priests and Levites.
- **The Three Festivals:** The Festival of Unleavened Bread, the Feast of Weeks, and the Feast of Tabernacles are celebrated as commanded in the Law of Moses.
- **Ophir Gold:** Maritime voyages in cooperation with Hiram's men bring large quantities of gold from Ophir to Jerusalem.
