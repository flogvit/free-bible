**Main Theme:** Paul explains the apostles' role as servants of Christ and warns the Corinthians against pride.

**Content:** Paul describes the apostles as stewards of God's mysteries, where faithfulness is paramount—judgment belongs to the Lord alone. He contrasts the Corinthians' self-satisfaction with the apostles' sufferings and humility. As their spiritual father, he urges them to imitate him and sends Timothy to remind them of his ways in Christ. He warns of his upcoming visit, stating that he will test their power, not just their words.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Stewards (οἰκονόμοι):** The apostles are entrusted servants who must be faithful to God, not seeking human judgment.
- **Spectacle (θέατρον):** The apostles have become a spectacle for the world, for angels and for people—despised and suffering.
- **Spiritual Father:** Paul "fathered" the Corinthians through the gospel and has a unique relationship with them as a father, not merely as a guide.
- **Power vs. Words:** God's kingdom consists not in words but in power—true spirituality is shown in action.
