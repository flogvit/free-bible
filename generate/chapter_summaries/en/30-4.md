**Main Theme:** God accuses Israel of social injustice and hypocritical worship, showing how the people have rejected all His calls to repentance.

**Content:** Amos directs sharp criticism against the wealthy women of Samaria ("the cows of Bashan") who oppress the poor. He mocks Israel's zealous but hypocritical worship at Bethel and Gilgal. Then he lists the plagues God has sent—famine, drought, blight, pestilence, and war—each followed by the lamenting refrain "yet you did not return to me." The chapter culminates in the warning "prepare to meet your God" and a praise of the Lord as Creator of the world.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The cows of Bashan:** A sarcastic term for the upper-class women of Samaria who live in luxury at the expense of the poor.
- **"Yet you did not return":** A repeated refrain (five times) that emphasizes Israel's stubborn disobedience.
- **Bethel and Gilgal:** Israelite holy sites where worship has become hypocritical sin rather than genuine devotion to God.
- **Prepare to meet your God:** A solemn warning that Israel must answer before the Lord, the mighty Creator of the universe.
