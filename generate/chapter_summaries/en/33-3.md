**Main Theme:** Micah proclaims God's judgment against Israel's corrupt leaders, false prophets, and priests who pervert justice and exploit the people.

**Content:** The chapter contains three pronouncements of judgment. First, the leaders are accused of hating good and loving evil—they strip the people and devour their flesh (vv. 1–4). Next, the false prophets are condemned for prophesying peace in exchange for payment, but declaring war against those who give nothing (vv. 5–7). Micah contrasts himself by stating he is filled with the Spirit of the Lord and power to proclaim Israel's sin (v. 8). Finally, the accusation is repeated against all leaders—judges, priests, and prophets—who act for bribes while claiming the Lord's protection (vv. 9–11). The judgment is total: Zion shall be plowed like a field, and Jerusalem become ruins (v. 12).

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Strip the skin / devour flesh (vv. 2–3):** A brutal image of how leaders exploit and oppress the people they were meant to protect.
- **Night and darkness (v. 6):** Symbolizes that the false prophets will lose their visions—God will be silent.
- **Filled with the Spirit of the Lord (v. 8):** Micah's divine authority in contrast to the false prophets.
- **Zion plowed like a field (v. 12):** The final judgment—the total destruction of Jerusalem and the temple hill.
