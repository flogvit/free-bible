**Main Theme:** Paul demonstrates through Abraham that righteousness comes by faith, not by works of the law or circumcision.

**Content:** Abraham was counted as righteous because of his faith while he was still uncircumcised, and circumcision became a seal of the righteousness that came by faith. David also testifies to the blessing for the one who receives righteousness without works. Therefore, Abraham is the father of all who believe—both the circumcised and the uncircumcised. God's promise rests on grace through faith, and this applies also to us who believe in him who raised Jesus from the dead—he who was delivered up for our trespasses and raised for our justification.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Imputed Righteousness (λογίζομαι):** Faith is counted as righteousness, not as earned wages but as a gift.
- **Circumcision as a Seal:** Circumcision was not the basis of righteousness but an outward sign of the righteousness Abraham already had by faith.
- **Father of Many Nations:** Abraham is the ancestor of all believers, both Jews and Gentiles, because righteousness comes by faith.
- **Hope Against Hope:** Abraham believed God's promise despite his and Sarah's advanced age, and was strengthened in faith.
