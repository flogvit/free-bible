**Main Theme:** God’s final judgment against Babylon as vengeance for the destruction of the temple and the oppression of His people.

**Content:** The Lord raises up the Medes and their allies to completely destroy Babylon. Babylon is described as a golden cup in God’s hand that has made the nations drunk, but now the city itself will fall and never be rebuilt again. God’s people are urged to flee from the city before judgment strikes. The chapter concludes with Jeremiah sending the prophecies via Seraiah to Babylon, where the scroll is to be read aloud and then sunk into the Euphrates as a sign of Babylon’s final downfall.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The Golden Cup** (v.7): Babylon as God’s instrument to intoxicate and judge the nations, now itself under judgment.
- **The Medes** (vv.11,28): The historical people whom God raises up as His instrument of punishment against Babylon.
- **The Scroll in the Euphrates** (vv.59–64): A symbolic act in which the prophecy is sunk into the river as a sign that Babylon will sink and never rise again.
- **Bel** (v.44): The chief god of Babylon, who will be punished and lose his power over the nations.
