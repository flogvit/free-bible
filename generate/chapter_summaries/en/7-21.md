**Main Theme:** The Israelites find wives for the surviving Benjamites to preserve the tribe, despite the oath they had sworn.

**Content:** After the civil war, the Israelites regret that Benjamin was nearly wiped out. They had sworn at Mizpah that no one would give their daughters to Benjamin. To resolve this, they attack Jabesh Gilead, which had not participated in the assembly, and take 400 virgins. When this is not enough, they allow the Benjamites to abduct women during the Lord's festival at Shiloh. The book concludes with these words: "In those days there was no king in Israel; everyone did what was right in his own eyes."

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The Oath at Mizpah:** The Israelites' oath not to give their daughters to Benjamin creates the dilemma that drives the narrative.
- **Jabesh Gilead:** The city that failed to appear is punished, and its virgins are given to Benjamin.
- **The Lord's Festival at Shiloh:** An annual feast where the Benjamites abduct women from the dancing.
- **No King in Israel:** The book's closing refrain explaining the chaos and moral decay during the time of the judges.
