**Main Theme:** Eliphaz's first speech continues with an exhortation to Job to seek God and receive His discipline.

**Content:** Eliphaz warns that anger kills the fool, and describes how he has seen the house of the wicked become cursed. He praises God's greatness—He does unfathomable things, sends rain, and lifts up the lowly. Eliphaz declares blessed the one whom God corrects (v.17), for God both wounds and heals. He promises that the one who turns to God will be protected in six and seven troubles, and die full of days.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The fool taking root (v.3):** A picture of apparent prosperity among the wicked that ultimately ends in ruin.
- **God's discipline (vv.17–18):** The suffering God sends is for blessing—He wounds but binds up; He strikes, yet His hands heal.
- **Six and seven troubles (v.19):** A poetic expression of complete protection—no matter how many trials come, God will preserve.
- **Covenant with the stones of the field (v.23):** A picture of total harmony with creation for those who live rightly before God.
