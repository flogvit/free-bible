**Main Theme:** Israel's fall and exile to Assyria as a result of persistent apostasy and idolatry.

**Content:** King Hoshea of Israel rebels against Assyria, leading to the siege of Samaria and the deportation of its people (722 B.C.). The chapter explains that this occurred because Israel had sinned against the Lord, worshiped other gods, and rejected the warnings of the prophets. Assyria resettles foreign peoples in Samaria, who practice syncretism—they fear the Lord yet also worship their own gods.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The Exile:** Israel's deportation to Assyria as God's judgment for persistent disobedience and covenant breaking.
- **High Places (bamot):** Illegal cult sites where Israel worshiped foreign gods, symbolizing their apostasy.
- **Syncretism:** The practice of the new inhabitants who feared the Lord while also worshiping their own gods—false worship.
- **The Prophets' Warning:** God sent repeated warnings through the prophets, but the people hardened themselves and refused to listen.
