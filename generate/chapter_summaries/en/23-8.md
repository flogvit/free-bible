**Main Theme:** God warns Judah through prophetic signs about the coming invasion of Assyria and calls the people to fear Him alone rather than political threats.

**Content:** Isaiah is instructed to write the name "Maher-shalal-hash-baz" (hastening spoil, speeding plunder) as a sign that Assyria will soon plunder both Damascus and Samaria. Because the people rejected the waters of Siloah (God's quiet care) and feared humans, God will send "the mighty waters of the river"—Assyria's army—against them. Isaiah is urged not to follow the people's fear of conspiracies but to sanctify the Lord and seek God's law rather than consult mediums. The chapter ends with an image of darkness and distress over the land.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Maher-shalal-hash-baz:** A prophetic name meaning "hastening spoil, speeding plunder"—a sign of Assyria's imminent plundering.
- **The Waters of Siloah vs. The River:** The quiet waters of Siloah symbolize God's gentle provision; the flood of the river (Euphrates) represents Assyria's devastating invasion.
- **A Stone of Stumbling and a Sanctuary:** God will be a sanctuary for the faithful but a stone of stumbling for those who do not believe (v. 14).
- **Immanuel ("God with us"):** The name is used both as comfort and warning—God is present in both judgment and salvation (vv. 8, 10).
