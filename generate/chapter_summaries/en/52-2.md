**Main Theme:** Paul defends his apostolic ministry and expresses his deep love for the church in Thessalonica.

**Content:** After suffering in Philippi, Paul preached the gospel in Thessalonica with boldness, purity, and without ulterior motives. He treated the congregation both as a nursing mother who cares for her children and as a father who encourages and exhorts his own children, working night and day so as not to be a burden to anyone. The Thessalonians received the message as God's word, not merely human words, and they themselves became imitators of the churches in Judea by suffering at the hands of their fellow countrymen. Paul longs to see them again, but Satan has hindered him; yet they are his hope, joy, and crown of glory when he stands before the Lord Jesus at His coming.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Mother and Father:** Paul describes his care using two parental images—a nursing mother who warms her children (v.7) and a father who exhorts and encourages (vv.11–12).
- **God's Word:** The Thessalonians received the message not as human words, but as it truly is: God's word at work in those who believe (v.13).
- **Parousia (the coming of Jesus):** The congregation is Paul's crown of glory and joy when he stands before the Lord Jesus at His coming (vv.19–20).
