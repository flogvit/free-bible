**Main Theme:** God calls His backslidden people to repentance and promises restoration despite their spiritual unfaithfulness.

**Content:** God uses the imagery of marriage and divorce to describe the unfaithfulness of Israel and Judah through idolatry. Although Israel was "divorced" (sent into exile), Judah did not learn from her sister's fate but continued in sin—and God declares that backslidden Israel is more righteous than faithless Judah. Nevertheless, God invites both to turn back and promises to give them shepherds after His own heart, gather them in Zion, and make Jerusalem His throne where all nations will be gathered.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Spiritual adultery/unfaithfulness:** Idolatry is described as adultery and prostitution—the people have "scattered their ways to strangers."
- **Backslidden Israel vs. faithless Judah:** Judah saw Israel's judgment but returned to God only with deceit, not with all her heart.
- **"Return" (שׁוּב):** A repeated call to repentance with wordplay on "backsliding children"—God desires to heal their backsliding.
- **Shepherds after My own heart:** The promise of leaders who will shepherd the people with knowledge and understanding.
