**Main Theme:** Saul’s disobedience in offering sacrifices without Samuel leads to the rejection of his dynasty.

**Content:** Jonathan attacks a Philistine outpost, and the Philistines gather an overwhelming army. The Israelites hide in fear, and while Saul waits for Samuel at Gilgal, he performs the burnt offering himself when the prophet is delayed. Samuel rebukes him for disobedience and announces that God has chosen another man after His own heart to lead Israel. The chapter concludes by describing Israel’s vulnerable situation—without blacksmiths and nearly without weapons.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Burnt Offering (עֹלָה):** Saul’s unauthorized sacrifice symbolizes his lack of trust in God and disrespect for the prophet’s role.
- **"A man after God’s heart":** Samuel’s prophecy points forward to David as God’s chosen one.
- **No blacksmith in Israel:** The Philistine oppression is illustrated by the Israelites’ inability to make weapons.
- **Gilgal:** The place where Saul was crowned now becomes the place where his dynasty is rejected.
