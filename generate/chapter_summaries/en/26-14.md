**Main Theme:** God warns against idolatry and declares that even the most righteous cannot save others from His judgment.

**Content:** Some of Israel's elders come to Ezekiel, but God reveals that they have idols in their hearts. God urges the people to turn away from their idols and warns that He will answer idolaters according to their many idols. Even if Noah, Daniel, and Job were present in a land under judgment, they could only save themselves by their own righteousness. God describes four judgments—sword, famine, wild beasts, and pestilence—that will strike Jerusalem. A remnant will survive, and when the people see what has happened to them, they will understand that God's judgment was just.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Idols in the heart** (גִּלּוּלִים עַל־לֵב): Idolatry as an inner attitude, not merely outward action—one cannot sincerely seek God with a divided heart.
- **Noah, Daniel, and Job**: Three examples of exceptionally righteous men illustrating that personal righteousness cannot be transferred to others.
- **Four judgments** (sword, famine, wild beasts, pestilence): God's complete judgment upon a sinful people.
- **Stumbling block** (מִכְשׁוֹל עָוֺן): Sin that causes people to fall and hinders their relationship with God.
