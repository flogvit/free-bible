**Main Theme:** The psalm praises the blessing of confession and forgiveness, and exhorts believers to seek God with trust.

**Content:** David begins by praising those whose sins are forgiven and covered. He describes his own experience: when he remained silent, his body wasted away, but when he confessed, God forgave him. The psalm urges the righteous to pray while God may be found, and warns against being foolish like a horse or mule. It concludes with the assurance that those who trust in the Lord are surrounded by grace, and calls the righteous to rejoice.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Maskil:** A genre designation indicating an instructional psalm or wisdom poem.
- **Blessed (אַשְׁרֵי):** A declaration of happiness over those who receive forgiveness – the starting point of the psalm.
- **Confession:** David's open acknowledgment of sin led to God forgiving his guilt.
- **Refuge (סֵתֶר):** God is the believer's shelter and protection against trouble.
- **Horse and mule:** A warning against foolishness, showing that some need force to draw near to God.
