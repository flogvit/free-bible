**Main Theme:** Job defends God's incomprehensible greatness and omnipotence in response to Bildad's inadequate speech.

**Content:** Job begins with sarcastic questions about what "help" Bildad has given the powerless. He then describes God's sovereign power: Sheol lies naked before Him, He hangs the earth on nothing, binds up the waters in His clouds, and crushes the sea monster Rahab. Job concludes that all this is merely the "outer edges" of God's ways—who can comprehend the thunder of His might?

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Sheol and Abaddon (v.6):** The realm of the dead and the place of destruction lie open and naked before God's omniscient gaze.
- **The earth hung on nothing (v.7):** A poetic image of God's creative power—He holds up the universe without visible support.
- **Rahab and the serpent (vv.12–13):** Mythological forces of chaos that God overcomes, symbols of His victory over all that threatens creation.
- **The outer edges of His ways (v.14):** Humanity perceives only a faint whisper of God's greatness; His full power is incomprehensible.
