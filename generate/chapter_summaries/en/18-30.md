**Main Theme:** Job laments his total degradation—from a respected man to one mocked by the lowest in society, abandoned by God.

**Content:** Job describes how he is now scorned by young men from society's outcasts—those who lived in caves and ate wild plants (vv. 1-8). They spit on him and attack him without restraint (vv. 9-15). His body is afflicted with disease and pain (vv. 16-19, 30). Most seriously: Job accuses God of having become cruel toward him and not answering his cries (vv. 20-23). Despite Job himself showing compassion to the suffering, he meets only darkness (vv. 25-26).

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Mockery and spitting (vv. 9-10):** Job has become a song of mockery and an object of contempt for the lowest in society.
- **Brother to jackals (v. 29):** Job compares himself to the lonely creatures of lamentation in the desert—a symbol of isolation and sorrow.
- **The harp turned into a dirge (v. 31):** Job's musical instruments have become instruments of mourning, an image of total life upheaval.
- **God as cruel (v. 21):** Job directly accuses God of having become his enemy—a bold theological statement.
