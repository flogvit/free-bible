**Main Theme:** Through the vineyard parable and six woes, God announces judgment against Israel for their injustice and moral decay.

**Content:** The chapter opens with a song about a vineyard (Israel) that, despite all care, produced only bad fruit. God expected mishpat (justice) but found mispach (bloodshed); He expected tsedaqah (righteousness) but heard tse'aqah (a cry). Six woes condemn sins such as greed, drunkenness, moral confusion, pride, and a corrupt legal system. As punishment, God will whistle for a distant nation that will come like invincible lions.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Vineyard (כֶּרֶם):** A symbol of Israel as God's chosen people who disappoint His expectations.
- **The Woes (הוֹי):** Six declarations of judgment revealing the people's specific sins against God and their fellow humans.
- **Mishpat/mispach wordplay:** Illustrates the gap between God's expectation of justice and Israel's bloodshed.
- **The roaring lion:** A picture of the coming enemy army from which no one can be saved.
