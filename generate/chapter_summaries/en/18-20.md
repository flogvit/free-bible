**Main Theme:** Zophar's second speech concerning the fleeting happiness and inevitable judgment of the wicked.

**Content:** Zophar argues that the triumph of the wicked is short-lived—even if he reaches to heaven, he will vanish like a dream. The evil one swallows up wealth, but God forces him to vomit it back up again. His sin becomes like snake venom in his inner being. Finally, heaven and earth will testify against him, and everything he owns will be torn away on the day of God's wrath—this is the wicked person's lot from God.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Dream/Night Vision (v.8):** The wicked vanishes without a trace, like a dream no one can find again.
- **Snake Venom (vv.14–16):** The sweetness of sin in the mouth turns into deadly poison in the stomach—a picture of how evil destroys from within.
- **Fire Not Kindled by Man (v.26):** God's judgment comes as a supernatural fire, not lit by human hands.
- **Heaven and Earth as Witnesses (v.27):** The entire creation rises up to expose the guilt of the wicked.
