**Main Theme:** Eliphaz's second speech in which he accuses Job of pride and describes the inevitable destruction of the wicked.

**Content:** Eliphaz criticizes Job for speaking empty words and undermining reverence for God (vv. 2–6). He claims that no one can be pure before God—even the heavens are not pure in His sight (vv. 14–16). The bulk of the speech describes the fate of the wicked: they are tormented by fear, lose their wealth, and their prosperity withers like a grapevine that loses its grapes (vv. 20–35).

**Key Words/Images:**
- **East Wind (v. 2):** Symbol of empty, destructive speech—Eliphaz accuses Job of filling himself with wind.
- **The Fear of the Wicked (vv. 20–24):** The wicked live in constant anxiety; even in times of peace, they await destruction.
- **Grapevine and Olive Tree (v. 33):** Images of lost fruitfulness—the wicked lose their vitality before their time.
- **To Conceive Evil (v. 35):** He who conceives injustice gives birth to trouble for himself.
