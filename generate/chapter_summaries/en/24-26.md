**Main Theme:** Jeremiah is threatened with death for his prophecy, but is acquitted through God's protection.

**Content:** At the beginning of King Jehoiakim's reign, God commands Jeremiah to prophesy in the temple courtyard that if the people do not repent, the temple will become like Shiloh and the city will be laid waste. Priests and prophets seize Jeremiah and demand his execution, but the officials and elders of the land defend him by citing the prophet Micah, who delivered a similar message during Hezekiah's time without being put to death. In contrast, the account tells of Uriah the prophet, who prophesied the same thing but was brought back from Egypt and killed by King Jehoiakim. Ahikam son of Shaphan protects Jeremiah so that he is not handed over to the people.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Shiloh:** The former sanctuary that was destroyed – used as a warning about the possible fate of the temple.
- **Temple Courtyard:** The place where Jeremiah must proclaim his message – symbolizing public confrontation with religious authority.
- **Micah of Moresheth:** A earlier prophet who warned of Jerusalem's destruction during Hezekiah's reign – cited as precedent for sparing Jeremiah.
- **Innocent Blood:** Jeremiah's warning that killing him would bring bloodguilt upon the city and its inhabitants.
