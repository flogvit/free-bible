**Main Theme:** The contrast between powerless idols and the Lord, the living God and eternal King, who alone deserves reverence.

**Content:** Jeremiah warns Israel against following the nations' customs of making idol images from wood adorned with silver and gold—they can neither speak, walk, nor do good or evil. In contrast stands the Lord, who created the earth by His power, established the world by His wisdom, and stretched out the heavens by His understanding. The prophet laments the coming destruction from the north because the shepherds have acted foolishly. He acknowledges that man cannot direct his own steps and asks for God's discipline in justice—not in wrath—while God's anger must fall upon the nations that have devoured Jacob.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The Idol (vv. 3–5):** Wood cut from the forest, decorated with silver and gold, yet as lifeless as a scarecrow—unable to speak or walk.
- **The Lord as Creator (vv. 12–13):** He who created the earth by His power, established the world by His wisdom, and stretched out the heavens by His understanding.
- **Jacob's Portion (v. 16):** The God of Israel is not like the idols—He is the One who formed all things, and Israel is His inheritance.
- **The Shepherds (v. 21):** The leaders who have acted foolishly and failed to seek the Lord, resulting in the flock being scattered.
