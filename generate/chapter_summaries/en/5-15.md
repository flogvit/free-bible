**Main Theme:** Laws concerning debt release, the freeing of slaves, and the consecration of firstborn male animals as expressions of God's care and Israel's obedience.

**Content:** Every seventh year (the sabbatical year), all debts between Israelites are to be forgiven, though debts owed by foreigners may still be collected. The people are urged to be generous toward the poor, who will always be present in the land. Hebrew slaves are to be freed after six years of service and given generously upon their release, but they may choose to remain permanently. All firstborn male animals must be consecrated to the Lord and eaten at the sanctuary.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Shemitta (שְׁמִטָּה):** The sabbatical year involving debt forgiveness, expressing God's liberating care.
- **Open hand:** A symbol of generosity toward the poor and willingness to lend without reluctance (vv. 8, 11).
- **Ear piercing:** A ritual in which a slave who loves his master chooses permanent service (v. 17).
- **Firstborn:** Male animals belonging to the Lord that are to be eaten at the sanctuary rather than used for labor.
