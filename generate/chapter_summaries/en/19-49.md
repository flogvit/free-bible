**Main Theme:** A wisdom psalm by the sons of Korah reflecting on the emptiness of wealth in the face of death, and the hope of God's redemption.

**Content:** The psalmist addresses all people, both rich and poor, with a word of wisdom. He warns against trusting in riches, for no one can buy their way free from death—even the wise die and must leave their wealth to others. Humanity in its splendor does not endure but is like the animals that perish. Yet there is hope: God will redeem the soul of the righteous from the power of the grave.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Redemption (פִּדְיוֹן):** No one can pay a ransom for their own soul, but God can redeem from the grip of the grave.
- **The Grave (שְׁאוֹל):** The rich are driven there like sheep, while the righteous have hope of deliverance.
- **Like the animals (כַּבְּהֵמוֹת נִדְמוּ):** A refrain emphasizing that human splendor is fleeting without insight.
