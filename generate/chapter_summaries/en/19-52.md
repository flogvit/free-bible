**Main Theme:** The psalm contrasts the wicked person's deceit and judgment with the righteous one's security in God's faithful love.

**Content:** Written in connection with Doeg the Edomite, who betrayed David to Saul, this psalm rebukes those who boast of evil and use their tongue for destruction and deception. God will tear down the wicked person and uproot them from the land of the living, while the righteous observe and stand in awe. In contrast to the fate of the wicked, the psalmist compares themselves to a flourishing olive tree in God's house and declares trust in God's faithfulness forever.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The tongue as a sharpened razor (v.4):** Symbolizes the dangerous and deceitful speech of the wicked.
- **Uprooted by the roots (v.7):** God's total judgment upon the wicked, completely removed from life.
- **A flourishing olive tree (v.10):** The righteous person thrives securely in God's presence, unlike the destruction of the wicked.
- **God's חֶסֶד (faithful love) (vv.3, 10):** The foundation of the righteous one's trust and hope.
