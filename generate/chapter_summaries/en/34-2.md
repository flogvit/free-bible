**Main Theme:** God’s judgment on Nineveh involves the total destruction of Assyria and freedom for Judah.

**Content:** The chapter opens with a message of peace to Judah: a messenger proclaims peace because the oppressor is about to be destroyed. Then Nineveh’s fall is depicted in vivid imagery—the attacker advances, chariots rage through the streets, and the city is plundered of its immense wealth. The Lord Himself declares that He is coming against Assyria, compared to a ravenous lion, and will utterly wipe out its power.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Feet of the Peace Bringer (v.1):** The messenger who proclaims peace on the mountains symbolizes the good news of Nineveh’s fall and Judah’s deliverance.
- **The Lion’s Den (vv.12–14):** Nineveh is compared to a lion’s den full of prey—an image of Assyria’s violent oppression of other nations.
- **The Water Gate (v.7):** The river gates are opened, and the palace trembles—a reference to Nineveh’s fall through flooding or a breach in its defenses.
- **Empty, emptied, plundered (v.11):** Three Hebrew words with the same root (בּוּקָה וּמְבוּקָה וּמְבֻלָּקָה) create an onomatopoeic effect that underscores total destruction.
