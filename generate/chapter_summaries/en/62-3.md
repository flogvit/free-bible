**Main Theme:** God's children are characterized by righteousness and practical love for one another, in contrast to the world's hatred and the devil's works.

**Content:** John explains that we already are God's children, loved by the Father, and that at Jesus' return we will become like him. Whoever is born of God does not live in sin, for Jesus came to take away sin and destroy the devil's works. Love for fellow believers is the central proof that we have passed from death to life—a love that must be shown in action, not merely in words. By believing in Jesus and loving one another, we keep God's commands and have confidence before God, and the Spirit confirms that he remains in us.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Children of God (τέκνα θεοῦ):** Our identity as loved by the Father, unknown to the world but destined to become like Christ.
- **Sin and lawlessness (ἁμαρτία/ἀνομία):** Sin is defined as lawbreaking; Jesus came to remove sin and break the devil's power.
- **Brotherly love (ἀγαπᾶν τοὺς ἀδελφούς):** The defining mark of God's children, demonstrated by Jesus' self-sacrifice and called to be shown in action.
- **Cain:** A negative example of hatred leading to murder, contrasting with the love believers are to show.
- **Confidence (παρρησία):** The assurance we have before God when our heart does not condemn us, because we keep his commands.
