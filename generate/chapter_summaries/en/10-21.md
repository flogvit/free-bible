**Main Theme:** The chapter deals with how David makes atonement for Saul’s sin against the Gibeonites and how his men defeat the Philistine giants.

**Content:** A three-year famine strikes Israel, and God reveals that the cause is Saul’s breach of covenant with the Gibeonites. David hands over seven of Saul’s descendants to the Gibeonites, who execute them. Rizpah, Saul’s concubine, faithfully guards the bodies from birds and animals, moving David to retrieve the bones of Saul and Jonathan and bury them together with those executed. In the final part of the chapter, David’s men fight four giants from Gath, descendants of Rapha, and defeat them all.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Gibeonites:** A people the Israelites had sworn to protect, but whom Saul tried to destroy in his zeal for Israel.
- **Rizpah:** Saul’s concubine who guarded the dead bodies from birds and animals, an image of faithful mourning and care.
- **Descendants of Rapha:** Four Philistine giants from Gath whom David’s men defeated, including one with twenty-four fingers and toes.
- **The lamp of Israel:** An expression used for David—his men forbade him to go into battle so as not to "extinguish the lamp of Israel."
