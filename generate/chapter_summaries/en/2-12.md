**Main Theme:** The institution of the Passover feast and Israel's departure from Egypt following the tenth plague.

**Content:** God gives Moses and Aaron detailed instructions regarding the Passover meal: a flawless lamb is to be slaughtered, its blood applied to the doorframes as a sign, and the meat eaten with unleavened bread and bitter herbs while ready for travel. That same night, the Lord strikes down all the firstborn in Egypt, and Pharaoh orders the Israelites to leave immediately. After 430 years in Egypt, the people set out—about 600,000 men, besides women and children. The chapter concludes with regulations concerning who may partake of the Passover meal: only those who are circumcised; foreigners and uncircumcised persons are excluded.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Passover Lamb (פֶּסַח):** A one-year-old lamb without defect, whose blood protects against judgment and whose meat is eaten in community.
- **Blood on the Doorframes:** The sign that causes the Lord to "pass over" (pasach) the houses of the Israelites when He strikes Egypt.
- **Unleavened Bread (מַצּוֹת):** A symbol of the haste of the departure; it is to be eaten for seven days as an annual memorial festival.
- **Night of Watching (לֵיל שִׁמֻּרִים):** The night when the Lord kept watch to bring Israel out, which is to be observed as holy by all generations.
