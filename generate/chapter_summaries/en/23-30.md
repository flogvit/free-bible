**Main Theme:** A warning against seeking help from Egypt rather than trusting in the Lord, with a promise of grace for those who turn back.

**Content:** Isaiah rebukes Judah for forming alliances with Egypt without consulting God—Egypt is called "Rahab who sits still," powerless to help. The people's sin is compared to a crumbling wall that suddenly collapses. God offers salvation through repentance and trust: "In quietness and confidence shall be your strength" (v.15). The chapter concludes with promises of God's gracious care for the faithful and his judgment over Assyria at Topheth.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Rahab who sits still (v.7):** A mythical sea monster used as a mocking name for Egypt—mighty in reputation, but paralyzed in action.
- **The broken wall (vv.13–14):** An image of how sin leads to sudden and total collapse, shattered like an earthen vessel.
- **Quietness and trust (v.15):** God's way of salvation stands in contrast to the people's pursuit of military alliances.
- **Topheth (v.33):** A burning place of sacrifice outside Jerusalem, here representing God's judgment on the king of Assyria.
