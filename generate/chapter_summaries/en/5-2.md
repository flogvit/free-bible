**Main Theme:** God's guidance of Israel through the wilderness, respecting the inheritance lands of other peoples and achieving victory over enemies.

**Content:** Israel travels around Mount Se'ir and is instructed to avoid conflict with Edom, Moab, and Ammon, since God has given these lands to the descendants of Esau and Lot. Thirty-eight years pass from Kadesh-Barnea until the entire generation of fighting men has died out, just as God had sworn. At the Arnon River, God commands them to take the land from King Sihon of Heshbon. Sihon refuses peaceful passage, and Israel completely defeats him.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The forty years in the wilderness (v.7):** God has blessed and provided for the people—they lacked nothing.
- **Inheritance lands (vv.5, 9, 19):** God has assigned specific territories to other nations (Esau, Moab, Ammon), which Israel must respect.
- **Death of the generation of fighting men (vv.14–16):** The fulfillment of God's judgment—those who were disobedient at Kadesh-Barnea had to die before the conquest could begin.
- **Sihon, king of Heshbon (vv.24–36):** God's first granted victory east of the Jordan, where God hardened Sihon's heart so that he would be delivered into Israel's hand.
