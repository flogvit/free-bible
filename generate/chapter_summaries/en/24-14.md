**Main Theme:** A severe drought over Judah serves as the backdrop for a confrontation between God’s judgment, false prophecy, and the people’s plea for mercy.

**Content:** The chapter opens with a dramatic description of the devastation caused by the drought in Judah—both people and animals suffer. Jeremiah prays for the people, but God forbids him to intercede because the judgment is decreed. False prophets promise peace, yet God exposes them as liars who themselves will be struck by sword and famine. The chapter concludes with the people’s collective confession of sin and their plea that God remember His covenant.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The Drought (הַבַּצָּרוֹת):** A symbol of God’s judgment and the people’s spiritual condition—the lack of water reflects their absence from God.
- **False Prophets:** Those who prophesy peace and lies in God’s name without being sent by Him.
- **Prohibition on Intercession:** God refuses to let Jeremiah pray for the people—a sign that the judgment is irreversible.
- **The Covenant (בְּרִית):** The people appeal to God’s faithfulness to His covenant as their last hope of salvation.
