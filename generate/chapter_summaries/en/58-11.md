**Main Theme:** Hebrews 11 presents faith as the foundation for pleasing God, illustrated through examples from Israel's history.

**Content:** The chapter opens with a definition of faith as "the assurance of things hoped for, the conviction of things not seen" (v. 1). It then follows with numerous examples of faith from the Old Testament: Abel, Enoch, Noah, Abraham, Sarah, Isaac, Jacob, Joseph, Moses, Rahab, and many others. All of these acted in faith on God's promises, even though they did not experience their fulfillment during their lifetimes. Many also suffered for their faith—they were tortured, stoned, and killed. The chapter concludes by stating that God had planned something better, so that they would not be made perfect apart from us (v. 40).

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Faith (πίστις):** Assurance of the unseen and the foundation of hope, necessary for pleasing God.
- **Testimony (μαρτυρέω):** Believers received a good testimony through their faith, even without seeing the fulfillment of the promises.
- **Stranger and Pilgrim:** Believers confessed that they were strangers on earth and sought a heavenly homeland.
- **The City with Foundations:** A symbol of the heavenly hope Abraham awaited—a city built by God Himself.
- **A Better Resurrection:** The motivation to suffer rather than deny the faith.
