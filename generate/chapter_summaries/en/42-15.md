**Main Theme:** God's boundless joy over sinners who repent, illustrated through three parables Jesus tells in response to the Pharisees' criticism.

**Content:** Tax collectors and sinners gather around Jesus, causing the Pharisees and teachers of the law to grumble. In response, Jesus tells three parables: about the lost sheep, the lost coin, and the lost son. All three emphasize the same truth: heaven celebrates when what was lost is found and sinners turn back to God. In the parable of the two sons, the bitterness of the older brother is also challenged – he represents those who do not understand God's gracious heart.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The Lost (sheep, coin, son):** Represents sinners who are separated from God and need to be found.
- **Joy in Heaven:** Angels and God Himself celebrate when one sinner repents (v.7, 10).
- **The Father's Embrace:** A picture of God's unconditional love and forgiveness – He runs toward His son and fully restores him.
- **The Older Brother:** Represents the self-righteous who do not understand grace, such as the Pharisees.
