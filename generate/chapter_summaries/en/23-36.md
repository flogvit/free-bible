**Main Theme:** Assyria's military and psychological threat against Jerusalem during King Hezekiah's reign.

**Content:** In the fourteenth year of Hezekiah's reign, the Assyrian king Sennacherib invaded Judah and captured all its fortified cities. He sent his supreme commander, the Rabshakeh, to Jerusalem with a large army to demand surrender. The Rabshakeh mocked Judah's trust in Egypt and in the LORD, claiming that no god had been able to stand against Assyria. Despite Hezekiah's officials begging him to speak Aramaic, he shouted his message in Hebrew to the people on the wall. Hezekiah's servants returned to the king with torn clothes and reported the Rabshakeh's words.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Rabshakeh:** Assyria's supreme commander who waged psychological warfare against Jerusalem through threats and propaganda.
- **Broken Reed (Egypt):** A symbol of Egypt's unreliability as an ally—those who lean on it are pierced.
- **"Which of the gods has saved his land?":** The Rabshakeh's arrogant challenge that places the LORD on par with powerless idols.
- **Torn Clothes:** A sign of deep grief and distress, showing the seriousness of the Assyrian threat.
