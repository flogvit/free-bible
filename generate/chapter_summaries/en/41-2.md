**Main Theme:** Jesus' authority challenges religious traditions through forgiveness, fellowship with sinners, and a new interpretation of the Sabbath.

**Content:** Jesus heals a paralyzed man and forgives his sins, provoking the scribes who believe only God can forgive. He calls the tax collector Levi and eats with tax collectors and sinners, explaining that he has come to call sinners, not the righteous. When asked about fasting, Jesus answers with parables about new wine in new wineskins—the new cannot be forced into old forms. When the Pharisees criticize the disciples for picking grain on the Sabbath, Jesus declares that the Son of Man is Lord of the Sabbath.

**Key Terms/Images:**
- **Son of Man (ὁ υἱὸς τοῦ ἀνθρώπου):** Jesus' self-designation emphasizing his divine authority to forgive sins and be Lord of the Sabbath.
- **Forgiveness (ἀφίενταί αἱ ἁμαρτίαι):** Jesus' declaration of forgiveness demonstrates his divine authority.
- **Bridegroom (ὁ νυμφίος):** A picture of Jesus himself and the messianic time of salvation that has broken in.
- **New wine in new wineskins:** A parable showing that Jesus' message requires new frameworks, not merely adaptation to old traditions.
