**Main Theme:** The fall of Assyria as a warning to Egypt that God humbles the proud.

**Content:** God commands Ezekiel to speak to Pharaoh and use Assyria as a cautionary example. Assyria was like a mighty cedar in Lebanon—more beautiful than all the trees in God's garden, with nations seeking shelter in its shade. But because its heart became proud, God handed the cedar over to the mightiest of the nations, who cut it down. Assyria was sent down to Sheol along with other fallen nations. This is also what will happen to Pharaoh and his entire army.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The Cedar in Lebanon:** A symbol of Assyria's greatness and power, more beautiful than all other trees, representing the nations.
- **Pride (רָם לְבָבוֹ):** The cause of its fall—its heart became proud because of its own height and majesty.
- **Sheol (שְׁאוֹל):** The destination for fallen world powers, where they lie among the uncircumcised.
- **God's Garden/Eden:** A reference point for greatness—even the trees of Eden were envious of the cedar.
