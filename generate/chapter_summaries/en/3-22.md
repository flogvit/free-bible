**Main Theme:** Rules regarding the priests' purity in handling holy offerings and requirements for unblemished sacrificial animals.

**Content:** The chapter gives instructions that priests must be ritually clean to eat from the holy offerings, and describes various forms of uncleanness that temporarily disqualify them. It establishes who in the priest's household may eat from the offerings, along with rules concerning unintentional consumption of holy food. The second main section deals with requirements for sacrificial animals: they must be without defect, at least eight days old, and a mother and her offspring must not be slaughtered on the same day. The chapter concludes with an exhortation not to profane God's holy name.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Purity (טָהֵר):** Priests had to be ritually clean in order to handle and eat from the holy offerings.
- **Defect (מוּם):** Sacrificial animals had to be without physical blemishes to be accepted by the Lord.
- **Holy Offerings (קֹדֶשׁ):** The sacrifices that the people brought to the Lord, which were reserved for the priests.
- **Profane (חָלַל):** To treat something holy in an unworthy manner, which is strictly forbidden.
