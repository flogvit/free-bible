**Main Theme:** God rejects His people through the image of a shepherd who breaks his staffs and warns of a coming evil shepherd.

**Content:** The chapter opens with words of judgment against Lebanon and Bashan. The prophet is commissioned to be a shepherd for "the sheep destined for slaughter" – the people exploited by their leaders. He uses two staffs, "Favor" and "Union," but is rejected by the people and valued at only 30 silver pieces, which are thrown to the potter in the temple. He breaks both staffs as a sign that God's covenant is annulled and the brotherhood between Judah and Israel is severed. The chapter concludes with a warning about a worthless shepherd who will come.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The Two Staffs (Favor and Union):** Symbolize God's covenant with the peoples and the unity between Judah and Israel, both of which are broken.
- **30 Silver Pieces:** The contemptible price at which the shepherd is valued, thrown to the potter – a motif pointing toward Jesus' betrayal.
- **The Sheep Destined for Slaughter:** God's mistreated people who are exploited by their own leaders and buyers without anyone showing compassion.
- **The Worthless Shepherd:** A future evil leader who will have no care for the flock but will himself be struck by God's judgment.
