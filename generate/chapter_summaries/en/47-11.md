**Main Theme:** Paul defends his apostolic ministry against false apostles and points to his sufferings as proof of his authenticity.

**Content:** Paul asks the Corinthians to bear with his "foolishness" as he defends himself, for he is jealous over them with a godly jealousy and fears they may be led astray just as the serpent deceived Eve. He warns against false apostles who disguise themselves as servants of Christ but are actually deceivers—just as Satan himself disguises himself as an angel of light. Paul lists his sufferings: beatings, imprisonment, shipwrecks, dangers on journeys, and daily concern for all the churches. He concludes by recounting his escape from Damascus, where he was lowered in a basket through the city wall.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Superapostles (ὑπερλίαν ἀποστόλων):** An ironic label for opponents who boast of their qualifications.
- **Satan as an angel of light:** A picture of how false teachers can appear attractive while leading people astray.
- **Foolish speech (ἀφροσύνη):** Paul's ironic approach in which he "boasts" to expose the superficiality of his opponents.
- **Catalog of sufferings:** A detailed list of Paul's trials as evidence of genuine apostolic service.
- **The basket in Damascus:** A concrete example of the danger and humiliation Paul endured for the sake of the gospel.
