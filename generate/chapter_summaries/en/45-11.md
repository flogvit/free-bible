**Main Theme:** God has not rejected Israel – his plan of salvation includes both Jews and Gentiles through his sovereign grace.

**Content:** Paul explains that God has not rejected his people; a believing remnant exists by the election of grace. He uses the olive tree as an illustration: natural branches (Israel) were broken off because of unbelief, while wild branches (Gentiles) were grafted in. Paul warns Gentiles against arrogance and reveals a mystery: Israel's partial hardening is temporary – it lasts until the full number of Gentiles has come in, and then all Israel will be saved. The chapter concludes with a doxology praising God's unfathomable wisdom.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Remnant (λεῖμμα):** As in Elijah's time, there is a remnant chosen by grace, not by works (v.5-6).
- **Olive Tree:** An illustration of God's people where Gentiles are grafted in as wild branches, but the root (the patriarchs) supports them (v.16-24).
- **Mystery (μυστήριον):** Israel's partial hardening is temporary – all Israel will ultimately be saved (v.25-26).
- **God's Irrevocable Call:** God's gifts and calling are irrevocable, guaranteeing Israel's future salvation (v.29).
