**Main Theme:** God Calls Israel to Account – Election Brings Greater Responsibility and Stricter Judgment.

**Content:** The Lord reminds Israel that He has chosen them above all other nations, but precisely for this reason He will punish them for their sins (vv. 1–2). Through a series of rhetorical questions, Amos shows that everything has a cause—including the coming judgment (vv. 3–8). God reveals His plans to the prophets before He acts. The chapter concludes with specific judgments: an enemy will surround the land, the altars at Bethel will be destroyed, and the palaces and ivory houses of the rich will perish (vv. 11–15).

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The Lion's Roar (v. 8):** When God speaks, the prophet must proclaim—just as inevitably as fear comes when a lion roars.
- **Cause and Effect (vv. 3–6):** Rhetorical questions show that judgment does not come by chance, but as a consequence of Israel's sin.
- **The Ivory Houses (v. 15):** A symbol of the luxury and oppression of the upper class, which will be struck by God's judgment.
- **The Altars at Bethel (v. 14):** The illegitimate place of worship that will be destroyed when God punishes Israel's rebellion.
