**Main Theme:** The Return of the Eastern Tribes and the Altar as a Witness to Unity in Faith in the Lord.

**Content:** Joshua sends the tribes of Reuben, Gad, and half-tribe of Manasseh back to their territories east of the Jordan after they faithfully helped conquer Canaan. He exhorts them to love the Lord and keep His commandments. At the Jordan, they build a large altar, which causes the western tribes to fear apostasy and gather for war. However, the delegation led by the priest Phinehas learns that the altar is not for sacrifices but serves as a witness that the eastern tribes also belong to the Lord. The conflict is resolved, and unity is preserved.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The Altar at the Jordan:** A memorial testifying to the eastern tribes' belonging to the God of Israel, not a competing place of sacrifice.
- **Phinehas:** The priest who leads the delegation and confirms that the tribes have not been unfaithful to the Lord.
- **Unfaithfulness (ma'al):** The fear that one tribe's sin could bring God's judgment upon the entire people, as seen at Peor and with Achan.
- **Unity in Faith:** The tribes' shared confession of faith in the Lord despite geographical separation.
