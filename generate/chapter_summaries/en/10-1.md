**Main Theme:** David receives the news of Saul’s and Jonathan’s death and honors them with a lament.

**Content:** An Amalekite comes to David wearing Saul’s crown and armband, claiming that he killed the mortally wounded king at his own request. David and his men tear their clothes, mourn, weep, and fast until evening. David orders the Amalekite executed for having slain the Lord’s anointed. Then David composes the lament called "The Bow," in which he honors Saul and Jonathan as beloved, mighty warriors.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The Lord’s Anointed (מְשִׁיחַ יְהוָה):** Saul’s holy status as God’s chosen king, whom no one has the right to harm.
- **The Lament "The Bow":** David’s poetic tribute to the fallen, which was to be taught to the people of Judah.
- **"Swifter than eagles, stronger than lions":** A poetic description of Saul’s and Jonathan’s courage in battle.
- **David’s love for Jonathan:** David describes their friendship as more wonderful than the love of women.
