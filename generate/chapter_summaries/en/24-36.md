**Main Theme:** God's word cannot be destroyed—even when King Jehoiakim burns the scroll, the message is rewritten with additions.

**Content:** God commands Jeremiah to write down all his prophecies on a scroll, hoping that the people will repent. Since Jeremiah is prevented from going to the temple, he sends his scribe Baruch to read the scroll aloud on a fast day. When King Jehoiakim hears its contents, he cuts the scroll into pieces and burns it on the hearth. God then commands that a new scroll be written with the same content plus additional words, and He announces judgment against Jehoiakim and his descendants.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Scroll (מְגִלָּה):** God's word in written form, preserved and continued despite human opposition.
- **Hearth (הָאָח):** Symbolizes the king's contempt for God's word, yet also demonstrates the word's invincibility.
- **Baruch:** Jeremiah's faithful scribe who delivers the message when the prophet himself is hindered.
- **Repentance:** The purpose of reading the scroll was to give the people an opportunity to turn from their evil ways.
