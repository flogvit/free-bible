**Main Theme:** God's judgment on the enemies and the people's prayer that He would intervene as their Father and Redeemer.

**Content:** The chapter opens with a vision of God coming from Edom in blood-red garments after having trodden "the winepress of vengeance" alone against the nations (vv. 1–6). This is followed by a praise of the Lord's faithfulness throughout history, especially the exodus from Egypt, but also an acknowledgment that the people grieved His Holy Spirit (vv. 7–14). The chapter concludes with an intense prayer in which the people confess God as their Father and Redeemer, asking Him to tear open the heavens and come down to help them (vv. 15–19).

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The Winepress:** A picture of God's judgment where He alone tramples the enemies, and their "juice" sprays upon His garments (v. 3).
- **The Holy Spirit:** The people grieved God's Spirit through their disobedience, so He became their enemy (v. 10).
- **God as Father:** Israel's confession of the Lord as their true Father and eternal Redeemer, in contrast to the patriarchs who can no longer help them (v. 16).
- **"Tear open the heavens":** A desperate cry for God to intervene dramatically, just as He did in times past (v. 19).
