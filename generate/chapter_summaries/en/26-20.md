**Main Theme:** God confronts the elders of Israel with the people's repeated rebellion throughout history, yet promises future restoration for His name's sake.

**Content:** Elders from Israel come to seek God, but He refuses to be consulted and commands Ezekiel to remind them of their ancestors' abominations. Through three generations—in Egypt, in the wilderness, and among their children—Israel rebelled by following idols and profaning the Sabbath. Each time, God restrained His wrath for His name's sake. God warns against becoming like the nations and promises to gather the people, bring them into "the wilderness of the peoples" for judgment, purge out the rebels, and restore the faithful to His holy mountain.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The Sabbath as a sign (vv. 12, 20):** The Sabbath is the covenant sign between God and Israel, showing that the Lord sanctifies them.
- **"For My name's sake" (vv. 9, 14, 22, 44):** God acts not because of Israel's merit, but to preserve His holy name among the nations.
- **The wilderness of the peoples (v. 35):** A future place of judgment where God will meet Israel face to face, just as in their first wilderness journey.
- **God's holy mountain (v. 40):** The place of future worship where all Israel will serve the Lord with their offerings.
