**Main Theme:** The chapter describes the succession of kings in both Judah and Israel, focusing on faithfulness or unfaithfulness to the Lord.

**Content:** In Judah, Abijam reigns first for three years and walks in his father's sins, but God preserves the dynasty for David's sake. His son Asa reigns for 41 years, does what is right, and removes idolatry, including deposing his grandmother Maacah. In conflict with Baasha of Israel, Asa allies himself with Aram by using temple treasures as a bribe. In Israel, Baasha kills King Nadab and wipes out the entire house of Jeroboam, fulfilling the words of the prophet Ahijah.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **David's lamp (nir):** God preserves the dynasty of Judah for David's sake, despite the sins of its kings.
- **High places:** Even the godly Asa did not remove the high places—a recurring problem in the history of Judah.
- **The sin of Jeroboam:** The repeated formula about walking in the sins of Jeroboam describes the persistent apostasy of the northern kingdom.
- **The Ben-Hadad alliance:** Asa's use of temple treasures for a political alliance illustrates the tension between faith and political pragmatism.
