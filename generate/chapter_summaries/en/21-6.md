**Main Theme:** The chapter explores the emptiness of wealth and earthly goods when one cannot enjoy them, and humanity's limited ability to understand life.

**Content:** The Preacher describes a man who has received riches, possessions, and honor from God, yet is denied the ability to enjoy them—instead, a stranger enjoys them. Even if a man fathers a hundred children and lives many years, a stillborn child is better off if its soul never finds satisfaction. All human toil is for the mouth, yet the appetite is never filled. The chapter concludes with the question of what is good for humanity during life's fleeting days, since no one can tell what will happen afterward.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Hevel (הֶבֶל):** Emptiness, futility, or meaninglessness—a recurring theme regarding life's blessings when there is no ability to enjoy them.
- **The stillborn child (הַנָּפֶל):** A symbol that non-existence may be preferable to a life without satisfaction.
- **A stranger who enjoys (אִישׁ נָכְרִי):** Illustrates the tragedy when others gain pleasure from what one has gathered.
- **Shadow (כַּצֵּל):** A metaphor for the brevity and transience of life.
