**Main Theme:** A period of political instability, violence, and Assyrian threat characterizes both Israel and Judah.

**Content:** In Judah, Azariah (Uzziah) reigned for 52 years and did what was right, but he was struck with leprosy so that his son Jotham ruled in his place. Israel experienced chaos with several royal murders: Zechariah was killed by Shallum, who was killed by Menahem; Menahem's son Pekahiah was killed by Pekah, who in turn was killed by Hoshea. Menahem paid tribute to the Assyrian king Pul, and later Tiglath-Pileser took large areas in the north and deported the people to Assyria. Jotham ruled righteously in Judah, but the high places remained, and he was succeeded by Ahaz.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Leprosy (צָרַעַת):** God's judgment on King Azariah, who had to live in isolation while his son ruled.
- **The dynasty of Jehu ends:** The death of Zechariah fulfilled the prophecy that Jehu's descendants would reign for four generations.
- **Assyrian threat:** Both Pul and Tiglath-Pileser represent the growing Assyrian power threatening Israel.
- **High places (הַבָּמוֹת):** Even under good kings, the people continued to offer sacrifices on these illegal high places.
