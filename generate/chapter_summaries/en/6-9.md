**Main Theme:** The Gibeonites' deception leads to a covenant with Israel that cannot be broken.

**Content:** When the Gibeonites hear about Israel's victories over Jericho and Ai, they use deception to avoid destruction. They dress in worn-out clothes and carry dry bread to create the impression that they come from a distant land. The Israelites examine their provisions but do not inquire of the Lord, and they make a covenant with them. Three days later, they discover the deceit, but the oath prevents them from killing the Gibeonites. Joshua curses them and makes them woodcutters and water carriers for the congregation and for the altar of the Lord.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Deception (עָרְמָה):** The Gibeonites' trickery with worn clothes and dry bread illustrates a desperate instinct for survival.
- **Covenant (בְּרִית):** A binding agreement that Israel must keep even though it was made under false pretenses.
- **Inquiring of the Lord:** The Israelites "did not ask at the mouth of the Lord" (v. 14) – a warning about the consequences of acting without God's guidance.
- **Woodcutters and Water Carriers:** The Gibeonites' permanent service role at the sanctuary as a result of their deception.
