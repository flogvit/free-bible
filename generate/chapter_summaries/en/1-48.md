**Main Theme:** Jacob adopts and blesses Joseph's sons, giving the younger Ephraim precedence over the firstborn Manasseh.

**Content:** When Jacob is near death, Joseph brings his sons Manasseh and Ephraim to him. Jacob adopts the two boys as his own sons, on equal footing with Reuben and Simeon, so that they become tribal ancestors in Israel. During the blessing, Jacob deliberately crosses his hands and places his right hand on the younger Ephraim. Despite Joseph's protest, Jacob insists that Ephraim's descendants will become greater than Manasseh's. Jacob also gives Joseph Shechem as an additional inheritance portion.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Crossed Hands:** Jacob intentionally crosses his hands to give the birthright to the younger—a recurring pattern in Genesis.
- **Adoption:** By equating Ephraim and Manasseh with Reuben and Simeon, Jacob secures their status as distinct tribes.
- **El Shaddai:** Jacob recalls God's revelation at Luz (Bethel) as the foundation for the blessing.
- **Shechem:** An additional inheritance portion given to Joseph, taken from the Amorites with sword and bow.
