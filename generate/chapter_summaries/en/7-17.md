**Main Theme:** Religious decline in Israel illustrated through Micah's private shrine and illegitimate priesthood.

**Content:** Micah from the hill country of Ephraim steals 1,100 silver pieces from his mother but confesses after hearing her curse. His mother dedicates the silver to the LORD, yet paradoxically uses it to make a carved image. Micah establishes a private shrine with an ephod and household gods, first appointing his son as priest, then hiring a traveling Levite for the role. All this happens because "there was no king in Israel; everyone did what was right in their own eyes."

**Key Terms/Images:**
- **Pesel and massekah (carved image and cast image):** Idolatrous objects made from silver dedicated to the LORD – demonstrating syncretism.
- **Ephod and teraphim:** Cultic items that belonged in the tabernacle, but here used in private idol worship.
- **No king in Israel:** A refrain explaining the lawlessness and spiritual decay during the period of the judges.
- **The Levite as priest:** Micah mistakenly believes that having a Levitical priest will secure God's blessing over his unlawful shrine.
