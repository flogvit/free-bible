**Main Theme:** Solomon's preparations for building the temple through an alliance with Tyre and the organization of the workforce.

**Content:** Solomon sends a message to King Hiram of Tyre, requesting timber and a skilled craftsman for the temple construction. He emphasizes that the temple is to be built for the God of Israel, who is greater than all gods—even though the heavens of heaven cannot contain him. Hiram responds positively, praises the God of Israel, and sends the craftsman Huram-Abi along with a promise of timber from Lebanon. Solomon organizes 153,600 foreigners living in Israel as laborers: carriers, stonecutters, and overseers.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **God's greatness:** Solomon acknowledges that even the heavens of heaven cannot contain God—the temple is merely a place for worship and sacrifice (v. 5–6).
- **International cooperation:** The alliance with Tyre shows how pagan nations contribute to God's house, and Hiram praises the God of Israel.
- **Huram-Abi:** A master craftsman with an Israelite mother and a Tyrian father, sent to lead the artistic work.
- **Forced labor:** The foreigners in Israel are counted and assigned to heavy work—a pattern that later creates division within the kingdom.
