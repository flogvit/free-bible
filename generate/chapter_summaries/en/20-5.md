**Main Theme:** A father's exhortation to avoid the foreign woman and hold fast to his own wife.

**Content:** The chapter warns against the seductive foreign woman, whose words are sweet as honey but lead to death and destruction (vv. 3-6). The son is urged to stay away from her in order to avoid losing honor, years, and wealth to others (vv. 7-14). Instead, he should rejoice in his own wife, portrayed through the image of drinking from one's own well (vv. 15-19). The chapter concludes with the reminder that the Lord sees all human ways, and that the wicked are caught by their own sins (vv. 21-23).

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The foreign woman (זָרָה):** Represents sexual temptation outside of marriage, whose alluring words conceal deadly danger.
- **Honey and wormwood:** The contrast between the sweet appearance of temptation (v. 3) and its bitter consequences (v. 4).
- **One's own well/spring:** A picture of marital faithfulness—finding satisfaction in one's own wife (vv. 15-18).
- **The eyes of the Lord:** God sees and weighs all human ways, underscoring accountability for one's own actions (v. 21).
