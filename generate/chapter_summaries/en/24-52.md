**Main Theme:** The fall and destruction of Jerusalem as a result of God’s judgment on Judah’s sin and rebellion.

**Content:** King Zedekiah did what was evil in the Lord’s sight and rebelled against Babylon, which led to Nebuchadnezzar besieging Jerusalem. After a prolonged famine, the city fell; Zedekiah’s sons were killed before his eyes, he was blinded, and taken to Babylon. The temple and the city were burned, the walls were torn down, and the people were carried into exile—a total of 4,600 persons. The chapter concludes with Jehoiachin being released after 37 years in captivity by King Evil-Merodach and given an honorable place at the royal court.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The Lord’s anger (v. 3):** The destruction occurred because God’s wrath was upon Jerusalem and Judah until He cast them away from His presence.
- **Blinded and bound (v. 11):** Zedekiah was blinded and put in bronze chains—a picture of the total humiliation of judgment.
- **The temple’s destruction (vv. 13, 17–23):** The burning of the Lord’s house and the plundering of the temple articles mark the end of an era.
- **Jehoiachin’s restoration (vv. 31–34):** Jehoiachin’s release offers a glimpse of hope amid judgment—the line of David continues.
