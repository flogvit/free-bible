**Main Theme:** The Parable of the Rusty Pot and the Death of Ezekiel’s Wife as a Sign of Jerusalem’s Inevitable Judgment.

**Content:** On the exact day Babylon’s siege of Jerusalem begins, God gives Ezekiel the parable of a cooking pot full of rust (sin) that cannot be cleansed—a picture of "the city of blood," Jerusalem. On that same day, Ezekiel’s wife dies, but he is forbidden to mourn publicly. This becomes a prophetic sign: when the temple falls, the people will no longer be able to mourn normally, but will instead waste away in their guilt. A fugitive will come and confirm the prophecy.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The Rusty Pot:** Jerusalem is so permeated with sin (rust/uncleanness) that even the judgment of fire cannot cleanse it.
- **City of Blood:** Jerusalem has openly shed innocent blood without covering it up—a shameless sin demanding retribution.
- **Ezekiel’s Wife ("the delight of your eyes"):** Her death and Ezekiel’s prohibition against mourning symbolize how the people will lose both the temple and their children without being able to mourn.
- **Sign (mofet):** Ezekiel himself becomes a living sign for the people, showing them how they will face the coming catastrophe.
