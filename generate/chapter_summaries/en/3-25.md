**Main Theme:** The sabbatical year and the Year of Jubilee as institutions for rest, release, and restoration in the land God gives to Israel.

**Content:** Every seventh year the land must rest (the sabbatical year), and every fiftieth year the Year of Jubilee is celebrated with the release of slaves and the return of property. When selling land, the price is calculated based on the number of years remaining until the next Jubilee, and the seller or his kinsman-redeemer has the right to redeem it. The Israelites are to help poor countrymen without charging interest, and Hebrew servants are to be treated as hired workers, not slaves. All this is grounded in the fact that the land belongs to God, and the Israelites are His servants whom He brought out of Egypt.

**Key Terms/Images:**
- **Sabbatical year (shemittah):** The seventh year when the land must rest and not be cultivated.
- **Year of Jubilee (yovel):** The fiftieth year when property is returned and slaves are set free.
- **Kinsman-redeemer (goel):** A close relative who has the right and duty to buy back property for a poor brother.
- **"The land is mine":** God owns the land; the Israelites are strangers and guests with Him.
