**Main Theme:** Moses reminds Israel that they do not deserve the Promised Land because of their own righteousness, but receive it because of God’s promise to the fathers and the wickedness of the other nations.

**Content:** Israel is to cross the Jordan to take possession of the land from powerful nations, but this will not happen because of Israel’s righteousness—they are a stiff-necked people. Moses recalls the incident with the golden calf at Horeb, when God intended to destroy the nation, but Moses interceded for forty days and nights. He also mentions the rebellions at Taberah, Massah, and Kibroth-hattaavah. Moses appealed to God’s promise to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, asking God not to look upon the people’s stubbornness and sin—and God heard his prayer.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Stiff-necked people (עַם־קְשֵׁה־עֹרֶף):** Describes Israel’s repeated resistance and disobedience toward God throughout their wilderness journey.
- **Devouring fire (אֵשׁ אֹכְלָה):** A picture of God Himself going before Israel to destroy and subdue their enemies.
- **Stone tablets (לוּחֹת הָאֲבָנִים):** The covenant tablets written by the finger of God, which Moses broke before the eyes of the people when he saw their sin.
- **Moses’ intercession:** Moses fasted for forty days and nights and prayed for the people and Aaron to turn away God’s anger.
