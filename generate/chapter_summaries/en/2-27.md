**Main Theme:** Instructions for the construction of the altar of burnt offering, the courtyard of the tabernacle, and the arrangement for the lamp oil.

**Content:** The chapter first describes the altar of burnt offering made of acacia wood overlaid with bronze, featuring horns at its corners and carrying poles. It then gives detailed measurements for the courtyard: 100 cubits long and 50 cubits wide, surrounded by hangings of finely twisted linen on pillars with bronze bases and silver bands. The entrance gate is to have a colorful screen. Finally, the Israelites are commanded to bring pure, pressed olive oil for the lamp, which Aaron and his sons must keep burning from evening until morning—an everlasting ordinance.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Altar of Burnt Offering:** A square altar (5x5x3 cubits) made of acacia wood overlaid with bronze, serving as the center for sacrifices.
- **Courtyard:** The holy, enclosed area around the tabernacle where the people could approach God.
- **Bronze:** The primary material for the altar and the courtyard bases, symbolizing judgment and endurance.
- **Pure Oil (שֶׁמֶן זַיִת זָךְ):** Pressed olive oil for the perpetual light, a symbol of God's enduring presence.
- **Everlasting Ordinance (חֻקַּת עוֹלָם):** The lamp service as a lasting obligation through all generations.
