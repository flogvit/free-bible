**Main Theme:** The bridegroom's praise of the bride's beauty and her invitation to love.

**Content:** The bridegroom praises the bride's beauty through a series of poetic images—from eyes like doves to a neck like David's tower (vv. 1-5). He declares her flawless and invites her down from the mountains of Lebanon (vv. 7-8). The bride is described as a locked garden and a sealed spring, full of precious fruits and spices (vv. 12-15). The chapter concludes with the bride's invitation to the bridegroom to come into her garden (v. 16).

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Locked Garden (גַּן נָעוּל):** A symbol of the bride's purity, exclusivity, and faithfulness—she is reserved for her bridegroom alone.
- **David's Tower:** The bride's neck is compared to a majestic tower, an image of dignity and beauty.
- **Lebanon:** A mountainous region symbolizing what is exalted and pure; the bride's fragrance is likened to Lebanon's cedar trees.
- **Spices and Fragrance:** Myrrh, frankincense, nard, and cinnamon represent the bride's attractiveness and preciousness.
