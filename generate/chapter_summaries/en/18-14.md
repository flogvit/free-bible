**Main Theme:** Job laments the brevity and transience of human life and longs for hope of renewal after death.

**Content:** Job describes humanity as short-lived and full of turmoil, like a flower that withers and a shadow that vanishes. Unlike a tree that can sprout again after being cut down, humans have no such hope—when they die, they do not rise again. Job expresses a longing for God to hide him in Sheol until His anger passes, yet acknowledges that instead, God watches his sins closely. The chapter ends bleakly: humanity is overcome by God, its face changes, and it does not even know the fate of its children—only its own pain and sorrow remain.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Flower and shadow (v.2):** Images of the brevity and transience of human life—it withers quickly and vanishes without lasting endurance.
- **The tree that sprouts again (vv.7–9):** A contrast to humanity; the tree has hope for renewal, but humans do not.
- **Sheol/the realm of the dead (v.13):** The place where Job wishes to be hidden until God’s anger passes—an expression of longing for rest from suffering.
- **Sealed sin (v.17):** An image that God keeps careful account of human transgressions, stored away in a bag.
