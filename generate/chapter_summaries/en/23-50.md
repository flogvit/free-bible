**Main Theme:** God’s faithfulness to Israel and the obedient suffering of the Lord’s servant as an example of trust in God.

**Content:** God defends Himself against accusations of having rejected Israel—there is no bill of divorce; the people’s own sins are the cause of their situation. The Lord’s servant appears as a loyal disciple who endures suffering and humiliation without turning back, because he trusts in God’s help. The chapter concludes with an exhortation to the godly to trust in the Lord, and a warning to those who rely on their own strength.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Bill of Divorce (v.1):** A rhetorical question showing that God has not rejected Israel—their suffering is due to their own sin.
- **Face like flint (v.7):** The servant’s resolute endurance in suffering, because he knows God will help him.
- **Light and darkness (vv.10–11):** Contrast between walking in darkness while trusting in God, and kindling one’s own fire, which leads to judgment.
