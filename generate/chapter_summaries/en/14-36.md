**Main Theme:** The last kings of Judah lead the nation to destruction through unfaithfulness, but God gives hope for restoration.

**Content:** After Josiah's death, four kings rule over Judah: Jehoahaz, Jehoiakim, Jehoiachin, and Zedekiah—all do evil in the Lord's sight. Both leaders, priests, and people are unfaithful and dishonor the temple, repeatedly rejecting God's prophets. Nebuchadnezzar destroys Jerusalem and the temple, leading the people into exile in Babylon for 70 years. The chapter concludes with Persian king Cyrus proclaiming that the Jews may return home and rebuild the temple.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Unfaithfulness (מַעַל):** The repeated rebellion of the people and their leaders against God, which leads to judgment.
- **The Prophets:** God's patient warnings through messengers whom the people mocked and rejected.
- **Exile:** 70 years of captivity in Babylon as fulfillment of Jeremiah's prophecy and the land's Sabbath rest.
- **Cyrus's Proclamation:** God's sovereign action through a pagan king to restore His people.
