**Main Theme:** Israel's holiness as God's chosen people is demonstrated through dietary laws and tithing regulations.

**Content:** The Israelites are God's children and a holy people, and therefore must not cut themselves or shave their foreheads in mourning over the dead. This chapter provides detailed rules regarding which animals, birds, and fish are clean and unclean, as well as a prohibition against cooking a young goat in its mother's milk. The annual tithe is to be brought to the place God chooses and eaten there with joy, or exchanged for money if the journey is too long. Every third year, the tithe is to be stored locally to help Levites, foreigners, fatherless children, and widows.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Holy people (עַם קָדוֹשׁ):** Israel's election as God's treasured possession is the foundation for all the regulations in this chapter.
- **Clean and unclean:** The distinction between permitted and forbidden animals marks Israel's separation from other nations.
- **Tithe (מַעְשַׂר):** A tenth of the harvest to be used for worship and fellowship with God.
- **Levite, foreigner, fatherless child, and widow:** The vulnerable groups in society who are to be cared for through the third-year tithe.
