**Main Theme:** Abimelech's violent path to power and God's judgment on his wickedness.

**Content:** Abimelech, the son of Gideon and a servant woman from Shechem, kills 70 of his brothers to become king. Only Jotham survives and tells a fable about trees that chose a thornbush as their king—a warning of destruction. God sends an evil spirit between Abimelech and the men of Shechem, and the conflict ends with Abimelech destroying Shechem. He himself dies at Thebez when a woman crushes his head with a millstone, fulfilling Jotham's curse.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Thornbush (האטד):** In Jotham's fable, it represents Abimelech—a worthless and destructive ruler.
- **Fire from the thornbush:** The prophecy of mutual destruction between Abimelech and Shechem (vv. 15, 20).
- **Salt on the ruins:** Abimelech sows salt over Shechem as a sign of total devastation and curse.
- **Millstone:** A woman's weapon that ends Abimelech's life—a humiliating death for the tyrant.
