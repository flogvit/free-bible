**Main Theme:** The chapter describes the consecration ritual for Aaron and his sons as priests, along with the daily offering and God's promise to dwell among Israel.

**Content:** Aaron and his sons are to be washed, clothed in priestly garments, and anointed with oil. Three animals are sacrificed: a bull as a sin offering, one ram as a burnt offering, and one ram as the consecration offering—where blood is applied to the priests' right ear, thumb, and toe. The consecration lasts seven days. Then the daily offering is established with two year-old lambs, one in the morning and one at twilight. The chapter concludes with God's promise to meet Israel at the tabernacle and dwell among them.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Consecration Ram (אֵיל מִלֻּאִים):** The second ram is used to consecrate the priests; its blood is applied to their bodies as a sign of sanctification.
- **Continual Burnt Offering (עֹלַת תָּמִיד):** Two lambs are sacrificed daily, morning and evening, as a perpetual offering to the Lord.
- **God's Presence:** God promises to meet with Israel and dwell among them—the ultimate purpose of the tabernacle.
- **Seven Days:** The consecration period symbolizes complete sanctification and transition into priestly service.
