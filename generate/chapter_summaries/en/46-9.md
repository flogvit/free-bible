**Main Theme:** Paul defends his apostolic freedom and explains why he voluntarily renounces his rights for the sake of the gospel.

**Content:** Paul argues that apostles have a right to material support from the churches, grounding this in both the Mosaic Law and the Lord's own command. Yet he himself has chosen not to exercise this right so as not to place any obstacle in the way of Christ's gospel. He describes how he adapts himself to all people—Jews, Gentiles, and the weak—in order to win as many as possible. The chapter concludes with an exhortation to self-discipline, illustrated through athletic imagery, urging believers to run so as to reach the goal and not be disqualified themselves.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Freedom and Rights (ἐξουσία):** Paul has full right to support, yet he uses his freedom to voluntarily renounce this right.
- **The Ox That Treads Grain:** An image from Deuteronomy showing that the worker deserves their wages—the one who works should share in the fruit of their labor.
- **"All Things to All People":** Paul's missionary strategy of adapting to different people and cultures without compromising the truth of the gospel.
- **The Race and the Crown:** The athletic image portrays the Christian life as requiring discipline and perseverance, with an imperishable crown as the goal.
- **Self-Discipline (ἐγκράτεια):** Paul "strikes" and "brings into subjection" his own body so that after preaching to others he himself will not be disqualified.
