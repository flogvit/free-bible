**Main Theme:** The Song of Songs opens with an intense love dialogue between a woman and her beloved, attributed to Solomon.

**Content:** The woman longs for her beloved's kiss and presence, asking him to tell her where he pastures his flock. She defends her dark skin color before the daughters of Jerusalem—she is sunburned because her brothers set her to guard vineyards. The man praises her beauty and compares her to Pharaoh's magnificent mare. The two exchange declarations of love and dream of their shared home.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Vineyard (כֶּרֶם):** A symbol for the woman herself—she guarded others' vineyards but neglected her own.
- **Perfume/Myrrh (נֵרְד, מֹר):** Expresses the beloved's preciousness and closeness.
- **Pharaoh's Mare (סוּסָה):** The man compares the woman's beauty to royal horses—an image of splendor and dignity.
- **Dark and Beautiful (שְׁחוֹרָה וְנָאוָה):** The woman's self-description that unites humility with pride.
