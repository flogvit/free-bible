**Main Theme:** Joseph meets his brothers again and tests them without revealing his identity.

**Content:** A famine drives ten of Jacob's sons to Egypt to buy grain, while Benjamin stays at home. Joseph, now ruler over Egypt, recognizes his brothers but pretends not to know them and accuses them of spying. He holds Simeon as a hostage and demands that they bring Benjamin back as proof of their innocence. The brothers confess among themselves that this is punishment for what they did to Joseph, while Joseph listens and weeps. When the sons return with grain and the mysteriously returned money, Jacob refuses to send Benjamin.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Famine** – The crisis that brings the family together again; the brothers bow before Joseph, thus fulfilling his childhood dreams (v. 6, 9).
- **Recognition** – Joseph recognizes his brothers, but they do not recognize him—a dramatic turning point.
- **Guilt and remorse** – The brothers interpret their misfortune as punishment for their sin against Joseph (v. 21–22).
- **Simeon as a hostage** – The guarantee meant to ensure that the brothers return with Benjamin.
- **The money in the sacks** – A disturbing sign that intensifies the brothers' fear of God's judgment.
