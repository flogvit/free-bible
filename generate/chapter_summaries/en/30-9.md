**Main Theme:** God's inevitable judgment upon Israel is followed by promises of restoration and eternal blessing.

**Content:** Amos sees the Lord standing beside the altar, ready to strike Israel with a judgment from which no one can escape—not in Sheol, heaven, Carmel, or even at the bottom of the sea. God reminds Israel that they hold no special status above other nations He has led. Nevertheless, He promises not to completely destroy the house of Jacob but to sift them like grain in a sieve. The chapter concludes with promises to rebuild David's fallen tent, abundance in the land, and that Israel will be securely planted in their land forever.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The Altar (v.1):** The place where God reveals His judgment; it symbolizes that even the sanctuary offers no protection from punishment.
- **David's Fallen Tent (v.11):** A promise of restoring David's dynasty and Israel's greatness, interpreted messianically in the New Testament.
- **The Sieve (v.9):** An image of God's refining judgment that separates sinners from the faithful.
- **Abundance in the Land (vv.13–14):** Harvesting and sowing occurring together—an image of eschatological blessing and fruitfulness.
