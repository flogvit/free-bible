**Main Theme:** Jesus reveals his identity as the Messiah and calls people to follow him through suffering.

**Content:** Jesus feeds 4,000 people with seven loaves and a few small fish, and rejects the Pharisees' demand for a sign. He warns the disciples against the yeast of the Pharisees and Herod, and heals a blind man in Bethsaida gradually. At Caesarea Philippi, Peter confesses that Jesus is the Messiah, but Jesus predicts his suffering, death, and resurrection—and rebukes Peter for trying to prevent him. Jesus teaches that true discipleship requires self-denial and taking up one's cross.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Feeding of the 4,000:** Jesus demonstrates his care and divine power by feeding the crowd with seven loaves.
- **Yeast:** A symbol of the false teaching of the Pharisees and Herod that the disciples must guard against.
- **The Blind Man in Bethsaida:** The gradual healing reflects the disciples' slow journey to understanding who Jesus is.
- **Peter's Confession:** The turning point where Jesus is acknowledged as the Messiah (Χριστός).
- **Taking Up the Cross:** The call to self-denial and willingness to suffer for Jesus and the gospel.
