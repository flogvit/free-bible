**Main Theme:** The chapter provides regulations for the sin offering (חַטָּאת) that makes atonement for sins committed unintentionally.

**Content:** God gives Moses instructions regarding the sin offering for four categories: the anointed priest, the entire congregation of Israel, a leader (נָשִׂיא), and a common Israelite. The priest and the congregation are to offer a young bull, whose blood is sprinkled before the veil and applied to the altar of incense, while the remainder is burned outside the camp. The leader offers a male goat, and the common Israelite offers a female goat or a female lamb. For all cases, the procedures include laying on of hands, slaughter, blood rituals at the altar of burnt offering, and burning of the fat. The priest makes atonement, and the sinner receives forgiveness.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Sin in unintentionality (בִשְׁגָגָה):** Unintentional violations of God's commands that still require atonement.
- **Laying on of hands:** The sinner identifies with the sacrificial animal by placing a hand on its head.
- **Blood rituals:** The blood carries atoning power and is applied to the altar and/or sprinkled before the veil.
- **Atonement and forgiveness (כִפֶּר/וְנִסְלַח):** The priest performs atonement, and God grants forgiveness for the sin.
