**Main Theme:** Israel's sin creates separation from God, but the Lord Himself intervenes as Savior and Redeemer.

**Content:** The chapter begins by affirming that God's arm is not too short to save, but the people's sins and lies have created a barrier between them and God. Righteousness, truth, and justice are lacking in society—evil and violence prevail. When God sees that no one steps forward, He intervenes Himself as a warrior, with righteousness as His armor and salvation as His helmet. A Redeemer will come to Zion, and God establishes an eternal covenant: His Spirit and word shall never depart from the people.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **God's arm (v.1, 16):** Symbol of God's saving power—it is not too short, but sin hinders its effect.
- **Darkness and blindness (vv.9–10):** Images of the consequences of sin—the people grope like the blind and walk in darkness.
- **Warrior's armor (v.17):** God puts on righteousness as a breastplate, salvation as a helmet, and zeal as a cloak.
- **The Redeemer (v.20):** One who comes to Zion to save those who turn from sin.
- **The eternal covenant (v.21):** God's promise that His Spirit and word will remain with the people forever.
