**Main Theme:** God commands Moses to count the able-bodied men of Israel and gives the Levites a special service at the tabernacle.

**Content:** In the wilderness of Sinai, in the Tent of Meeting, on the first day of the second month of the second year after the exodus from Egypt, God commands Moses and Aaron to count all men twenty years old and older who are able to go out to war. A leader from each tribe helps with the counting, and a total of 603,550 able-bodied men are counted. The Levites are excluded from this count because they are given responsibility for the tabernacle—they are to carry it, camp around it, and guard it so that God’s wrath does not fall on the people.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Census (פָּקַד):** Mustering the people was both a military preparation and a confirmation of God’s promises regarding a numerous people.
- **Twenty years old and older:** The age limit for those who were able-bodied and could go out to war.
- **The special status of the Levites:** They were not counted with the other tribes but were given responsibility to serve at and protect the tabernacle.
- **Tabernacle (מִשְׁכַּן הָעֵדֻת):** God’s dwelling place in the midst of the camp, the center of Israel’s religious life.
