**Main Theme:** Ruth’s faithful choice to follow her mother-in-law Naomi and Naomi’s God, despite loss and uncertainty.

**Content:** Due to a famine during the time of the judges, Elimelek, Naomi, and their two sons leave Bethlehem and settle in Moab. There Elimelek dies, and the sons marry Moabite women, Orpah and Ruth. But after ten years, the sons also die. When Naomi hears that the Lord has given His people bread again, she decides to return home. While Orpah stays in Moab, Ruth makes her famous pledge: "Your people shall be my people, and your God my God." They arrive in Bethlehem at the beginning of the barley harvest, and Naomi asks to be called Mara because the Lord has made her life bitter.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Famine/Bread:** The famine drives the family from the "House of Bread" (Bethlehem), but the Lord’s care brings them back.
- **To Return (שׁוּב):** A key word that is repeated—both a physical journey home and a spiritual turning back to the Lord’s people.
- **Hesed (Faithful Love):** Naomi blesses her daughters-in-law, asking that the Lord show them hesed as they showed kindness to the dead and to her.
- **Mara (Bitterness):** Naomi’s new name expresses her pain and sense of God’s judgment.
- **Ruth’s Covenant Pledge:** Ruth’s words mark a radical choice of belonging to Israel’s God and people.
