**Main Theme:** God rejects empty religiosity and demands genuine justice and mercy.

**Content:** A delegation from Bethel asks whether they should continue to fast in the fifth month, as they have done for seventy years. God answers through Zechariah that their fasting was never truly directed toward Him, and challenges them to practice true justice: righteous judgments, and mercy toward widows, fatherless children, foreigners, and the poor. The chapter warns by reminding them that their ancestors refused to listen to the earlier prophets, and therefore God’s wrath came upon them, resulting in their scattering among the nations.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Fasting (צום):** The people's religious practice which God rejects because it was self-centered rather than directed toward Him.
- **Justice and mercy (משפט אמת, חסד ורחמים):** God’s requirement for genuine piety—righteous judgments and care for vulnerable groups.
- **Stubborn disobedience (כתף סררת):** A picture of the ancestors’ resistance to God’s word; they turned their backs and hardened their hearts like flint.
- **The desirable land made desolate (ארץ חמדה לשמה):** The consequence of disobedience—the land was destroyed and abandoned.
