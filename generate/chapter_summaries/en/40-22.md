**Main Theme:** Jesus encounters resistance from religious leaders through parables and debates that reveal his divine wisdom and authority.

**Content:** Jesus tells the parable of the king's son's wedding feast, where those invited refuse to come and new guests are brought in from the roads—but one is cast out because he lacks wedding clothes. The Pharisees and Herodians try to trap Jesus with a question about paying taxes to Caesar, but he answers: "Give back to Caesar what belongs to Caesar, and to God what belongs to God." The Sadducees challenge him regarding the resurrection, and Jesus responds that those who are raised will be like angels and that God is the God of the living. A lawyer asks about the greatest commandment, and Jesus answers with love for God and love for neighbor. Finally, Jesus silences the Pharisees with his question about how the Messiah can be both David's son and David's Lord.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The Wedding Feast:** The kingdom of God portrayed as a royal celebration—the invitation goes out to all, but requires the right response.
- **Caesar's Coin:** Illustrates the distinction between earthly and divine obligations.
- **The Double Commandment of Love:** Loving God and loving neighbor summarize the entire Law and the Prophets.
- **David's Lord:** Jesus hints at his identity as more than merely a descendant of David—he is the Messiah with divine authority.
