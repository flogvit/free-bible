**Main Theme:** The Preacher examines whether pleasure, wealth, and wisdom can give life lasting meaning, but concludes that everything is vanity.

**Content:** The Preacher tests life's joys through projects, wealth, pleasures, and wisdom, yet finds that nothing provides lasting satisfaction. Although wisdom has an advantage over folly like light over darkness, both the wise and the foolish share the same fate—death. Frustration is intensified by the fact that one must leave one's life's work to a successor who may be unwise. The conclusion is that the best a person can do is to find joy in their labor, for this joy is a gift from God.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Hevel (הֶבֶל):** "Vanity" or "breath"—all human striving is fleeting and without lasting value.
- **Under the sun:** An expression that limits the perspective to earthly life and its limitations.
- **Wisdom vs. Folly:** Wisdom has a relative advantage (like light over darkness), but it does not save from death.
- **God's gift:** The ability to find joy in food, drink, and work comes from God's hand.
