**Main Theme:** Paul warns against idolatry by using Israel's history as an example and provides guidance on Christian freedom within the community.

**Content:** Paul points out that all the Israelites were baptized into Moses, received spiritual food and drink, yet still fell into sin and were punished—these serve as warnings for us. He urges the Corinthians to flee from idolatry and explains that the cup of thanksgiving and the bread at the Lord's Supper create fellowship with Christ, which is incompatible with fellowship with demons. Everything is permissible, but not everything builds up; therefore, believers should seek the good of others, be sensitive to conscience, and do everything for God's glory.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The Rock that was Christ (v.4):** The spiritual rock that accompanied the Israelites in the wilderness is identified as Christ—he was the source of their life.
- **Fellowship/κοινωνία (vv.16–20):** The Lord's Supper creates fellowship with Christ; sacrifices to idols create fellowship with demons—one cannot have both.
- **Temptation and a way out (v.13):** God is faithful and will not let believers be tempted beyond what they can bear, but always provides a way of escape.
- **Everything for God's glory (v.31):** The overarching principle for all actions—whether eating, drinking, or doing anything else.
