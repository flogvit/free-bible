**Main Theme:** Boaz acts as the kinsman-redeemer for Ruth, and their son Obed becomes an ancestor of King David.

**Summary:** At the city gate, Boaz settles the matter of redemption with a closer relative, who relinquishes his right by removing his sandal. Boaz purchases Elimelech’s property and takes Ruth as his wife to preserve the name of the deceased. The people and the elders bless the marriage, referencing Rachel, Leah, and Perez. Ruth gives birth to a son named Obed, whom the women praise as a redeemer for Naomi; he becomes David’s grandfather.

**Key Terms/Images:**
- **Kinsman-redeemer (גֹּאֵל):** The relative who has both the right and the duty to buy back property and continue the family line.
- **City gate:** The public place where legal agreements were made with elders as witnesses.
- **Sandal:** A symbol of the transfer of rights in Israel—the nearer kinsman-redeemer removes his sandal as confirmation.
- **Obed:** Ruth’s son, who becomes Jesse’s father and David’s grandfather—the link between the Moabite woman Ruth and the royal house of Israel.
