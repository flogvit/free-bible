**Main Theme:** Lamentations 2 portrays the destruction of Jerusalem as a result of the Lord's wrath against His own people.

**Content:** The chapter describes how God Himself has acted as an enemy toward Jerusalem and Judah—he has torn down walls, destroyed the temple, and rejected both king and priest. The people suffer under such extreme famine that mothers eat their own children, and the prophets have failed by proclaiming false messages instead of exposing sin. The poet urges personified Zion to cry out to the Lord and pour out tears day and night.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The Lord as Enemy (vv. 4–5):** God is described as one who has drawn His bow against His own people and destroyed them like an adversary.
- **False Prophets (v. 14):** The prophets are criticized for seeing only emptiness and deception, failing to reveal the people's sin.
- **Zion's Tears (vv. 18–19):** Jerusalem is personified and urged to weep without ceasing and pray to the Lord.
- **Total Destruction (vv. 21–22):** Young and old lie dead in the streets, and no one escaped the day of the Lord's wrath.
