**Main Theme:** God announces his inevitable judgment over Jerusalem through the sword, using Babylon as his instrument.

**Content:** The chapter opens with a prophecy about fire that will consume the forest in the south—a picture of judgment against Jerusalem. God commands Ezekiel to sigh and lament, for his sword will destroy both the righteous and the wicked. The king of Babylon stands at the crossroads and uses divination to choose his path—the lot falls on Jerusalem. The prince of Israel shall lose his crown until "he comes who has the right to it." The people of Ammon will also be struck by God's judgment.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The Sword (חֶרֶב):** God's instrument of judgment, sharpened and polished for slaughter, placed in the hands of a killer.
- **The Two Ways:** The king of Babylon chooses between Jerusalem and Rabbah through divination—the lot falls on Jerusalem.
- **The Crown and Turban:** Symbols of royal authority that will be removed from Israel's wicked prince.
- **"Until he comes who has the right":** A messianic prophecy about a future ruler who will restore righteous rule.
