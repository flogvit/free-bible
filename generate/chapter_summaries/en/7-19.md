**Main Theme:** The chapter illustrates the moral decay in Israel during the period of the judges through a horrific crime in Gibeah.

**Content:** A Levite from the hill country of Ephraim retrieves his concubine from Bethlehem after she had left him, and they stay with her father for several days. On their way home, they choose to spend the night in Gibeah in Benjamin rather than in the foreign city of Jebus. An old man takes them in, but during the night wicked men surround the house and demand that the Levite be handed over. Instead, his concubine is given to them and abused all night until she dies. The Levite then cuts her body into twelve pieces and sends them throughout Israel to stir the people to action.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **No king in Israel** – This phrase opens the chapter and explains the moral anarchy that prevailed.
- **Gibeah** – The city in Benjamin where the atrocity occurred, paralleling the account of Sodom.
- **Sons of Belial** – A designation for the wicked men, showing their total moral collapse.
- **The twelve pieces** – The Levite's drastic action to call Israel to confront sin.
