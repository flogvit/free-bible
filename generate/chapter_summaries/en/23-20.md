**Main Theme:** A symbolic prophetic act warning against relying on Egypt and Cush for protection against Assyria.

**Content:** When the commander of the Assyrian king Sargon's army, Tartan, captured Ashdod, the Lord commanded the prophet Isaiah to go naked and barefoot for three years as a sign. This symbolized how the king of Assyria would lead away Egyptians and Cushites as captives—naked, barefoot, and with their buttocks exposed in shame. The message warned the inhabitants of the coastal region that those who placed their trust in Egypt and Cush would be disappointed and ask themselves how they could possibly escape.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Naked and Barefoot:** Isaiah's condition for three years symbolized the humiliation and shame of war captives.
- **Sign and Symbol (אוֹת וּמוֹפֵת):** The prophetic act was a concrete, visual message from God about future events.
- **Egypt and Cush:** Great powers that Judah was tempted to ally with, but which themselves would fall to Assyria.
- **Inhabitants of the Coastal Region:** Those who had placed their hope in human alliances rather than in God, and who now stood without deliverance.
