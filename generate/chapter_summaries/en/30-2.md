**Main Theme:** God proclaims judgment against Moab, Judah, and Israel for their repeated transgressions and rejection of His kindness.

**Content:** The chapter continues Amos's words of judgment, now directed at Moab (for burning the bones of Edom’s king), Judah (for rejecting the Lord’s law), and Israel. Israel receives the most comprehensive accusation: they sell the righteous for silver, oppress the poor, profane God’s name through sexual sin, and misuse pledged garments and fines at the altar. God reminds them of His kindnesses—he destroyed the Amorites, brought them out of Egypt, and raised up prophets and Nazirites—but Israel gave wine to the Nazirites and forbade the prophets from speaking. Judgment is unavoidable: no one can escape, neither the swift, nor the strong, nor the brave.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **"For three transgressions... for four"**: A poetic formula expressing that sin has reached its limit and judgment is inevitable.
- **The Amorites as cedar trees**: An image of the mighty peoples God destroyed on Israel’s behalf, demonstrating His power and care.
- **A wagon full of sheaves**: An image of how God will press the people down under the weight of judgment.
- **Nazirites and prophets**: Representing God’s spiritual gifts to the people, which they rejected and oppressed.
