**Main Theme:** Peter writes to persecuted Christians about the living hope and the call to holy living, grounded in Christ's resurrection and redemption through his blood.

**Content:** Peter greets the chosen exiles scattered abroad and praises God for being born again into a living hope and an imperishable inheritance kept in heaven. Trials refine faith, which is more precious than gold, and lead to salvation—something prophets searched into and angels long to see. Believers are urged to live holy lives, since they have been redeemed from empty ways of life not with silver or gold, but with the precious blood of Christ, like a lamb without blemish, and to love one another deeply as those born again through God's living word.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Living hope (ἐλπὶς ζῶσα):** The hope that springs from Christ's resurrection and gives believers assurance amid suffering.
- **Imperishable inheritance (κληρονομία ἄφθαρτος):** Salvation described as an eternal inheritance kept in heaven for believers.
- **Proof of faith (δοκίμιον τῆς πίστεως):** Trials are compared to gold refined by fire—they purify and demonstrate the genuineness of faith.
- **Lamb without blemish (ἀμνὸς ἄμωμος):** Christ is presented as the perfect sacrificial lamb whose blood redeems believers.
- **God's living word (λόγος ζῶν θεοῦ):** The word that endures forever and through which believers are born again, in contrast to all that is perishable.
