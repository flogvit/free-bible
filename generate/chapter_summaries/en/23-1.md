**Main Theme:** God accuses Judah of apostasy and empty rituals, but offers cleansing and restoration through repentance.

**Content:** The prophet Isaiah delivers God's message to Judah and Jerusalem in the eighth century B.C.E. The people have rebelled against God—they understand less than an ox or a donkey that knows its master. God rejects their sacrifices and festivals because they are full of injustice and bloodguilt. He calls them to repentance with the promise that their sins can become white as snow, but warns of judgment upon those who continue in rebellion. The chapter concludes with a promise of cleansing and restoration of Zion as "the city of righteousness."

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The ox and the donkey (v.3):** Even animals know their owner, but Israel does not understand its God—an image of the people's spiritual blindness.
- **Scarlet and snow (v.18):** God's offer of forgiveness—even the deepest sins can be washed clean.
- **The harlot Zion (v.21):** Jerusalem, once faithful, has become like an unfaithful woman—filled with injustice and murder.
- **Dross and pure silver (vv.22–25):** God's judgment as a cleansing process—the impurity must be removed so that only the genuine remains.
