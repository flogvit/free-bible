**Main Theme:** God gives Israel laws concerning clean and unclean animals to set them apart as a holy people.

**Content:** The chapter classifies which animals the Israelites may eat: land animals must have completely split hooves and chew the cud, water creatures must have fins and scales, and certain birds and locusts are permitted. Touching the carcass of an unclean animal makes a person ritually unclean until evening and requires washing of clothes. The rationale is theological: God is holy and brought Israel out of Egypt; therefore, they too must be holy and distinguish between clean and unclean.

**Key Terms/Images:**
- **Clean/Unclean (טָהוֹר/טָמֵא):** Ritual categories that determine what may be eaten or touched.
- **Holiness (קָדוֹשׁ):** God's attribute, which Israel is called to reflect through obedience to the food laws.
- **To Separate (לְהַבְדִּיל):** The purpose of the chapter—to teach Israel to distinguish between clean and unclean.
- **Detestable (שֶׁקֶץ):** A term for unclean animals that should evoke revulsion and be completely avoided.
