**Main Theme:** Job complains that God does not hold court sessions and allows the wicked to go unpunished.

**Content:** Job asks why God does not set fixed times for judgment so that those who know Him may see justice done. He describes in detail how the wicked move boundary stones, exploit orphans and widows, and force the poor to live like wild animals. Furthermore, he portrays "the rebels of darkness"—murderers, thieves, and adulterers who operate in the dark and fear the light of day. Job concludes by challenging anyone to disprove his claims.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Court sessions (עִתִּים):** Job longs for fixed times when God visibly judges the wicked.
- **Rebels of darkness:** Those who hate the light and commit their sins in the cover of night (murder, theft, adultery).
- **Wild donkey in the wilderness:** A picture of the poor who must search for food in the wasteland to survive.
- **Shadow of death (צַלְמָוֶת):** For the wicked, morning is like the shadow of death—they fear the light.
