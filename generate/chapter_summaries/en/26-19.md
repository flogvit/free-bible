**Main Theme:** A lament over the princes of Israel and the fall of the nation.

**Content:** The chapter consists of two poetic images. First, Israel is compared to a lioness who raised two cubs (kings), but both were captured and taken away—the first to Egypt, the second to Babylon. Then Israel is described as a lush vine with strong branches (rulers), which was uprooted in anger and planted in the wilderness. The fire that consumed it came from its own branch—the destruction was self-inflicted.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Lioness and cubs:** Represent Judah and its kings; the captured cubs symbolize the kings Jehoahaz (to Egypt) and Jehoiachin/Zedekiah (to Babylon).
- **Vine:** A common image for Israel; here it shows the nation's former greatness and current destruction.
- **Lament (qinah):** A special genre of mourning poetry; the chapter begins and ends with this word, emphasizing the tragedy.
- **Fire from its own branch:** Symbolizes that the destruction came from within through poor leadership, not merely from outside forces.
