**Main Theme:** Detailed instructions for the construction of the tabernacle, God’s dwelling place among his people.

**Content:** The chapter gives precise directions for building the tabernacle: ten curtains of fine linen with cherubim are to form the inner structure, covered by eleven goat-hair curtains and additional layers of red-dyed ram skins and taḥash skins on top. Frames of acacia wood overlaid with gold, set on silver bases, are to be held together by crossbars. A curtain (פָּרֹכֶת) is to separate the Holy Place from the Most Holy Place, and a screen (מָסָךְ) at the entrance is to be supported by five pillars on bronze bases.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The Tabernacle (הַמִּשְׁכָּן):** God’s "dwelling place" – a portable sanctuary tent that symbolizes God’s presence in the midst of his people.
- **The Curtain (פָּרֹכֶת):** The dividing veil marking the boundary between the Holy Place and the Most Holy Place, where only the high priest could enter once a year.
- **Cherubim (כְּרֻבִים):** Heavenly beings embroidered on the curtains, testifying to God’s holy presence and throne room.
- **Gold, Silver, and Bronze:** The value of these materials reflects the degree of holiness – gold in the innermost part at the Most Holy Place, bronze at the outermost point near the entrance.
