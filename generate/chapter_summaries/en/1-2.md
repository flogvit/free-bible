# Genesis 2 – Summary

**Main Theme:** God completes the work of creation with Sabbath rest and provides a detailed account of humanity’s creation and the origin of marriage in the Garden of Eden.

**Content:** The chapter begins with God resting on the seventh day and sanctifying it after finishing His creative work (vv. 1–3). This is followed by a more detailed creation narrative in which God forms man from the dust of the ground and breathes into his nostrils the breath of life (vv. 4–7). God plants the Garden of Eden with the tree of life and the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, placing man there to work it and keep it—with a command not to eat from the tree of the knowledge of good and evil (vv. 8–17). Since it is not good for man to be alone, God forms the animals and brings them to Adam to name, but none prove to be a suitable helper (vv. 18–20). God then creates woman from the man’s rib, and Adam receives her with joy—the foundation of marriage is laid (vv. 21–25).

**Key Words/Images:**

- **Sabbath (שָׁבַת):** The seventh day that God blesses and sanctifies, establishing rest as a holy institution (vv. 2–3).

- **Dust and Breath of Life (עָפָר / נִשְׁמַת חַיִּים):** Humanity’s dual nature—formed from the earth yet made alive by God’s breath—reveals both vulnerability and dignity (v. 7).

- **Garden of Eden (גַּן־עֵדֶן):** The paradisiacal place where God positions humanity, symbolizing perfect harmony between God, humankind, and nature (vv. 8–15).

- **Tree of the Knowledge of Good and Evil (עֵץ הַדַּעַת טוֹב וָרָע):** The tree representing human limits and freedom—the command shows that relationship with God requires trust and obedience (vv. 9, 17).

- **"One Flesh" (בָשָׂר אֶחָד):** An expression of the deep unity of marriage, where man and woman leave their parents and become one inseparable union (v. 24).
