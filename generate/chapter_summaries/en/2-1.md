**Main Theme:** Israel's growth in Egypt and Pharaoh's attempt to oppress and exterminate the people.

**Content:** Jacob's descendants (70 souls) grew into a mighty nation in Egypt after Joseph's death. A new pharaoh, who did not know Joseph, feared the strength of the Israelites and forced them into hard labor building the store cities of Pithom and Ramses. When this failed to stop their growth, he ordered the Hebrew midwives, Shiphrah and Puah, to kill all male infants – but they feared God more than Pharaoh and let the boys live. God blessed the midwives, and finally Pharaoh commanded that every newborn boy be thrown into the Nile.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Fertility and Growth:** The Israelites "were fruitful, multiplied greatly, became numerous and very powerful" – fulfilling God's promise to Abraham.
- **Hard Labor (פָּרֶךְ):** Brutal oppression with mortar, bricks, and fieldwork – a picture of the harshness of slavery.
- **The Midwives' Fear of God:** Shiphrah and Puah feared God more than Pharaoh – civil disobedience grounded in faithfulness to God.
- **The Nile:** From a life-giving river to an instrument of death – Pharaoh's final decree to drown the male infants.
