**Main Theme:** The chapter gives exhortations about self-control, wisdom in dealing with others, and warnings against temptations such as wealth, gluttony, and immorality.

**Content:** The chapter opens with warnings to be cautious at the ruler's table and not to chase after riches that can quickly vanish. It encourages protecting the rights of orphans and disciplining children to guide them rightly. The danger of drunkenness is vividly described, with its seductive beginning and bitter consequences. Throughout the chapter, the son is urged to bring joy to his parents by living uprightly and staying away from the traps of the prostitute and wine.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Wealth that flies away (v.5):** Wealth is compared to an eagle that suddenly spreads its wings and flies toward heaven – a warning against exhausting oneself for material goods.
- **The rod of discipline (v.13-14):** Physical discipline is presented as a means to save the child's soul from death.
- **The poison of wine (v.31-32):** Wine looks inviting in the cup, but ends up like a snake that bites – an image of sin's seduction.
- **The deep pit (v.27):** The prostitute is described as a deep hole and a narrow well that traps its victims.
