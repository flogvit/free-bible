**Main Theme:** The rivalry between Rachel and Leah over bearing children for Jacob, and Jacob’s agreement with Laban regarding wages.

**Content:** Rachel is jealous of Leah because she is childless, so she gives her maidservant Bilhah to Jacob; she bears Dan and Naphtali. Leah responds by giving her maidservant Zilpah, who bears Gad and Asher. Leah herself has more children: Issachar, Zebulun, and a daughter named Dinah. God hears Rachel’s prayer, and she gives birth to Joseph. Jacob makes an agreement with his father-in-law Laban to receive the speckled and dark-colored animals as wages, and through wise breeding methods, Jacob becomes very wealthy.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Dudaim (love apples):** Plants that Reuben finds, which Rachel trades for a night with Jacob—symbolizing a longing for fertility.
- **Maidservants as surrogate mothers:** Bilhah and Zilpah bear children "on the knees" of their mistresses, a custom to secure heirs.
- **Speckled and striped animals:** Jacob’s wages from Laban, which he increases through breeding methods using peeled branches.
- **Joseph ("he adds"):** Rachel’s firstborn son, whose name expresses her prayer for more children.
