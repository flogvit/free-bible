**Main Theme:** The chapter gives instructions for the burnt offering (olah), which is brought voluntarily to the Lord as a pleasing aroma.

**Content:** The Lord speaks to Moses from the tent of meeting and gives rules for the burnt offering. The sacrificial animal may be cattle, sheep or goats (a lamb or a kid), or birds (a turtledove or a young pigeon). Cattle and sheep must be unblemished males. The offerer lays his hand on the animal's head for atonement; then it is slaughtered, its blood is dashed around on the altar, and the priest burns the entire offering as a pleasing aroma to the Lord.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Burnt Offering (olah):** An offering that is burned completely on the altar, symbolizing total surrender to God.
- **Unblemished (tamim):** The sacrificial animal must be without defect, expressing that the best is given to God.
- **Laying on of Hands (samakh):** The offerer lays his hand on the animal's head, connecting the person with the offering and atonement.
- **Pleasing Aroma (rei'ach nichoach):** The burnt offering rises as a fragrant scent to the Lord, a sign of God's acceptance.
