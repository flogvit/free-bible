**Main Theme:** Jesus reveals his messianic identity and invites the weary to rest, while warning against unbelief.

**Content:** John the Baptist sends his disciples from prison to ask if Jesus is the Messiah, and Jesus answers by pointing to his miracles. Jesus praises John as the greatest among humans but criticizes the generation for rejecting both John's asceticism and Jesus' fellowship with sinners. He pronounces woes upon the cities of Chorazin, Bethsaida, and Capernaum for their lack of repentance, before thanking the Father for revealing truth to the humble. The chapter concludes with Jesus' invitation to all who are burdened to find rest in him.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The Coming One (ὁ ἐρχόμενος):** A messianic title that John asks about – Jesus confirms his identity through his deeds.
- **Greater than a prophet:** John is Elijah who was to come, the forerunner of the Messiah (cf. Mal 3:1).
- **The Woes:** Prophetic declarations of judgment against cities that saw miracles but did not repent.
- **Yoke (ζυγός):** A metaphor for discipleship and teaching – Jesus' yoke is easy, in contrast to slavery under the law.
