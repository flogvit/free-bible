**Main Theme:** God’s rules for warfare reveal both his care for his people and his judgment against the sin of the Canaanites.

**Content:** The chapter opens with the priest encouraging the army not to fear, because the Lord himself fights for them. Then certain men are exempted from military service: those who have built a new house, planted a vineyard, become engaged, or are afraid. For distant cities, Israel is first to offer peace—if the city accepts, its people become forced laborers; if they refuse, the men are killed and the rest taken as plunder. The Canaanite peoples, however, must be completely devoted to destruction to prevent Israel from learning their idolatry. During a siege, fruit trees are to be spared.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **"The Lord your God is with you"** – The foundation of Israel’s courage in war is God’s presence, not military strength.
- **Exemption from warfare** – Shows God’s care for life’s milestones (home, harvest, marriage) and human vulnerability.
- **Devotion to destruction (חֵרֶם)** – Total destruction of the Canaanites as God’s judgment and protection against idolatry.
- **Fruit trees spared** – Even in war, stewardship applies; destruction has limits.
