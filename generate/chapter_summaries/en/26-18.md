**Main Theme:** God establishes the principle of individual responsibility—each person will be judged according to their own deeds, not those of their parents.

**Content:** Ezekiel rejects the proverb that children suffer for their fathers' sins and declares: "The one who sins is the one who will die." Through examples spanning three generations, God shows that righteousness and wickedness are not inherited—each generation is responsible for itself. God emphasizes that repentance is always possible: the wicked who turn from sin will live, while the righteous who turn away will die. When Israel accuses God of being unfair, He responds that it is their own ways that are unjust. The chapter concludes with a heartfelt appeal to repent, for God does not desire anyone's death.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **"Sour grapes" proverb (v. 2):** A popular saying falsely claiming that children are punished for their parents' sin—this is firmly rejected.
- **"The one who sins will die" (vv. 4, 20):** The foundational principle of individual responsibility, repeated as the chapter's central message.
- **Three generations (vv. 5–18):** A righteous father, a wicked son, and a righteous grandson—illustrating that each generation is judged on its own merits.
- **Repentance (vv. 21–23, 27–28):** The opportunity to turn from sin and live—God does not hold past transgressions against those who repent.
- **"I do not want anyone to die" (v. 32):** God's heart toward people—He desires repentance and life, not death.
