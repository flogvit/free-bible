**Main Theme:** The chapter describes the Nazirite vow and the Aaronic blessing—two ways of being consecrated to the Lord.

**Content:** The Nazirite vow is a voluntary commitment in which a person dedicates themselves to God for a specific period. Those who take this vow must abstain from wine and anything produced by the grapevine, let their hair grow uncut, and avoid contact with dead bodies—even close family members. If defiled, the Nazirite must undergo purification and begin the vow anew. When the vow period ends, the Nazirite is to bring specific offerings and shave their head. The chapter concludes with the Aaronic blessing that priests are to pronounce over the people of Israel.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Nazirite** – A person who has made a voluntary vow to dedicate themselves to the Lord for a period of time.
- **Hair** – Symbolizes consecration; it must not be cut during the vow and is burned on the altar at its conclusion.
- **The Aaronic Blessing** – The threefold blessing God gives priests to pronounce: blessing and keeping, grace and light, peace.
- **Holiness** – The Nazirite is "holy to the Lord" throughout the entire vow period and must avoid all defilement.
