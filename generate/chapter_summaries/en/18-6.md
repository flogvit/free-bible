**Main Theme:** Job defends his complaint against his friends and accuses them of failing him in his suffering.

**Content:** Job claims that his suffering is heavier than the sand of the sea and that God's arrows have pierced him. He wishes that God would crush him and give him death as deliverance, since he has not denied the words of the Holy One. Job accuses his friends of being like dried-up streams—full of water in winter but empty when travelers need them most. He challenges them to show him where he has erred, instead of treating his desperate words as mere wind.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **God's arrows (v.4):** A picture of how Job experiences suffering as a direct attack from God, where the poison penetrates his spirit.
- **Dried-up streams/wadis (vv.15–20):** The friends are compared to seasonal streams that fail when needed—a powerful image of false friends.
- **The sand of the sea (v.3):** An expression of the overwhelming weight of suffering, explaining why Job's words seem wild.
- **The words of the Holy One (v.10):** Job asserts his integrity by stating that he has never denied God's words, even in suffering.
