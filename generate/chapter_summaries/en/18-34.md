**Main Theme:** Elihu defends God's justice and accuses Job of speaking unjustly against God.

**Content:** Elihu quotes Job's claims that he is righteous while God has denied him justice (vv. 5–6), and sharply rejects this. He argues that God never acts unjustly—he repays people according to their deeds and holds the life of all creation in his hand (vv. 11–15). Elihu emphasizes that God sees all human ways and that no one can hide from him (vv. 21–22). The chapter concludes with Elihu wishing for Job to be tested to the utmost, because he adds rebellion to his sin by speaking against God (vv. 36–37).

**Key Words/Images:**
- **God's justice (vv. 10–12):** Elihu declares it unthinkable that God would act unjustly or pervert justice.
- **God's life-giving Spirit (vv. 14–15):** All life depends on God's Spirit; if he were to withdraw it, all flesh would perish.
- **God's omniscience (vv. 21–22):** No darkness or shadow of death can hide wicked deeds from God's eyes.
- **Job's rebellion (v. 37):** Elihu accuses Job of adding rebellion to his sin by speaking many words against God.
