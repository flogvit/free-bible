**Main Theme:** Paul preaches the gospel in Thessalonica, Berea, and Athens, encountering both opposition and response.

**Content:** Paul and Silas preach in Thessalonica and Berea, where the Bereans distinguish themselves by examining the Scriptures daily. After facing persecution, Paul travels to Athens, where he is deeply troubled by the city's idolatry. On the Areopagus, he delivers his famous speech, using the altar "To an unknown god" as a starting point to proclaim the true God as Creator and Judge. The response is mixed—some mock, while others believe, including Dionysius and Damaris.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The nobility of the Bereans:** They received the message with eagerness and examined the Scriptures daily to test whether it was true.
- **"To an unknown god":** The altar in Athens that Paul used as a rhetorical starting point to proclaim the true God.
- **The Areopagus speech:** Paul's contextualized preaching, where he quotes Greek poets and appeals to creation.
- **The resurrection:** The central point of Paul's preaching, which sparked both mockery and faith.
