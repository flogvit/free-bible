**Main Theme:** Lamentations 1 is a poetic lament over the fall of Jerusalem, where the city is personified as an abandoned widow mourning the Lord's righteous judgment.

**Content:** The chapter is an acrostic poem depicting Jerusalem's destruction and Judah's exile. The city, once mighty among the nations, is now forsaken and without comfort. Jerusalem acknowledges that its suffering results from its own sins and rebellion against the Lord, and that His judgment is just. From verse 12, the city speaks directly, crying out to the Lord to see her distress and punish her enemies.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Widow (אַלְמָנָה):** Jerusalem is compared to a widow—moving from honor and power to loneliness and grief.
- **No comforter (אֵין מְנַחֵם):** A recurring motif that emphasizes the city's total abandonment by both allies and God.
- **Sin and uncleanness (חֵטְא/טֻמְאָה):** Jerusalem's suffering is portrayed as a direct consequence of the people's rebellion against God.
- **The Lord's wrath (חֲרוֹן אַף):** God is not passive but has actively judged and punished the city for its transgressions.
