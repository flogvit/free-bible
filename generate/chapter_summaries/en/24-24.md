**Main Theme:** God reveals through a vision of two baskets of figs the different destinies of Judah's people—blessing for those taken into exile and judgment over those who remain.

**Content:** After King Jehoiachin and the leaders were taken to Babylon, God showed Jeremiah two baskets of figs in front of the temple. The good figs represent those taken into exile, whom God will protect, bring back, and give a heart to know Him. The bad figs represent King Zedekiah, those remaining in Jerusalem, and those living in Egypt—they will be struck by sword, famine, and pestilence and become an object of scorn among the nations.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **The Two Baskets of Figs:** A prophetic image distinguishing two groups in Judah with opposite destinies.
- **Good Figs:** Symbolizing those taken into exile whom God will bless, restore, and give a new heart.
- **Bad Figs:** Symbolizing those who remain and will face God's judgment through sword, famine, and pestilence.
- **The Exile to Babylon:** Paradoxically portrayed as the path to salvation and renewal for God's people.
