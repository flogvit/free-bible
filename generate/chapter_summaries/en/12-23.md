**Main Theme:** King Josiah's extensive religious reforms and covenant renewal, followed by his death and successors.

**Content:** Josiah gathers the people and renews the covenant with the Lord based on the rediscovered Book of the Law. He carries out radical reforms: removing idolatry from the temple, destroying the high places from Geba to Beer-sheba, and cleansing the land of foreign cults, including Topheth in the Valley of Hinnom. He celebrates Passover as it had not been celebrated since the days of the judges. Although no king turned to the Lord with all his heart and soul like Josiah did, God's anger against Judah remained because of Manasseh's sins. Josiah falls in battle against Pharaoh Neco at Megiddo, and his son Jehoahaz is deposed by the Egyptians, who install Jehoiakim as king.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Covenant Renewal:** The king and the people commit themselves to keep the Lord's commands with all their heart and all their soul.
- **The High Places (bamot):** Illegal worship sites that Josiah systematically destroys and desecrates using human bones.
- **Topheth:** The place in the Valley of Hinnom where children were sacrificed to Molech, which Josiah renders unclean.
- **Passover Celebration:** Restored according to the Book of the Law; the greatest since the days of the judges.
- **The Lord's Continuing Anger:** Despite the reforms, judgment against Judah remains fixed because of past sins.
