**Main Theme:** God’s judgment on King Belshazzar for pride and desecration of the temple vessels.

**Content:** King Belshazzar holds a feast and drinks wine from the sacred vessels taken from Jerusalem’s temple while praising lifeless idols. A human hand writes a mysterious inscription on the wall. When the wise men cannot interpret it, Daniel explains the writing: "Mene, Mene, Tekel, Parsin": God has numbered the kingdom, weighed the king and found him wanting, and the kingdom will be given to the Medes and Persians. That same night Belshazzar is killed.

**Key Words/Images:**
- **Temple Vessels:** The sacred vessels from Jerusalem are used for profane drinking, symbolizing contempt for the God of Israel.
- **The Hand on the Wall:** God’s direct intervention and judgment that cannot be ignored or avoided.
- **Mene, Mene, Tekel, Parsin:** Words of judgment meaning "numbered, weighed, divided"—God keeps account with rulers.
- **The Scale:** Belshazzar was weighed and found wanting—an image of God’s righteous evaluation of human lives.
