# Historical and Cultural Context for Psalm 111

## Literary Structure

Psalm 111 is an **acrostic psalm** in which each half-line begins with a new letter of the Hebrew alphabet, from aleph to tav. The psalm has 10 verses containing 22 half-lines that cover all 22 letters. This sophisticated literary form served both as artistic expression and as a memory aid in oral tradition. The alphabetical structure also symbolized completeness—God’s deeds are described "from A to Z."

## Events and Customs

The psalm refers to central events in salvation history without naming them directly. The phrase "he has made his wonders to be remembered" (v. 4) and "he gives food to those who fear him" (v. 5, Hebrew *ṭeref* – "prey" or "food") allude to the provision of manna in the wilderness and the Passover meal. "The inheritance of the nations" (v. 6) points to the fulfillment of the promise of land. The word *pedût* ("redemption," v. 9) was a technical term for the release of firstborns and slaves, here used to describe the exodus from Egypt.

## Key Terms

The phrase **"in the company of the upright and in the assembly"** (v. 1, Hebrew *sôd yešārîm wĕ-ʿēdāh*) describes two contexts for worship: the smaller circle of the godly (*sôd*) and the larger congregation (*ʿēdāh*). This reflects post-exilic worship life in which the synagogue emerged alongside temple worship.

The closing verse, "The fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom" (*rēʾšît ḥokmāh yirʾat YHWH*), is a central statement of wisdom literature. The word *yirʾāh* ("fear") does not denote terror, but reverence and respectful submission as the foundation for right living.
