# Historical and Cultural Context for Joshua 24

## Places

**Shechem** (שְׁכֶם) was one of the most significant religious and political centers in ancient Israel. The city lay in a strategic valley between Mount Ebal and Mount Gerizim, about 50 km north of Jerusalem. Shechem held deep patriarchal significance: here Abraham built his first altar in Canaan (Gen 12:6-7), here Jacob purchased land (Gen 33:18-19), and here Joseph’s bones were later buried (v. 32). Archaeological excavations at Tell Balata have uncovered a substantial Canaanite temple from the Middle Bronze Age and remains of city walls from various periods. The choice of Shechem for renewing the covenant connected the new generation to the faith heritage of the patriarchs and marked the site as a central gathering place for the tribes.

**Timnath-Serah** (תִּמְנַת־סֶרַח), where Joshua was buried (v. 30), is identified with Khirbet Tibneh, about 25 km southwest of Shechem in the hill country of Ephraim. According to tradition, the Israelites honored Joshua’s burial place "north of Mount Gaash".

## Events and Customs

Joshua’s assembly at Shechem represents a **covenant renewal ceremony** that follows a pattern known from Hittite vassal treaties of the second millennium BC. The structure includes: a historical prologue recounting God’s acts of kindness (vv. 2-13), a call for loyalty (v. 14), a choice between masters (v. 15), the people’s affirmation (vv. 16-18, 21, 24), and witnesses to the covenant (vv. 22, 26-27). This form was well known throughout the ancient Near East and gave the ceremony legal weight.

**The raising of a memorial stone** (vv. 26-27) under the oak tree at the sanctuary reflects a widespread ancient Near Eastern custom in which stones served as silent "witnesses" to covenant agreements. The stone was meant to remind future generations of their obligation. Such covenant stones are known from several cultures in the region.

## Key Terms

The expression **"beyond the river"** (בְּעֵבֶר הַנָּהָר, vv. 2, 14, 15) refers to Mesopotamia, the area east of the Euphrates, from which Israel’s ancestors originally came. This underscores that Abraham was called out from a background of idol worship.

The word **קְשִׂיטָה** (kesita, v. 32) is an ancient unit of weight for silver that appears only three times in the Bible. Its exact value is unknown, but the word likely derives from a Semitic root meaning "to weigh" and represents an archaic form of payment from patriarchal times.

**"Gods of foreign origin"** (אֱלֹהֵי הַנֵּכָר, v. 23) indicates that idol worship remained a problem among the Israelites, which aligns with findings of household gods and amulets in archaeological excavations from Israelite settlements.

## Chapter-Specific Findings

Joseph’s grave in Shechem (v. 32) is still honored today at a burial site near Tell Balata. Although the current structure dates from a much later period, it confirms the enduring tradition of Joseph’s burial at this location, linking the fulfillment of the promise in Gen 50:25 and Exod 13:19 to the land of Shechem.
