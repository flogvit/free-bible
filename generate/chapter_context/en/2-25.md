# Historical and Cultural Context for Exodus 25

## Events and Customs

The chapter contains God's instructions regarding the collection of materials and detailed construction guidelines for the ark of the covenant, the mercy seat with the cherubim, the table of showbread, and the menorah (the seven-branched lampstand). Voluntary offerings (*terumah*) for building a sanctuary were known in surrounding cultures, but the text emphasizes that these gifts should come from the heart (v. 2). What was radically different about Israel's sanctuary was its purpose: not to house an idol statue, but to serve as a meeting place between God and His people (vv. 8, 22).

## Key Terms

**Terumah** (תְּרוּמָה) – "heave offering" or "gift" – refers to something that is "lifted up" and set apart for holy use. The term implies voluntariness and devotion.

**Mishkan** (מִשְׁכָּן) – "dwelling" or "tabernacle" – comes from the verb "to dwell" (*shakan*). This underscores that God desired to dwell among His people (v. 8), not in inaccessible distance.

**Aron** (אֲרוֹן) – "ark" or "chest" – the ark of the covenant was to contain the "testimony" (*edut*), the tablets of the law (vv. 16, 21). Arks for sacred documents or idol statues were known in Egypt and Mesopotamia.

**Kapporet** (כַּפֹּרֶת) – traditionally translated as "mercy seat" – likely comes from the verb "to cover" or "to atone" (*kipper*). This was the lid of the ark where God met with Moses (v. 22).

**Kerubim** (כְּרֻבִים) – winged guardian creatures known throughout the ancient Near East. In Assyrian and Babylonian art, similar figures (*karabu*/*lamassu*) guarded temples and palaces. The Israelite cherubim were unique in that they were not objects of worship but guardians of God's presence.

**The materials in vv. 3-7**: *Tekhelet* (blue purple), *argaman* (red purple), and *tola'at shani* (scarlet) were costly dyes. *Orot techashim* (v. 5) is debated – possibly dolphin, manatee, or dugong skins, or another rare material. *Atzei shittim* (acacia wood) was available on the Sinai Peninsula and known for its durability.

## Chapter-Specific Findings

Archaeological discoveries from the Bronze Age provide context for the objects described in this chapter. In Tutankhamun's tomb (c. 1323 BC), gilded wooden chests with carrying poles and gold-plated furniture were found that resemble descriptions of the ark. Multi-branched lamps have been discovered in Canaanite contexts, although the seven-branched menorah design appears to be uniquely Israelite. The detailed description of almond-shaped buds (*meshukadim*) on the menorah (vv. 33-34) may hold symbolic significance, since "almond" (*shaked*) is linked in a Hebrew wordplay to "watching" or "being awake" (*shoqed*).
