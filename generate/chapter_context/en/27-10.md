## People

### Cyrus (Koresh) of Persia
The chapter is dated to "the third year of Cyrus king of Persia" (v. 1), that is, about 536–535 B.C. This is approximately three years after Cyrus issued the decree allowing the Jews to return to Jerusalem (538 B.C.). By this time, the first group under Zerubbabel had already returned and begun rebuilding the temple but faced opposition. Daniel’s three weeks of mourning and fasting may be connected to concern over these difficulties.

### Michael
Michael (מִיכָאֵל, "Who is like God?") is described as "one of the chief princes" (v. 13) and "their prince" (v. 21), that is, Israel’s heavenly protector. He comes to help the messenger in the struggle against "the prince of Persia."

### The Man Clothed in Linen (vv. 5–6)
Daniel sees a figure clothed in linen with a belt of pure gold from Uphaz. The description is majestic: body like chrysolite, face like lightning, eyes like flaming torches, arms and feet like gleaming bronze, and voice like the sound of a multitude. This appearance parallels the description of God’s glory in Ezekiel 1.

## Places

### Tigris (Hiddekel)
Daniel is located by "the great river Hiddekel" (v. 4), the Hebrew name for the Tigris. Great rivers were often places of prophetic revelation (cf. Ezekiel at the Kebar).

## Events and Customs

### Mourning and Fasting
Daniel’s three weeks of abstinence (vv. 2–3) involved avoiding "choice food" (לֶחֶם חֲמֻדוֹת), meat, wine, and anointing with oil. This represents partial fasting combined with mourning rituals. Abstaining from anointing was a typical sign of grief, since oil symbolized joy. The period extends over the Passover season, which normally was a festival time.

## Key Terms

### "The Prince of the Kingdom of Persia" (שַׂר מַלְכוּת פָּרַס)
The term "prince" (שַׂר, sar) is used here for spiritual beings who stand behind earthly kingdoms. The messenger was hindered by "the prince of Persia" for 21 days (v. 13) and must continue the struggle, while "the prince of Greece" (v. 20) is mentioned as a coming opponent.

### "The Writing of Truth" (כְתָב אֱמֶת)
The expression in verse 21 refers to a heavenly book in which future events are recorded. The messenger will reveal to Daniel what is written there concerning Israel’s future.

### "A Man Greatly Loved" (אִישׁ חֲמֻדוֹת)
Daniel is addressed twice as "a man greatly loved" or "highly esteemed" (vv. 11, 19). This expression underscores Daniel’s special standing with God.
