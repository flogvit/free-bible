## Places

Psalm 76 mentions two important place names: **Salem** (שָׁלֵם, *shalem*) and **Zion** (צִיּוֹן, *tsiyon*). Salem is an ancient name for Jerusalem and appears elsewhere only in Genesis 14:18, where Melchizedek is described as "king of Salem". The name is related to the Hebrew word for peace (*shalom*), and the connection between "his dwelling" (סֻכּוֹ, *sukko*) and Salem emphasizes that God has chosen this place as his home. Zion was originally the designation for the Jebusite fortress that David captured, but later came to refer to the entire temple hill and symbolically to all of Jerusalem as God’s holy city.

## Events and Customs

The psalm describes a dramatic military victory in which the enemies’ weapons – "the flaming arrows of the bow" (רִשְׁפֵי־קָשֶׁת, *rishfe-qashet*), shields, swords, and war – are shattered. Many scholars link this to the failed siege of Jerusalem by the Assyrian king Sennacherib in 701 B.C., described in 2 Kings 18–19 and Isaiah 36–37, where according to the text 185,000 Assyrian soldiers were struck down by the angel of the Lord in one night. The expression that "the proud of heart" (אַבִּירֵי לֵב, *abbire lev*) fell asleep and could not find their hands (v. 6) fits well with the image of an army paralyzed without a fight. The psalm’s call to make vows and bring offerings (v. 12) reflects the common Israelite practice of coming to the temple with thank offerings after divine deliverance.

## Key Terms

The expression **"mountains of prey"** (הַרְרֵי־טָרֶף, *harere taref*) in verse 5 is debated. Some interpret it as mountains where predators dwell, that is, hostile powers, while others see it as a poetic reference to the enemy’s military strength or fortresses. God is described as more "radiant" and "majestic" than these threatening forces. The word **"his dwelling"** (סֻכּוֹ, *sukko*) uses a term that usually means a temporary leafy shelter, which may hint at the early tabernacle tradition before the temple was built, or serve as a poetic variant for God’s home.
