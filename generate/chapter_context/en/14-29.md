## People

Hezekiah (יְחִזְקִיָּהוּ) was the son of King Ahaz and began to reign at the age of twenty-five. His mother Abijah (אֲבִיָּה) was the daughter of Zechariah. In this chapter, Hezekiah appears as a reformer who acts according to David’s example (verse 2), and he takes the initiative to cleanse and reopen the temple already in his first month of reign.

The chapter mentions fourteen Levites by name who led the cleansing work (verses 12–14), organized according to the traditional Levitical families: the Kohathites (Mahath and Joel), the Merarites (Kish and Azariah), the Gershonites (Joah and Eden), as well as descendants of Elizaphan (Shimri and Jeiel), Asaph (Zechariah and Mattaniah), Heman (Jehiel and Shimei), and Jeduthun (Shemaiah and Uzziel). The prophets Gad (David’s seer) and Nathan are mentioned as the source of the arrangements for temple music (verse 25).

## Places

The Kidron Valley (נַחַל־קִדְרוֹן) is mentioned as the place where impurities from the temple were thrown out (verse 16). This valley lies between Jerusalem and the Mount of Olives and traditionally served as a location for removing idolatrous symbols and unclean objects.

The "East Square" (רְחוֹב הַמִּזְרָח) where Hezekiah gathered the priests and Levites (verse 4) was likely an open area east of the temple complex used for public assemblies.

## Events and Customs

The temple cleansing followed a careful schedule: work began on the first day of the first month (Nisan). On the eighth day, they reached the porch (אוּלָם), and by the sixteenth day the entire cleansing was completed (verse 17). This timing meant that the temple was ready just before Passover season, although it became too late to celebrate Passover that month (compare chapter 30).

The sin offering of seven male goats "for the kingdom, for the sanctuary, and for Judah" (verse 21) represented a special act of atonement. The people laying their hands on the sin offering animals (verse 23) was a symbolic transfer of sin to the sacrificial animal, in accordance with the instructions in Leviticus 4. The expression "for all Israel" (verse 24) shows Hezekiah’s vision that the tribes of the northern kingdom should also be included in the atonement.

The scale of the offerings was considerable: seventy bulls, one hundred rams, and two hundred lambs as burnt offerings, along with six hundred bulls and three thousand sheep as holy gifts (verses 32–33). There were too few priests to skin all the burnt offerings, so the Levites had to help them (verse 34).

## Key Terms

The expression "to fill the hands" (מִלֵּאתֶם יֶדְכֶם, verse 31) is an idiom for consecration to service, originally used concerning priestly ordination. Here it is extended to apply to all the people who have now renewed their covenant with the Lord.

The word "niddah" (הַנִּדָּה, verse 5) means "impurity" or "abomination" and refers to the idolatrous symbols and foreign cult objects that Ahaz had placed in the temple. The term carries strong connotations of ritual defilement requiring extensive cleansing.

Verse 36 concludes with the statement that "this happened so suddenly" (בְּפִתְאֹם הָיָה הַדָּבָר), emphasizing that the rapid restoration of temple service was seen as a sign of God’s guidance.
