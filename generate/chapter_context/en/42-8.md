## People

Luke 8 introduces several important women who supported Jesus' ministry financially. **Mary Magdalene** (from the town of Magdala on the western shore of the Sea of Galilee) is mentioned as having been freed from seven evil spirits. **Joanna** was married to **Chusa**, who served as a steward (Greek: *epitropos*) for Herod Antipas – this was a high-ranking administrative position at the court. The fact that a woman from the Herodian court followed Jesus demonstrates the gospel's reach across social strata. **Susanna** is mentioned only here in the New Testament.

**Jairus** was a synagogue ruler (*archōn tēs synagōgēs*), an honored position that entailed responsibility for the synagogue's operation, maintenance, and worship arrangements. The fact that such a leader approached Jesus publicly shows that Jesus' reputation had reached religious leaders.

## Places

The **region of the Gerasenes** (verse 26) was located on the eastern side of the Sea of Galilee, in the Decapolis region. This was predominantly Gentile territory, which explains the presence of a large herd of pigs – pigs were unclean animals according to Jewish law and were not kept in Jewish areas. Archaeologists have identified steep cliffs descending toward the sea in this area, matching the account about the pigs. The city of Gerasa (modern Jerash in Jordan) is located about 50 kilometers from the sea, but the name was used for the entire district.

## Events and Customs

The women's financial support of Jesus' traveling group (verse 3) was unusual in that time. The Greek word *diēkonoun* ("served") and the phrase "out of their own means" indicate that wealthy women acted as patrons – a practice more familiar from Greco-Roman than Jewish culture. This shows how Jesus broke with conventional gender roles.

The woman with the bleeding issue (verses 43-48) had been ritually unclean for twelve years according to Leviticus 15:25-27. Her condition excluded her from temple worship and normal social interaction, since everything she touched became unclean. That she touched Jesus' garment was bold – it could have made him ritually unclean. The **tassel/fringe of the garment** (*kraspedon*) were the law-required tassels that Jews wore on their garments according to Numbers 15:38-39.

The mourning rituals at Jairus' house (verse 52) included loud weeping and wailing (*eklaion... ekoptonto*) – the latter word describes beating one's breast as an expression of grief. Professional mourners were common at deaths in wealthy families.

## Key Terms

**Legion** (verse 30) was a Roman military term for a military unit of 4,000-6,000 soldiers. The name suggests both the number of demons and the presence of the Roman occupation in the people's consciousness.

**Abyssos** (verse 31, "the abyss") refers in Jewish apocalyptic thought to the prison or dwelling place of demons and evil powers before the final judgment (cf. Revelation 9:1-11; 20:1-3).

**Epistata** (verses 24, 45) is Luke's preferred form of address for Jesus from the disciples, meaning "master" or "leader" – a word that emphasizes authority and shows Jesus' role as teacher and guide.
