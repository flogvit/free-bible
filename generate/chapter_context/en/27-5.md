## People

**Belshazzar** (בֵּלְשַׁאצַּר) was the Babylonian king who held the feast described in this chapter. When the text refers to Nebuchadnezzar as his "father" (אֲבוּהִ, verses 2, 11, 18), it reflects Semitic usage where "father" can mean ancestor or predecessor in a dynastic sense. The offer to become "third ruler in the kingdom" (verses 7, 16, 29) makes historical sense if Belshazzar himself was second in command under a superior ruler.

**The Queen** (מַלְכְּתָא), who entered the banquet hall (verse 10), was likely the queen mother. Her knowledge of Daniel from Nebuchadnezzar's time and her authority to speak uninvited to the king suggest a prominent position at court.

**Daniel** (דָּנִיֵּאל) is introduced here to King Belshazzar as an unknown figure, despite his previously prominent role under Nebuchadnezzar. The queen must explain who he is (verses 11–12). He receives the Babylonian name Belteshazzar (בֵּלְטְשַׁאצַּר, verse 12).

## Events and Customs

The feast that Belshazzar held for his thousand nobles (verse 1) was typical of Mesopotamian royal courts, where grand banquets demonstrated the king's wealth and power. The fact that the king drank wine "before" (לָקֳבֵל) the thousand guests indicates an elevated position where all could see him. Using sacred vessels from Jerusalem's temple as drinking cups (verses 2–3) was not only sacrilege but also a religious-political act demonstrating Babylon's gods' supposed victory over Judah's God. At the same time, praising gods made of gold, silver, bronze, iron, wood, and stone (verse 4) represents Mesopotamian polytheism.

The categories of wise men mentioned—sorcerers (אָשְׁפַיָּא), Chaldeans (כַּשְׂדָּאֵי), and astrologers (גָּזְרַיָּא)—constituted the professional priesthood and scholars whom Babylonian kings consulted to interpret omens and dreams.

## Key Terms

**The Writing on the Wall** (verse 25) – "MENE MENE TEKEL UPHARSIN" (מְנֵא מְנֵא תְּקֵל וּפַרְסִין) – consists of Aramaic words that are also units of currency or weight: *mene* (mina), *tekel* (shekel), and *parsin* (half-mines). This duality creates a wordplay: the words can be read as accounting terms, but Daniel's interpretation reveals their prophetic meaning. *Mene* is linked to "number" (מְנָה), *tekel* to "weigh" (תְּקַל), and *peres* to both "divide" (פְּרַס) and "Persia" (פָּרָס).

**Purple and the Gold Chain** (אַרְגְּוָנָא og הַמְנִיכָא דִי־דַהֲבָא) were royal honors. Purple fabric was extremely costly, and a gold chain around the neck symbolized high rank and royal favor.

## Chapter-Specific Findings

Verses 30–31 briefly state that Belshazzar was killed that same night. This marks a dramatic transition where the feast that began with the desecration of the sacred vessels ends with the king's death and the fall of his kingdom—all within one night.
