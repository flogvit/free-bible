## Key Concepts

### Wisdom and Foolishness as Personified Women
The chapter presents two contrasting female figures: **חָכְמוֹת** (chokmot, "Wisdom") and **אֵשֶׁת כְּסִילוּת** (eshet kesilut, "the woman of foolishness"). The Hebrew form *chokmot* is an unusual plural or intensive form that emphasizes the perfection and majesty of Wisdom. This literary technique portrays a radical choice between two ways of life.

### The Seven Pillars
The expression **עַמּוּדֶיהָ שִׁבְעָה** (ammudeiha shiv'ah, "her seven pillars") in verse 1 has been interpreted in various ways. The number seven symbolizes completeness and wholeness, and a house with many pillars indicated wealth and stability.

### Refaim – the Dead
Verse 18 mentions **רְפָאִים** (refa'im), often translated as "the dead" or "shades." This word is used in the Hebrew Bible both for a people group (often understood as giants) and for the dead in Sheol. Here it describes the final destiny of those who follow the woman of foolishness—her guests are already in the depths of the realm of the dead (**בְּעִמְקֵי שְׁאוֹל**). This stands in sharp contrast to Wisdom's promise of life (verses 6, 11).

## Events and Customs

### The Feast
Wisdom's preparations in verses 2–5—slaughtering animals (**טָבְחָה טִבְחָהּ**), mixing wine (**מָסְכָה יֵינָהּ**), and setting the table—describe a formal feast. Wine was usually mixed with water or spices, and such a meal was a sign of hospitality. In contrast, the woman of foolishness offers "stolen water" and "bread eaten in secret" (verse 17), suggesting illicit and hidden pleasures.

### The Invitation from the Heights of the City
Both women call out from **מְרֹמֵי קָרֶת** (meromei qaret, "the heights of the city"). In ancient cities, the high places were central locations for public gatherings and trade. That both use the same arena emphasizes that the choice between wisdom and foolishness meets people in the public arenas of life.
