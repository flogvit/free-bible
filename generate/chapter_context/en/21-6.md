## Key Concepts

The chapter contains several central Hebrew expressions that require cultural explanation. The word **נֵפֶל** (*nefel*, v. 3) means "stillborn child" or "miscarriage" and represents a shocking comparison in the Israelite context. The Preacher claims that a stillborn child is better off than a rich man who cannot enjoy his wealth—a radical statement in a culture where many children and long life were regarded as God's blessing (Ps 127:3-5). The expression **אִישׁ נָכְרִי** (*ish nokhri*, v. 2) refers to a "foreign man" who inherits the wealth, which in Israelite law and culture was especially tragic since inheritance ideally should remain within the family and tribe.

The phrase **קְבוּרָה לֹא־הָיְתָה לּוֹ** (v. 3) – "he had no burial" – touches on a deep cultural taboo. In the ancient Near East, including Israel, a proper burial was absolutely essential for honor and legacy. To lie unburied was the ultimate shame, associated with God's judgment upon the wicked (Jer 22:19; Isa 14:19-20).

## Events and Customs

The chapter reflects the ancient understanding of wealth and inheritance in the Near East. The situation described in verse 2 – where a foreigner takes over a man's possessions – could arise through several scenarios: military conquest, debt slavery, or lack of legitimate heirs. Sudden loss of wealth through political upheavals or arbitrary confiscation were well-known phenomena in antiquity. The idea that God "gives" wealth but does not "give him power to enjoy it" (v. 2) challenges traditional wisdom theology which viewed material prosperity as a direct sign of divine favor.
