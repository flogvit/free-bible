## Key Concepts

Psalm 94 opens with the Hebrew expression **אֵל נְקָמוֹת** (*El neqamot*), "the God of vengeance." The word *neqamah* does not denote primitive vindictiveness, but rather legal retribution—the righteous restoration of order when injustice has occurred. In Old Testament thought, divine vengeance was tied to the covenant relationship: God as Judge intervenes to protect the weak and punish oppressors. The verb **הוֹפִיעַ** (*hofia*) in verse 1, "appear in splendor," is used of theophanies—God's powerful manifestation in the world.

Verse 12 contains the term **תּוֹרָה** (*torah*), which here refers to God's instruction and guidance in a broader sense than just the Mosaic Law. The expression **שַׂרְעַפִּים** (*sar'appim*) in verse 19 is rare and means anxious thoughts or inner turmoil—a word that captures the emotional state of the oppressed.

## Events and Customs

Verse 6 mentions three vulnerable groups: **the widow**, **the foreigner**, and **the fatherless**. Protecting these groups was a central ethical requirement in Israelite law and throughout the ancient Near East. Law codes from Mesopotamia to Egypt emphasized the king's duty to protect the weak. In Israel, this was rooted in the covenant—to oppress these groups was not only socially unacceptable but a direct rebellion against Yahweh himself.

Verse 20 refers to **כִּסֵּא הַוּוֹת** (*kisse havvot*), "the throne of destruction," which "frames mischief by statute." This describes corrupt authorities who use legal structures to legitimize oppression. Such institutionalized injustice was well known in ancient societies, where the powerful could manipulate the judicial system for their own gain.
