## Events and Customs

Job 28 provides a remarkably detailed description of mining in antiquity, reflecting advanced technical knowledge. The text describes shaft digging (v. 4), the extraction of silver, gold, iron, and copper (vv. 1–2), as well as methods for breaking through rock and mountains (vv. 9–10). Mining was well known in the ancient Near East, particularly in areas such as the Sinai Peninsula (Egyptian copper and turquoise mining), the Arabah Valley south of the Dead Sea, and Ophir (mentioned in v. 16), likely located in South Arabia or East Africa. The description of setting "a boundary for darkness" (v. 3) and digging channels through rock (v. 10) testifies to knowledge of underground mine shafts equipped with lighting and water management systems.

## Places

The chapter mentions **Ophir** (v. 16), a legendary place known for its exceptionally fine gold. Ophir is mentioned several times in the Old Testament as a destination for Solomon’s merchant fleet. Its location is debated, but candidates include South Arabia (Yemen), Somalia, India, or Zimbabwe. **Cush** (v. 19) refers to Nubia/Ethiopia, known for its precious stones, especially topaz (Hebrew: *pitdah*). These geographical references show that the author was familiar with distant trade routes and exotic goods from the ancient world.

## Key Terms

The Hebrew word **חָכְמָה** (*chokmah*, "wisdom") is the central concept of the chapter and appears as a refrain throughout the text. In wisdom literature, *chokmah* denotes not only intellectual knowledge but also a deeper insight into the order of existence and the art of living. The term **בִּינָה** (*binah*, "understanding/insight") is used parallel to *chokmah* (vv. 12, 20, 28) and refers to the ability to discern and comprehend relationships.

**תְּהוֹם** (*tehom*, v. 14) refers to the primeval deep or ocean depths, a concept with mythological overtones in the ancient Near East. **אֲבַדּוֹן** (*Abaddon*, v. 22) means "destruction" or "perdition" and personifies the realm of the dead—a place that has only heard rumors about wisdom.

The list of precious stones and metals (vv. 15–19) includes **סְגוֹר** (*segor*, "sealed gold" or "gold bars"), **שֹׁהַם** (*shoham*, likely onyx or carnelian), **זְכוֹכִית** (*zekukit*, glass or crystal), **רָאמוֹת** (*ra'mot*, coral) and **פְּנִינִים** (*peninim*, pearls or red coral).

## Chapter-Specific Findings

Archaeological excavations at Timna in the Negev Desert have uncovered extensive copper mines from the Bronze Age, complete with shafts, smelting facilities, and workers’ dwellings. These findings confirm the technical knowledge described in Job 28. Egyptian sources from the New Kingdom document mining operations in Sinai using methods that correspond to the chapter’s descriptions: shaft digging, use of lamps in darkness, and water channels for draining groundwater. Such discoveries show that the text reflects genuine knowledge of mining technology during the second and first millennia BCE.
