## Events and Customs

Psalm 26 contains several important references to temple rituals and purification ceremonies. In verse 6, handwashing in innocence ("אֶרְחַץ בְּנִקָּיוֹן כַּפָּי" – "I will wash my hands in innocence") is mentioned, followed by walking around the altar. This reflects actual practice in temple service, where priests washed their hands and feet in the bronze basin before approaching the altar (Exodus 30:18–21). Processions around the altar were part of festival celebrations, especially the Feast of Tabernacles, when pilgrims walked in procession around the altar while singing songs of praise. The psalmist uses this ritual language to express his moral purity and belonging to the holy community.

## Places

The expression "the dwelling of your house" and "the place where your glory dwells" (verse 8) refer to the tabernacle or temple as God's earthly residence. The Hebrew word "מִשְׁכַּן" (mishkan) is used here, the same word that denotes the tabernacle in the wilderness. This underscores the continuity between the movable sanctuary tent and the permanent temple in Jerusalem. For the Israelites, this was the place where God's visible glory (kavod) manifested itself, especially in the Most Holy Place.

## Key Concepts

The word "תֻּמִּי" (tummi, "my integrity/in innocence") which frames the psalm (verses 1 and 11), shares the same root as "tummim" in the priests' oracle instruments "Urim and Thummim." The root means wholeness, completeness, or innocence. The psalmist does not claim sinlessness, but wholehearted faithfulness to the covenant.

The expression "כִלְיוֹתַי וְלִבִּי" (verse 2, "my kidneys and my heart") reflects ancient Hebrew psychology in which the kidneys were regarded as the seat of emotions and conscience, while the heart was the seat of will and understanding. Together they represent the entire inner life of a person that God examines.
