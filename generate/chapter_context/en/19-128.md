## Places

Psalm 128 mentions **Zion** and **Jerusalem** (verse 5) as the source of the Lord’s blessing. Zion represents the temple hill where God’s presence dwelt, while Jerusalem encompasses the entire holy city. For pilgrims who sang this psalm on their way up to the annual festivals, these places were the final destination—where blessing was conveyed through temple service and fellowship with God’s people. The blessing "from Zion" connects an individual family’s happiness to the spiritual center of the whole nation.

## Events and Customs

The psalm reflects the Israelite ideal of **family life and household**. The image of the wife "in the innermost parts of your house" (בְּיַרְכְּתֵי בֵיתֶךָ) mirrors the traditional house structure in which the woman was responsible for the inner life of the home, while the man worked in the public sphere. Children around the table as "olive shoots" point to the significance of the olive tree in Israelite economy—a tree that required generations of patience before it bore full fruit, but then yielded harvests for centuries. To see grandchildren (verse 6) was a sign of God’s enduring blessing and the family’s continued existence within the covenant community.

## Key Terms

The word **אַשְׁרֵי** (*ashre*, "blessed" or "happy") opens the psalm and is repeated in verse 2. This is not merely a feeling, but describes an objective state of blessing that follows those who fear the Lord. The expression **יְגִיעַ כַּפֶּיךָ** ("the labor of your hands") denotes hard physical work, and the blessing consists in being able to enjoy the fruit of one’s own toil—in contrast to the curse in Deuteronomy 28:30–33 where foreigners take the fruit of one’s labor. The closing phrase **שָׁלוֹם עַל־יִשְׂרָאֵל** ("peace upon Israel") expands the perspective from the individual family to the entire people, showing that personal blessing and national shalom are inseparable.
