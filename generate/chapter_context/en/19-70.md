## Key Concepts

### "Lehazkir" (לְהַזְכִּיר) – "For a Reminder"
The heading contains the Hebrew expression *lehazkir*, which means "for a reminder" or "to bring to remembrance." This is a rare liturgical term that appears in the headings of Psalm 38 and 70. The term is connected to the *azkara* offering, part of the grain offering where a handful of flour and oil was burned as a "memorial" before the Lord (Lev 2:2). This suggests that the psalm may have been used in connection with specific sacrificial rituals in the temple, possibly as a prayer asking God to "remember" the petitioner in distress.

### "Heach, heach" (הֶאָח הֶאָח) – A Mocking Exclamation
In verse 4 we find the onomatopoeic expression *heach heach*, a mocking cry of triumph from enemies. This is an expressive sound word that conveys the sound of gloating and scorn. Similar expressions are found in other laments (Ps 35:21,25; 40:16) and reflect the intense honor culture of ancient Israel, where public shame was one of the worst forms of suffering.

## Events and Customs

### The Parallel Psalm in Psalm 40
Psalm 70 is almost identical to Psalm 40:14–18. Psalm 70 functions as an independent prayer psalm that may have been separated for its own liturgical use. The most striking difference is the use of God’s name: Psalm 70 primarily uses *Elohim* (God), while the parallel in Psalm 40 uses *YHWH* (the Lord), reflecting different liturgical practices.
