# Historical and Cultural Context for 2 Kings 12

## People

**Joash** (Hebrew: יְהוֹאָשׁ, also spelled יוֹאָשׁ) became king at the age of seven and reigned for forty years in Jerusalem (vv. 1-2). His mother, Zibiah, was from Beersheba (v. 2). The text highlights that he "did what was right in the eyes of the Lord" as long as the priest Jehoiada guided him (v. 3), but the high places were not removed (v. 4). By the end of the chapter, he is murdered by his own servants (vv. 21-22).

**Jehoiada** (יְהוֹיָדָע, "the Lord knows") was the priest who guided Joash (v. 3) and played a key role in the temple restoration. He confronted the priests together with the king regarding their lack of progress (v. 8) and initiated the offering box system that solved the funding problem (v. 10). In verse 11, he is called "הַכֹּהֵן הַגָּדוֹל" (the high priest).

**Hazael** (חֲזָאֵל) was king of Aram (Syria). In this chapter, he captured Gath and then threatened to attack Jerusalem, but withdrew after Joash sent him all the temple treasures and the gold from the palace as tribute payment (vv. 18-19).

**Jozabad and Jehozabad** (יוֹזָבָד and יְהוֹזָבָד) were the servants who conspired against and murdered King Joash at Beth-Millo (vv. 21-22). Jozabad was the son of Shimeath, and Jehozabad was the son of Shomer.

**Amaziah** (אֲמַצְיָה) was Joash's son who became king after his father's death (v. 22).

## Places

**Jerusalem** (יְרוּשָׁלַ͏ִם) was the capital where Joash reigned for forty years (v. 2) and where the temple to be restored was located.

**Beersheba** (בְּאֵר שָׁבַע) was the hometown of Joash's mother Zibiah (v. 2), far south in Judah.

**Gath** (גַּת) was a strategic city that Hazael captured before turning his attention to Jerusalem (v. 18). The capture demonstrates the extent of the Aramean threat.

**Beth-Millo** (בֵּית מִלֹּא), literally "the house of filling," was the place where Joash was murdered. The text specifies it as "on the way down to Silla" (הַיּוֹרֵד סִלָּא) (v. 21). This was likely a fortified building in Jerusalem.

**City of David** (עִיר דָּוִד) was the burial place where Joash was buried with his fathers (v. 22).

## Events and Customs

**Temple Restoration and Funding**: The chapter describes a detailed system for collecting funds for temple repairs. Verse 5 mentions several sources of income: "money from those who pass by" (כֶּסֶף עוֹבֵר – current coin/circulating silver), "redemption money for persons according to the assessment" (כֶּסֶף נַפְשׁוֹת עֶרְכּוֹ – perhaps based on Leviticus 27:2-8), and voluntary gifts.

**The Failed Collection System**: Originally, the priests were to collect money through their "makkarim" (contacts/connections) and carry out the repairs themselves (v. 6). After twenty-three years, the work was still not done (v. 7), which led to a confrontation between the king and the priests (v. 8).

**The Offering Box System**: Jehoiada's solution was a chest with a hole in its lid, placed on the right side of the altar at the entrance (v. 10). The gatekeeper priests (שֹׁמְרֵי הַסַּף) put all collected money into the chest. When there was much money, the king's scribe and the high priest counted it together and gave it to the foremen of the workers (vv. 11-12).

**Trust-Based Administration**: Remarkably, those who managed the funds for the craftsmen were not held accountable, "for they acted faithfully" (כִּי בֶאֱמֻנָה הֵם עֹשִׂים, v. 16).

**Tribute Payment to Hazael**: To prevent an attack on Jerusalem, Joash emptied both the temple and palace treasuries. He also sent holy objects that had been dedicated by his forefathers Jehoshaphat, Joram, and Ahaziah (v. 19).

## Key Terms

**"Bedek habbajit"** (בֶּדֶק הַבַּיִת) – "the breach of the house" – is a technical term for structural repairs used throughout the chapter (vv. 6, 7, 8, 9, 13). The word *bedek* (בֶּדֶק) refers to cracks or damage in the masonry.

**"Kesef over"** (כֶּסֶף עוֹבֵר) in verse 5 literally means "passing money" and likely refers to current coin or circulating silver that was in use.

**"Kesef nefasjot erko"** (כֶּסֶף נַפְשׁוֹת עֶרְכּוֹ) – "money for persons according to the assessment" (v. 5) – refers to redemption money based on an evaluation of a person's value.

**Temple Utensils That Were Not Made**: Verse 14 lists items that the money was *not* used for: silver basins (סִפּוֹת), snuffers or shears (מְזַמְּרוֹת), bowls (מִזְרָקוֹת), trumpets (חֲצֹצְרוֹת), and other gold and silver objects. Everything went to the actual building repairs.

**Guilt Offering and Sin Offering Money** (כֶּסֶף אָשָׁם וְכֶסֶף חַטָּאוֹת): This money went to the priests, not to temple repairs (v. 17).
