## Places

### The Potter’s House (בֵּית הַיּוֹצֵר)

The potter’s workshop was likely located in the southern part of Jerusalem, near the Valley of Hinnom, where there was access to clay and water. The potter’s wheel (הָאָבְנָיִם, literally "the two stones") consisted of two horizontal stone slabs connected by a vertical axle—the lower one was turned with the feet, while the upper one held the clay.

## Events and Customs

### Potter’s Work as a Prophetic Illustration

The potter’s craft was among the most visible and well-known trades in ancient Israel. Everyone knew how a potter could discard a failed vessel and start again with the same clay. This gave the image immediate impact for its listeners. God’s sovereignty over nations is compared to the potter’s absolute control over the clay—a picture also found in other Old Testament texts (Isa 29:16; 45:9; 64:7).

### Conspiracy Against Jeremiah

In verse 18, Jeremiah’s opponents plan to "strike him with the tongue"—that is, to attack him with false accusations and malicious speech. They justify this by claiming that "the law will not perish from the priest, nor counsel from the wise, nor the word from the prophet"—an ironic assertion that they have other religious authorities and do not need Jeremiah.

### Jeremiah’s Lament and Intercession

Verses 19–23 contain one of Jeremiah’s most intense laments. He reminds God that he has stood as an intercessor for the people (v. 20), but now asks that God should not forgive them. This tension between the prophet’s earlier intercession and his current plea for judgment reflects the deep conflict in Jeremiah’s ministry.

## Key Terms

### "Relent" (נָחַם)

The Hebrew verb *nacham* in verses 8 and 10, often translated as "relent", does not mean that God admits error, but expresses a change in God’s course of action in response to human repentance or rebellion. The term describes God’s relational and responsive nature within the covenant relationship.

### "Stubbornness of heart" (שְׁרִרוּת לִבּוֹ)

The expression in verse 12 (*sherirut lev*) describes a stubborn, self-willed attitude. This is a characteristic phrase used by Jeremiah that emphasizes the people’s deliberate choice to follow their own ways rather than God’s will.
