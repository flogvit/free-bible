## People

**Joshua (יְהוֹשֻׁעַ)** is introduced here in his new role as Israel's leader. His full name is "Joshua son of Nun" (בִּן־נוּן), and he is described as "Moses' servant" (מְשָׁרֵת מֹשֶׁה). The title *mesharet* denotes a personal assistant or aide, which differs from *eved* (servant/slave) used to describe Moses' relationship with God.

**The tribes of Reuben, Gad, and the half-tribe of Manasseh** are specifically mentioned because they had already received their land allocations east of the Jordan under Moses' leadership (Num 32). Joshua reminds them of their obligation to help the other tribes conquer Canaan before returning to their own territories.

## Places

**The Jordan River (הַיַּרְדֵּן)** forms the central geographical marker in this chapter—the boundary that must be crossed to enter the Promised Land. The Israelites are on the east side and will cross within three days (v. 11).

**The borders of the land** described in verse 4 extend from the wilderness (הַמִּדְבָּר) in the south to Lebanon (הַלְּבָנוֹן) in the north, and from the Mediterranean Sea ("the great sea," הַיָּם הַגָּדוֹל) in the west to the Euphrates River ("the great river," הַנָּהָר הַגָּדוֹל) in the east. "The land of the Hittites" (אֶרֶץ הַחִתִּים) is mentioned as part of this promised area.

## Key Terms

**"Be strong and courageous" (חֲזַק וֶאֱמָץ)** is repeated three times by God to Joshua (vv. 6, 7, 9) and once by the people to Joshua (v. 18). The expression combines physical strength (*hazaq*) with mental resolve (*amats*), and it is used during leadership transitions in the OT (cf. Deut 31:7, 23).

**"The Book of the Law" (סֵפֶר הַתּוֹרָה)** in verse 8 refers to Moses' teaching and law. The command to "meditate on it day and night" (וְהָגִיתָ בּוֹ יוֹמָם וָלַיְלָה) implies reading aloud and meditation—success in leadership is directly tied to faithfulness to God's word.

**"Armed" / "in battle formation" (חֲמֻשִׁים)** in verse 14 describes how the warriors from the eastern tribes were to cross the Jordan. The word indicates military readiness for the conquest that awaited them.
