## People

**Rehoboam** (רְחַבְעָם) was the son of Solomon who went to Shechem to be installed as king (v. 1). The chapter highlights his fatal political blunder: he rejected the wise counsel of the elders to show kindness, and instead followed the arrogant advice of the young counselors to increase oppression (vv. 13–14).

**Jeroboam son of Nebat** (יָרָבְעָם בֶּן־נְבָט) returned from exile in Egypt (v. 2), where he had fled from King Solomon. He was summoned by the people to lead negotiations with Rehoboam (v. 3). Verse 15 mentions Ahijah’s prophecy as background for these events.

**The elder counselors** (הַזְּקֵנִים) had served under Solomon and advised Rehoboam to show kindness and speak good words to the people (vv. 6–7). **The young counselors** (הַיְלָדִים) had grown up with Rehoboam and urged him to answer harshly and threaten increased oppression (vv. 8–11).

**Ahijah of Shiloh** (אֲחִיָּהוּ הַשִּׁילֹנִי) is mentioned in verse 15 as the prophet who had given Jeroboam the promise of kingship over the northern tribes. This prophecy explains the theological background of the events.

**Hadoram** (הֲדֹרָם) was in charge of forced labor (עַל־הַמַּס). He was sent by Rehoboam, perhaps as a negotiator, but was stoned to death by the Israelites (v. 18). In 1 Kings 12:18 he is called Adoram or Adoniram.

## Places

**Shechem** (שְׁכֶם) was the place where all Israel gathered to install Rehoboam as king (v. 1). Choosing Shechem instead of Jerusalem signaled the northern tribes’ demand for influence—the king had to come to their territory. The site had a long tradition as a gathering place for the tribes (Josh 24).

**Jerusalem** (יְרוּשָׁלָם) is mentioned in verse 18 as the place Rehoboam fled to with his chariot after Hadoram was stoned.

**Egypt** (מִצְרַיִם) is noted as the place where Jeroboam had been in exile (v. 2).

## Events and Customs

**Royal acclamation and popular consent**: The chapter shows that the Israelite monarchy was not purely dynastic. Although Rehoboam was the heir, he had to go to Shechem to be "made king" (לְהַמְלִיךְ) by the people (v. 1). This reflects a covenantal understanding of kingship in which the people had the right to set conditions.

**Forced labor** (מַס, v. 18) and the heavy yoke (עֹל כָּבֵד, vv. 4, 10–11) were the main complaints of the people. They asked for relief from Solomon’s harsh work requirements, showing that the burden had become unbearable for the population.

**Division of the kingdom**: When Rehoboam rejected the people’s demands, the northern tribes declared their independence with the rebellious cry, "To your tents, O Israel!" (v. 16). Only those Israelites living in the cities of Judah remained under Rehoboam’s rule (v. 17).

## Key Terms

**"Scorpions"** (עֲקְרַבִּים) in verses 11 and 14 likely refer to a type of whip with metal spikes or hooks at the end, causing far more pain than ordinary whips (שׁוֹטִים).

**"To your tents, O Israel"** (אִישׁ לְאֹהָלֶיךָ יִשְׂרָאֵל) in verse 16 is a rebellious cry signaling rejection of the authority of the Davidic dynasty. The same expression was used during Sheba’s rebellion (2 Sam 20:1).

**Nesibba** (נְסִבָּה) in verse 15, translated "turning" or "change of events", is a rare word expressing that the events were directed by God. The Chronicler emphasizes that the division was "from God" (מֵעִם הָאֱלֹהִים) to fulfill the Lord’s word through Ahijah.
