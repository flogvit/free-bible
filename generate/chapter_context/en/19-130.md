## Key Concepts

Psalm 130 contains several theologically charged Hebrew expressions. The word **מַעֲמַקִּים** (*ma'amaqqim*, "depths") in verse 1 is used metaphorically to describe extreme distress or despair, and may carry overtones of the depths of death or chaotic waters that threatened life. The expression **סְלִיחָה** (*selikhah*, "forgiveness") in verse 4 is rare in the Old Testament and occurs only here, in Nehemiah 9:17, and Daniel 9:9—always as a divine attribute. This word later became central to Jewish liturgy, where prayers for forgiveness called *selikhot* took their name from it.

The verb **תִּוָּרֵא** (*tiwware'*, "be feared") in verse 4 links forgiveness with the fear of God in a surprising way: God's grace does not lead to indifference, but to reverence and worship. The concept **חֶסֶד** (*khesed*, "grace/steadfast love") in verse 7 denotes God's covenantal faithfulness and devotion toward His people. The word **פְדוּת** (*pedut*, "redemption") has its background in release from slavery or debt, and is used here to describe liberation from sin.

## Events and Customs

The image of watchmen waiting for the morning (verse 6) reflects a familiar experience in ancient Israel. Night guards on city walls or at the temple kept watch through the dark hours and longed for dawn, which brought both safety and relief.
