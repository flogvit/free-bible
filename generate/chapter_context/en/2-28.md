# Historical and Cultural Context for Exodus 28

## People

The chapter designates Aaron and his four sons—Nadab, Abihu, Eleazar, and Ithamar—for priestly service. The four sons are named by name (v. 1), and the priestly line was to continue through them.

## Events and Customs

The chapter describes detailed instructions for priestly garments, which have parallels in surrounding cultures. Egyptian high priests wore breastpieces with precious stones, and linen (שֵׁשׁ, shesh) was widespread in Egyptian cultic practice. The bells on the hem of the robe (vv. 33–35) have parallels in Canaanite and Mesopotamian practices, where sound was used to mark the priest's movements in holy spaces. The Hebrews gave this a distinct theological meaning tied to service for YHWH.

## Key Terms

**Ephod (אֵפוֹד)**: A garment resembling a vest worn over the shoulders to which the breastpiece was attached. The word also appears outside priestly contexts and may have had broader cultic significance in early Israel.

**Hoshen mishpat (חֹשֶׁן הַמִּשְׁפָּט)**: "Breastpiece of judgment"—the name indicates it was connected with seeking divine guidance in important decisions.

**Urim and Thummim (אוּרִים וְתֻמִּים)**: Objects used to seek God's will, likely a form of sacred lot-casting. The meaning of the words is uncertain but traditionally interpreted as "lights" and "perfections." The use of objects to seek divine guidance has parallels in other ancient cultures.

**Tekhelet (תְּכֵלֶת)**: A blue-violet color, traditionally produced from the Murex trunculus sea snail (Hexaplex trunculus). This was an extremely costly color in antiquity, and archaeological finds of dye residues and murex shells along the Phoenician coast confirm its production.

## Chapter-Specific Findings

Archaeological discoveries provide valuable context for this chapter. Excavations have uncovered pomegranate-shaped ornaments in bronze and pottery from the Iron Age that resemble the description of the pomegranates on the hem of the priestly robe (vv. 33–34). Stone seals with engraved Hebrew names have been found in large numbers, confirming the techniques described in verses 11 and 21 for engraving names "like a seal" (פִּתּוּחֵי חֹתָם). Gemstones set in gold settings are also documented in Egyptian and Mesopotamian priestly jewelry from the Bronze Age.
