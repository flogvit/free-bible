# Historical and Cultural Context for Leviticus 20

## Events and Customs

### The Molech Cult and Child Sacrifice
The chapter opens with a strict prohibition against "giving any of your offspring to Molech" (לַמֹּלֶךְ, *lamolekh*). Molech was a Canaanite deity associated with child sacrifice by fire. Archaeological discoveries in Carthage and other Phoenician colonies have uncovered so-called *tophet* areas—sacred sites containing urns with burned remains of young children. Assyrian and Babylonian texts also mention similar practices in the region. This cult represented the ultimate breach of Israel's covenant with Yahweh, combining idolatry with the murder of the innocent.

### Necromancy and Divination
Verses 6 and 27 refer to *'ovot* (אֹבֹת, "familiar spirits") and *yidde'onim* (יִדְּעֹנִים, "wizards"). These were widespread practices in the ancient Near East, where mediums claimed to contact the dead or receive supernatural knowledge. Mesopotamian texts describe extensive systems of divination, and such practices were integrated into the religious and political life of surrounding cultures. The prohibition marked a sharp distinction between Israelite faith and the religious customs of neighboring peoples.

## Key Terms

### "Their blood is upon them" (דְּמֵיהֶם בָּם)
The phrase *demeihem bam* ("their blood is upon them") is repeated several times in the chapter (vv. 9, 11–13, 16, 27). This legal expression indicates that the condemned person bears responsibility for their own death—the community assumes no guilt for shedding blood by carrying out the sentence.

### Holiness and Separation (קָדוֹשׁ)
The term *kadosh* (holy) and the verb *hivdil* (to set apart) are central to the chapter (vv. 7–8, 24–26). Holiness is understood concretely as separation—Israel was to be set apart from other nations through their way of life, just as clean animals were distinguished from unclean ones. This connection between ritual purity and ethical conduct is especially emphasized in this chapter.

### To Die Childless (עֲרִירִים)
The penalty *'aririm* ("childless") in verses 20–21 was extremely serious in the Israelite context. Childlessness meant that one's family line would be cut off, and there would be no descendants to preserve one's memory or inheritance. This was regarded as a form of being erased from history.
