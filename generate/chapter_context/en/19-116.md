## Places

The psalm concludes with a clear geographical reference to "the courts of the Lord’s house" and "Jerusalem" (v. 19). This points to the temple as the central place for public worship and thank offerings. The courts (חַצְרוֹת, *ḥatsrot*) were the open areas in the temple complex where Israelites could gather to participate in rituals and hear promises fulfilled. This location emphasizes that the psalmist’s personal experience of deliverance was to be confessed publicly, before all the people, right at the heart of Israel’s religious life.

## Events and Customs

The psalm contains several references to cultic practices in Israel. "The cup of salvation" (כּוֹס־יְשׁוּעוֹת, *kos yeshuʿot*, v. 13) likely refers to a thank-offering meal where wine was lifted as a sign of gratitude to God. "Thank offering" (זֶבַח תּוֹדָה, *zevaḥ todah*, v. 17) was a specific type of sacrifice described in Leviticus 7:12–15, brought when someone had experienced God’s help in distress. The fulfillment of vows (נְדָרַי, *nedarai*, vv. 14, 18) "before all his people" shows that such thank offerings were public acts where testimony was given in the hearing of the congregation.

## Key Terms

The expression "the cords of death" (חֶבְלֵי־מָוֶת, *ḥevlei mavet*, v. 3) and "the distresses of Sheol" (מְצָרֵי שְׁאוֹל, *metsarei sheʾol*) are poetic images for life-threatening danger, where death is personified as a hunter who snares its prey. "Simple ones" or "inexperienced" (פְּתָאיִם, *petaʾim*, v. 6) do not denote foolishness but vulnerability and helplessness—those lacking the resources to save themselves. The confession "I am your servant, the son of your female servant" (בֶּן־אֲמָתֶךָ, *ben-amatekha*, v. 16) is an expression of total devotion and belonging, where the psalmist identifies as one born into God’s service.
