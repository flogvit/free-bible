# Historical and Cultural Context for Micah 1

## People

**Micah of Moresheth** (מִיכָה הַמֹּרַשְׁתִּי, verse 1) – The prophet is identified with the village of Moresheth-Gath in the lowlands of Judah (Shephelah). His name means "Who is like Yahweh?"

**Jotham, Ahaz, and Hezekiah** (verse 1) – The three kings of Judah who reigned during the period approximately 750–687 B.C., amidst Assyrian expansion.

## Places

**Samaria** (verses 1, 5-6) – The capital city of the northern kingdom of Israel, founded around 880 B.C. The city is identified as "the transgression of Jacob" (verse 5) and threatened with total destruction—made into a ruin heap and a vineyard (verse 6). The Assyrians conquered the city in 722 B.C.

**Jerusalem** (verses 1, 5, 9, 12) – The capital of Judah is identified as "the high places of Judah" (verse 5). The chapter warns that judgment will reach all the way to "the gate of my people"—Jerusalem (verses 9, 12).

**Moresheth-Gath** (verse 14) – Micah's hometown in the Shephelah region. The name is used in a wordplay with שִׁלּוּחִים ("dowries" or "farewell gifts"), as if the city were to be sent away like a bride.

**Lachish** (verse 13) – Judah's second most important city after Jerusalem. It is called "the beginning of sin for the daughter of Zion"—possibly because the city was a center of Egyptian influence or idolatry. The Sennacherib reliefs from Nineveh (around 700 B.C.) depict the dramatic Assyrian siege of the city in 701 B.C.

**Wordplay series in verses 10-15** – A series of cities in the Shephelah are mentioned with wordplays on their names:
- Gath (גַת) – "winepress" (cf. 2 Sam 1:20)
- Beth-leaphrah (בֵּית לְעַפְרָה) – "house of dust" → roll yourself in the dust
- Shaphir (שָׁפִיר) – "beauty" → will go forth in shame and nakedness
- Saanannim (צַאֲנָן) – resembles "going out" → do not come out
- Maroth (מָרוֹת) – "bitterness" → waits in vain for good
- Achzib (אַכְזִיב) – "deceit" → will become a lie to the kings of Israel
- Mareshah (מָרֵשָׁה) – resembles "heir" → an invader shall come

## Events and Customs

**Mourning rituals** (verses 8, 16) reflect ancient Near Eastern customs. Going naked or barefoot (verse 8) was a sign of extreme grief. Shaving the head bald "like an eagle/vulture" (verse 16) was a mourning act associated with losing children to exile. The Hebrew word נֶשֶׁר can mean both eagle and vulture—both known for their bare heads.

**Idolatry and wages of prostitution** (verse 7) – The wealth of Samaria's idol temples is called אֶתְנַן ("wages of a prostitute"). This alludes to cultic prostitution in Canaanite fertility rites and the metaphor of idolatry as spiritual adultery.

## Key Concepts

**Theophany** (verses 3-4) – The description of Yahweh "going forth from His place" and treading upon "the high places of the earth" while mountains melt, uses classic theophanic language (cf. Judges 5, Psalm 18). The term בָּמוֹת ("high places") has a double meaning—both geographical heights and cultic shrines.

**פֶּשַׁע** ("transgression/rebellion", verse 5) – The word means rebellion or breach of covenant, stronger than simply "sin." Samaria is identified as "the transgression of Jacob" and Jerusalem as "the high places of Judah."

## Chapter-Specific Findings

The **Sennacherib reliefs** from Nineveh (ca. 700–681 B.C.) provide visual documentation of the Assyrian conquest of Lachish (verse 13). The reliefs show inhabitants being led into captivity—the fate Micah warns about in verse 16 when he calls for mourning over children who "go into exile from you."
