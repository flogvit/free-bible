# Historical and Cultural Context for 2 Chronicles 15

## People

**Azariah, son of Oded** is a prophet mentioned only in this chapter. The name Azariah (עֲזַרְיָהוּ) means "The Lord has helped" and was very common in ancient Israel. Verse 8 refers to the prophecy as "the prophecy of Oded the prophet" (עֹדֵד הַנָּבִיא), while verse 1 mentions "Azariah son of Oded." This has sparked discussion among interpreters regarding whether Oded was the prophet's father or an alternative name for the same person. The prophet's message follows the pattern of encouragement prophecy, linking the people's faithfulness to God with national blessing.

**Maacah** was the queen mother (גְּבִירָה) who was deposed by Asa. The text calls her "the king Asa's mother" (אֵם אָסָא הַמֶּלֶךְ), but based on the genealogy, she was likely his grandmother (Rehoboam's wife). The title "gebira" (great lady/queen mother) was an official position with significant power in the Judean court. Asa removing her from this role demonstrates the seriousness of the reform and his willingness to break family loyalty for the sake of faith. Her association with Asherah worship illustrates how deeply Canaanite religion had penetrated even into the royal family.

## Places

**The hill country of Ephraim** (הַר אֶפְרָיִם) refers to cities in the Northern Kingdom that Asa had conquered. This shows that the border between the two kingdoms was fluid at this time, and that Judah under Asa had expanded northward. These cities were included in the religious reform.

**The Kidron Valley** (נַחַל קִדְרוֹן) runs through a deep valley between the Temple Mount and the Mount of Olives, east of Jerusalem's city walls. This was traditionally the place where idols were destroyed and burned. The same location was later used by Hezekiah (2 Chron 29:16) and Josiah (2 Kgs 23:6) for similar cleansing actions.

## Events and Customs

**The renewal of the covenant in Asa's fifteenth year of reign** (v. 10) took place in the third month, which likely coincided with the Feast of Weeks (Shavuot/Pentecost). This festival marked the grain harvest and the tradition of lawgiving at Sinai, making it an appropriate time to renew the covenant with God. The offering of 700 head of cattle and 7,000 small livestock from war spoils underscores the solemn character of the covenant and follows the principle of giving God the firstfruits of victory.

**The death penalty for idolatry** (v. 13) reflects legislation in Deuteronomy 17:2-7, which prescribed that anyone who worshipped other gods should die. The fact that this was decreed for "small and great, man and woman" shows that the covenant was binding on the entire community without exception. This radical commitment was unusual and demonstrates the profound nature of the reform.

## Key Terms

**Mifletset** (מִפְלֶצֶת) in verse 16 is a rare Hebrew word appearing only here and in 1 Kings 15:13. The word likely comes from the root פלץ meaning "to tremble" and can refer to something that inspires terror or abhorrence. It denotes an idol or cult object associated with Asherah worship, possibly of obscene character.

**Asherah** (אֲשֵׁרָה) was a Canaanite goddess of fertility often worshipped alongside Baal. Cult objects associated with her could be wooden poles or carved images. The fact that the queen mother had made such an object shows that syncretism was widespread even among leading circles in Judah.
