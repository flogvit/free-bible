# Historical and Cultural Context for Nehemiah 10

## People

The chapter mentions Nehemiah with the title "Tirshata" (הַתִּרְשָׁתָא), a Persian title meaning "governor" or "his excellency." This shows that Nehemiah served as the official representative of the Persian king in Judah. The list of signatories is organized into three groups: priests (verses 2–9), Levites (verses 10–14), and heads of the people (verses 15–28). Zidkijah is mentioned alongside Nehemiah in verse 2, possibly as the scribe or secretary for the document. Several family names among the heads of the people correspond with family names from the lists of returnees, showing continuity in leadership.

## Events and Customs

The covenant renewal in this chapter follows a known pattern from the ancient Near East where peoples entered into binding agreements through written documents sealed (חָתוּם). The expression "to enter into a curse and an oath" (בְּאָלָה וּבִשְׁבוּעָה) in verse 30 refers to a self-cursing formula common in ancient covenants—those who broke the covenant called down God's judgment upon themselves. The prohibition against marrying "the peoples of the land" (verse 31) reflects concern over religious assimilation. The promise to let the land rest in the seventh year and to cancel debts (verse 32) follows Sabbath-year legislation found in Exodus 23:10–11 and Deuteronomy 15:1–2.

## Key Terms

The temple tax of "one-third shekel" (שְׁלִשִׁית הַשֶּׁקֶל) in verse 33 is remarkable because the regulation in the books of Moses in Exodus 30:13 sets it at half a shekel. This may reflect economic realities in the poor post-exilic community, or a separate arrangement for an annual contribution distinct from the one-time tax in the books of Moses. The "wood offering" (קֻרְבַּן הָעֵצִים) mentioned in verse 35 is not explicitly named in the books of Moses but was necessary to keep the altar fire burning (Leviticus 6:12–13). Josephus (Jewish War 2.17.6) mentions a "wood-offering festival" (Xylophoria) as an established practice in the second temple period.

## Chapter-Specific Findings

Archaeological discoveries of seal impressions (bullae) from the Persian period in Jerusalem confirm the practice of sealing official documents. The storage rooms (לְשָׁכוֹת) mentioned in verses 38–40 for tithes and offerings correspond to structures identified in the temple area, where large storerooms were necessary to receive and store contributions from all Judah. The chapter concludes with the powerful declaration: "We will not neglect our God's house" (וְלֹא נַעֲזֹב אֶת־בֵּית אֱלֹהֵינוּ), which summarizes the entire purpose of the covenant.
