# Historical and Cultural Context for Joshua 14

## People

**Caleb son of Jephunneh** is described as "the Kenizzite" (הַקְּנִזִּי), suggesting that he had non-Israelite origins. The Kenizzites were a people group in Canaan (Gen 15:19), and Caleb was likely incorporated into the tribe of Judah. This illustrates how early Israel included foreigners who showed loyalty to the God of Israel. Forty-five years had passed since the spying mission at Kadesh-barnea (Num 13–14) until the division of the land, and Caleb’s perseverance throughout this entire period makes him a model of faithfulness. His claim that he was as strong at eighty-five as he had been at forty reflects the biblical understanding that God’s blessing could preserve physical strength.

**Eleazar the priest** and **Joshua** are mentioned together with the tribal leaders as those responsible for dividing the land. This threefold division of authority—priestly, military/administrative, and tribal-related—shows how decisions were made collectively in early Israel.

## Places

**Hebron** (חֶבְרוֹן), also called **Kiriath-arba** ("City of Arba"), is located about 30 km south of Jerusalem in the hill country of Judah, at an elevation of approximately 930 meters above sea level. The name Kiriath-arba here is linked to Arba, who is described as "the greatest man among the Anakim" (v. 15). The city held deep significance as the burial place of the patriarchs (the cave of Machpelah) and was a regional center in the hill country.

**Gilgal** (בַּגִּלְגָּל), where Caleb presents his case, served as the main base for the Israelites after crossing the Jordan and as the location for dividing the land.

## Events and Customs

**Casting lots** (גוֹרָל) was the God-given method for dividing the land. In ancient Israel, the lot was regarded as a means to reveal God’s will (Prov 16:33). By using lots, human favoritism was removed, confirming that the distribution was divinely directed. The fact that Caleb’s inheritance was given separately, based on Moses’ earlier promise, shows that such promises took precedence.

**The exclusion of the Levites** from the regular land division is explained by noting that they were to receive cities and pasturelands scattered among all the tribes rather than a contiguous territory. This ensured their presence throughout the land to carry out religious duties.

## Key Terms

The expression **"fully followed the Lord"** (מִלֵּא אַחֲרֵי יְהוָה) appears three times in this chapter (vv. 8, 9, 14) and is key to understanding Caleb’s character. The Hebrew verb מִלֵּא ("to fill"), used with "after", creates an image of complete devotion—following without reservation or divided loyalty.

**The Anakim** (עֲנָקִים) were a people known for their great height who lived in the hill country around Hebron. They had caused fear among the spies forty-five years earlier (Num 13:33), but Caleb demonstrates his faith by requesting precisely that area.
