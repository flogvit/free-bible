## Events and Customs

The chapter describes how Hosea buys back a woman for fifteen shekels of silver, one homer of barley, and half a homer (letek) of barley (verse 2). This mixture of silver and goods as payment reflects common trade practices in ancient Israel. The total value has traditionally been calculated at about thirty shekels of silver—the price of a slave according to Exodus 21:32—although this calculation depends on assumed grain prices. That the payment is partly made in barley (a cheaper grain than wheat) may indicate that the woman had low status, possibly as a temple prostitute or debt slave. The purchase or redemption of a woman from such a status was legally recognized in the ancient Near East.

The raisin cakes (אֲשִׁישֵׁי עֲנָבִים, *ashishei anavim*, literally "grape cakes") mentioned in verse 1 were not only delicacies but also had cultic significance. They were used in Canaanite fertility rituals and as offerings to goddesses such as Astarte. The prophet's accusation that Israel "loves raisin cakes" therefore points to religious unfaithfulness through participation in pagan cults.

## Key Terms

Verse 4 lists six things Israel will be without for "many days": king, prince, sacrifice, pillar (מַצֵּבָה, *matsevah*), ephod, and household gods (תְּרָפִים, *terafim*). Pillars were upright stones used both in legitimate Yahweh worship and in Canaanite cults. The ephod could be a priestly garment or an object used to seek divine guidance. Terafim were small deity figures used in household worship and for divination purposes. That both legitimate (king, sacrifice) and illegitimate (terafim) institutions would disappear emphasizes that the exile would involve total religious and political dissolution.

The expression "David their king" in verse 5 is not used of the historical David but as a messianic designation for a future ideal king from David's line. This reflects the significance of the Davidic covenant tradition in prophetic expectation.
