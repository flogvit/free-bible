# Historical and Cultural Context for Psalms 75

## Persons

The psalm is attributed to **Asaph** (לְאָסָף). In this psalm, the text has a clearly prophetic character in which God Himself speaks directly (vv. 3–6, 11), which is characteristic of several Asaph psalms.

## Key Terms

**"Horn" (קֶרֶן, qeren)** – This word appears five times in the psalm (vv. 5, 6, 11) and is a central symbol. In ancient Near Eastern culture, the horn represented power, strength, and authority. The image is drawn from oxen and wild oxen, whose horns were their weapons and signs of dominance. To "lift up the horn" meant to act arrogantly and self-confidently, while to "cut off the horns" meant to crush someone’s power. In iconography from the ancient Middle East, gods and kings are sometimes depicted with horns as symbols of power.

**"The cup in the Lord's hand" (כוֹס בְּיַד־יְהוָה)** – The cup or chalice that God holds out (v. 9) is a powerful prophetic image for judgment. The wine that is "mixed" (מֶסֶךְ) refers to the ancient practice of adding spices or other ingredients to strengthen the wine. That the wicked must "drink its dregs" (שְׁמָרֶיהָ) – the bitter, sedimented remainder – emphasizes that judgment is complete and unavoidable. This image is also found among the prophets (Isa 51:17, 22; Jer 25:15–17; Ezek 23:31–34).

## Events and Customs

The expression **"appointed time" (מוֹעֵד)** in verse 3 (אֶקַּח מוֹעֵד, "I will set a time") carries rich meaning in Israelite religion. The word is used for the fixed festivals of the calendar and for the "tent of meeting" (אֹהֶל מוֹעֵד). Here God Himself uses the term to refer to the right moment for judgment. This reflects an understanding that history moves toward divinely appointed turning points, not governed by chance or human power.
