# Historical and Cultural Context for Joshua 18

## Places

**Shiloh** (שִׁלֹה) is the central location in this chapter. The city was located in the hill country of Ephraim, about thirty kilometers north of Jerusalem. Here the tent of meeting (אֹהֶל מוֹעֵד) was set up (verse 1), and Shiloh thus became established as Israel’s religious center and gathering place for all the tribes. The casting of lots for the remaining tribes took place "before the Lord" right there (verses 6, 8, 10).

**The territory of Benjamin** is described in detail with boundary lines in verses 11–20. The territory lay between the tribe of Judah to the south and Joseph’s sons (Ephraim and Manasseh) to the north (verse 11). The boundaries include:
- **East**: The Jordan (הַיַּרְדֵּן) as the eastern border (verses 12, 20)
- **North**: From the Jordan via the slope of Jericho up into the hill country toward Beth-Aven (בֵּית אָוֶן) by the wilderness (verse 12)
- **West**: Via Luz/Bethel (verses 13: לוּזָה... הִיא בֵּֽית־אֵל), Ataroth-addar, Lower Beth-horon, to Kiriath-baal which is Kiriath-jearim (קִרְיַת־בַּעַל הִיא קִרְיַת יְעָרִים, verse 14)
- **South**: From Kiriath-jearim via the spring of Me-neftoah, the Valley of Ben-hinnom (גֵּי בֶן־הִנֹּם, verse 16), En-rogel, En-shemesh, Geliloth by the ascent of Adummim, the stone of Bohan, Beth-hogla, to the bay of the Salt Sea (לְשׁוֹן יָם־הַמֶּלַח, verse 19)

Benjamin’s cities are listed in two groups: twelve cities with their villages (verses 21–24) and fourteen cities with their villages (verses 25–28). Among these **Jerusalem** (יְרוּשָׁלִַם) is mentioned, identified as "the Jebusite" (הַיְבוּסִי) in verse 28.

## Events and Customs

**Land surveying and casting of lots** represent a unique process in this chapter. Joshua rebukes the seven tribes who have not yet taken possession of their inheritance, saying "עַד־אָנָה אַתֶּם מִתְרַפִּים" – "How long will you be slack?" (verse 3). The term **מִתְרַפִּים** (mitrapim) suggests spiritual laziness or hesitation, not merely practical delay.

Joshua orders each tribe to select three men who are to go through the land, describe it, and write it down in a book (עַל־סֵפֶר, verse 9). This is one of the earliest references to systematic land surveying in the Bible. The land was to be divided into seven parts, while Judah remained in the south and the house of Joseph in the north (verse 5).

**Casting lots** (גּוֹרָל) "before the Lord" (לִפְנֵי יְהוָה) at Shiloh was a sacred practice in which the decision was left to God. Verse 7 explains why only seven tribes participated: the Levites had no earthly inheritance because "the priesthood of the Lord is their inheritance" (כְּהֻנַּת יְהוָה נַחֲלָתוֹ), and Gad, Reuben, and half of Manasseh had already received their inheritance east of the Jordan through Moses.

**Benjamin’s lot** came up first (verse 11), and the rest of the chapter describes this territory in detail – both its boundaries and its cities.
