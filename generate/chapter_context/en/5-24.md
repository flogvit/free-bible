# Historical and Cultural Context for Deuteronomy 24

## Events and Customs

### Divorce Practices and the Bill of Divorce (vv. 1–4)
The expression "סֵפֶר כְּרִיתֻת" (séfer keritut, "bill of divorce") describes a formal legal document that granted a woman the right to remarry. This was an important protection in a culture where women were economically dependent on men. The Hebrew phrase "עֶרְוַת דָּבָר" (ervat davar, "something indecent or shameful") became the subject of intense rabbinic debate between the schools of Hillel and Shammai regarding what constituted legitimate grounds for divorce.

### Exemption from Military Service for Newlyweds (v. 5)
The law that a newly married man should be "נָקִי" (naki, "free/exempt") for one year from military service and public duties reflects both practical and theological considerations. This protected the new family and ensured that the husband could "make his wife happy" (cf. Deut 20:7).

### Millstones as Pledge (v. 6)
The hand mill ("רֵחַיִם וָרָכֶב", rechayim varachev – both the lower and upper stones) was absolutely essential for daily bread production in Israelite households. Taking this as a pledge would deprive a family of the ability to prepare food.

### Harvest Regulations for the Vulnerable (vv. 19–22)
The practice of leaving "עֹמֶר" (omer, "sheaf") in the field, as well as fruit on olive trees and grapevines, institutionalized a system of charity that ensured foreigners, fatherless children, and widows had access to food.

## Key Terms

### Kidnapping (v. 7)
The expression "גֹּנֵב נֶפֶשׁ" (gonev nefesh, "to steal a soul/person") describes kidnapping with the intent to sell the victim as a slave. This was punishable by death.

### Skin Disease and the Example of Miriam (vv. 8–9)
The word "צָרַעַת" (tsara'at) is traditionally translated as "leprosy," but it likely covered several serious skin diseases and conditions with ritual significance. The reference to Miriam points back to Numbers 12:10–15, where she was struck with this affliction as punishment for criticizing Moses. This serves as a warning to follow the priests' guidance in such matters.

### Individual Responsibility (v. 16)
The principle "אִישׁ בְּחֶטְאוֹ יוּמָתוּ" (ish becheto yumatu, "each shall die for their own sin") represented an important legal reform that limited the scope of punishment to the guilty individual alone.

## People

### Miriam (v. 9)
Miriam is mentioned as a warning example. She was the sister of Moses and Aaron, a prophetess (Exod 15:20), and a central figure during the Exodus from Egypt. Her punishment with skin disease (Num 12) serves here as a reminder of the seriousness of following the Lord's commands, especially regarding matters of ritual purity.
