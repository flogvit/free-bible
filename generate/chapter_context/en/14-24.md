## People

**Joash** (Hebrew: יוֹאָשׁ) was king of Judah from the age of seven and reigned for forty years in Jerusalem (v. 1). His mother, Zibiah (צִבְיָה), came from Beersheba in southern Judah. The chapter portrays a divided character: a godly king under Jehoiada’s guidance who later falls away dramatically after the priest’s death.

**Jehoiada** (יְהוֹיָדָע, "the Lord knows") was high priest and Joash’s foster father and advisor. He chose two wives for Joash (v. 3) and led the temple restoration together with the king. His exceptional age of 130 years at death (v. 15) even surpasses Moses’ 120 years, underscoring his spiritual greatness. That he was buried among the kings in the City of David (v. 16) was an unprecedented honor for a non-royal person and reflects his decisive role in preserving the Davidic dynasty.

**Zechariah son of Jehoiada** (זְכַרְיָה בֶּן־יְהוֹיָדָע הַכֹּהֵן, v. 20) was the high priest’s son and prophesied against the king and the people’s apostasy. His execution in the temple courtyard by the king’s command (v. 21) represents a particularly grave betrayal—Joash killed the son of the man who had saved and raised him. Zechariah may be the one referred to in Matthew 23:35, although this identification is debated.

**Zabad and Jehozabad** (זָבָד og יְהוֹזָבָד, v. 26) were the servants who conspired against and killed King Joash. Zabad was the son of Shimeath the Ammonite woman, and Jehozabad was the son of Shimrith the Moabite woman. The fact that both mothers were foreign women underscores the foreign influence at court following the apostasy.

## Places

**The temple courtyard** (חֲצַר בֵּית יְהוָה, v. 21) where Zechariah was stoned was normally a holy area for worship and sacrifice. That a prophet’s murder took place there represented an extreme desecration of the sacred space.

**The City of David** is mentioned twice with important contrast: Jehoiada was buried "with the kings" (עִם־הַמְּלָכִים, v. 16) as an honor, while Joash was buried in the City of David but "not among the royal tombs" (וְלֹא קְבָרֻהוּ בְּקִבְרוֹת הַמְּלָכִים, v. 25). This distinction carried great symbolic significance.

## Events and Customs

**The collection chest** (אֲרוֹן אֶחָד, "one chest", v. 8) was placed at the entrance to the house of the Lord (בְּשַׁעַר בֵּית־יְהוָה חוּצָה). This system of daily emptying of the chest under royal and priestly supervision (v. 11) shows an early form of financial control and accountability in temple administration.

**The temple tax from Moses’ time** (מַשְׂאַת מֹשֶׁה עֶבֶד־יְהוָה, v. 6, 9) refers to the half-shekel that every Israelite was required to pay according to Exodus 30:11–16. The fact that this tax had lapsed during Athaliah’s reign (v. 7) shows how seriously neglect of temple service had become.

**Burial with the kings** was an honor reserved for kings who had served faithfully. That Jehoiada received this honor while Joash was denied it represents an ironic reversal—the priest received royal honor, while the king was stripped of it.

## Key Terms

The expression **"the Spirit of God clothed"** (וְרוּחַ אֱלֹהִים לָבְשָׁה, v. 20) is a rare and powerful expression for prophetic inspiration. The verb "to clothe" or "to put on" (לָבַשׁ) suggests that the Spirit completely enveloped Zechariah and gave him authority to speak God’s word. The same expression is used of Gideon in Judges 6:34.

The designation **"the wicked Athaliah"** (עֲתַלְיָהוּ הַמִּרְשַׁעַת, v. 7) uses a strong moral characterization. The adjective מִרְשַׁעַת ("wicked" or "evil") explicitly marks her as one who acted actively against God. That her sons "broke into" the temple (פָרְצוּ) and used holy objects for Baal worship explains the temple’s ruined condition.

## Chapter-Specific Findings

The Aramean invasion in verses 23–24 mentions that the plunder was sent to "the king of Damascus" (מֶלֶךְ דַּרְמָשֶׂק). That a small Aramean force (בְמִצְעַר אֲנָשִׁים, "with few men", v. 24) could defeat a much larger Judahite army is interpreted theologically as God’s judgment on apostasy: "because they had forsaken the Lord, the God of their fathers" (כִּי עָזְבוּ אֶת־יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵי אֲבוֹתֵיהֶם).
