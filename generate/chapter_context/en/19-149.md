# Historical and Cultural Context for Psalm 149

## Places

**Zion** (צִיּוֹן, v. 2) is mentioned as the place where "the children of Zion" should rejoice in their king. Here, Zion refers to Jerusalem and the temple hill as God’s dwelling among His people. The expression "children of Zion" (בְּנֵי־צִיּוֹן) emphasizes the close relationship between the people and their holy city.

## Events and Customs

The psalm describes a form of worship that combines singing, dancing, and instruments (v. 3). **Machol** (מָחוֹל, dance) accompanied by the **tof** (תֹּף, hand drum) and **kinnor** (כִּנּוֹר, lyre) were central elements in Israelite celebration and cultic practice. This combination of movement and music is also found in other texts describing victory celebrations and festivals (cf. Exod 15:20; 2 Sam 6:14).

The dramatic shift in verses 6–9, from praise to images of war with a **two-edged sword** (חֶרֶב פִּיפִיּוֹת) and chains for kings, reflects the concept of "holy war" where God fights on behalf of His people. The expression "the judgment written" (מִשְׁפָּט כָּתוּב, v. 9) may refer to prophetic judgments against the nations or to God’s sovereign decree concerning justice.

## Key Terms

**Chasidim** (חֲסִידִים, "the faithful/pious") appears three times in the psalm (vv. 1, 5, 9) and denotes those who live in covenant faithfulness with God. The word is derived from *chesed* (covenant love) and describes people who both receive and practice this loyalty.

**Anavim** (עֲנָוִים, "the humble/poor", v. 4) refers to those who, in their vulnerability, are completely dependent on God. The statement that the Lord "adorns them with salvation" suggests a change of status—from humiliation to honor.
