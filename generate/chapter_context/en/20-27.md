# Historical and Cultural Context for Proverbs 27

## Events and Customs

Verses 23–27 form a coherent section on livestock farming and agriculture, offering valuable insight into the agrarian society of ancient Israel. The description of sheep, goats, and their products reflects a pastoral economy where wealth was measured primarily in herds rather than coinage. The phrase "lambs for your clothing" (verse 26) refers to wool production, while "goat's milk" (verse 27) was a major source of nourishment. The wording that "riches do not last forever" and that "the crown does not endure from generation to generation" (verse 24) may reflect political instability during the monarchy, when dynasties could fall and fortunes vanish.

## Key Terms

The word **מַכְתֵּשׁ** (*maktesh*, verse 22) denotes a stone mortar in which grain was crushed with a pestle (**עֱלִי**, *ʿeli*). This was an everyday tool in Israelite households, and the image of crushing a fool in a mortar along with grain kernels underscores how deeply ingrained foolishness is—it cannot be "ground away." The word **שְׁאוֹל** (*sheol*, verse 20) refers to the realm of the dead in Hebrew thought—a shadowy underworld where the deceased reside. The parallel between sheol's insatiable appetite and human eyes illustrates the boundless nature of human desire.

## Chapter-Specific Findings

Verse 21 mentions **מַצְרֵף** (*matsref*) for silver and **כּוּר** (*kur*) for gold—respectively, a refining crucible and a smelting furnace. Archaeological excavations at Timna in the Negev and other sites throughout the Levant have uncovered metallurgical installations from the Bronze Age and Iron Age. These findings confirm the advanced metalworking technology that existed in the region. The image of refinement testing the purity of metal is here applied to how praise tests a person's character.
