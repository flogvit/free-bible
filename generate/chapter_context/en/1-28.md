# Historical and Cultural Context for Genesis 28

## People

**Mahalath** (מָחֲלַת) – Esau’s new wife is mentioned in verse 9 as the daughter of Ishmael and sister to Nebajoth. The name likely means "sickness" or "harp/flute." In Genesis 36:3, this same woman is called Bashemath. By marrying into Ishmael’s lineage, Esau demonstrates his attempt to please his father Isaac by choosing a wife from Abraham’s family, even though it was from the "rejected" branch.

## Places

**Beersheba** (בְּאֵר שֶׁבַע) – The starting point of Jacob’s journey (verse 10). Jacob leaves his family home and travels north toward Haran.

**Paddan-Aram** (פַּדֶּנָה אֲרָם) – The region around Haran in northwest Mesopotamia, where Isaac sends Jacob to find a wife from his mother’s family. The name means "plain of Aram." Jacob is sent specifically to Bethuel’s house, Rebekah’s father, to marry one of Laban’s daughters (verses 2, 5).

**Bethel** (בֵּית־אֵל, "House of God") – Jacob gives this place its new name (verse 19) after the dream in which he sees God. The place was originally called Luz (לוּז). The name change marks the site’s transformation from a common stopping point to a holy place where God revealed Himself.

## Events and Customs

**Stone Monument (matstsevah)** – Jacob’s action of setting up the stone as a מַצֵּבָה (matstsevah) and anointing it with oil (verse 18) reflects widespread practice in the ancient Near East. Such standing stones marked sacred places. The pouring out of oil was a consecration act that dedicated the object to divine use.

**Vow-Making (neder)** – Jacob’s vow in verses 20–22 follows the classic structure for conditional vows in the ancient Near East: "If [the deity does X], then I will do Y." The giving of a tenth (verse 22) as part of the vow shows Jacob’s commitment to give back to God from what he receives.

## Key Terms

**El Shaddai** (אֵל שַׁדַּי) – Isaac’s blessing in verse 3 uses this divine name, traditionally translated "God Almighty." In this context, Isaac connects the blessing with the God of the patriarchs and the covenant promises.

**Sullam** (סֻלָּם) – The Hebrew word for "ladder" or "stairway" in verse 12 appears only here in the entire Old Testament. The image illustrates the connection between heaven and earth, with God’s angels going up and down.

**Abraham’s Blessing** (בִּרְכַּת אַבְרָהָם) – In verse 4, Isaac explicitly transfers the covenant promises to Jacob. This includes the promise of land, descendants, and blessing for all families of the earth (verses 13–14), which God Himself confirms in the dream.

## Chapter-Specific Insights

Jacob’s dream of a סֻלָּם (sullam) with angels going up and down (verse 12) may reflect cultural familiarity with Mesopotamian temple stairways (ziggurats), which symbolized the connection between heaven and earth. The dream marks a turning point where Jacob personally encounters God and receives confirmation of the covenant promises given to Abraham and Isaac. The chapter shows the transfer of the blessing from Isaac to Jacob, confirmed both by his father (verses 1–4) and by God Himself (verses 13–15).
