## Places

Psalm 147 mentions Jerusalem and Zion by name (vv. 2, 12–14) in a context that clearly points to the rebuilding after the Babylonian exile. References to the Lord "building up Jerusalem" and "gathering the exiles of Israel" (v. 2), as well as his having "strengthened the bars of your gates" (v. 13), reflect the historical situation under Nehemiah, when the city’s walls and gates were rebuilt (Nehemiah 6:15–16; 7:1). The mention of "peace within your borders" and an abundance of wheat (v. 14) suggests a period of relative stability following the return.

## Events and Customs

The psalm’s description of God as one who "heals the brokenhearted and binds up their wounds" (v. 3) reflects the experience of the people in exile. The loss of land, temple, and national identity created deep collective grief, and this poetic expression conveys the spiritual healing that came with the return. The call to sing a song of thanksgiving with the harp (כִּנּוֹר, *kinnor*, v. 7) points to the resumption of temple music, which was central to post-exilic worship under the leadership of Levitical singers.

## Key Terms

The expression "the exiles of Israel" (נִדְחֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל, *nidchei Yisrael*, v. 2) is a technical term for those scattered during the exile and diaspora. The word חֶסֶד (*chesed*, v. 11), translated as "steadfast love" or "faithfulness," denotes God’s covenant loyalty that the people experienced through deliverance from Babylon. The conclusion in verses 19–20 highlights Israel’s special status by noting that they have received "his words" (דְּבָרָיו), "his statutes" (חֻקָּיו), and "his rules" (מִשְׁפָּטָיו) – a threefold expression for the Torah, which was reinstated under Ezra.
