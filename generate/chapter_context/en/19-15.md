# Historical and Cultural Context for Psalm 15

## Places

The psalm mentions two central places: God's "tent" (*ʾohel*) and his "holy mountain" (*har qodshekha*). The "tent" refers to the tabernacle, the portable sanctuary that Israel used during the wilderness wanderings and in the early settlement period. The "holy mountain" points to Zion, the hill in Jerusalem where David placed the ark of the covenant and where the temple was later built. This double designation may reflect a transitional period—either from the tabernacle to the temple, or a deliberate use of the older expression to emphasize continuity in Israel's worship.

## Events and Customs

Psalm 15 belongs to a genre called "entrance liturgies" or "temple entrance liturgy." This type of text was likely used at the temple gate, where pilgrims asked the question, "Who may enter?" and the priest responded with ethical requirements. Parallels are found in Psalm 24:3–6 and Isaiah 33:14–16. Similar entrance requirements are known from Egyptian and Mesopotamian temples, but in Israel the requirements are strikingly ethical rather than ritual—they concern justice, truthfulness, and integrity, not cultic purity or sacrifices.

## Key Terms

The word *tamim* ("blameless," verse 2) describes wholeness and integrity, not sinlessness. It is also used for sacrificial animals that had to be without blemish. *Neshekh* ("usury," verse 5) literally means "bite" and refers to interest rates that "bite" into the borrower's economy. The Mosaic law forbade Israelites from charging interest to fellow Israelites, especially the poor (Exodus 22:24; Leviticus 25:36–37). The expression *lo yimmot leʿolam* ("shall never be moved") uses a verb that describes stumbling or falling, and it gives a promise of lasting security for those who live righteously.
