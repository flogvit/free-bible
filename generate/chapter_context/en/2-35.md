# Historical and Cultural Context for Exodus 35

## People

**Bezalel son of Uri son of Hur** (בְּצַלְאֵל בֶּן־אוּרִי בֶן־חוּר, v. 30) from the tribe of Judah is introduced as the chief craftsman, "called by name" by God—an expression indicating special selection. His name means "in God’s shadow/protection." His abilities are described as divinely given rather than based on human experience alone.

**Oholiab son of Ahisamach** (אָהֳלִיאָב בֶּן־אֲחִיסָמָךְ, v. 34) from the tribe of Dan is mentioned as Bezalel’s assistant. His name may mean "my father’s tent" or "father’s tent." The fact that representatives from both Judah and Dan worked together illustrates that the sanctuary work united the entire people across tribal boundaries.

## Events and Customs

**The Sabbath command in verses 2–3** is repeated specifically before the construction work begins, establishing an important principle: even holy work for God must yield to Sabbath rest. The prohibition against kindling fire (לֹא־תְבַעֲרוּ אֵשׁ, v. 3) on the Sabbath is unique to this chapter and later became the basis for extensive rabbinic discussion about what constitutes "work."

**Freewill offering** (תְּרוּמָה/נְדָבָה) stands at the center of the chapter. Unlike imposed taxes, the sanctuary gifts had to come from "everyone whose heart moves him" (כָּל־נְדִיב לִבּוֹ, v. 5). The materials brought in verses 21–29—gold, silver, bronze, blue, purple, and scarlet yarn, fine linen, goat hair, tanned rams’ skins, and "tahash" skins—represent valuable resources that the people gave willingly.

## Key Terms

**"Wise of heart"** (חֲכַם־לֵב, v. 10, and similar expressions concerning women in vv. 25–26) is a Hebrew expression describing craftsmanship skill. In biblical thought, the "heart" was regarded as the seat of both intellect and will, so "wisdom in the heart" denotes practical knowledge and technical ability—not merely theoretical understanding.

**Terumah** (תְּרוּמָה)—"heave offering" or "gift"—comes from a root meaning "to lift up." The term indicates that the gift is "lifted" from the profane to holy use. This distinguished it from other types of offerings in that the material was not burned but used directly in the sanctuary.
