# Historical and Cultural Context for Exodus 3

## People

**Moses** (מֹשֶׁה, *Moshe*) appears here in his role as shepherd to his father-in-law Jethro (Hebrew: יִתְרוֹ, *Yitro*), who was a priest in Midian. Moses had fled Egypt and now lived as a shepherd in Midian—a background that gave him familiarity with the wilderness landscape where God's mountain was located.

**Jethro** is described as "כֹּהֵן מִדְיָן" (*kohen Midyan*), "priest of Midian." The Midianites were descendants of Abraham through Keturah (Gen 25:2) and had their own religious tradition. That Jethro was a priest suggests that the Midianites had organized worship.

## Places

**Horeb/God's Mountain** (חֹרֵב, *Chorev*) is the place where Moses meets God in the burning bush. Horeb and Sinai are often used interchangeably in the Old Testament and refer to the same holy mountain region. The phrase "behind the wilderness" (אַחַר הַמִּדְבָּר) indicates that Moses led the flock to remote grazing areas.

**The Land That "Flows with Milk and Honey"** (אֶרֶץ זָבַת חָלָב וּדְבָשׁ) describes Canaan. This was a standard phrase in the ancient Near East for fertile land. "Honey" (דְּבָשׁ) can refer to both bee honey and date syrup, as both were common sweeteners in the region. The six nations mentioned (Canaanites, Hittites, Amorites, Perizzites, Hivites, and Jebusites) represent the ethnic diversity of the area.

## Events and Customs

**Removing Sandals** (שַׁל־נְעָלֶיךָ מֵעַל רַגְלֶיךָ, "take your sandals off your feet") on holy ground was a widespread custom in the ancient Near East. The action marks the transition from profane to sacred space and expresses humility and reverence. That Moses covers his face out of fear of seeing God (v. 6) reflects awe for the holy.

**The Council of Elders** (זִקְנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל, "the elders of Israel") whom Moses is instructed to gather (v. 16) was an established institution in Israelite society. The elders functioned as leaders and representatives for their tribes and clans, and their involvement was necessary for the people to accept Moses' message.

## Key Terms

**God's Name – אֶהְיֶה אֲשֶׁר אֶהְיֶה** (*Ehyeh asher ehyeh*), often translated "I am who I am" or "I will be what I will be," is revealed for the first time in this chapter (v. 14). The verb הָיָה (*hayah*) means "to be" or "to become," and the form אֶהְיֶה is in the imperfect tense (incomplete aspect), giving it a sense of both present and future. The name expresses God's self-existence, unchangeability, and faithful presence. It relates to the tetragrammaton יהוה (YHWH), traditionally pronounced "Yahweh." Verse 15 emphasizes that this name is to be used "forever" (לְעֹלָם).

**The Burning Bush** (הַסְּנֶה, *hasseneh*) was likely a thornbush of the type that grows in the Sinai region. The fact that the bush burned without being consumed (vv. 2–3) symbolizes God's presence as powerful yet not destructive. Here, fire is a sign of divine revelation (theophany).

**"Hebrews"** (הָעִבְרִיִּים, *ha'ivrim*) in verse 18 is the term the Israelites were to use when speaking to Pharaoh. The expression "the LORD, the God of the Hebrews" (יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵי הָעִבְרִיִּים) was used strategically in communication with the Egyptians.

**"Wonders"** (נִפְלְאֹת, *nifla'ot*) in verse 20 refers to the mighty deeds God would perform in Egypt. This prepares the reader for the plagues that follow in subsequent chapters.
