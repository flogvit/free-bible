## Key Concepts

### "Bor sha'on" and "tit hayaven" (v. 3)
The expressions "the pit of destruction" (בּוֹר שָׁאוֹן) and "the miry clay" (טִיט הַיָּוֵן) are powerful images drawn from everyday Israelite life. Water wells and cisterns were often used as temporary prisons (compare Jeremiah in the muddy cistern, Jer 38:6). Gloomy, mud-filled pits symbolized the chaos and hopelessness of the realm of the dead. Being lifted up onto "the rock" (סֶלַע) represents the opposite—stability and security in a landscape where solid ground was essential for life.

### "You have dug ears for me" (v. 7)
The Hebrew expression אָזְנַיִם כָּרִיתָ לִּי is unusual and debated. The verb "kara" (to dig/bore) suggests that God has shaped the psalmist's ears to hear and obey. This stands in contrast to the sacrificial terminology in the same verse and points toward a deeper understanding of worship: attentiveness rather than ritual practice. The Septuagint translated this as "a body you have prepared for me," an interpretation later used in Hebrews 10:5.

### "The scroll" (v. 8)
מְגִלַּת־סֵפֶר refers to a parchment or papyrus scroll. In temple service and at the royal court, scrolls were authoritative documents. The phrase "it is written about me" may refer to the king's obligations recorded in covenant laws (compare Deut 17:18), or to God's plan for each individual.

## Events and Customs

### Sacrifice versus obedience (v. 7-9)
The psalm reflects a tension in Israelite religion between external sacrificial acts and inner obedience. The verses mention four types of offerings—slaughter offering (זֶבַח), grain offering (מִנְחָה), burnt offering (עוֹלָה), and sin offering (חֲטָאָה)—but declare that God does not primarily desire these. This is not a rejection of temple service, but a prioritization also found among the prophets (1 Sam 15:22; Hos 6:6; Micah 6:6-8). The psalmist emphasizes that true worship involves having God's law "in one's inner being" and willingly carrying out His will.

### Proclamation in the assembly (v. 10-11)
"The great assembly" (קָהָל רָב) was the gathered congregation during festivals and important occasions at the temple. Public testimony of God's salvation was an integral part of the thank offering, where the delivered person shared their experience before the community. The psalmist mentions five aspects he has proclaimed: righteousness, faithfulness, salvation, grace, and truth—all central covenant concepts.
