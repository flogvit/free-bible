## Places

### Debir – the Most Holy Place

In verse 2, the psalmist lifts his hands toward "your holy debir" (דְּבִ֥יר קָדְשֶֽׁךָ). Debir was the term for the innermost room of the sanctuary, the Most Holy Place, where the ark of the covenant stood. The word may mean "the rear chamber" or possibly "the place of speech"—the location where God spoke to his people. That the psalmist prays toward this room shows that the sanctuary served as a focal point for prayer, even when one was not physically present there. This practice of turning toward the sanctuary during prayer later became standardized in Jewish piety (cf. Dan 6:11).

## Events and Customs

### Lifted Hands in Prayer

The expression "when I lift up my hands" (בְּנָשְׂאִ֥י יָדַ֗י) in verse 2 describes a common posture of prayer in ancient Israel. Raised hands symbolized both surrender and reception—a gesture of openness toward heaven. This bodily posture for prayer is well documented in Old Testament texts and in depictions from throughout the ancient Near East.

## Key Terms

### Tsur – Rock

The Hebrew צוּרִי (tsuri, "my rock") in verse 1 is a core expression for God as an unshakable refuge. In Israel's rocky landscape, mountain clefts and stone formations provided concrete protection from enemies, so the metaphor carried strong resonance.

### Meshiach – the Anointed One

Verse 8 mentions "his anointed" (מְשִׁיח֣וֹ), an expression that primarily referred to the reigning king. The king was God's anointed representative, and the welfare of the people was tied to his rule. The term later acquired messianic significance in Jewish and Christian tradition.
