## Places

Chapter 41 gives a detailed description of the temple building itself in Ezekiel’s vision, focusing on the Holy Place (הֵיכָל, *heikhal*) and the Most Holy Place (קֹדֶשׁ הַקֳּדָשִׁים, *qodesh haqqodashim*, v. 4). The dimensions given—the Holy Place at 40 by 20 cubits (v. 2) and the Most Holy Place at 20 by 20 cubits (v. 4)—are identical to Solomon’s temple as described in 1 Kings 6:2, 17, 20.

## Events and Customs

The chapter describes side chambers (צְלָעוֹת, *tsela'ot*) in three stories around the temple (vv. 5–11), totaling 33 rooms (v. 6). These correspond functionally to the side chambers of Solomon’s temple, which were used for storing temple equipment and treasures. The architectural solution in which the rooms were not built directly into the temple wall but rested on ledges (v. 6) was known from Mesopotamian and Levantine temple architecture. The wooden altar (v. 22), called "הַשֻּׁלְחָן אֲשֶׁר לִפְנֵי יְהוָה" (the table that is before the Lord), connects the sacrificial altar with the table of showbread, pointing to the daily worship involving bread offerings in the Holy Place.

## Key Terms

The cherubim (כְּרוּבִים) and palm motifs (תִּמֹרִים) that decorated the walls are central symbols (vv. 18–20, 25). In this chapter, the cherubim have only two faces—the human face (פְּנֵי אָדָם) and the lion’s face (פְּנֵי כְפִיר) (v. 19)—in contrast to the four faces in Ezekiel 1 and 10. The palm tree was a widespread symbol of life and fertility throughout the ancient Near East. The term "אֲטֻמוֹת" (vv. 16, 26) regarding the windows likely means "tapered" or "with latticework," a window type known from First Temple period architecture that allowed light while limiting visibility. The expression "עָב עֵץ" (v. 25) refers to a wooden threshold or beam at the entrance, an architectural element marking the transition into the holy room.
