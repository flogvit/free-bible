## People

The chapter introduces several historical figures confirmed through other sources. **Zedekiah** (Heb. צִדְקִיָּהוּ, "Yahweh is my righteousness") was Judah's last king, installed by Nebuchadnezzar as a vassal ruler after the deportation of his nephew Coniah (Jehoiachin). **Jehukal son of Shelemiah** is also mentioned in Jeremiah 38:1 and has been remarkably confirmed archaeologically (see below). **Shephatiah son of Maaseiah** was a priest who served as an intermediary between the king and Jeremiah on several occasions (cf. 21:1; 29:25). **Irijah son of Shelemiah son of Hananiah** served as an officer at the Benjamin Gate and arrested Jeremiah for alleged desertion. **Jonathan the scribe** owned the house that was converted into a prison—scribes (סֹפֵר) were high-ranking officials in the Judean court.

## Places

The **Benjamin Gate** (שַׁעַר בִּנְיָמִן) was located in the northern part of Jerusalem and led out toward the territory of the tribe of Benjamin. This was a natural exit route for Jeremiah, who himself came from Anathoth in Benjamin. **Jonathan's house** served as a prison and is described with the words בֵּית הַבּוֹר וְהַחֲנֻיוֹת ("the house of the cistern with cells"), indicating that it contained an underground cistern used as a dungeon. **The Guard Court** (חֲצַר הַמַּטָּרָה) was an open area within the royal palace where prisoners could be held under milder conditions than in Jonathan's house.

## Events and Customs

The Egyptian military expedition under Pharaoh Hophra (Apries, reigned c. 589–570 BC), mentioned in verses 5–11, is a well-documented historical phenomenon. Egypt repeatedly attempted to support revolts against Babylon to maintain a buffer zone. The temporary withdrawal of Babylonian forces gave Judah's leaders false hope, which Jeremiah explicitly warned against. The practice of sending messengers to a prophet to request intercession (v. 3) reflects a common custom in times of crisis, also known from 2 Kings 19:2–4.

## Key Terms

The expression "אֶל־הַכַּשְׂדִּים אַתָּה נֹפֵל" (v. 13, "you are falling/deserting to the Chaldeans") uses the verb נפל in the sense of "defect to the enemy"—a technical term for desertion that also appears in Akkadian texts. The word מְדֻקָּרִים (v. 10, "pierced") describes mortally wounded soldiers and underscores the inevitability of the prophecy: even a defeated enemy would complete Jerusalem's destruction.

## Chapter-Specific Findings

In 2005, a **bulla** (clay seal) was discovered in excavations in the City of David with the inscription "ליהוכל בן שלמיהו בן שבי" ("Belonging to Jehukal son of Shelemjahu son of Shovi"). This is almost certainly the same person mentioned in verse 3, making him one of very few biblical figures confirmed both in the text and archaeologically. The discovery confirms the existence of the aristocratic network surrounding King Zedekiah.
