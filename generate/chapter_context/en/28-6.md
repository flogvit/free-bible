## Places

**Gilead** (verse 8) was a mountainous region east of the Jordan River, part of the Northern Kingdom in Hosea’s time. When Hosea calls it "a city of evildoers, full of bloody footprints," he suggests that the area had become a center for violence and lawlessness, possibly linked to political assassinations or banditry along trade routes.

**Shechem** (verse 9) was one of the oldest and most important cities in Israel, strategically located between the mountains of Gerizim and Ebal. The city served as a city of refuge and had long been a central place of worship. Hosea’s accusation that priests commit murder "on the way to Shechem" may refer to actual ambushes along the pilgrimage route or serve as a symbolic critique of the priesthood’s moral decay.

## Key Terms

**Chesed** (חֶסֶד, verses 4 and 6) is a central covenant term meaning loyal love or covenant faithfulness. When God says that Israel’s *chesed* is "like the morning mist and like the dew that disappears early" (verse 4), he describes a shallow, fleeting devotion lacking endurance. In verse 6, God declares that he desires *chesed* rather than sacrifice—not because sacrifice is wrong in itself, but because ritual practice without heartfelt faithfulness is worthless.

**"Two days... on the third day"** (verse 2) uses a common Semitic expression for a short, limited period ending in restoration. The image of "living before his face" expresses hope for renewed fellowship with God. However, the context (verses 4–6) shows that God’s response to this confession is to point out the people’s fleeting faithfulness.

## Events and Customs

Verse 6 expresses a prophetic critique of cultic practice found among several prophets (cf. 1 Sam 15:22; Isa 1:11–17; Amos 5:21–24). Hosea rejects sacrifice without *chesed* (covenant faithfulness) and *da'at Elohim* (knowledge of God, which implies a personal relationship). This was not a rejection of the sacrificial system itself, but of formal religion detached from ethical living.

Assyrian annals from the 700s BCE document political instability in Israel/Ephraim marked by repeated royal assassinations and power struggles. Several Israelite kings were killed by their own officials during this period (cf. 2 Kgs 15). Hosea’s description of Gilead as "full of bloody footprints" and priests who "murder on the way" (verses 8–9) aligns with this picture of a society characterized by political violence and moral collapse.
