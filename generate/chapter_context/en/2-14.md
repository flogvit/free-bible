# Historical and Cultural Context for Exodus 14

## Places

The chapter mentions several geographical locations that have been the subject of much discussion. **Pi-Hahiroth** (פִּי הַחִירֹת) likely means "mouth of the canals" or "the passage," but its exact location remains unknown. **Migdol** (מִגְדֹּל) means "tower" or "fortress" in Hebrew and was a common place name in Egyptian border regions; Egyptian texts mention several places called "Migdol." **Baal-Zephon** (בַּעַל צְפֹן) was probably a place named after the Canaanite god Baal-Zaphon, who was associated with seafaring and storms. This suggests that there was a temple or shrine dedicated to this god in the area.

The "sea" (הַיָּם) itself in this chapter has traditionally been identified as the Red Sea. The expression "Yam Suph" (יַם־סוּף), used in Exodus 15:4 and elsewhere for this sea, means "Sea of Reeds" or "Reed Sea." Modern scholars have proposed several alternatives for its location, including lakes on the Isthmus of Suez such as Lake Timsah or the Bitter Lakes.

Egyptian sources confirm the use of border fortresses and watchtowers (migdol) along Egypt's eastern frontier. Papyrus Anastasi VI (13th century BCE) describes Egyptian border guards reporting on population movements—relevant background for the places mentioned in verses 2 and 9.

## Events and Customs

Pharaoh's pursuit with chariots reflects Egyptian military practice from the New Kingdom period. The text mentions 600 "chosen chariots" (רֶכֶב בָּחוּר) plus all of Egypt's other chariots. The term **shelishim** (שָׁלִשִׁם) in verse 7, often translated as "officers" or "commanders," has an uncertain etymology. A common interpretation links it to the number three (שָׁלוֹשׁ), possibly referring to a third man on the chariot or an officer rank.

The Israelites are described in verse 8 as going out "with a high hand" (בְּיָד רָמָה), an expression denoting boldness and triumph—they left Egypt openly, not as fugitives. Pharaoh's reaction reveals the economic reality: the loss of slave labor was a significant cost to Egypt, which motivated the pursuit.

## Key Terms

**God's glory** (וְאִכָּבְדָה) is a central theme in this chapter. The Hebrew verb כבד (kavod) means to become "weighty" or "glorified." God repeatedly declares that he will be glorified through Pharaoh—his power will be demonstrated so that both Egypt and Israel will recognize who YHWH is.

**The morning watch** (אַשְׁמֹרֶת הַבֹּקֶר) in verse 24 refers to the last of the three night watches, approximately from 02:00 until sunrise. This was the time when God intervened and caused confusion in the Egyptian camp.

The expression **YHWH will fight for you** (יְהוָה יִלָּחֵם לָכֶם) in verse 14 introduces an important theological concept: "holy war," where God himself is the warrior, and the people's role is to stand still and witness his salvation.
