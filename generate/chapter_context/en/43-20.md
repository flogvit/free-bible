# Historical and Cultural Context for John 20

## People

**Mary Magdalene** (Μαρία ἡ Μαγδαληνή) is the first witness to the empty tomb and the risen Jesus. The fact that a woman was the first resurrection witness was culturally startling, since women's testimony held limited legal value in Jewish judicial practice.

**Thomas** (Θωμᾶς), also called Didymos (Δίδυμος, "twin"), was absent during Jesus' first appearance (v. 24) and demands physical proof before he will believe (v. 25). His nickname Didymos is the Greek translation of the Aramaic "Te'oma" (twin).

## Places

**The Tomb** (τὸ μνημεῖον) is described as a rock-cut tomb with a rolling stone. Such tombs were typical for wealthy Jewish families in the Jerusalem area at that time. Archaeological finds in the Jerusalem region show examples of first-century burial chambers with low entrances requiring one to bend down (παρακύψας, v. 5) to look inside.

**The Room with Locked Doors** (vv. 19, 26) where the disciples were gathered was likely a private house in Jerusalem. The expression "for fear of the Jews" (διὰ τὸν φόβον τῶν Ἰουδαίων) refers to the Jewish authorities, not the Jewish people in general.

## Events and Customs

**Burial Practices**: The text specifically mentions the linen cloths (ὀθόνια) and the face cloth (σουδάριον) that were left in the tomb. Jewish burial custom involved washing and anointing the body, then wrapping it in linen. The face cloth was used to cover the face. That these lay neatly behind, with the face cloth "rolled up in a place by itself" (ἐντετυλιγμένον εἰς ἕνα τόπον), indicates this was not a grave robbery.

**The Time Reference** "on the first day of the week" (τῇ μιᾷ τῶν σαββάτων) refers to Sunday, the day after the Sabbath. That Mary came "while it was still dark" (σκοτίας ἔτι οούσης) reflects the Jewish custom of waiting until the Sabbath had ended before completing burial rituals.

## Key Terms

**Ραββουνι** (Rabbouni, v. 16) is an Aramaic honorific title meaning "my master/teacher." The form is more intimate and reverent than the usual "rabbi" and was used as a form of address for highly respected teachers. John translates it into Greek as "Teacher" (Διδάσκαλε).

**"Μή μου ἅπτου"** (v. 17), traditionally translated "do not touch me," more precisely means "do not hold on to me" or "stop holding on to me." The verb form (present imperative with negation) indicates stopping an ongoing action. Jesus explains that he has not yet "ascended" (ἀναβέβηκα) to the Father.

**Εἰρήνη ὑμῖν** ("Peace be with you," vv. 19, 21, 26) reflects the Hebrew greeting "shalom aleichem." In the resurrection context, this takes on deeper meaning as the fulfillment of Jesus' promise of peace in John 14:27.

**Τύπον τῶν ἥλων** ("the mark of the nails," v. 25) confirms the method of crucifixion. Archaeological finds, including the skeleton from Giv'at ha-Mivtar (1968) with a nail through the heel bone, confirm that nails were used in Roman crucifixions. This find also shows that crucified individuals could receive a dignified Jewish burial.
