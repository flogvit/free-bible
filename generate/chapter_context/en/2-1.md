# Historical and Cultural Context for Exodus 1

## People

**Pharaoh** is mentioned without a name, which is typical for Egyptian kings in biblical texts. The expression "a new king who did not know Joseph" (v. 8) may refer to a dynastic change, a new political era, or a deliberate political distancing from earlier alliances.

**Shiphrah and Puah** are the two midwives named (v. 15). Their names are Semitic, not Egyptian: Shiphrah (שִׁפְרָה) means "beauty," and Puah (פּוּעָה) may mean "glory" or be related to birth cries. The fact that only two midwives are mentioned for a large people may indicate that they were leaders of a larger group of midwives.

## Places

**Pithom and Rameses** (v. 11) are the store cities built by the Israelites. Rameses (רַעַמְסֵס) is usually identified with Pi-Ramesses in the Nile Delta, built during the reign of Ramesses II (c. 1279–1213 BC). Pithom (פִּתֹם, Egyptian Per-Atum, "House of Atum") is less certain but is often linked to Tell el-Retaba or Tell el-Maskhuta in the Wadi Tumilat. These cities were strategically located in the eastern Delta region, near Goshen where the Israelites lived.

**The Nile** (הַיְאֹר, ha-ye'or) is mentioned in v. 22 as the place where male infants were to be thrown. The Hebrew word is a loanword from Egyptian (*itrw/iteru*) and is used specifically for the Nile, not rivers in general.

## Events and Customs

**Forced labor and slavery** are described using several Hebrew terms. The expression *sarei missim* (שָׂרֵי מִסִּים, "overseers of forced labor") corresponds to the Egyptian institution of organized corvée labor. Egyptian sources from the New Kingdom document the use of Semitic workers (called *'apiru* or *shasu*) in construction projects, which aligns with the biblical account.

**The birth stools** (הָאָבְנָיִם, ha-ovnajim, literally "the stones") in v. 16 refer to a birthing device consisting of two stones or bricks that a woman sat on during childbirth. This practice is documented in Egyptian texts and illustrations and was common throughout the ancient Near East.

## Key Terms

**"Hebrew"** (עִבְרִיּוֹת/עִבְרִים) is used in this chapter as an external designation (vv. 15, 16, 19), here in the feminine form referring to Hebrew women. The word may be related to *'apiru/'habiru*, a social term known from many ancient texts of the second millennium BC for wandering or marginalized groups. In Exodus, it is used especially in conversations with Egyptians.

**"With harshness"** (בְּפָרֶךְ, be-farekh) in vv. 13–14 is a strong expression for brutal oppression that occurs only in connection with Egyptian slavery and later in the Mosaic Law as a warning against treating others in such a way (Lev 25:43, 46).

## Chapter-Specific Findings

Egyptian papyri from the reign of Ramesses II (Leiden Papyrus 348) document the distribution of rations to *'apiru* workers who hauled stones for construction projects in Pi-Ramesses. This corresponds remarkably well with the Bible's description of the Israelites' work with bricks and building projects. Egyptian illustrations from tombs in Thebes show Semitic workers engaged in brick production, which aligns with the chapter's description of labor involving clay and bricks (v. 14).
