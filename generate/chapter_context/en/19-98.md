# Historical and Cultural Context for Psalm 98

## Events and Customs

Psalm 98 is described as a "new song" (שִׁיר חָדָשׁ, *shir chadash*), an expression that in ancient Israel typically marked a fresh response to a new act of salvation from God. This type of song was sung when the people experienced divine intervention—whether military victory, deliverance from enemies, or restoration after exile. The psalm's language stating that "the nations have seen his salvation" may be linked to the period following the return from the Babylonian exile, when Israel's restoration became a visible testimony to the surrounding peoples.

The psalm mentions several musical instruments that were central to temple worship: *kinnor* (כִּנּוֹר, lyre/harp), *chatsotsrot* (חֲצֹצְרוֹת, silver trumpets), and *shofar* (שׁוֹפָר, ram's horn). According to Numbers 10, the silver trumpets were reserved for the priests and used during festivals and special occasions. The combination of these instruments suggests a solemn, liturgical setting—possibly connected to New Year celebrations or a enthronement liturgy where the Lord was hailed as king.

## Key Terms

The expression "his right hand and his holy arm" (יְמִינוֹ וּזְרוֹעַ קָדְשׁוֹ) is powerful imagery that throughout the Old Testament denotes God's direct, mighty intervention. The right hand symbolized strength and honor throughout the ancient Near East, and "God's arm" was a fixed expression for his saving power, especially linked to deliverance from Egypt.

The verb "clap hands" (יִמְחֲאוּ־כָף) applied to the rivers is a poetic personification in which creation participates in praise. This reflects a Near Eastern concept that all of cosmos—seas, rivers, and mountains—responds to divine presence. The Lord coming to "judge" (לִשְׁפֹּט) the earth refers primarily not to punishment but to establishing righteous rule and restoring proper order in the world.
