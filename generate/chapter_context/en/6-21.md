## Places

Joshua 21 contains an extensive list of 48 Levitical cities distributed throughout Israel. These cities represent a unique settlement pattern in which the Levites were scattered among all the tribes instead of receiving a contiguous territory. Shiloh is mentioned as the meeting place where the Levites presented their request (v. 2), and at that time it served as Israel's religious center where the tabernacle was located. Among the key cities are Hebron (Kiriath-Arba, vv. 11–13), which was one of the cities of refuge. Other cities of refuge explicitly mentioned are Shechem (v. 21), Golan in Bashan (v. 27), Kedesh in Galilee (v. 32), and Ramoth in Gilead (v. 38). Several of these cities have been confirmed through archaeology, including Gezer (v. 21), Taanach (v. 25), and Beth-Shemesh (v. 16).

## Events and Customs

The allocation of Levitical cities represents a unique arrangement in the ancient Near East. While priests in surrounding cultures were often tied to specific temples or sanctuaries, Israel's Levites were dispersed throughout the land. The term "מִגְרָשׁ" (migrash), often translated as "pastureland" or "open country," refers to an area around each city intended for the Levites' livestock. This arrangement ensured that the Levites had economic support while being able to serve the people religiously and educationally in their respective regions.

## Key Terms

The word "גּוֹרָל" (goral), meaning "lot" or "casting lots," appears several times in the chapter (vv. 4–8, 10, 20, 40) and emphasizes that the distribution was carried out under divine guidance rather than human preference. This was an accepted method in Israel for deciding important matters where God's will was sought. The distinction between "בְּנֵי אַהֲרֹן" (Aaron's sons)—the priestly Kohathites—and the other Levitical families (Kohath, Gershon, and Merari) is clear in the text. Only Aaron's descendants could serve as priests, while the other Levites had supporting roles in the sanctuary service.
