## Places

Joshua 16 describes the southern boundary of the inheritance of Joseph’s tribes, with particular focus on Ephraim’s territory. **Jericho** is mentioned as the starting point by the Jordan River, a strategically important oasis city in the Jordan Valley. **Bethel** (also called Luz) was an important religious center even before the arrival of the Israelites, known from the patriarchal narratives. **Upper and Lower Beth-Horon** were twin cities that controlled a key mountain pass between the coastal plain and the highlands—one of the most strategic routes in the entire region.

**Gezer** is specifically mentioned in verse 10 as a Canaanite city that Ephraim failed to drive out. Archaeologically, Tell Gezer has revealed significant fortifications from the Bronze Age. The city remained under Canaanite control until the time of Solomon (1 Kings 9:16), confirming the description in this chapter.

## Events and Customs

The conclusion of the chapter (verse 10) describes a practice in which the Canaanites in Gezer were put to **forced labor** (Hebrew: *mas-oved*). This was a widespread custom in the ancient Near East, where defeated peoples were required to perform labor instead of being driven out or killed. This solution represented a compromise that departed from the complete command to drive out the Canaanites, and the text suggests this had lasting consequences for the tribe of Ephraim.

## Key Terms

The expression **"goral"** (גּוֹרָל) in verse 1 refers to the casting of lots used to distribute the land. This was not gambling, but understood as a way for God to decide the distribution in a fair manner. The results were regarded as divinely guided.
