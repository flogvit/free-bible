# Historical and Cultural Context for Psalm 36

## People

The heading identifies the psalm as belonging to "the servant of Yahweh, David" (לְעֶבֶד־יְהוָה לְדָוִד). The title "servant of Yahweh" is remarkable and appears rarely in psalm headings—only here and in Psalm 18. This is an honorific title that elsewhere in the Old Testament is primarily given to Moses, marking a special status and closeness to God. That David bears this title underscores his role as God’s chosen king and the ideal ruler in Israel’s tradition.

## Key Terms

The psalm contains several central theological concepts important to understand. **Chesed** (חֶסֶד, "steadfast love" or "grace") occurs three times (vv. 6, 8, 11) and is a key word describing God’s covenantal faithfulness and loving care. The term denotes a love that goes beyond what is expected and remains faithful to the covenant relationship.

The expression **"in the shadow of your wings"** (בְּצֵל כְּנָפֶיךָ, v. 8) is a powerful image drawn from two possible sources: a mother bird’s care for her young, or the cherubim wings over the ark of the covenant in the Most Holy Place. This image expresses God’s protection and refuge for those who seek him.

**"The fountain of life"** (מְקוֹר חַיִּים, v. 10) is an image that carried deep resonance in the dry climate of the Middle East, where access to water literally meant the difference between life and death. The term is used here metaphorically for God as the source of all life and every blessing.

## Events and Customs

The description in verse 9 of being "satisfied with the abundance of your house" and drinking from "the river of your delights" likely refers to sacrificial meals in the temple. After certain sacrifices, worshippers ate portions of the sacrificial meat in a sacred meal within the courts of the sanctuary. This was a concrete experience of fellowship with God and his blessing, where believers were literally satisfied in God’s house. The image is expanded to describe the spiritual satisfaction and joy that come from living in God’s presence.
