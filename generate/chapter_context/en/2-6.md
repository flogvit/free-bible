# Historical and Cultural Context for Exodus 6

## People

This chapter presents a detailed genealogy of the tribe of Levi, with special focus on the lineage of Moses and Aaron. **Amram** married his aunt **Jochebed** (v. 20), which was permitted in this early period but later forbidden under the Mosaic Law (Lev 18:12–13). The genealogy serves to legitimize the Levitical priesthood and demonstrate the authority of Moses and Aaron.

The sons of **Aaron** are named: Nadab, Abihu, Eleazar, and Ithamar (v. 23). **Phinehas**, son of Eleazar, is also mentioned (v. 25). The genealogy begins with Reuben and Simeon (vv. 14–15)—the two eldest brothers—before focusing on the tribe of Levi (the third son), following the traditional birth order of Jacob’s sons up to the relevant tribe.

## Key Terms

**"I am the LORD" (אֲנִי יְהוָה)** is repeated several times and frames God’s promises in verses 6–8. This expression emphasizes God’s sovereign authority and covenant faithfulness. The divine name **YHWH** is revealed here in a new dimension—the patriarchs knew God as **El Shaddai** (בְּאֵל שַׁדָּי, "God Almighty"), but now the name YHWH is to be understood through the act of salvation.

The phrase **"uncircumcised lips" (עֲרַל שְׂפָתָיִם)** in verses 12 and 30 is a Hebrew idiom meaning speech difficulties or lack of eloquence. The term "uncircumcised" is used metaphorically for something hindered or inadequate—the same expression is applied to the heart (Lev 26:41) and ears (Jer 6:10).

**"Shortness of spirit" (מִקֹּצֶר רוּחַ)** in verse 9 literally means "shortness of breath"—an expression for exhaustion and discouragement resulting from brutal slave labor. The combination of physical fatigue and spiritual despair made the Israelites unable to receive the message of hope.

## Events and Customs

The **promises of deliverance** in verses 6–8 ("I will bring you out," "I will rescue you," "I will redeem you," "I will take you as my people") form a structured covenant formula resembling ancient Near Eastern treaty forms. These promises later became central to Jewish Passover liturgy, where four cups of wine are traditionally linked to the four statements of deliverance.

**"With an outstretched arm and great judgments" (בִּזְרוֹעַ נְטוּיָה וּבִשְׁפָטִים גְּדֹלִים)** is imagery often used in Egyptian royal inscriptions to describe Pharaoh’s military power. Here, the expressions are applied ironically to YHWH, who will defeat Pharaoh with the very kind of power the Egyptians boasted about. Archaeological finds of Egyptian reliefs show Pharaoh with a raised arm striking down enemies—now the God of Israel will demonstrate true power.
