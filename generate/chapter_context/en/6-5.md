# Historical and Cultural Context for Joshua 5

## Places

**Gilgal** (גִּלְגָּל) was Israel's first campsite west of the Jordan River and served as the operational base for the conquest of Canaan. The name is explained in verse 9 through a wordplay with the verb "to roll" (גָּלַל), since God "rolled away" the disgrace of Egypt from the people. The place was located in "the plains of Jericho" (עַרְבוֹת יְרִיחוֹ), the fertile lowland west of the Jordan.

**Gibeah-Haaralot** (גִּבְעַת הָעֲרָלוֹת, "Hill of Foreskins") was the place where mass circumcision took place. This place name preserved the memory of this significant event.

## Events and Customs

**Circumcision with flint knives**: God's command to use "חַרְבוֹת צֻרִים" (knives of flint/stone) reflects an ancient practice that was maintained even after metal tools became available. Flint knives were considered traditionally appropriate for sacred rituals (cf. Exodus 4:25). The Egyptians used similar stone knives for circumcision, which is confirmed both in texts and archaeological finds.

**The Passover celebration** (verse 10) was the first Passover celebrated in the Promised Land. The date "on the fourteenth day of the month, at evening" corresponds with the instruction in Exodus 12:6. That they were able to celebrate Passover required that the men be circumcised (Exodus 12:48), which explains the sequence of events in the chapter.

**The transition from manna to the produce of the land** (verses 11–12) marked the end of the wilderness period. The expression "מֵעֲבוּר הָאָרֶץ" (from the produce of the land) and "קָלוּי" (roasted grain) show that they ate from the local harvest—likely the barley harvest that ripens during this period (March–April).

## Key Terms

**"The disgrace of Egypt"** (חֶרְפַּת מִצְרַיִם, verse 9) is an expression with several possible interpretations: it may refer to the shame of slavery in Egypt, the Egyptians' mockery of Israel's wilderness journey, or the state of being uncircumcised which associated them with pagan peoples. Through circumcision, the covenant sign was restored for the new generation.

**"The commander of the Lord's army"** (שַׂר־צְבָא־יְהוָה, verse 14) is a title indicating a heavenly figure of high rank. The parallel to Moses' experience at the burning bush (Exodus 3:5)—with the command to remove sandals because the place is holy—suggests that this is a theophany (divine revelation), possibly identical with "the angel of the Lord" (מַלְאַךְ יְהוָה) who appears with divine authority.

## Chapter-Specific Findings

Archaeological finds of flint knives from the Bronze Age in the region confirm that stone tools continued to be used for ritual purposes. Khirbet el-Mefjer north of Jericho is one of several proposed sites for biblical Gilgal.
