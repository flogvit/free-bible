## Events and Customs

Psalm 5 describes a morning prayer practice that was central to Israelite piety. The expression "in the morning" (בֹּקֶר, *boqer*) is repeated twice in verse 4, emphasizing the importance of early prayer. The verb "to arrange" or "to set forth" (אֶעֱרָךְ, *e'erakh*) is otherwise used for arranging offerings on the altar, suggesting that the psalmist understands his prayer as a spiritual offering. Morning was regarded as a particularly favorable time to seek God, and the tradition of morning prayer continued in later Jewish practice with the shacharit prayer.

The reference to "coming into your house" and "bowing down toward your holy temple" (verse 8) points to worship practices at the sanctuary. The Hebrew word היכל (*hekhal*) refers to the temple building itself or the holy place. To bow down (הִשְׁתַּחֲוָה, *hishtachavah*) was a physical act of deep reverence, often with the face toward the ground. This was the standard form of worship before a king or deity throughout the ancient Near East.

## Key Terms

The heading contains the rare expression "אֶל־הַנְּחִילוֹת" (*el hannechilot*), traditionally translated "for the flutes." This may refer to a wind instrument that was to accompany the psalm, or it could be a musical direction. The word is related to the Hebrew term for "to bore holes," supporting the interpretation of flutes or pipe-like instruments.

The term "חַסְדְּךָ" (*chasdekha*, 'your steadfast love') in verse 8 – often translated as "steadfast love" or "mercy" – denotes God's faithful covenantal love. This is one of the most theologically rich words in the Old Testament, expressing the enduring, binding love that connects God to his people.

The image of God as a "shield" (צִנָּה, *tsinnah*) in verse 13 refers to the large body shield that covered the soldier from neck to feet, as opposed to the smaller round shield (מָגֵן, *magen*). The verse states that God's favor (רָצוֹן, *ratson) "crowns" or "surrounds" the righteous like such a shield – an image of complete protection.
