## Places

The psalm mentions two geographical names with symbolic meaning: **Meshech** (מֶשֶׁךְ) and **Kedar** (קֵדָר). Meshech was a people and region in northern Anatolia (modern-day Turkey), known from Assyrian sources and mentioned in Ezekiel 27:13 as traders. Kedar was an Arab nomadic tribe dwelling in the Syrian-Arabian desert, descendants of Ishmael (Genesis 25:13). Geographically, these regions lay in opposite directions from Israel—Meshech to the north and Kedar to the southeast. This combination is therefore likely not meant literally but serves as a poetic expression for living among foreign, hostile peoples far from God’s people and the sanctuary.

## Key Terms

The phrase **שִׁיר הַמַּעֲלוֹת** ("song of ascents" or "pilgrimage song") in verse 1 identifies this psalm as the first in the collection of pilgrimage psalms.

The word **רְתָמִים** (retamim) in verse 4 refers to the broom bush (*Retama raetam*), a desert plant that grows in the Negev and Sinai. Charcoal from this bush was known to burn long and intensely, making it valuable as fuel. The image of "the glowing coals of the broom bush" together with "sharp arrows" thus describes the destructive and lasting harm caused by false words.

## Chapter-Specific Insights

The psalm’s focus on **deceitful speech** and a **treacherous tongue** (שְׂפַת־שֶׁקֶר, לָשׁוֹן רְמִיָּה) forms its central theme. The psalmist cries out to the Lord in distress (v. 1) and asks for deliverance from those who speak falsely (v. 2). The rhetorical question in verse 3 suggests that God will punish the treacherous tongue, and verse 4 provides the answer with the image of a warrior’s sharp arrows and the glowing coals of the broom bush—an image of prolonged, intense judgment.
