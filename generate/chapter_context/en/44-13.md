# Historical and Cultural Context for Acts 13

## People

**Barnabas and Saul** were both prominent in the church at Antioch. This chapter marks the transition from "Barnabas and Saul" (v. 2) to "Paul and Barnabas" (v. 46), reflecting Paul's growing leadership role. The name change from "Saul" (Hebrew) to "Paul" (Roman) in verse 9 signals his ministry among the Gentiles.

**Simeon called Niger** ("the black one") suggests African origin. **Lucius of Cyrene** came from North Africa. **Manaen** (Greek form of the Hebrew Menahem) had grown up with Herod Antipas (σύντροφος means "foster brother" or "childhood companion at court"), showing that the gospel reached even the highest social levels.

**Sergius Paulus**, the proconsul of Cyprus, is historically confirmed. A Latin inscription from Soloi in Cyprus mentions a "Paulus" as proconsul, and an inscription from Rome mentions a "L. Sergius Paullus" as curator of the Tiber under Emperor Claudius. Cyprus was a senatorial province governed by a proconsul (ἀνθύπατος), exactly as Luke correctly states.

**Bar-Jesus/Elymas** represents Jewish magicians who were often associated with Roman officials as advisors. The name "Bar-Jesus" means "son of Joshua," while "Elymas" likely comes from the Arabic for "wise man."

## Places

**Antioch in Syria** was the third-largest city in the Roman Empire and a natural starting point for mission work westward. The city had a significant Jewish population and was known for its cosmopolitan character. From here, the travel route went via **Seleucia**, the port city 25 km west of Antioch at the mouth of the Orontes River.

**Cyprus** was Barnabas's homeland (4:36) and had a large Jewish population. The missionaries traveled from **Salamis** (on the east coast, with several synagogues) across the island to **Paphos** (on the west coast), where the Roman administration was based. Archaeological excavations in Paphos have uncovered significant Roman structures from this period.

**Perga in Pamphylia** was a major city about 12 km from the coast. Here, John Mark left the group. **Antioch in Pisidia** (not to be confused with Antioch in Syria) was located in the interior of Asia Minor on a plateau at an elevation of about 1,100 meters. The city was a Roman colony founded by Augustus and had significant Latin influence. Archaeological excavations have uncovered a large synagogue and a temple dedicated to the imperial cult.

## Events and Customs

**Laying on of hands** (v. 3) was a Jewish practice for transferring blessing or authority. Here it marks the church's recognition of God's call, not ordination in the later ecclesiastical sense. The combination with fasting emphasizes the solemnity of the act.

**Synagogue worship** (vv. 14–15) followed a fixed pattern: Shema (the confession of faith), prayers, reading from the Torah (the Law), reading from the Prophets (haftarah), an exposition, and a blessing. The synagogue leaders (ἀρχισυνάγωγοι) were responsible for the order of worship and could invite visiting teachers with rabbinic training to speak. Paul's gesture of raising his hand (v. 16) was typical for Greco-Roman speakers.

**God-fearing Gentiles** (οἱ φοβούμενοι τὸν θεόν, vv. 16, 26) and **proselytes** (σεβομένων προσηλύτων, v. 43) represent two groups: God-fearers were Gentiles who worshiped the God of Israel and followed moral commandments but were not circumcised. Proselytes were fully converted to Judaism. These groups formed an important bridge for the spread of the gospel.

## Key Terms

**Λειτουργούντων** (v. 2, "while they were serving") refers to public service or liturgical action. The word gave rise to our term "liturgy" and suggests that the church was gathered for organized worship.

**Δικαιωθῆναι** (vv. 38–39, "to be justified") is a central Pauline concept. Paul's argument is that the Mosaic Law could not provide full justification, but faith in Jesus can.

**Τὰ ὅσια Δαυὶδ τὰ πιστά** (v. 34, "the holy and faithful promises to David") is a quotation from Isaiah 55:3 (Septuagint) and connects the resurrection with God's covenant promises.

## Chapter-Specific Findings

An inscription found near Antioch in Pisidia mentions "Lucius Sergius Paullus" as one of the "curatores riparum et alvei Tiberis" (overseers of the banks and bed of the Tiber) under Claudius. This supports the identification of the proconsul on Cyprus and suggests that the family of Sergius Paullus had landholdings in the Pisidia region, which may explain why Paul traveled there specifically after Cyprus.
