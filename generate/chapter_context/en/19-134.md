# Historical and Cultural Context for Psalms 134

## Places

The psalm mentions two important places: "the house of the Lord" (the temple) and "Zion." As the final song of ascents (Psalms 120–134), Psalm 134 marks the pilgrims' arrival at their ultimate destination—the temple in Jerusalem. The phrase "from Zion" in verse 3 emphasizes that the blessing originates from God's dwelling, the place where heaven and earth meet in Israelite thought.

## Events and Customs

The psalm describes night service in the temple, which was an established practice in the Israelite temple ritual. The Levites were responsible for praise and service both day and night. This continuous worship ensured that God's house was never "empty" of praise. Lifting the hands (verse 2) was a common prayer posture in the ancient Near East, documented both in biblical texts and in iconography from surrounding cultures. The gesture symbolized openness toward God and reception of His blessing.

## Key Terms

The expression "servants of the Lord" (*avdei YHWH*) refers specifically in this context to the Levites and priests who served in the temple, not to God's people in general. The Hebrew word *qodesh* ("holy place/holiness") in verse 2 can be translated either as "in holiness" or "toward the holy place," creating a deliberate double meaning—the hands are lifted both with holy intent and toward the sacred place.
