# Historical and Cultural Context for Isaiah 47

## Places

**Babylon** (בָּבֶל, *Bavel*) and **Chaldea** (כַּשְׂדִּים, *Kasdim*) are the central geographical references in this chapter. Babylon was the capital of the Neo-Babylonian Empire and stood on the Euphrates River. "Daughter of Chaldea" was a poetic designation for Babylon and its people. Babylon was regarded as the most magnificent city in the ancient world, and the prophet’s description of its fall from "mistress over kingdoms" (v. 5) to slave reflects the dramatic change that had been prophesied.

## Events and Customs

The chapter contains several references to Babylonian religious practices. **Sorcery** (כְּשָׁפִים, *keshafim*) and **spells** (חֲבָרִים, *chavarim*) in verses 9 and 12 point to the widespread magical practices in Babylon. The Babylonians were famous for their magicians and conjurers who used various rituals to influence the gods and fate. **Stargazers** (הֹבְרֵי שָׁמַיִם, *hovrei shamayim*) and **astrologers** (הַחֹזִים בַּכּוֹכָבִים, *hachozim bakochavim*) in verse 13 refer to the Babylonian astrologers who were renowned throughout the ancient world. The Babylonians developed advanced astronomical observations and monthly horoscopes to predict the future. From a Hebrew perspective, this practice was viewed as idolatry.

The description of grinding meal with a hand mill (v. 2) and wading through rivers with bare legs and an uncovered veil represents the labor and humiliation of a slave woman—a dramatic contrast to Babylon’s self-image as a "delicate" (עֲדִינָה, *adinah*) mistress.

## Key Terms

**"I am, and there is no one else"** (אֲנִי וְאַפְסִי עוֹד, *ani ve'afsi od*, vv. 8 and 10) is a central expression that parodies God’s own self-description in Isaiah 45:5–6. Babylon’s pride is shown by the city attributing divine uniqueness to itself. This represents ultimate hubris—taking God’s place.

**"Virgin, daughter of Babylon"** (בְּתוּלַת בַּת־בָּבֶל, *betulat bat-Bavel*) in verse 1 uses "virgin" as a designation for a city that has not yet been conquered or dishonored by foreign armies. That she must "sit down in the dust" symbolizes total humiliation and loss of status.

**Atonement** (כַּפְּרָה, *kapparah*) in verse 11 is a technical term from the sacrificial system meaning to cover over or make atonement for sin. In this context, it is used ironically—the prophet declares that no magical "averting" will be able to prevent the coming judgment.

## Chapter-Specific Findings

Thousands of cuneiform tablets from Babylon confirm the prophet’s description of Babylonian magic and astrology. Particularly relevant is the collection *Enuma Anu Enlil*, which contains over 7,000 astrological omens, as well as numerous spell texts found in temple libraries. These show that astrologers indeed provided monthly reports to the king, just as verse 13 implies with the phrase "from month to month" (לֶחֳדָשִׁים). The Babylonian belief that the stars controlled fate stands in sharp contrast to the prophet’s message that Israel’s God alone controls history.
