# Historical and Cultural Context for Joshua 11

## People

**Jabin, king of Hazor**, appears as the leader of the northern coalition against Israel. The name "Jabin" (יָבִין) may mean "he understands" or "the wise one," and seems to have been a dynastic title for the kings of Hazor, since another Jabin is mentioned in Judges 4. His role as the initiator of the coalition reflects Hazor's position as "head of all these kingdoms" (v. 10). The text also mentions kings of Madon, Shimron, and Achshaph, as well as many unnamed kings from the hill country, the Arabah valley, the lowlands, and the heights of Dor.

**The Anakim** (הָעֲנָקִים) were a people known for their great height and posed a special fear to the Israelites since the report of the spies in Numbers 13. That Joshua destroyed them from the hill country, while some survived in Gaza, Gath, and Ashdod (v. 22), explains how Goliath from Gath could later be described as a giant.

## Places

**Hazor** (חָצוֹר) was the largest Canaanite city-state in northern Israel, located at Tell el-Qedah north of the Sea of Galilee. Archaeological excavations led by Yigael Yadin (1955–1958, 1968–1969) and later by Amnon Ben-Tor have revealed a massive city covering about 80 hectares in the Bronze Age—far larger than any other city in Canaan. The text's description of Hazor as "head of all these kingdoms" is confirmed by the Mari texts from the 17th century BC, which mention Hazor as an important trading city with diplomatic connections to Babylon.

**The Waters of Merom** (מֵי מֵרוֹם), where the coalition gathered, are traditionally identified either with Lake Huleh (now drained) or nearby springs. The site lies strategically on the main road between Egypt and Mesopotamia, making it a natural gathering place for the northern kings.

**Great Sidon** (צִידוֹן רַבָּה) and **Misrephoth-maim** (מִשְׂרְפוֹת מַיִם) mark the outermost points of the Israelites' pursuit northward and westward toward the coast. Misrephoth-maim likely means "burning waters" and may refer to hot springs or lime burning in the area.

## Events and Customs

**Hamstringing horses** (עִקֵּר, *ʿiqqer*) and **burning chariots** (vv. 6, 9) represent a deliberate rejection of advanced military technology. Horse-drawn chariots were the most feared weapons of the Bronze Age, and the Canaanites' military superiority lay precisely here. By destroying these resources rather than using them, Israel demonstrated that their victories came from the Lord, not from military strength. This principle is repeated in Deuteronomy 17:16, which forbids kings from acquiring many horses.

**Herem** (חֵרֶם) – dedication to destruction – is practiced consistently throughout this chapter. Only Hazor was burned (v. 13), while the other cities that "stood on their mounds" (עַל־תִּלָּם) were spared structural destruction. This may reflect a practical strategy in which these cities were to be taken over as future settlements.

## Key Terms

**"Cities that stood on their mounds"** (הֶעָרִים הָעֹמְדוֹת עַל־תִּלָּם, v. 13) refers to *tell* formations – artificial hills formed by repeated layers of settlement over centuries. These were strategically valuable and preserved for future Israelite use.

**"The Lord hardened their hearts"** (v. 20) expresses a theological understanding that the enemies' persistent hostility served God's purpose. The term parallels the hardening of Pharaoh's heart in Exodus and raises questions about divine sovereignty and human responsibility discussed throughout the biblical tradition.

## Chapter-Specific Findings

Archaeological excavations at Tell Hazor have revealed a massive destruction layer from the 12th century BC, with thick ash layers, collapsed walls, and shattered cult objects. The head of a statue of a Canaanite god was found hacked off – possibly deliberately destroyed. This destruction layer matches the text's description that Joshua burned Hazor with fire (v. 11). Scholars continue to debate the exact dating and who was responsible for the destruction, but the find shows that the city underwent violent devastation during this period.
