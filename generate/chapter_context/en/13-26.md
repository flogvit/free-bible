## People

The chapter introduces several prominent Levitical families with specific areas of responsibility. **Meshlemiah** (also called Shelemiah) from the family of Korah led the eastern gate guard. His son **Zechariah** is described as "a wise counselor" (יוֹעֵץ בְּשֶׂכֶל, v. 14), suggesting that the leaders of the gatekeepers had administrative functions beyond mere guarding. **Obed-Edom** is mentioned with 62 descendants and received special blessing from God (v. 5) – this is likely the same Obed-Edom who housed the ark of the covenant (1 Chron 13:14), and his blessing explains the large size of his family.

**Shebuel** (בֶּן־גֵּרְשׁוֹם בֶּן־מֹשֶׁה), a descendant of Moses through Gershom, had oversight of the treasuries (v. 24). This shows that Moses' descendants retained prominent positions in temple service. **Shelomith** (שְׁלֹמִית) and his brothers managed war spoils and holy gifts that David, Samuel the seer, Saul, Abner, and Joab had dedicated (vv. 26-28).

## Places

**Jazer in Gilead** (יַעְזֵיר גִּלְעָד, v. 31) was a city east of the Jordan. There David found "capable men" from the family of Hebron who were placed over the tribes of Reuben, Gad, and half-tribe of Manasseh. The city lay in a strategically important area for administering the Transjordanian tribes.

The chapter also mentions the various gates of the temple by compass direction: the east gate, north gate, south gate, and west gate at **Shalleketh Gate** (שַׁעַר שַׁלֶּכֶת, v. 16). This gate was located at "the way that goes up" and was probably a gate for removing waste or unused sacrificial remains from the temple area. **Parbar** (פַּרְבָּר, v. 18) was an area or structure on the west side of the temple. The meaning of the word is uncertain, but it likely refers to an outer court or annex.

## Events and Customs

**Casting lots** (vv. 13-16) was used to distribute the gates among the families "without distinction between young and old". This ensured that the distribution was regarded as divinely determined and fair, not based on human favoritism. Casting lots was common in Israel for important decisions where God's guidance was sought.

**The treasuries** (אוֹצָרוֹת, *otserot*) contained two types of wealth: gifts for temple operations and holy objects dedicated to the Lord. The chapter specifically mentions war spoils that were dedicated "to maintain the house of the Lord" (v. 27). This practice of dedicating war spoils to a deity was common throughout the ancient Near East.

## Key Terms

The term **שֹׁעֲרִים** (*sho'arim*, "gatekeepers") refers not only to guards but to trusted officials responsible for security, storage, and access control. The expression **גִּבּוֹרֵי חַיִל** (*gibborei chayil*, "capable men") in verses 6-8 describes not military strength but competence and reliability in service.

**מְלָאכָה הַחִיצוֹנָה** (*melakha hachitsonah*, "the outer work", v. 29) refers to administrative tasks outside the temple area – civil administration where Levites served as "overseers and judges" over Israel. This shows that the role of the Levites included both religious and civil administration in David's kingdom.
