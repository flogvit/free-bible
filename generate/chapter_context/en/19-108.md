# Historical and Cultural Context for Psalm 108

## Composition and Literary Background

Psalm 108 is a composite psalm, assembled from material taken from two earlier psalms: verses 2–6 are drawn from Psalm 57:8–12, and verses 7–14 from Psalm 60:7–14. The fact that the psalm bears the superscription "of David" (לְדָוִד) indicates that it was attributed to the Davidic tradition even though it is an editorial compilation.

## Places

The psalm mentions several geographical areas that reflect the historical kingdom of David. **Shechem** and the **Valley of Succoth** (verse 8) represent respectively the central highlands west of the Jordan and the Jordan Valley on the east side—areas under David’s control. **Gilead**, **Manasseh**, and **Ephraim** (verse 9) were northern tribal territories, while **Judah** is described as the king’s "scepter" (מְחֹקְק), emphasizing Judah’s role as the center of the kingdom.

**Moab**, **Edom**, and **Philistia** (verse 10) were neighboring peoples whom David subdued. The expression "my washbasin over Moab" and "cast my sandal upon Edom" are images of dominion and possession. The "fortified city" (עִיר מִבְצָר, verse 11) may refer to an Edomite fortress town.

## Key Terms

The expression **"I will awaken the dawn"** (אָעִירָה שָׁחַר, verse 3) is poetic imagery suggesting that the psalmist’s song of praise begins before daybreak—a way of saying that the worship is so eager it "awakens" the very day.

The term **חֶסֶד** (verse 5), often translated as "mercy" or "steadfast love", describes God’s covenantal love that extends "above the heavens"—a cosmic image of God’s boundless faithfulness to His people.
