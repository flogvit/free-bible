## Places

**Zion/Jerusalem** (צִיּוֹן / יְרוּשָׁלִַם): In this chapter, Zion and Jerusalem appear as the center of future restoration. Verse 5 mentions "every dwelling on the mountain of Zion" and "the meeting places", suggesting both private homes and public gathering spots, possibly including the temple area where the people assembled for festivals.

## Key Terms

**The Branch of the Lord** (צֶמַח יְהוָה): The expression in verse 2 uses the Hebrew word *tsemach*, meaning "shoot" or "branch". This later becomes a messianic title in prophetic literature (cf. Jer 23:5; Zech 6:12). Here it may refer either to a coming Davidic ruler or more generally to God’s blessing that causes the land to flourish again after judgment.

**Disgrace/Shame** (חֶרְפָּה): In verse 1, *cherpah* is used for the social disgrace of being unmarried and childless. In ancient Israel, this was a serious social stigma for women, since identity and economic security were tied to marriage and motherhood. The fact that seven women will share one man illustrates the dramatic population decline following judgment and war.

**The Cloud and the Fire** (עָנָן / אֵשׁ): Verse 5 clearly alludes to the wilderness journey, where God led Israel with a cloud by day and fire by night (Exod 13:21–22). This Exodus motif signals a new deliverance and divine protection.

## Events and Customs

**The Number Seven and Marriage Custom**: The fact that seven women seize hold of one man (verse 1) is a dramatic reversal of normal practice. Normally, the groom paid the bride price (*mohar*) and provided his wife with food and clothing (Exod 21:10). Here, the women offer to support themselves—they break all conventions just to bear a man’s name and avoid the shame of childlessness. The number seven symbolizes completeness and underscores total catastrophe.

**Purification and Judgment** (verse 4): The image of the Lord "washing away filth" (צֹאַת) uses terminology from ritual cleansing. *Tso'ah* denotes excrement or dirty waste. The "Spirit of judgment" (*ruach mishpat*) and the "Spirit of burning" (*ruach ba'er*) combine legal and purifying aspects—judgment that cleanses, not merely punishes.
