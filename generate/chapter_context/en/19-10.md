# Historical and Cultural Context for Psalm 10

## Events and Customs

Psalm 10 describes a social reality in which the rich and powerful exploit the weak through economic oppression. The images of the wicked who "lies in ambush in the villages" (v. 8) and "hides like a lion in the thicket" (v. 9) reflect the dangers of travel and trade in the ancient Near East. Highway robbery and attacks on defenseless travelers were widespread problems. The wicked is also described as one who "seizes the poor when he drags him into his net" (v. 9), an image that may allude to debt traps and economic exploitation, where the poor were forced into debt slavery or lost their land and property.

## Key Terms

The Hebrew word *ḥēlkāh* (חֵלְכָה) in verses 8, 10, and 14 is rare and is often translated as "helpless" or "weak." The word occurs only in this psalm in the Old Testament and describes the most vulnerable members of society. The term *bōṣēaʿ* (בֹּצֵעַ) in verse 3, meaning "the greedy one" or "one who seeks unjust gain," refers to someone who acquires wealth through oppression. This concept is closely linked to the prophets' criticism of social injustice. The phrase "says in his heart" (*ʾāmar bĕlibbô*, אָמַר בְּלִבּוֹ) is repeated three times (vv. 6, 11, 13) and reveals the inner thought pattern of the wicked—a conviction that God neither sees nor intervenes.

## Chapter-Specific Insights

The text reflects a social pattern well documented in Old Testament law and prophetic literature. Laws concerning the protection of widows, orphans, and the poor show that exploitation of vulnerable groups was a persistent problem. This type of social injustice is well attested as an actual condition in Israelite society, confirming that Psalm 10 addresses real situations rather than purely theoretical scenarios.
