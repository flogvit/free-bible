## People

**Jonah son of Amittai** (יוֹנָה בֶן־אֲמִתַּי) is introduced as the recipient of God’s word. He responds to the call by fleeing in the opposite direction from Nineveh, which establishes the central conflict of the chapter.

**The sailors** (הַמַּלָּחִים, *hammallaḥim*) and **the ship’s captain** (רַב הַחֹבֵל, *rav haḥovel*) are Gentiles who first cry out to their own gods (v. 5), but by the end of the chapter fear the LORD, offer sacrifices to him, and make vows (v. 16). Their spiritual development stands in contrast to Jonah’s flight.

## Places

**Nineveh** (נִינְוֵה) is described as "the great city" (הָעִיר הַגְּדוֹלָה), a place of wickedness that Jonah is commissioned to preach against. Instead, Jonah chooses to flee in the opposite direction.

**Tarshish** (תַּרְשִׁישׁ) is mentioned three times in the chapter as Jonah’s destination for flight—a distant location in the western Mediterranean, opposite Nineveh. This place highlights the extent of Jonah’s escape from God’s call.

**Joppa** (יָפוֹ) was the port city on the Mediterranean coast where Jonah found a ship bound for Tarshish. The city served as Israel’s main gateway to the Mediterranean Sea.

## Events and Customs

**Casting lots** (גוֹרָלוֹת, *goralot*) is used by the sailors to identify who caused the storm (v. 7). This was a common practice in ancient times for discerning divine will, also known in Israel (Prov 16:33). The lot falls on Jonah, and the result is confirmed by his own confession.

**Sacrifices and vows** (v. 16) reflect the sailors’ conversion to worship of the LORD. The vows (נְדָרִים, *nedarim*) likely involved commitments to future actions or gifts to the God of Israel.

## Key Terms

The expression **"from the presence of the LORD"** (מִלִּפְנֵי יְהוָה, *milliphne YHWH*) is repeated three times in the chapter (v. 3 twice, v. 10) and emphasizes Jonah’s attempt to escape God’s presence and service.

The verb **"to go down"** (יָרַד, *yarad*) is used repeatedly: Jonah goes down to Joppa (v. 3), down into the ship, down into the inner part of the ship (v. 5). This downward movement symbolizes his flight away from God, in contrast to the command "arise" (קוּם, *qum*) in verses 2 and 6.
