## People

**Eliashib** (אֶלְיָשִׁיב) was the high priest in Jerusalem at this time and had a family connection with Tobiah, one of Nehemiah’s main opponents. By giving Tobiah a room within the temple complex itself, Eliashib committed a serious violation of the sanctuary’s holiness.

**Tobiah** (טוֹבִיָּה) was an Ammonite and had been one of the leading opponents of Jerusalem’s rebuilding (Neh 2:10; 4:3). Ironically, his name is theophoric ("YHWH is good"), which may indicate that his family had some connection to Israelite religion, perhaps through mixed marriages.

**Sanballat the Horonite** (סַנְבַלַּט הַחֹרֹנִי) was likely the governor of Samaria. The fact that one of the high priest’s grandsons married Sanballat’s daughter (verse 28) shows how political alliances were formed through marriage.

## Events and Customs

**Exclusion of Ammonites and Moabites** (verses 1–3) refers to the legislation in Deut 23:4–6. The reason given was that these nations did not show hospitality during Israel’s wilderness journey and that Moab hired Balaam to curse Israel. The public reading of the Torah followed by immediate practical application was typical for post-exilic assemblies.

**Temple storage rooms** (לִשְׁכָּה) were chambers in the temple complex used to store tithes, offerings, incense, and sacred vessels. Giving such a room to a foreigner was a serious desecration. Nehemiah’s response—throwing out Tobiah’s belongings and ordering purification—followed the principles for ritually cleansing holy space.

**Sabbath breaking** (verses 15–22) illustrates the economic temptation that the day of rest presented. Wine pressing, transporting goods, and trading were all forbidden. Phoenician merchants from Tyre sold their wares in Jerusalem on the Sabbath. Nehemiah’s measures to close the city gates at sunset and post guards were practical solutions to a persistent problem.

## Key Terms

**"Judean"** (יְהוּדִית) in verse 24 refers to the Judean language, that is, Hebrew as spoken in Judah at that time. The fact that children could not speak this language but instead spoke "Ashdodite" (a Philistine language) and other foreign tongues was seen as a loss of cultural and religious identity.

**"Mixed crowd"** (עֵרֶב) in verse 3 means "mixture" or "foreign admixture" and is used here to describe people of mixed descent who were separated from the assembly. The term underscores concern for preserving the people’s religious and ethnic identity.

**"Remember me, O my God"** (זָכְרָה־לִּי) is a prayer formula that appears several times in this chapter (verses 14, 22, 31). This formula asks God to remember and reward good deeds.
