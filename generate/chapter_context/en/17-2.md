## People

**Mordecai** is introduced here with his full genealogy: son of Jair, son of Shimei, son of Kish, a Benjamite. This lineage deliberately connects him to the family of King Saul (1 Sam 9:1-2), which becomes significant later in the conflict with Haman the Agagite. The text places his family’s exile to Babylon during the deportation under King Jehoiachin in 597 BC, making Mordecai part of the Jewish diaspora in the third or fourth generation. He sat at the king’s gate (שַׁעַר־הַמֶּלֶךְ), an administrative center where royal officials worked—a position that gave him access to important information.

**Esther** (Hebrew: אֶסְתֵּר) bears a name likely derived from the Persian word for "star" or from the goddess Ishtar. Her Hebrew name, Hadassah (הֲדַסָּה), means "myrtle"—a plant that symbolizes righteousness in Jewish tradition. Having two names reflects the dual identity of Jews living in the diaspora. The text emphasizes that she concealed her Jewish background at Mordecai’s instruction (verses 10, 20). As an orphan raised by her cousin Mordecai, she represents the vulnerable members of society whom God exalts.

**Hegai** (הֵגַי/הֵגֶא) was a eunuch (סָרִיס) and overseer of the virgins. He showed special favor to Esther (וַתִּשָּׂא חֶסֶד לְפָנָיו), quickly providing her with beauty treatments, food rations, seven maidservants, and the best place in the women’s quarters (verse 9). Esther followed his advice regarding what she should take with her to the king (verse 15).

**Shashgaz** (שַׁעֲשְׁגַז) was the overseer of the concubines (הַפִּילַגְשִׁים)—women who had spent one night with the king but were not chosen as queen. They returned to "the second women’s quarters" under his supervision (verse 14).

**Bigthan and Teresh** (בִּגְתָן וָתֶרֶשׁ) were two of the king’s eunuchs who served as doorkeepers (מִשֹּׁמְרֵי הַסַּף). They became angry and plotted to lay hands on King Ahasuerus, but Mordecai learned of the conspiracy and informed Esther, who told the king in Mordecai’s name. The two men were hanged (verses 21-23).

## Places

**The Women’s Quarters** (בֵּית הַנָּשִׁים) in Susa was the harem where royal women lived. Archaeological excavations at Susa have uncovered a large palace complex with separate wings, consistent with the text’s description of distinct areas for virgins and concubines ("the second women’s quarters," verse 14).

## Events and Customs

**The Beauty Treatment** described lasted twelve months: six months with myrrh oil and six months with fragrant ointments and cosmetics. This reflects Persian luxury culture and the use of costly imported goods. Myrrh (מֹר) was especially valuable and imported from Arabia. The lengthy preparation period served both practical and ritual purposes, ensuring that the women were fully adapted to court life.

**The Coronation Feast** (מִשְׁתֵּה אֶסְתֵּר) and the tax relief (הֲנָחָה) following Esther’s elevation were typical Persian ways of marking important events. Persian kings were known for their generosity on such occasions, which strengthened loyalty to the crown.

## Key Terms

**Foster Father** (אֹמֵן) in verse 7 describes Mordecai’s role toward Esther. The word means "foster father" or "guardian," referring to someone who assumes full parental responsibility for a child. The same term is used regarding Naomi’s care for Obed (Ruth 4:16).

**The Month of Tebeth** (טֵבֵת) in verse 16 is the Babylonian name for the tenth month (December-January), showing how Jews had adopted the Babylonian calendar during the exile.

**The Book of Chronicles** (סֵפֶר דִּבְרֵי הַיָּמִים) is mentioned in verse 23—the official Persian court record where important events were documented. The fact that Mordecai’s exposure of the conspiracy was written there becomes crucial later in the story (chapter 6).

## Chapter-Specific Insights

Persian administrative texts from Persepolis, known as the "Persepolis Fortification Tablets," confirm the system of food rations and gifts ("rations") for court officials, as implied in verses 9 and 18. These texts also show that the court at Susa had an extensive administrative apparatus with many eunuchs in key positions, exactly as described in this chapter.
