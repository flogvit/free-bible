# Historical and Cultural Context for Exodus 40

## People

Moses stands central as the one who receives God's instructions and carries out the entire erection of the tabernacle. The repetition "as the Lord had commanded Moses" (כַּאֲשֶׁר צִוָּה יְהוָה אֶת־מֹשֶׁה) occurs in verses 19, 21, 23, 25, 27, 29, and 32, emphasizing his obedience and role as mediator between God and the people. Aaron and his sons are mentioned in connection with priestly consecration (vv. 12–15) and ritual washing (v. 31), marking the beginning of their priestly service.

## Events and Customs

The tabernacle was erected on the first day of the first month (בַּחֹדֶשׁ הָרִאשׁוֹן... בְּאֶחָד לַחֹדֶשׁ) in the second year after the exodus from Egypt (v. 17). The anointing ritual (מָשַׁח, *mashach*) described in verses 9–15 consecrated the tabernacle, its furnishings, and the priests for holy service. The oil symbolized divine selection and the transfer of holiness, marking Yahweh's exclusive ownership over what was anointed.

The ritual washing of the priests (vv. 12, 30–32) at the bronze basin (כִּיֹּר, *kiyyor*) expresses the requirement for cultic purity before holy acts. Verses 31–32 describe how Moses, Aaron, and his sons washed their hands and feet before entering the tent of meeting or approaching the altar.

## Key Terms

The expression **כְּבוֹד יְהוָה** (*kevod YHWH*, "the glory of the Lord") in verses 34–35 denotes God's visible, manifested presence. This term describes an overwhelming divine presence that fills the tabernacle so completely that even Moses cannot enter. This forms the climax of the chapter—God takes up residence among his people.

The cloud (עָנָן, *'anan*) and fire (אֵשׁ, *'esh*) serve as visible signs of God's presence and guidance—the cloud by day and fire by night (vv. 34–38). The movements of the cloud determine when Israel is to break camp or remain encamped (vv. 36–37), showing that God's presence now leads the people concretely through the wilderness.

The term **אֹהֶל מוֹעֵד** (*'ohel mo'ed*, "the tent of meeting") emphasizes the tabernacle's function as the place where God meets his people. The word *mo'ed* implies an appointed meeting or gathering, showing that the tabernacle was the divinely established meeting point between heaven and earth.
