# Historical and Cultural Context for Numbers 25

## People

**Phinehas** (פִּינְחָס) was the son of Eleazar and grandson of Aaron, thus belonging to the highest priestly line. His name is likely of Egyptian origin (Pa-nehasi, "the dark-skinned one"), reflecting the Israelites' stay in Egypt. His decisive action resulted in a "covenant of peace" (בְּרִית שָׁלוֹם) and a promise of an everlasting priesthood for his descendants.

**Zimri son of Salu** was a leader (נְשִׂיא) from the tribe of Simeon, meaning he was a man of significant rank. **Cozbi daughter of Zur** was the daughter of a Midianite tribal chief. Her name (כָּזְבִּי) may mean "my lie" or "the deceiver" in Hebrew, possibly reflecting a later interpretation of her name. The fact that both were of high social status underscores the seriousness of the event—these were not ordinary people but leaders who openly challenged Moses' authority.

## Places

**Shittim** (הַשִּׁטִּים, "the acacia trees") was the Israelites' last encampment before crossing the Jordan River into Canaan. The place was located in the plains of Moab, east of the Jordan, opposite Jericho. Its full name was Abel-shittim ("the meadow of acacias"). This area was fertile and lay along important trade routes, which explains the contact with Moabite and Midianite women. Archaeologically, the region is traditionally identified with Tell el-Kefrein or nearby sites in the Jordan Valley, though this identification remains debated.

## Events and Customs

**The Baal-peor cult** (בַּעַל פְּעוֹר) was a local variant of Baal worship associated with Mount Peor in Moab. Baal cults in the Canaanite cultural sphere often included ritual meals and sexually charged ceremonies intended to ensure fertility. The expression "the people began to commit sexual immorality with the daughters of Moab" (לִזְנוֹת אֶל־בְּנוֹת מוֹאָב) may refer both to actual sexual immorality and to spiritual "unfaithfulness" toward the Lord through idolatry—both meanings are likely intended.

**The execution method** described in verse 4 (וְהוֹקַע, "hang them up") probably involved killing the sinners and then hanging their bodies for public display "toward the sun," that is, in full daylight. This was a common practice throughout the ancient Near East to mark serious crimes and deter others. Assyrian and Babylonian texts describe similar practices.

**Phinehas' action** of driving his spear through the "tent" (הַקֻּבָּה) and into the woman's "body" (קֳבָתָהּ) uses rare Hebrew words. קֻּבָּה may refer to a special dome-shaped tent, possibly associated with cultic prostitution. The action stopped the plague and was understood as an act of atonement (וַיְכַפֵּר)—a term normally linked to sacrificial worship.

## Key Terms

The Hebrew verb **צָמַד** ("to join oneself," verse 3) describes an intimate attachment and is used here regarding Israel's connection to Baal-peor. The word suggests a covenant-like bond with the idol, the opposite of "holding fast" (דָּבַק) to the Lord.

The expression **בְּרִית שָׁלוֹם** ("covenant of peace," verse 12) given to Phinehas is unique. The word שָׁלוֹם in the Masoretic Text has a "broken" letter vav (וּ), which early rabbis interpreted symbolically—perhaps as a sign that true peace sometimes requires painful actions.

**קִנְאָה** ("zeal/jealousy," verses 11, 13) is a key word appearing five times in the passage. It describes both God's passionate demand for exclusive worship and Phinehas' intense response, which mirrored God's own attitude. This "holy zeal" became an ideal in later Jewish tradition.
