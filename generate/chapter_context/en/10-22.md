# Historical and Cultural Context for 2 Samuel 22

## People

The chapter is David's song of thanksgiving, sung "on the day when the Lord delivered him from all his enemies and from the hand of Saul" (v. 1). The psalm summarizes David's experiences of divine deliverance through his many conflicts.

## Key Terms

The chapter is rich in military and nature metaphors. The term *sela* (סֶלַע, "rock/cliff") in verse 2 was especially meaningful in the Israelite highlands, where cliffs provided natural protection against enemies. *Metsudah* (מְצֻדָה, "fortress") is used as a metaphor for God's protection, an image drawn from the mountain fortresses of the region. The expression *qeren yish'i* (קֶרֶן יִשְׁעִי, "the horn of my salvation") in verse 3 uses the horn as a symbol of strength, taken from the image of powerful oxen.

In verses 5–6, *beliyya'al* (בְלִיַּעַל) is used to describe the streams of death. This word literally means "without worth" or "destruction" and later became a name for evil personified. *Sheol* (שְׁאוֹל) refers to the realm of the dead in ancient Israelite cosmology.

The Hebrew term *shirah* (שִׁירָה, "song") in verse 1 marks this as a formal, poetic composition. The psalm also appears in almost identical form in Psalm 18.

## Events and Customs

The theophany (divine manifestation) in verses 8–16 describes God revealing Himself through natural forces such as earthquakes, fire, darkness, and storm—an established poetic pattern in the ancient Near East. Hebrew poetry depicts God riding on a *keruv* (כְּרוּב, cherub) in verse 11—winged beings known from both Israelite and broader Mesopotamian iconography.

## Chapter-Specific Findings

Verse 51 mentions the promise to David and his "offspring forever" (*le-zar'o ad-olam*), which links the psalm directly to Nathan's promise in 2 Samuel 7.
