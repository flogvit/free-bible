## Events and Customs

This chapter contains Eliphaz's third and final speech to Job, in which he moves from general arguments about God’s justice (verses 2–4) to direct accusations against Job (verses 5–11), before concluding with an appeal for repentance (verses 21–30).

Eliphaz’s accusations against Job reflect specific social obligations that were fundamental in the ancient Near East. The charge of taking a pledge from brothers "without cause" (verse 6) refers to the practice of seizing clothing or property as security for loans—actions considered serious breaches of social duty. Denying water to the thirsty and bread to the hungry (verse 7) constituted grave violations of the obligation of hospitality, one of the most basic virtues in nomadic desert societies. The accusation of sending widows away empty-handed and crushing the arms of orphans (verse 9) points to the most vulnerable groups in society, who were under divine protection according to ancient Near Eastern legal understanding.

## Places

Ophir is mentioned in verse 24 as a source of valuable gold. This was a legendary place known in antiquity for its exceptional gold. The reference serves as a cultural symbol of the highest form of wealth and prosperity.

## Key Terms

The Hebrew word "חוּג" (chug) in verse 14, translated "the vault of heaven," describes a circular structure or sphere. This reflects ancient Near Eastern cosmology, in which the sky was perceived as a dome or vault over the earth.

The expression "אִישׁ זְרוֹעַ" (ish zeroa) in verse 8, literally "man of arm," denotes a person with power and influence. The "arm" symbolized strength and authority in Hebrew, and the phrase describes one who uses their position to acquire land and wealth at the expense of others.
