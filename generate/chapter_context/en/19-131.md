# Historical and Cultural Context for Psalms 131

## People

The psalm is attributed to David (לְדָוִד), and the heading links it to the "Songs of Ascents" (שִׁיר הַמַּעֲלוֹת). The content of the psalm aligns with biblical portrayals of David as one who knew both greatness and humility—from his time as a shepherd to his patient attitude toward Saul, where he refused to seize power on his own initiative (1 Samuel 24; 26).

## Key Terms

The expression "גָּבַהּ לִבִּי" (my heart is not proud) uses "heart" (לֵב) in the Hebrew sense as the center of will and thought, not merely emotions. A "proud heart" was a well-known phrase for arrogance and rebellion against God (Proverbs 18:12; Ezekiel 28:2).

The image "כְּגָמֻל עֲלֵי אִמּוֹ" (like a weaned child with its mother) is central to the psalm. The word "גָּמֻל" means "weaned"—that is, a child who has passed the infant stage (usually around three years old in ancient Israel). This is a deliberate choice: the child does not cry for milk but rests contentedly with its mother without demanding anything. In Old Testament culture, weaning was an important transition that could be marked by celebration (Genesis 21:8).

The verbs "שִׁוִּיתִי" (I have made level/quiet) and "דוֹמַמְתִּי" (brought to silence) describe an active process—the psalmist has consciously worked to calm their soul, not merely passively accepted circumstances.
