# Historical and Cultural Context for Joshua 3

## Places

**Shittim (השטים)** was the Israelites' last encampment east of the Jordan, located in the plains of Moab. The name means "the acacia trees" and refers to the characteristic vegetation of the area. This place was also associated with Israel's sin involving Moabite women (Numbers 25), and departing from there marked a new beginning. The distance from Shittim to the Jordan River was approximately 8–10 kilometers.

**The Jordan River (הירדן)** was in flood at this time, "overflowing all its banks throughout the harvest season" (v. 15). Harvest took place in spring (April–May), when snowmelt from Mount Hermon caused the river to swell significantly. Normally, the Jordan is 27–30 meters wide and 1–3 meters deep, but during the flood it became considerably wider and much more difficult to cross.

**Adam (אדם)**, the city mentioned in verse 16, is identified with Tell ed-Damiyeh, about 25 kilometers north of the crossing point. **Zarethan (צרתן)** was located nearby. Interestingly, earthquakes in 1267 CE and 1927 CE are documented to have caused landslides precisely at this location, temporarily stopping the flow of the Jordan River.

## Events and Customs

**Consecration (התקדשו)** before a divine encounter was an established practice in Israel. This involved ritual cleansing, washing clothes, and sexual abstinence (cf. Exodus 19:10–15 before the revelation at Sinai). The purpose was to prepare the people to be in God's presence when He performed miracles.

**The position of the ark of the covenant** ahead of the people, at a distance of 2,000 cubits (about 890 meters), served several functions: it showed the way through unfamiliar terrain, demonstrated that God Himself was leading the people, and the distance highlighted the holiness of the ark. During the wilderness journey, the ark had also gone before the people (Numbers 10:33); here at the Jordan crossing, this aspect is especially emphasized.

## Key Terms

**"Lord of all the earth" (אדון כל הארץ)** is an unusual title linked to the ark of the covenant that appears twice in this chapter (vv. 11, 13). The expression underscores the universal sovereignty of Israel's God – He is not merely a local deity but ruler over everything, including the land and people of Canaan. This was a direct challenge to Canaanite religion.

**The seven nations** (v. 10) – Canaanites, Hittites, Hivites, Perizzites, Girgashites, Amorites, and Jebusites – represented a standard list symbolizing the complete population of the land. The number seven signified completeness in Hebrew thought.

## Chapter-Specific Findings

Geological studies at Adam/Tell ed-Damiyeh have confirmed that the area is prone to landslides from the high riverbanks along the Jordan. In addition to the historically documented cases in 1267 and 1927, there are geological traces of similar events in earlier times. Although this does not "prove" the biblical account, it shows that a natural mechanism exists exactly at the place mentioned in the text.
