# Historical and Cultural Context for 1 Chronicles 4

## People

**Jabez** (vv. 9–10) stands out in the genealogy with a personal narrative. His name (יַעְבֵּץ, *Ya'bets*) is linked to the Hebrew word for pain or sorrow (עֹצֶב, *'otseb*) through his mother's words: "for I bore him in pain" (כִּי יָלַדְתִּי בְּעֹצֶב). His prayer for blessing, an expanded territory, and protection from harm represents a theological reflection amidst the genealogies. The Chronicler likely included this story to show that individual faith and prayer can break through the collective history of the tribes.

**Caleb son of Jephunneh** (v. 15) is mentioned along with his sons Iru, Ela, and Na'am. Caleb is known from the wilderness wanderings as one of the two spies (along with Joshua) who trusted in God (Numbers 13–14). Kenaz is noted as Ela's son, linking this lineage to the Kenizzites—a people fully integrated into the tribe of Judah.

**Mered and Bithiah, Pharaoh's daughter** (v. 18) represent a remarkable connection between Judah and Egypt. The fact that an Israelite married an Egyptian princess testifies to contacts between these peoples not documented elsewhere in the Bible.

## Places

**Zorah** (v. 2, צָרְעָה) and many other cities mentioned in this chapter are located in the Shephelah region, the lowland between the coastal plain and the Judean hills. Zorah is later known as Samson's hometown (Judges 13:2). **Bethlehem** (v. 4) is identified here with Ephrathah, and Hur is called "the father of Bethlehem"—a designation indicating he was either the city's founder or a leading ancestor.

**Beersheba, Ziklag, Hormah**, and other cities (vv. 28–31) define the tribal territory of Simeon in the Negev desert. Ziklag is known from David's history as the town given to him by Achish, king of the Philistines (1 Samuel 27:6). Archaeological excavations at Tel Beersheba have revealed a significant Israelite settlement from the Iron Age.

**Gedor** (v. 39) and the area "eastward in the valley" where the Simeonites found pasture have traditionally been identified with the Gerar region near Philistine territory. The text notes that "Hamitic" inhabitants (מִן־חָם) lived there previously.

## Events and Customs

**Craft guilds** (vv. 14, 21, 23) offer a rare glimpse into economic organization in ancient Israel. "Geharashim" (גֵּיא חֲרָשִׁים) means "Valley of the Craftsmen," indicating that entire clans specialized in specific trades. Verse 21 mentions "the house of those who worked with linen" (בֵּית־עֲבֹדַת הַבֻּץ) in Beth Ashbea—linen was a valuable fine fabric. Verse 23 refers to the "potters" (הַיּוֹצְרִים) and those who lived in Netaim and Gederah "in the king's service"—early evidence of royal patronage of craftsmen.

**The expansion of the Simeonites** (vv. 39–43) describes military campaigns during the reign of King Hezekiah (c. 715–686 BC). They defeated the "Meunites" (הַמְּעוּנִים, likely a people from the Maon area) and took their pastures, and later attacked "the remainder of the Amalekites" in the mountains of Seir. This shows that tribe-based territorial expansion continued even during the monarchy period.

## Key Terms

The expression **"father of"** (אֲבִי, *'avi*) is used repeatedly not only for biological fatherhood but also to denote the founder or leader of a city or clan (e.g., "Hur... father of Bethlehem" v. 4; "Ashhur, father of Tekoa" v. 5; "Tehinnah, father of Ir-nahash" v. 12). This is a technical term in genealogical literature.

**"The records are ancient"** (וְהַדְּבָרִים עַתִּיקִים, v. 22) is a rare comment from the Chronicler himself, acknowledging that some of the information comes from very old sources. This provides insight into the author's method and historical awareness.

## Chapter-Specific Findings

Ostraca (inscribed pottery shards) found at Arad in the Negev from the 8th century BC mention several family names and place names corresponding to Simeonite settlements described in this chapter. Likewise, archaeological investigations have confirmed the existence of specialized craft centers in the Shephelah region, supporting the chapter's description of craftsmen clans serving under royal authority.
