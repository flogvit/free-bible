# Historical and Cultural Context for Jeremiah 44

## Places

The chapter mentions four specific locations in Egypt where Jewish refugees had settled: **Migdol** was a border fortress in the northeastern Nile Delta, near the traditional route into Egypt. **Tahpanhes** (Egyptian: Daphnai) was another border city in the delta where Jeremiah himself stayed (Jer 43:7-9). **Noph** is the Hebrew name for Memphis (Egyptian: Men-nefer), the ancient capital of Lower Egypt, a significant religious center with temples dedicated to Ptah and other Egyptian gods. **Pathros** refers to Upper Egypt (the southern region of Egypt), from the Egyptian "Pa-ta-resi," meaning "the southern land." This shows that the Jewish diaspora was already spread across a large geographical area in Egypt shortly after the fall of Jerusalem in 586 B.C.

## Events and Customs

Central to the chapter is the worship of the **Queen of Heaven** (מְלֶכֶת הַשָּׁמַיִם, *melekhet hashamayim*). This was likely the goddess Ishtar (Mesopotamian) or her Canaanite/Phoenician counterpart Astarte, possibly also associated with the Egyptian Isis in this Egyptian context. The cult included incense offerings (קִטֵּר) and drink offerings (נְסָכִים), as well as baking special cakes (כַּוָּנִים), which may have been shaped like the goddess or celestial bodies (v. 19, cf. Jer 7:18). The prominent role of women in this cult, with men's approval, reflects that this was a family-based household religion deeply rooted in Judah. The people's argument in verses 17–18—that prosperity followed when they worshipped her, while disaster came when they stopped—shows a clear cause-and-effect thinking linked to cultic practice.

## People

**Pharaoh Hophra** (Hebrew: חָפְרַע, Egyptian: Wahibre/Apries) is mentioned in verse 30 as a sign of the truth of prophecy. He ruled Egypt from about 589–570 B.C. and was the pharaoh who sent troops to help Zedekiah against Nebuchadnezzar (Jer 37:5–7), but had to withdraw. Historical sources, including Herodotus, confirm that Hophra was overthrown by his own general Amasis (Ahmose II) after a failed campaign against Cyrene in Libya. He was first deposed in 570 B.C. and later killed by his own countrymen around 567 B.C. Jeremiah's prophecy that Hophra would be handed over to his enemies was thus fulfilled in a way that contemporary listeners could verify.

## Chapter-Specific Findings

Archaeological findings confirm the presence of Jewish communities in Egypt at this time. The famous **Elephantine papyri** from the 400s B.C. document an established Jewish military colony on the island of Elephantine in Upper Egypt (the Pathros region), with its own temple for YHWH. Although these texts are from a later period, they testify to the continuity of Jewish settlement in Egypt after 586 B.C. In addition, archaeologists have found pottery with Hebrew inscriptions and Jewish personal seals in the Delta area, supporting the picture of Jewish presence in the places mentioned in Jeremiah 44.
