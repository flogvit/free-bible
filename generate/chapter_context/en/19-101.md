## Events and Customs

Psalm 101 is a "mirror for kings" or "prince's mirror"—a type of text in which the king publicly declares his ethical commitments and standards of governance. Such royal ideals were common in the ancient Near East; Egyptian and Mesopotamian texts contain similar royal declarations concerning justice and moral integrity, such as the prologue to the Code of Hammurabi (c. 1750 BCE) and Egyptian wisdom literature for kings. In an Israelite context, this was especially tied to the king's responsibility to uphold justice as God's representative. The phrase "morning by morning" (v. 8) likely refers to the king's daily administration of justice at the city gate, where legal cases were traditionally decided in the early hours.

## Key Terms

The expression "חֶסֶד־וּמִשְׁפָּט" (*ḥesed u-mishpat*, "steadfast love and justice") in verse 1 is a word pair describing the two pillars of good royal governance: covenant loyalty combined with fair administration of justice. "דְּבַר־בְּלִיָּעַל" (*devar-beliyya'al*, "worthless/evil matter") in verse 3 refers to what is morally abhorrent and destructive. The term "Beliyya'al" later developed in Jewish literature into a proper name for evil forces (cf. 2 Cor 6:15, "Belial"). The expression "רְחַב לֵבָב" (*reḥav levav*, "proud heart," literally: "wide heart") in verse 5 describes a person with excessive ambitions and arrogance—a trait especially dangerous within court circles.

## Chapter-Specific Insights

The psalm's focus on whom the king surrounds himself with reflects what is known from administrative texts from Mari and Ugarit regarding court officials and their reliability. The structure of the text—from personal piety (vv. 1–4) to public governance (vv. 5–8)—demonstrates the connection between the king's private character and his public responsibilities.
