## Animals and Nature

The chapter contains God’s continuing speech from the storm, focusing on wild animals that humans can neither control nor fully understand. Several animals known in the ancient Near East are mentioned:

**Mountain Goats and Deer** (vv. 1–4): The Hebrew *ya'alei-sela* ("rock goats") refers to the Nubian ibex, which still lives in the desert mountains of Judea and by the Dead Sea. These animals’ ability to give birth in inaccessible mountain areas was a picture of creation’s independence from human control.

**Wild Donkey** (vv. 5–8): The word *pere* describes the Asiatic wild ass (onager), an animal known for its untamable nature. It lived in desert regions (*'aravah*) and salt plains (*melechah*).

**Wild Ox** (vv. 9–12): *Re'em* likely refers to the aurochs (*Bos primigenius*), which became extinct in modern times but lived in the ancient Near East in antiquity.

**Ostrich** (vv. 13–18): The Hebrew *renanim* ("joyful wings") and *chasidah* ("stork") are used in a contrast between birds. In ancient times, the ostrich was seen as a paradox—a bird that could not fly yet was faster than horses. The description that God "denied it wisdom" (v. 17) reflects an old popular view of the ostrich as thoughtless.

**War Horse** (vv. 19–25): The portrayal of the war horse’s courage and eagerness is among the Bible’s most vivid animal descriptions. The Hebrew *ra'mah* (v. 19) means "mane" or "rushing mane." The description of the horse’s reaction to the sound of the shofar (vv. 24–25) shows familiarity with military practice.

**Hawk and Eagle** (vv. 26–30): *Nets* (hawk) and *nesher* (eagle or griffon vulture-eagle) were both known for their sharp sight and ability to fly at enormous heights. The statement that the hawk "spreads its wings toward the south" (v. 26) refers to the annual migration of birds.
