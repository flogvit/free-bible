## People

**Joseph (Ἰωσήφ)** is introduced as Mary’s betrothed and described as "righteous" (δίκαιος). He belongs to the line of David, which is crucial for Jesus’ messianic legitimacy. His devotion is shown in his righteous yet merciful response to Mary’s pregnancy.

**Mary (Μαρία)** is presented as Joseph’s betrothed. Her role as a virgin (παρθένος) is directly linked to the prophecy in Isaiah 7:14. The four women mentioned in the genealogy—Tamar, Rahab, Ruth, and "the wife of Uriah" (Bathsheba)—are all unusual in a Jewish family list. They were either non-Israelites or had unusual circumstances surrounding their marriages, which may prepare the reader for the unusual nature of Mary’s role.

## Events and Customs

**Betrothal (μνηστευθείσης)** was in Jewish culture a legally binding agreement that lasted about one year before the marriage was consummated. A betrothed woman was regarded as married in a legal sense, and infidelity during this period was punished as adultery. Joseph had the right to accuse Mary publicly, but instead chose to divorce her "quietly" (λάθρᾳ ἀπολῦσαι), which shows his mercy.

**Genealogies (γένεσις)** were very important in Jewish culture for documenting tribal affiliation, land rights, and priestly qualifications. Matthew structures the genealogy into three periods of fourteen generations each (v. 17). The number fourteen can be linked to David (דוד), whose Hebrew letters have a numerical value of 14 (ד=4, ו=6, ד=4).

## Key Terms

**Christos (χριστός)** is the Greek translation of the Hebrew "Messiah" (משיח), meaning "the anointed one." In the Old Testament, kings and priests were anointed with oil as a sign of God’s selection. By calling Jesus "Christos," Matthew asserts that he is the promised king from David’s line.

**Emmanuel (Ἐμμανουήλ)** is Hebrew for "God with us" (עִמָּנוּאֵל). Matthew quotes Isaiah 7:14 from the Septuagint, which uses παρθένος (virgin) for the Hebrew עַלְמָה (young woman). This name summarizes the main theological point of the chapter: that God himself is present in Jesus.

**Jesus (Ἰησοῦς)** is the Greek form of the Hebrew Joshua (יהושע/ישוע), meaning "the Lord saves." Matthew makes this connection explicit: "he will save his people from their sins" (αὐτὸς γὰρ σώσει τὸν λαὸν αὐτοῦ ἀπὸ τῶν ἁμαρτιῶν αὐτῶν).
