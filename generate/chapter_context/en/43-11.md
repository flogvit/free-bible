# Historical and Cultural Context for John 11

## People

**Lazarus, Martha, and Mary** formed a sibling group with whom Jesus had a close friendship (v. 5). The name Lazarus (Λάζαρος) is the Greek form of the Hebrew Eleazar, meaning "God has helped." The family appears to have belonged to a wealthy household in Bethany. The fact that many Jews from Jerusalem came to comfort the sisters (v. 19) indicates that the family held considerable social status.

**Thomas**, also called Didymus (Δίδυμος, "the Twin"), is mentioned in v. 16 where he urges the other disciples to follow Jesus to Judea. His statement, "Let us also go, that we may die with him," shows both a realistic assessment of the danger and deep loyalty toward Jesus.

**Caiaphas** was the high priest that year (v. 49, 51), serving from approximately 18–36 AD. His long tenure suggests he was skilled at balancing Jewish interests against Roman demands. In 1990, archaeologists discovered an ossuary in Jerusalem with the inscription "Joseph son of Caiaphas," which may have belonged to this high priest.

## Places

**Bethany** (Βηθανία) was located on the eastern side of the Mount of Olives, about 15 stadia (approximately 3 km) from Jerusalem (v. 18). The name likely means "house of the poor" or "house of dates/figs." The present-day village of el-Azariyeh (an Arabic derivation of "Lazarus") marks the site. Archaeological excavations have uncovered remains from the first century, including a tomb that has been identified since the fourth century as "the tomb of Lazarus."

**Ephraim** (v. 54) was a town near the wilderness, usually identified with the village of et-Taiyibeh, about 20 km northeast of Jerusalem. Jesus withdrew there after the council decided to kill him, since the area lay on the outskirts of Judea and was safer than regions closer to Jerusalem.

## Events and Customs

**Burial customs** in Jewish culture at this time involved burying the deceased on the same day as death, usually in a rock-cut tomb sealed with a rolling stone. The body was wrapped in linen cloths (κειρίαι, v. 44), and the face was covered with a cloth (σουδάριον). The mourning period traditionally lasted seven days (*shiva*), during which family and friends came to comfort the bereaved—as we see in verses 19 and 31.

**Four days in the tomb** (v. 17, 39) carries special significance. A widespread Jewish belief at that time held that the soul remained near the body for three days in hope of returning, but departed definitively on the fourth day when decay began. Martha's comment, "he already smells" (v. 39), emphasizes that Lazarus was unquestionably dead, making the miracle all the more remarkable.

**The Sanhedrin** (συνέδριον, v. 47) was the Jewish council consisting of 71 members, including high priests, elders, and scribes. They had considerable autonomy under Roman rule in religious and some civil matters, but could not carry out a death sentence without Roman approval.

## Key Terms

**"The resurrection and the life"** (ἡ ἀνάστασις καὶ ἡ ζωή, v. 25) is Jesus' response to Martha's confession of faith in a future resurrection "on the last day" (v. 24). Belief in a future resurrection was common among Pharisees and many Jews. Jesus redefines resurrection from being merely a future event to being connected with his own person here and now.

**Ἐνεβριμήσατο** (v. 33, 38) is an unusually strong expression describing Jesus' reaction. The verb originally refers to "snorting" or "braying" (like a horse) and expresses deep indignation or intense emotional agitation. It may indicate Jesus' response to the power of death and the destruction sin has caused in the world.
