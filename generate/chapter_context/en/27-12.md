# Historical and Cultural Context for Daniel 12

## Persons

**Michael** (מִיכָאֵל, "Who is like God?") is mentioned in verse 1 as "the great prince" (הַשַּׂר הַגָּדוֹל) who stands guard over the people of Israel. In Jewish tradition, Michael was understood as Israel's special guardian angel, in contrast to the angelic princes of other nations (see Daniel 10:13, 20-21).

**The man clothed in linen** (לְבוּשׁ הַבַּדִּים, verses 5-7) refers back to the figure introduced in Daniel 10:5-6. The linen garments (בַּדִּים) were the ritual attire of priests (see Leviticus 16:4), giving this figure a holy, mediating character.

**The wise ones** (הַמַּשְׂכִּלִים) are mentioned in verses 3 and 10 as those who will "shine like the brightness of the sky" and who "will understand." The term refers to those who have insight into God's ways and who lead others to righteousness (מַצְדִּיקֵי הָרַבִּים).

## Places

**The river** (הַיְאֹר) in verses 5-7 points to the Tigris River, where the entire vision from chapter 10 takes place (see 10:4). The scene with two figures on opposite banks and a third hovering above the water creates a cosmic tableau that emphasizes the supernatural origin of the message.

## Key Terms

**"Those who sleep in the dust of the earth"** (יְשֵׁנֵי אַדְמַת־עָפָר) in verse 2 is one of the clearest expressions of bodily resurrection in the Old Testament. The expression alludes to Genesis 2:7 and 3:19, where humanity is formed from the dust of the ground. Verse 2 distinguishes between a resurrection to "eternal life" (חַיֵּי עוֹלָם) and one to "shame and everlasting contempt" (חֲרָפוֹת לְדִרְאוֹן עוֹלָם).

**"The regular sacrifice"** (הַתָּמִיד) in verse 11 refers to the daily morning and evening offerings in the temple (Numbers 28:3-8). The term became central when Antiochus IV stopped these offerings in 167 BC. **"The abomination that causes desolation"** (שִׁקּוּץ שֹׁמֵם) likely refers to the pagan altar or idol erected in the temple.

**"Sealing"** (סְתֹם/חֲתֹם in verse 4, סְתֻמִים/חֲתֻמִים in verse 9) reflects ancient Near Eastern practice where important documents were sealed to preserve them until the proper time. This preservation "until the time of the end" (עַד־עֵת קֵץ) underscores the future-oriented character of the prophecy.

## Events and Customs

**The oath-taking** in verse 7, where the heavenly figure raises both hands toward heaven and swears by "him who lives forever" (חֵי הָעוֹלָם), follows a solemn pattern. Raising both hands is unusual (normally one hand, see Genesis 14:22), which intensifies the seriousness and credibility of the oath.

**The time periods** of 1,290 and 1,335 days (verses 11-12) have been the subject of much discussion. These numbers are close to "a time, times and half a time" (מוֹעֵד מוֹעֲדִים וָחֵצִי, "three and a half years") mentioned in verse 7, which corresponds to approximately 1,260 days. The variations may reflect different phases of restoration after the desecration of the temple.
