## Key Concepts

Psalm 17 contains several theologically charged Hebrew terms that are central to its understanding. The word **צֶדֶק** (*tsedeq*, "righteousness") in verses 1 and 15 frames the entire psalm—the presupposition of the prayer is that the one praying stands in a right relationship with God. The verb **צָרַף** (*tsaraf*, "refine/test") in verse 3 is a metallurgical term for melting precious metals to remove impurities, used metaphorically for God's testing of a person's inner character.

The expression **כְּאִישׁוֹן בַּת־עָיִן** (*ke'ishon bat-'ayin*, "as the pupil of the eye") in verse 8 is an idiomatic phrase for what is most precious and vulnerable. The pupil was regarded as the most protected part of the body, and this image expresses intense care. The expression **בְּצֵל כְּנָפֶיךָ** ("in the shadow of your wings") can refer both to a bird protecting its young and to the wings of the cherubim over the ark of the covenant in the sanctuary.

## Events and Customs

Verse 3 describes God's nighttime "visit" (פָּקַדְתָּ לַּיְלָה, *paqadta layla*), reflecting the Old Testament view of night as a time for divine testing and revelation. Dreams and nighttime experiences were regarded as moments when God could speak to or test people. The psalmist's prayer to pass this nighttime trial points to a cultic practice in which those seeking refuge in the temple could stay there overnight, awaiting a divine answer or vindication.

The image in verse 12, where the enemy's "likeness" is compared to a **lion lying in wait** (דִּמְיֹנוֹ כְּאַרְיֵה יִכְסוֹף לִטְרוֹף), was well known in ancient Israel, where lions still existed in the wild. Royal hunting texts from Assyria and Egypt depict lion hunting as a symbol of the struggle against chaotic forces.
