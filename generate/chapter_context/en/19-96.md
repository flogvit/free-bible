# Historical and Cultural Context for Psalm 96

## Events and Customs

Psalm 96 is reproduced almost word-for-word in 1 Chronicles 16:23–33, where it is linked to a specific historical event: David’s transfer of the ark of the covenant to Jerusalem. This places the psalm in a context of great national and religious celebration. Such processional festivals likely included singing, music, and public worship in the temple courts. The exhortation "Bring an offering and come into his courts" (v. 8) reflects the practice whereby worshippers brought *minḥah* (מִנְחָה)—a grain offering or gift—as part of temple worship.

The expression "Worship the Lord in holy splendor" (v. 9, בְּהַדְרַת־קֹדֶשׁ) probably describes the solemn atmosphere in which priests and Levites were dressed in sacred garments, while the people approached God’s presence with reverence. This was a combination of awe and joy that characterized Israelite worship.

## Key Terms

The Hebrew word *ĕlîlîm* (אֱלִילִים) in verse 5 is a deliberate play on words. It resembles *ĕlōhîm* (gods), but means "nothing" or "emptiness." It is a polemical term that exposes the powerlessness of foreign gods in contrast to Yahweh, who created the heavens.

The expression *hadrat-qōdeš* (הַדְרַת־קֹדֶשׁ) in verse 9 can mean both "holy splendor," describing God’s majesty, and "holy garments," which the priests wore. This duality reinforces the psalm’s focus on God’s holiness and its visible expression in worship.

The proclamation "The Lord is king" (*YHWH mālak*, יְהוָה מָלָךְ) in verse 10 is a central formula in the so-called "enthronement psalms" and expresses God’s universal rule over all peoples and the entire creation.
