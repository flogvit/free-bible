## People

**John the Baptist** is mentioned in verse 1 as a model for prayer practice. It was common for prominent rabbis and religious leaders to teach their disciples specific prayers that marked the group's identity. The fact that the disciples ask Jesus to teach them to pray "as John taught his disciples" shows they desire a prayer practice that defines them as followers of Jesus.

**The Queen of the South** (verse 31) refers to the queen of Sheba who visited Solomon (1 Kings 10:1-13). "South" (νότος) points toward Sheba, identified with present-day Yemen or Ethiopia. Josephus and other ancient sources refer to her as ruler over Egypt and Ethiopia. She represents a Gentile who sought God's wisdom.

**Zechariah** (verse 51) is likely the priest mentioned in 2 Chronicles 24:20-22, who was killed in the temple courtyard. Abel and Zechariah represent respectively the first and last martyrdoms in the order of the Hebrew Bible (Genesis – 2 Chronicles), thus encompassing "all the blood of the prophets."

## Places

**Nineveh** was the capital of the Assyrian Empire, located by the Tigris River near present-day Mosul in Iraq. Nineveh's repentance through Jonah's preaching was known in Jewish tradition as an example of Gentiles' receptiveness to God's word.

## Events and Customs

**Ritual handwashing before meals** (verse 38) was not required by the Mosaic Law, but was a Pharisaic tradition that extended the priests' purity requirements into daily life. The word ἐβαπτίσθη (baptizō) indicates this went beyond simple handwashing and involved immersion of the hands. Archaeological finds of stone pools (miqva'ot) in Jewish homes from this period confirm the widespread use of such cleansing rituals.

**Tithing of herbs** (verse 42), such as mint (ἡδύοσμον), rue (πήγανον), and other vegetables, went far beyond the Mosaic Law's requirement to tithe grain, wine, and oil. The Mishnah (Ma'aserot 1:1) documents later rabbinic discussions about exactly which plants were subject to tithing, showing this was a real point of contention.

**Places of honor in synagogues** (verse 43, πρωτοκαθεδρία) refer to the foremost seats facing the congregation, near the Torah ark. Archaeological finds from synagogues in Galilee show stone benches along the walls with specially decorated seats for prominent members.

**Unmarked graves** (verse 44) were a serious problem because contact with a grave made a person unclean for seven days (Numbers 19:16). It was therefore customary to whitewash graves, especially before festivals, so that people could avoid them.

## Key Terms

**Beelzebul** (Βεελζεβούλ, verses 15, 18-19) is derived from Ba'al-Zebub ("lord of the flies"), the Philistine god of Ekron (2 Kings 1:2). In intertestamental times, the name was used for the prince of demons. The spelling "Beelzebul" may mean "ruler over the dwelling" and was understood as a reference to Satan as the head of demons.

**God's finger** (δακτύλῳ θεοῦ, verse 20) is a deliberate parallel to Exodus 8:19, where Egyptian magicians acknowledge that the plagues come from "God's finger." The expression signals direct divine intervention and links Jesus' exorcisms to the power demonstrations of the Exodus tradition.

**Ἀναίδεια** (verse 8), often translated as "persistence" or "shamelessness," denotes a shameless perseverance. In the Mediterranean culture's honor-shame system, this was a strong expression emphasizing that persistent prayer is not inappropriate before God.
