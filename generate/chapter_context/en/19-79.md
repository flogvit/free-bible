# Historical and Cultural Context for Psalms 79

## Historical Background

The psalm describes the destruction of Jerusalem and the temple. The historical background most likely points to the Babylonian destruction of Jerusalem in 586 B.C.E. under Nebuchadnezzar II, when the temple was burned down, the walls were torn down, and large parts of the population were either killed or taken into exile. The description of unburied corpses (vv. 2–3) reflects one of the worst fates that could befall a person in Old Testament thought—to be denied a dignified burial was both a shame and a curse. This was a deliberate humiliation by the conquerors.

The lament that pagan nations have "entered your inheritance" (נַחֲלָה, *nachalah*) touches on the deep theological crisis that arose when God's covenant people saw their holy land and temple desecrated. The inheritance was not merely territory but God's promised gift to His people.

## Places

Jerusalem and the temple ("your holy temple," הֵיכַל קָדְשֶׁךָ) stand at the center of the psalm. The expression that Jerusalem was laid "in ruins" (לְעִיִּים, *le'iyyim*) describes total destruction. Archaeological excavations in Jerusalem have confirmed extensive layers of destruction from 586 B.C.E., including signs of fire and collapsed structures in the City of David and on the temple mount.

## Key Terms

The expression "those condemned to death" (בְּנֵי תְמוּתָה, *benei temutah*, v. 11) literally means "children of death" and refers to prisoners awaiting execution. The prayer that God should "repay sevenfold" (שִׁבְעָתַיִם, *shiv'atayim*, v. 12) reflects the Old Testament concept of justice in which the number seven symbolizes complete retribution. The psalm's closing image of Israel as "your people and the sheep of your pasture" (v. 13) uses the well-known shepherd metaphor that emphasizes the covenant relationship between God and Israel.
