## People

**Solomon (שְׁלֹמֹה)** is introduced here as David's son and successor. The expression "strengthened himself" (וַיִּתְחַזֵּק) in verse 1 is a technical term for the consolidation of royal power, showing that Solomon established his rule with God's help.

**Bezalel son of Uri son of Hur** (verse 5) is mentioned as the maker of the bronze altar. He was the craftsman whom God called to lead the work on the tabernacle during the wilderness journey (Exodus 31:1-5). This reference connects Solomon's worship directly to Mosaic tradition and legitimizes the sacrificial service at Gibeon.

## Places

**Gibeon (גִבְעוֹן)** was located about 10 km northwest of Jerusalem, identified with modern el-Jib. During this period, Gibeon housed the tabernacle and the bronze altar from the wilderness journey, while the ark of the covenant was in Jerusalem in a tent that David had set up. This division of cultic objects created an unusual situation where two sanctuaries existed simultaneously—something the chapter explains in verses 3-5.

**Kiriath-Jearim** is mentioned as the place from which David brought the ark. The ark had stood there for several decades after the Philistines returned it (1 Samuel 7:1-2), until David retrieved it.

## Events and Customs

**A thousand burnt offerings** (עֹלוֹת אָלֶף) represent an extraordinarily large sacrifice. The burnt offering (*olah*) was completely consumed by fire as a sign of total surrender to God. Such a massive offering at the king's accession signaled both piety and wealth, and was intended to secure divine favor for the new reign.

**A vision from God in the night** (verse 7) – The text says that God appeared to Solomon "that night" (בַּלַּיְלָה הַהוּא), after he had offered sacrifices at the sanctuary. God's offer, "Ask what I shall give you," is unique and underscores the special relationship between God and the Davidic dynasty.

## Key Terms

**Wisdom and knowledge (חָכְמָה וּמַדָּע)** – Solomon asks for two related but distinct qualities. *Hokmah* (wisdom) refers to practical discernment and the ability to govern rightly, while *madda* (knowledge) denotes insight and understanding. The combination shows that Solomon sought both practical judgment and deeper understanding in order to lead the people.

## Chapter-Specific Findings

The horse trade between Egypt and the Hittite kingdoms (verses 16-17) is confirmed by archaeological and textual sources. Egypt was known for its horse breeding, while Anatolia (the Hittite kingdom) produced high-quality chariots. The prices mentioned—600 shekels for a chariot and 150 for a horse—align well with what we know about values in the late Bronze Age and early Iron Age. Solomon apparently acted as an intermediary in this lucrative trade, which contributed significantly to his wealth.
