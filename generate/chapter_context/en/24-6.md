## Places

The chapter opens with a call to flee Jerusalem toward the south and mentions three strategic locations. **Tekoa** (תְּקוֹעַ) is located about 16 km south of Jerusalem in the hill country of Judah, known as the hometown of the prophet Amos. **Beth-Hakkerem** (בֵּית הַכֶּרֶם, "house of the vineyards") was likely a height between Jerusalem and Bethlehem, perhaps identical with today's Ramat Rachel, where archaeological excavations have uncovered a significant Judean fortress from the 600s BCE. These places formed a warning chain with signal fires that could alert southern areas to enemies coming from the north. The territory of the tribe of Benjamin (v. 1) bordered Jerusalem to the north and was the first line of defense against invasion from the north.

## Events and Customs

The chapter describes siege methods typical of Neo-Assyrian and Neo-Babylonian warfare. The expression "cut down trees and build up a ramp" (v. 6) refers to the construction of ramps against city walls—a technique well documented in Assyrian reliefs from Nineveh. To "consecrate war" (קַדְּשׁוּ מִלְחָמָה, v. 4) points to religious rituals that hostile armies performed before attacks, including sacrifices and seeking oracles. The description of shepherds camping around the city (v. 3) is an image of invading armies' method of surrounding and starving out a city.

Verse 20 mentions **frankincense from Sheba** (לְבוֹנָה מִשְּׁבָא) and **the sweet calamus** (קָנֶה הַטּוֹב, "the good reed") from distant lands. Sheba (South Arabia, today's Yemen) was the main source of costly frankincense in ancient times, transported along caravan routes through Arabia. Calamus (aromatic rush grass) was imported from India or East Africa. These exotic goods were used in temple worship, but God rejects them because the people's hearts do not accompany their offerings—a theme repeated by several prophets (cf. Isa 1:11-17; Amos 5:21-24).

## Key Terms

**"Uncircumcised ears"** (עֲרֵלָה אָזְנָם, v. 10) is a powerful image that transfers the theology of circumcision to the ability to hear—the people's ears are "covered" from God's word, parallel to Jer 4:4 concerning the circumcision of the heart. **"Ancient paths"** (נְתִיבוֹת עוֹלָם, v. 16) refers to the ancient ways—the covenant traditions from Moses and the fathers that the people have abandoned. **"Refused silver"** (כֶּסֶף נִמְאָס, v. 30) uses metallurgical terminology: the people are like dross left behind when base metals cannot be separated from silver, an image of failed purification.
