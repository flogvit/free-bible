# Historical and Cultural Context for Joshua 12

## People

The chapter mentions two kings east of the Jordan who had already been defeated during Moses' time: **Sihon, king of the Amorites** in Heshbon, and **Og, king of Bashan**. Sihon controlled the area from the Arnon River to the Jabbok, while Og is described as "of the remaining descendants of the Rephaim" (יֶתֶר הָרְפָאִים) – a designation pointing to an ancient tradition about a people of unusually large stature. These two kings represent the first phase of the conquest, defeated under Moses' leadership (Numbers 21; Deuteronomy 2–3).

## Places

The chapter provides a comprehensive geographical overview of the conquered land. **East of the Jordan**, the territory stretches from the Arnon Valley (the border with Moab) to Mount Hermon in the north. Important locations include **Lake Kinneret** (Sea of Galilee), the **Salt Sea** (Dead Sea), and **Ashtoth-Pisgah** (the slopes of Pisgah). **West of the Jordan**, thirty-one kings are mentioned from **Baal-gad** in the Valley of Lebanon in the north to **the bare mountain rising toward Seir** in the south. Several of the listed cities – such as **Lachish**, **Hazor**, **Megiddo**, and **Gezer** – are well-documented archaeologically as significant Canaanite city-states with massive fortifications from the Bronze Age.

## Events and Customs

The list of defeated kings functions as an official victory record, a document type known from Egyptian and Mesopotamian sources where rulers listed their military triumphs. The number **thirty-one kings** west of the Jordan illustrates the political fragmentation in Canaan at that time – the land consisted of many small city-states, each with its own king (מֶלֶךְ). This is confirmed by the Amarna letters from the 14th century BC, which show Canaanite petty kings writing to Pharaoh about local conflicts.

## Key Terms

The expressions **"to strike"** (הִכָּה) and **"to take possession"** (יָרַשׁ) are central terms for the conquest. The Hebrew word for "inheritance" or "possession" (יְרֻשָּׁה) emphasizes that the land is given as an inheritance from God, not merely as a military gain. The designation **"servant of the Lord"** (עֶבֶד־יְהוָה) is used for Moses in verse 6 – an honorific title underscoring his unique role as leader and lawgiver.

## Chapter-Specific Findings

Archaeological excavations at several of the mentioned cities show destruction layers from the Late Bronze Age. **Hazor**, the largest Canaanite city, has a clear fire layer from around 1230 BC that may possibly be linked to the Israelite conquest. **Lachish** shows similar traces from the same period. **Megiddo** and **Taanach** were strategic cities controlling the Jezreel Valley. The Amarna letters (around 1350 BC) mention several of these cities and their kings, confirming the biblical picture of Canaan as a patchwork of rival city-states before the arrival of the Israelites.
