## People

The chapter mentions several historical figures. **Uzziah** (עֻזִיָּה) was king of Judah around 783–742 BC, also known as Azariah. **Jeroboam II** (יָרָבְעָם בֶּן־יוֹאָשׁ), son of Joash, ruled in Israel around 786–746 BC. These kings are mentioned in verse 1 as the timeframe for Amos's ministry.

**Hazael** (חֲזָאֵל) and **Ben-Hadad** (בֶּן־הֲדָד) (verse 4) were Aramean kings of Damascus. Hazael reigned around 842–796 BC and was known for his brutal campaigns against Israel and Gilead. Ben-Hadad (III) was his son and successor. Assyrian inscriptions confirm the existence of both.

## Places

**Tekoa** (תְּקוֹעַ) is mentioned in verse 1 as Amos's hometown, located about 16 km south of Jerusalem in the Judean highlands. The area was known for sheep farming.

**Zion** (צִיּוֹן) and **Jerusalem** (יְרוּשָׁלַיִם) are mentioned in verse 2 as the place from which the Lord roars – the starting point of the message of judgment against the nations.

**Gilead** (הַגִּלְעָד) was the area east of the Jordan, known for its fertile grazing land. Damascus's attack with "iron threshing sledges" (verse 3) refers to brutal invasions of this region. Gilead is also mentioned in verse 13 as a victim of Ammonite expansion.

**Bikath-Aven** (בִּקְעַת־אָוֶן, "valley of wickedness") and **Beth-Eden** (בֵּית עֶדֶן) were Aramean regions; the latter is identified with Bit-Adini by the Euphrates in Assyrian texts.

**Kir** (קִירָה) in verse 5 was the original homeland of the Arameans (cf. Amos 9:7), possibly in Mesopotamia. The judgment entails that they are sent back to where they came from.

The Philistine cities **Gaza** (עַזָּה), **Ashdod** (אַשְׁדּוֹד), **Ashkelon** (אַשְׁקְלוֹן) and **Ekron** (עֶקְרוֹן) made up four of the five Philistine city-states. Gath is not mentioned, possibly because the city was already weakened.

**Tyre** (צֹר) was the foremost Phoenician port city, known for its trade.

**Teman** (תֵימָן) and **Bozrah** (בָּצְרָה) (verse 12) were important cities in Edom. Teman was known as a center of wisdom; Bozrah was a capital city.

**Rabbah** (רַבָּה) (verse 14) was the capital of Ammon, today's Amman in Jordan.

## Events and Customs

The expression "threshing sledges of iron" (חֲרֻצוֹת הַבַּרְזֶל) in verse 3 describes an agricultural tool – a wooden board with sharp stones or iron teeth underneath – that was dragged over grain to separate the kernels from the chaff. Using this on people was a known brutality of war in ancient times.

"Full exile" (גָּלוּת שְׁלֵמָה) in verses 6 and 9 refers to the deportation of entire population groups. Gaza and Tyre handed over (הַסְגִּיר) captives to Edom.

"Brotherly covenant" (בְּרִית אַחִים) in verse 9 refers to a treaty between Tyre and Israel, possibly the one made between Solomon and Hiram (1 Kings 5:12).

Edom's sin is described in verse 11 as pursuing "his brother" with the sword and destroying his mercy (שִׁחֵת רַחֲמָיו) – a reference to the historical kinship between Esau/Edom and Jacob/Israel.

The Ammonites' act of "ripping open pregnant women in Gilead" (בִּקְעָם הָרוֹת הַגִּלְעָד) (verse 13) was a brutal war tactic to wipe out future generations of the enemy.

## Key Concepts

The expression **"for three transgressions... yes, for four"** (עַל־שְׁלֹשָׁה פִּשְׁעֵי... וְעַל־אַרְבָּעָה) is a numerical formula known from wisdom literature (cf. Prov 30:15–31). It does not indicate exact numbers, but completeness – the sins have reached their full limit.

The word **"noked"** (נֹקְדִים) in verse 1 means not just "shepherd", but specifically one who raises a certain type of small livestock. The same word is used for King Mesha of Moab in 2 Kings 3:4.

**"I will not turn it back"** (לֹא אֲשִׁיבֶנּוּ) is a formal declaration of judgment repeated for each nation (verses 3, 6, 9, 11, 13). The expression indicates that the punishment has been irrevocably decided.

**Carmel** (הַכַּרְמֶל) in verse 2 represents the most fertile area of the land; when even Carmel's top withers, it illustrates the totality of God's judgment.

## Chapter-Specific Findings

The **earthquake** (הָרָעַשׁ) mentioned in verse 1 has received archaeological confirmation. Excavations at Hazor have revealed a destruction layer dated to around 760 BC with clear signs of seismic activity. The earthquake was so significant that it was used as a chronological reference point (cf. Zech 14:5) for several hundred years.

Assyrian annals from Tiglath-Pileser III confirm the deportation of Arameans to Kir (verse 5), which took place in 732 BC – a fulfillment of Amos's prophecy.
