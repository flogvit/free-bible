# Historical and Cultural Context for Psalm 97

## Events and Customs

Psalm 97 belongs to a group of psalms (93, 95–99) that celebrate the Lord’s kingship with the proclamation "The Lord is king" (יְהוָה מָלָךְ). This formulation may have been used in a cultic setting, perhaps during the autumn festival (Feast of Tabernacles), where God’s rule over creation and the nations was celebrated. The theophanic description in verses 2–6, with cloud, darkness, fire, and earthquake, reflects classic descriptions of God’s revelation at Sinai (Exodus 19:16–18) and in prophetic literature. Such natural imagery was common throughout the ancient Near East to describe divine presence.

## Key Terms

The word **אֱלִילִים** (*elilim*, "idols") in verse 7 is a contemptuous play on words meaning "things of no value" or "empty things," in contrast to **אֱלֹהִים** (*elohim*, "gods"). The statement that "all gods shall bow before him" does not reflect polytheism, but rather a polemical claim that the gods of other peoples are powerless against Israel’s God. The phrase **צֶדֶק וּמִשְׁפָּט** ("righteousness and justice") in verse 2 describes the fundamental characteristics of God’s throne and rule—these were central values also in Mesopotamian and Egyptian royal ideology, but here they are attributed exclusively to Israel’s God.

## Places

**Zion** and **"the daughters of Judah"** (verse 8) refer to Jerusalem and the cities in the region of Judah. "The daughters of Judah" is a poetic designation for the smaller towns around Jerusalem that rejoiced over the Lord’s righteous judgments. This formulation emphasizes that God’s kingdom has its center in Zion, yet extends throughout the whole earth and to "many islands" (verse 1)—an expression often denoting distant coastal lands and nations around the Mediterranean Sea.
