## People

Chapter 18 is addressed directly to Aaron, and this is one of the few chapters in Numbers where God speaks to him without Moses as an intermediary (vv. 1, 8, 20). This underscores Aaron's unique role as high priest. The chapter comes immediately after Korah's rebellion (ch. 16) and Aaron's staff that budded (ch. 17), and it now confirms the authority of the Aaronic priesthood through concrete rights and duties. "Beit-av" (בֵּית־אָבִיךָ, "your father's house") in verse 1 refers to the extended Aaronic lineage within the tribe of Levi, which now receives its distinct area of responsibility defined.

## Events and Customs

The chapter establishes a hierarchical system for sanctuary service: The priests (Aaron and his sons) serve at the altar and behind the curtain, while the Levites perform supportive duties in the rest of the tabernacle, but without touching the holy objects (vv. 3–4). The term "zar" (זָר, "foreigner/outsider") in verses 4 and 7 denotes all who do not belong to the authorized group—for the Levites this means non-Levites; for the priests it means all non-priests, including the Levites.

The redemption of the firstborn (vv. 15–16) with five shekels of silver reflects the principle that everything firstborn belongs to God. This practice is documented through "pidyon haben" ceremonies still observed in Jewish tradition. The fixed amount (five shekels according to the sanctuary weight, that is, twenty gerahs) provided a standardized price that prevented arbitrary pricing.

## Key Terms

"B'rit melach olam" (בְּרִית מֶלַח עוֹלָם, "a covenant of salt forever") in verse 19 is a powerful expression. Salt was an ancient preservative and symbol of permanence, used in covenants throughout the ancient Near East. Sharing salt meant entering into an unbreakable fellowship. Describing the priests' rights with this expression emphasizes that the arrangement is permanent and irrevocable.

"Terumah" (תְּרוּמָה, "heave offering/contribution") occurs repeatedly in the chapter and denotes the portion that is "lifted up" from the ordinary to become holy. "Ma'aser" (מַעֲשֵׂר, "tithe") in verses 21–32 describes the tenth part of the produce that the Israelites gave to the Levites, from which the Levites again gave a tenth ("a tithe of the tithe") to the priests.

"Chelev" (חֵלֶב) is used with two meanings in the chapter: in verse 17 it means "fat" (from sacrificial animals burned on the altar), while in verses 12, 29–30, and 32 it means "the best" of oil, wine, and grain.

## Chapter-Specific Findings

The Arad ostraca from the 7th century BCE contain administrative notes about supplies for "the sons of Kohanim" (priests), showing that the system of priestly support was established practice during the monarchy. The Samaria ostraca (8th century BCE) document deliveries of oil and wine marked as religious contributions, which correspond with the firstfruits regulations in verses 12–13. These findings confirm that the economic system described in Numbers 18 was practiced throughout Israel's history, although details may have varied over time.
