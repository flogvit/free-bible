## Key Concepts

The psalm has a clear liturgical structure that reflects blessing formulas from temple service. The opening words of verse 2 ("May God be gracious to us and bless us, may he make his face shine upon us") are an almost direct parallel to the Aaronic blessing in Numbers 6:24–26. The Hebrew expression *יָאֵר פָּנָיו* ("make his face shine") is an idiom denoting God's favor and presence. In ancient Near Eastern culture, the "shining face" of a king or deity was an expression of grace and protection.

The word *יְשׁוּעָתֶךָ* (jeshu'atekha, "your salvation/saving act") in verse 3 encompasses more than spiritual deliverance—it includes concrete rescue, victory, and well-being. When the psalm prays that God's "way" (*דֶּרֶךְ*) may be known on earth, this refers to God's manner of acting and his will as revealed through his deeds.

## Events and Customs

The psalm was likely used as a harvest thanksgiving song, as indicated by verse 7: "The earth has yielded its increase." In the Israelite agricultural society, the harvest was a critical moment when God's blessing became visible and tangible. Harvest festivals—especially the Feast of Tabernacles (Sukkot)—were occasions when the people gathered at the temple to give thanks for the crops. The connection between material blessing and mission to the nations (verses 3–4) reflects a theology in which Israel's prosperity was meant to serve as a testimony to God's goodness for all nations. This universal perspective, where "all peoples" (*כָּל־גּוֹיִם*) are to praise God, points toward prophetic visions of the nations making pilgrimage to Zion.
