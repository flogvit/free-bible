# Historical and Cultural Context for Psalm 85

## Events and Customs

Psalm 85 is closely linked to a national restoration following a period of God’s judgment. The expression "you brought back the captives of Jacob" (שַׁבְתָּ שְׁבִית יַעֲקֹב, verse 2) likely points to the return from the Babylonian exile (538 B.C.). At the same time, the prayers in the psalm (verses 5–8) show that the community still faced difficulties, which fits well with the challenging rebuilding period described in Ezra and Nehemiah. Many scholars therefore date the psalm to the early Persian period, when the people had returned but were still struggling with poverty, drought, and opposition from neighboring nations.

## Key Terms

The psalm contains several theologically charged Hebrew words that are central to its message. The word **חֶסֶד** (khesed, "steadfast love/faithful kindness") and **אֱמֶת** (emet, "faithfulness/truth") meet in verse 11, together with **צֶדֶק** (tsedek, "righteousness") and **שָׁלוֹם** (shalom, "peace/wholeness"). These four concepts are personified poetically—they meet and kiss each other—and represent God’s covenant qualities that together create perfect harmony between heaven and earth.

The expression **כִסְלָה** (kislah, "folly") in verse 9 is rare and denotes moral foolishness or self-deception. The warning not to "turn back to folly" suggests that the exile was understood as a consequence of earlier sin, and that the people are now urged to avoid repeating the same mistake.

## Places

The reference to "our land" (אַרְצֵנוּ) in verses 10 and 13 emphasizes the strong connection between the people, the covenant, and the land of Israel. In Old Testament thought, the fertility of the land (verse 13: "our land will yield its increase") was directly linked to the spiritual condition of the people and God’s blessing. That "glory shall dwell in our land" (verse 10) reflects the hope that God’s presence would again fill the temple and the land, as the prophets Haggai and Zechariah proclaimed during the rebuilding period.
