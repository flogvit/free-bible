## People

**Hiram** is mentioned in verses 11 and 16 as the craftsman who carried out the bronze casting work for Solomon. In verse 16 he is referred to as "Hiram his father" (חוּרָם אָבִיו), an expression that may mean "Master Hiram" or refer to his advisory role toward the king.

## Places

**The Jordan Valley between Succoth and Zarethan** (verse 17) was the place where the large bronze works were cast. The phrase "in clay ground" (בַּעֲבִי הָאֲדָמָה) refers to clay soil suitable for making molds. Succoth is often identified with Tell Deir 'Alla in present-day Jordan, while Zarethan (Sarethan) was located nearby.

## Events and Customs

**The Bronze Sea** (verses 2–5) was a large round water basin that held 3,000 baths, carried by twelve bronze oxen arranged in groups of three facing each cardinal direction (verse 4). According to verse 6, the sea was used for ritual washing by the priests. The enormous size and placement of the basin within the temple underscored its ceremonial significance.

**The Ten Basins** (verse 6) were used to rinse items associated with the burnt offering, while the Bronze Sea was reserved for the priests' own washing. This distinction between different purposes of purification reflects the temple's system of purity.

**The Bronze Altar** (verse 1) was twenty cubits long, twenty cubits wide, and ten cubits high—significantly larger than the tabernacle altar.

## Key Terms

**"The Sea"** (הַיָּם, hajam) was the name given to the large bronze vessel. In the ancient Near East, the sea often symbolized chaotic forces, and a controlled "sea" in the temple could symbolize God's dominion over these forces.

**"The court of the priests"** (חֲצַר הַכֹּהֲנִים) and **"the great court"** (הָעֲזָרָה הַגְּדוֹלָה) in verse 9 refer to the layered structure of the temple area, where different zones had varying levels of access.

## Chapter-Specific Findings

The chapter mentions both bronze equipment (altar, sea, pillars, basins) and gold items (lampstands, tables, bowls). Verse 18 emphasizes that the amount of bronze was so extensive it could not be weighed. Archaeological findings at Tell Deir 'Alla have revealed metallurgical activity, supporting the identification of Succoth in this region.
