## Key Concepts

The heading contains the expression "עַל־הַגִּתִּית" (al-haggittit), which likely refers to a musical instrument or melody, possibly connected with the Philistine city of Gath. Alternatively, it may be linked to the Hebrew word "גַּת" (gat), meaning "winepress," thus suggesting a song associated with the grape harvest and its festivals. This expression also appears in Psalms 81 and 84.

The phrase "מְעַט מֵאֱלֹהִים" (me'at me'elohim) in verse 6 has been translated in various ways. The word "אֱלֹהִים" can mean "God," "gods," or "heavenly beings." The Greek translation (Septuagint) renders this as "angels" (ἀγγέλους), while others understand it as "a little lower than God himself." This reflects humanity's exalted position in creation.

## Events and Customs

The psalm has strong thematic parallels with the creation account in Genesis 1:26–28, where humans are given "dominion" over the animals. The list of creatures in verses 8–9 (flocks, herds, beasts of the field, birds of the sky, and fish of the sea) mirrors the same categorization found in the creation narrative. This "dominion" over nature reflects humanity's role as God's steward on earth.

The night sky (verse 4) played a central role in ancient Near Eastern thought. While surrounding cultures worshipped the moon and stars as deities, this psalm presents them as God's "handiwork"—created things under his control. This represents a polemic against astral worship that was widespread in Mesopotamia and Canaan.
