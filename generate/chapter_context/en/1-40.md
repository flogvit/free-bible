# Historical and Cultural Context for Genesis 40

## People

The chapter introduces two Egyptian court officials: the **chief cupbearer** (שַׂר הַמַּשְׁקִים, *sar hammashqim*) and the **chief baker** (שַׂר הָאוֹפִים, *sar ha'ofim*). These were high-ranking positions at the Egyptian court. The cupbearer was responsible for Pharaoh's drinks and enjoyed great trust since he had to protect the king from poisoning. Egyptian sources confirm that such officials often had direct access to Pharaoh and considerable political influence. Both are referred to as **סָרִיסִים** (*sarisim*), a word that can mean court officials or eunuchs—in this context, likely the former, since both held trusted positions requiring close contact with Pharaoh.

## Places

Joseph is in the prison attached to the house of the **chief captain of the guard** (שַׂר הַטַבָּחִים, *sar hattabbachim*), who is Potiphar from chapter 39. Egyptian prisons for court officials were often separate from ordinary prisons, which explains why high-ranking officials were placed together with Joseph. The terms **בֵּית הַסֹּהַר** (*bet hassohar*) and **בּוֹר** (*bor*, "cistern/well") are used for the dungeon, consistent with Egyptian practice where prisoners were often held in underground chambers.

## Events and Customs

**Dream interpretation** was a recognized science in Egypt, with professional interpreters attached to the court. The fact that the prisoners say "there is no one who can interpret it" (וּפֹתֵר אֵין אֹתוֹ) shows they lacked access to such experts. Joseph's statement "do not interpretations belong to God?" (הֲלוֹא לֵאלֹהִים פִּתְרֹנִים) marks a theological contrast to Egyptian practice.

**Pharaoh's birthday** (יוֹם הֻלֶּדֶת אֶת־פַּרְעֹה) with a feast is confirmed in Egyptian sources. Such occasions were used to grant pardons or carry out judgments—both happen here. The expression "lift up the head" (יִשָּׂא...אֶת־רֹאשׁ) carries a double meaning: restoration for the cupbearer and execution for the baker.

## Key Terms

The word **חֶסֶד** (*chesed*) in verse 14, often translated "mercy" or "faithful love," denotes a binding kindness that Joseph asks the cupbearer to show. **אֶרֶץ הָעִבְרִים** ("the land of the Hebrews") in verse 15 is the only biblical occurrence of this expression as a designation for Canaan, and it shows Joseph's ethnic identity from his own perspective.

## Chapter-Specific Findings

The cupbearer's dream describes him pressing grapes directly into Pharaoh's cup (verse 11), reflecting a practice known from Egyptian art, where cupbearers are depicted in similar scenes. This was a practice that ensured the wine was fresh and not poisoned. Bakers are also depicted with baskets on their heads in Egyptian art, which matches the baker's dream. Execution by hanging or impalement on a stake, followed by birds eating the body, is documented as an Egyptian method of punishment for treason against the king.
