## Events and Customs

Chapter 28 presents the covenant structure with blessings and curses that have parallels in ancient Near Eastern treaty forms, particularly Hittite vassal treaties from the second millennium B.C.E. These treaties typically included a prologue, stipulations, witnesses, and sections with blessings for obedience and curses for disobedience. The chapter's structure, with short, rhythmic blessings (verses 3–6) followed by expanded curses, follows this pattern, which the Israelites would have recognized as a formal covenant ceremony.

The description of cannibalism during siege (verses 53–57) reflects a historical reality in the ancient world. Assyrian and Babylonian texts document similar conditions during prolonged sieges, and this actually occurred during the sieges of Jerusalem both in 586 B.C.E. and 70 C.E., as described in Lamentations 2:20 and 4:10, as well as by Josephus.

## Key Terms

The expression "שְׁחִין מִצְרַיִם" (sjeḥin mitsrajim - "boils of Egypt," verse 27) likely refers to a severe skin disease known from Egypt. This links the curses back to the sixth plague in Exodus 9:9–11 and emphasizes that God, who struck Egypt, can do the same to a disobedient Israel.

The word "אֲסָמֶיךָ" (asamekha - "your storehouses," verse 8) describes the underground grain silos that were vital in agricultural societies. Archaeological excavations have uncovered such structures at several Israelite settlements.

## Chapter-Specific Findings

The treaty structure in this chapter has been compared to Esarhaddon's vassal treaties from 672 B.C.E., which contain remarkably similar curses, including threats of famine, disease, military defeat, and cannibalism. These parallels show that chapter 28 uses a well-known diplomatic genre from the ancient Near East to communicate the covenant obligations between God and Israel.
