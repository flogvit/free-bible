# Historical and Cultural Context for Psalms 35

## Events and Customs

The psalm describes several important cultural practices related to mourning and intercession. In verses 13–14, the psalmist tells how he clothed himself in sackcloth (שָׂק, *saq*), fasted, and walked bent with grief as if over a friend or brother, like one who mourns for his mother. This was standard mourning practice in ancient Israel—sackcloth was a coarse fabric made from goat hair worn directly against the skin as a sign of deep sorrow or repentance. The fact that the psalmist performed these mourning rituals for his enemies while they were sick shows an unusual degree of compassion and makes their later betrayal all the more painful.

Verse 11 mentions "false witnesses" (עֵדֵי חָמָס, *'edey chamas*), literally "witnesses of violence." This reflects the Israelite legal system in which two or three witnesses were required to convict someone (Deut 19:15). False testimony was a serious offense that could lead to the death penalty (Deut 19:16–21). The psalmist's complaint about being accused of things he knows nothing about suggests a trial based on fabricated charges.

## Key Terms

The psalm opens with the Hebrew word רִיבָה (*rivah*), meaning "plead my cause" or "contend for me"—a legal term from the courtroom. The psalmist asks God to be his defender in a cosmic lawsuit against his enemies. This legal language continues throughout the psalm with words such as מִשְׁפָּט (*mishpat*, "judgment/justice") in verse 23.

The expression "wink the eye" (יִקְרְצוּ־עָיִן, *yiqretsu-'ayin*) in verse 19 was a sign of malicious secret communication and conspiracy in the ancient Near East. It described people who plotted evil against an innocent party while pretending nothing was wrong outwardly.

Verses 1–3 use detailed military vocabulary: מָגֵן (*magen*, small shield), צִנָּה (*tsinnah*, large body shield), and חֲנִית (*chanit*, spear). This reflects the weapon equipment of an Israelite warrior. The small shield was used in close combat, while the large shield protected the whole body. Asking God to "draw out the spear" and "block the way" for his pursuers portrays him as a divine warrior fighting on behalf of the innocent.
