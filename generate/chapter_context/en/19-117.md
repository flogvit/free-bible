## Key Concepts

Psalm 117 contains several theologically charged Hebrew words that deserve attention. The word **גּוֹיִם** (*gojim*, "nations/peoples") refers specifically to non-Jewish peoples, while **אֻמִּים** (*ummim*, "people/clans") is a parallel and more poetic expression. This doubling emphasizes the universal scope of the psalm. The term **חֶסֶד** (*chesed*) is often translated as "mercy" or "grace," but it carries a richer meaning of faithful love and covenant loyalty—God's unshakable commitment to His people. The combination of *chesed* and **אֱמֶת** (*emet*, "truth/faithfulness") is a common phrase in the Psalms that expresses God's reliable and enduring character. The verb **גָּבַר** (*gavar*, "is mighty/overwhelming") describes how God's grace not only exists but actively triumphs and overwhelms.

## Chapter-Specific Findings

Psalm 117 is the shortest chapter in the Bible, containing just two verses. Despite its brevity, it expresses a universal call in which all peoples—not only Israel—are invited to praise the God of Israel. The basis for this praise is God's *chesed* (faithful love) that has "prevailed over us" (*gavar aleinu*), suggesting an overwhelming experience of God's goodness.
