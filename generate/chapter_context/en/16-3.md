## People

Nehemiah 3 contains the most extensive list of names in the entire book, with over forty named individuals and numerous groups who participated in the rebuilding. **Eliashib the high priest** (אֶלְיָשִׁיב הַכֹּהֵן הַגָּדוֹל, v. 1) leads the way and represents religious authority; his involvement in the practical work shows that the whole community, from top to bottom, was engaged. The chapter mentions several **district governors** (שַׂר פֶּלֶךְ), such as Rephaiah over half of Jerusalem's district (v. 9) and Malchijah over the district of Beth-hakkerem (v. 14).

Notable is the mention of **Shallum and his daughters** (v. 12), who worked on the wall – a rare example of women mentioned in such building projects in ancient texts. The **nobles of Tekoa** (v. 5) stand out negatively by refusing to "bend their necks" for the work (לֹא־הֵבִיאוּ צַוָּרָם, "they did not bring their neck"), an expression indicating reluctance to submit to the work or leadership.

## Places

The chapter gives a detailed topographical description of Jerusalem's wall, beginning with the **Sheep Gate** (שַׁעַר הַצֹּאן), traditionally located in the north or northeast near the temple area. This gate was likely connected to the trade of animals for sacrifices. The **Tower of the Hundred** (מִגְדַּל הַמֵּאָה) and the **Tower of Hananel** (מִגְדַּל חֲנַנְאֵל, v. 1) are also mentioned by the prophet Zechariah (14:10) and marked the northern fortification. The **Fish Gate** (שַׁעַר הַדָּגִים, v. 3) was located in the north and was probably linked to fish trade.

**Ophel** (הָעֹפֶל, v. 26) was the southern spur of the temple hill where the **Nethinim** lived. The **King's Garden** (גַן־הַמֶּלֶךְ) and the **Pool of Shelah** (בְּרֵכַת הַשֶּׁלַח, v. 15) can be located at the southern end of the City of David. The **Tombs of David** (קִבְרֵי דָוִיד, v. 16) were a well-known landmark in Nehemiah's time.

## Events and Customs

The expression "they consecrated it" (קִדְּשׁוּהוּ) is used twice in verse 1 – both concerning the Sheep Gate itself and the section up to the Tower of the Hundred. This may indicate a dedication ceremony, possibly because this gate and area had special significance for temple worship – animals for sacrifice were brought in here.

The work was organized so that many **built opposite their own houses** (נֶגֶד בֵּיתוֹ, v. 10, 23, 28–30). This principle ensured personal motivation and efficiency, since each family had an interest in seeing that their particular section was solidly completed.

## Key Terms

The Hebrew verb **הֶחֱזִיק** ("to strengthen/repair") recurs throughout the chapter and denotes restoration of existing structures rather than new construction. The expression **מִדָּה שֵׁנִית** ("a second section/part") in verses 11, 19, 20, 21, 24, 27, and 30 shows that some groups took responsibility for multiple sections of the wall.

The term **פֶּלֶךְ** ("district") refers to administrative units. The **Nethinim** (הַנְּתִינִים, v. 26), literally "the given ones", were servants attached to the temple and are mentioned here as living on Ophel.

## Chapter-Specific Findings

Archaeological excavations in the City of David have uncovered parts of Jerusalem's wall from the Persian period that may correlate with Nehemiah's description. Particularly interesting are findings at the **Pool of Shelah**, where remains of stone structures and water channels from this period have been discovered. Kathleen Kenyon's excavations in the 1960s and later work by Ronny Reich have identified a wall on the east side of the City of David that is significantly narrower and situated higher up the slope than the earlier wall from the monarchy – this corresponds to the fact that the post-exilic population was smaller and had to defend a smaller area.
