## People

**Jotham** was king of Judah and reigned approximately 750–735 B.C.E. He was the son of Uzziah (Azariah) and effectively took over the rule while his father was still alive, after Uzziah was struck with leprosy for entering the temple to burn incense (2 Chron 26:21). Jotham is described as a righteous king who learned from his father’s mistakes by respecting the limits of royal power—he "did not enter the temple of the Lord." His mother **Jersheh, daughter of Zadok**, is specifically mentioned, which may indicate that she belonged to a prominent priestly family, since Zadok was an important priestly name in Jerusalem.

## Places

**The upper gate of the house of the Lord** (v. 3) was a major entrance to the temple area. Its identification with the "Gate of Benjamin" mentioned elsewhere is uncertain, but the gate was likely located on the north side of the temple complex. **Ophel** (הָעֹפֶל) was a fortified hill south of the temple platform, between the City of David and the temple area. Archaeological excavations have uncovered massive wall structures in this region from the Iron Age, including what may be remains of Jotham’s building activities. Ophel served as an administrative and defensive hub.

**The hill country of Judah** and **the forested areas** where Jotham built cities, fortresses, and towers refer to the central highlands and the wooded regions on the outskirts of Judah. Such towers served both as watchtowers and as refuges for farmers and shepherds.

## Events and Customs

**The tribute payment from Ammon** (v. 5) is significant: 100 talents of silver (about 3.4 tons), 10,000 cors of wheat, and 10,000 cors of barley (one cor = about 220 liters) annually for three years. This represents enormous economic value and shows that Jotham had restored Judahite dominance over the Ammonites east of the Jordan. Such tribute in both precious metals and agricultural products was standard practice in the ancient Near East. The fact that the tribute lasted only three years may indicate that the Ammonites subsequently rebelled or that political changes occurred.

## Key Terms

The expression **"the people still acted corruptly"** (וְע֥וֹד הָעָ֖ם מַשְׁחִיתִֽים, v. 2) points out that although the king was faithful, the people continued with syncretistic worship on "the high places"—local sacrificial sites that competed with the Jerusalem temple. This highlights the tension between the king’s faithfulness and the people’s ongoing syncretism.

The phrase **"he prepared his ways before the Lord"** (הֵכִ֣ין דְּרָכָ֔יו לִפְנֵ֖י יְהוָ֥ה, v. 6) is a characteristic expression from the books of Chronicles that describes deliberate and consistent faithfulness, not merely isolated acts of obedience.
