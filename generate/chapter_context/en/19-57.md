# Historical and Cultural Context for Psalms 57

## Events and Customs

The heading places this psalm in a dramatic episode from David’s life: "when he fled from Saul and was in the cave." This likely refers to the events described in 1 Samuel 22:1 (the cave at Adullam) or 1 Samuel 24:1–4 (the cave at En-Gedi), where David hid while King Saul actively sought to kill him. The Judean wilderness contains many such caves that served as refuges throughout antiquity. The cave at En-Gedi is located on the western shore of the Dead Sea in a rocky desert landscape that provided natural protection from pursuers.

## Key Terms

The expression "al-tasjḥet" (אַל־תַּשְׁחֵת, "do not destroy") in the heading is a liturgical or musical instruction also found in Psalms 58, 59, and 75. Its meaning is debated but may refer to a well-known melody or a prayer formula. Some scholars connect it with Isaiah 65:8, where the expression is used regarding sparing clusters of grapes for the sake of wine.

The image of seeking refuge "in the shadow of your wings" (בְּצֵל־כְּנָפֶיךָ) in verse 2 draws from the protective behavior of birds but also carries cultic resonance: the cherubim in the tabernacle and later in the temple had wings that overshadowed the ark of the covenant (Exodus 25:20). This imagery conveys both divine care and the safety found within the sanctuary.

The musical instruments mentioned in verse 9—"harp and lyre" (נֵבֶל וְכִנּוֹר)—are well documented in ancient Israel. The kinnor (כִּנּוֹר) was a smaller, lyre-like instrument, while the nevel (נֵבֶל) was larger and produced a deeper sound.
