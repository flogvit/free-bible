## Places

The expression "from the ends of the earth" (מִקְצֵה הָאָרֶץ, *miqṣeh ha'areṣ*) in verse 3 suggests that the psalmist is far from Jerusalem and the sanctuary. If the psalm originates from David, this may point to periods when he was in exile—either fleeing from Saul in areas such as Philistia or the wilderness near En-Gedi, or later when he fled from Absalom and crossed the Jordan to Mahanaim. "The ends of the earth" was an idiom describing the outermost periphery of the known land, expressing both physical and emotional distance from God's presence in the sanctuary.

## Key Concepts

The word "rock" (צוּר, *ṣur*) in verse 3 is a significant image for God. In the rocky landscape of Judea, high cliffs served as natural refuges and vantage points. The image conveys stability, security, and inaccessibility to enemies—a rock "higher than I" (יָרוּם מִמֶּנִּי) emphasizes that salvation comes from a position the psalmist cannot reach on their own.

The expression "the shelter of your wings" (בְסֵתֶר כְּנָפֶיךָ) in verse 5 may refer to two overlapping images: the mother bird protecting her young (Deut 32:11), and the wings of the cherubim that covered the ark of the covenant in the Most Holy Place. For Israelites, this would evoke associations with both God's tender care and his majestic presence in the temple.

## Events and Customs

Verses 6 and 9 mention vows (נְדָרִים, *nedarim*) that the psalmist has made and commits to fulfilling. In Israelite practice, a vow was a voluntary obligation in which one promised God something—often a sacrifice or an action—if he answered a prayer. Fulfilling one's vows was a serious religious duty (Num 30:2; Eccl 5:3–4). The psalmist's vow to praise God's name "day after day" (יוֹם יוֹם) suggests a continuing gratitude that should be expressed through regular worship in the sanctuary.
