# Historical and Cultural Context for Proverbs 20

## Events and Customs

The chapter contains several references to agricultural practices in ancient Israel. Verse 4 mentions plowing in the autumn (מֵחֹרֶף, "from the autumn/cold"), reflecting the Palestinian agricultural calendar where farmers plowed after the autumn rains had softened the soil, typically in October–November. Anyone who neglected this work would face a lack of harvest when gathering crops in the spring. Verse 26 uses the image of threshing, describing how the king "makes the wheel go over" the wicked (וַיָּשֶׁב עֲלֵיהֶם אוֹפָן)—a reference to the threshing sledge driven over grain to separate the kernels from the chaff.

Trade practices are highlighted in verses 10, 14, and 23, which mention false weights and measures. Archaeological discoveries of standardized weight stones from the Iron Age show that cheating with weights and measures was a widespread problem. Merchants could possess two sets of weights—one for buying and another for selling.

## Key Terms

The Hebrew word **שֵׁכָר** (shekar) in verse 1, often translated as "strong drink," refers to fermented beverages made from grain, dates, honey, or pomegranates—distinct from wine (יַיִן). The term covers everything from beer to fruit wine and was a significant part of the drinking cultures of the ancient Near East.

The expression **נֵר יְהוָה** ("the lamp/light of the Lord") in verse 27 describes the human spirit or conscience as God's instrument for examining the deepest parts of the heart. This anthropological metaphor connects inner human life with divine illumination and moral insight.

The phrase **יָלַע קֹדֶשׁ** in verse 25, literally "swallows 'holy'," warns against rash religious vows. This reflects the serious obligation that accompanied vows made in the sanctuary.
