# Historical and Cultural Context for Leviticus 2

## Events and Customs

The chapter describes the *mincha* offering, a grain offering consisting of fine flour (*solet*), oil, and frankincense. This offering represented the fruits of human labor with the earth, in contrast to the animal sacrifices in chapter 1. The chapter outlines several preparation methods: baked in an oven (verse 4), cooked on a griddle (verses 5–6), or prepared in a pan (verse 7).

The prohibition against leaven (*chametz*) and honey in verse 11 likely reflects a rejection of fermentation processes at the altar, since fermentation was associated with decay and change. In contrast, salt (verse 13) was required for all offerings—salt was a preservative symbolizing permanence and covenant. The expression "your God’s covenant salt" (מֶלַח בְּרִית אֱלֹהֶיךָ) shows that salt was a covenant symbol; sharing salt meant entering into a binding agreement.

Verses 14–16 concern the firstfruits offering of grain (*minchath bikkurim*), which was to consist of new grain, roasted in fire and coarsely ground—this represented the earliest harvest.

## Key Terms

The Hebrew word *mincha* (מִנְחָה) is used specifically for a grain offering in this chapter, but the word actually means "gift" or "tribute" and is used elsewhere in the Bible for gifts given to kings or superiors. The term *azkarah* (אַזְכָּרָה), translated as "memorial portion" or "reminder portion" (verses 2, 9, 16), refers to the part that was burned on the altar. The word is related to the verb "to remember" and implies that this portion would bring the offerer before God’s remembrance.

The expression *solet* (סֹלֶת) denotes fine wheat flour of the highest quality—not ordinary barley flour, which was everyday food. This emphasizes that the offering should be made from the best that a person had. *Levona* (לְבֹנָה), frankincense or incense, was an expensive import from southern Arabia that produced a distinctive fragrant aroma when burned.

*Karmel* (כַּרְמֶל) in verse 14 refers to fresh, soft grain—grain that has not yet been fully dried. This was characteristic of the firstfruits offering brought early in the season.
