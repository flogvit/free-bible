## Places

**Mizpah (המצפה)** is the gathering point where all the Israelites meet to discuss the crisis (v. 1). This place, which means "watchtower" or "lookout," was an important religious and political center in pre-monarchic times. Its location is debated, but most scholars place it in the territory of Benjamin, possibly at Tell en-Nasbeh north of Jerusalem.

**Bethel (בית-אל)** is mentioned as the place where the Israelites sought the Lord's counsel (vv. 18, 26–28). The text confirms that the ark of the covenant was there at this time. The site lies about 16 km north of Jerusalem.

**Gibeah (הגבעה)** is the center of the conflict, the Benjamite city where the crime took place. The location is usually identified with Tell el-Ful, about 5 km north of Jerusalem.

**Rimmon Rock (סלע הרמון)** was the refuge where 600 surviving Benjamites hid for four months (vv. 45–47). This is identified with the modern village of Rammun, about 6 km east of Bethel, an area with many caves.

## People

**Phinehas, son of Eleazar, son of Aaron** is mentioned as the one who served before the ark of the covenant (v. 28). This is an important chronological marker that places the events relatively early in the period of the judges, only one or two generations after Joshua. Phinehas is known from Numbers 25 for his zeal, and his presence here emphasizes that these events occurred while there was still a direct connection to the wilderness generation.

## Events and Customs

**Tribal muster and assembly** are described in detail. The expression "from Dan to Beersheba" (v. 1) is a standard formula describing all of Israel's territory from north to south. The assembly functioned as a kind of national court where all the tribes participated as "the congregation of God" (קהל עם האלהים). The number of 400,000 armed men indicates a complete mobilization of the tribal confederation.

**Seeking an oracle** (שאל באלהים/ביהוה) took place when the Israelites asked yes/no questions of the Lord and received answers, likely through the Urim and Thummim administered by the priest. This was standard practice before military operations in ancient Israel.

**Fasting and burnt offerings** (v. 26) represent an intensification of religious seeking after the first two battles resulted in defeat. The combination of burnt offerings (עלות) and peace offerings (שלמים) was a complete sacrificial system that expressed both surrender and fellowship with God.

## Key Terms

"**Sons of Belial**" (בני בליעל) in verse 13 is a Hebrew expression meaning "worthless" or "wicked people." The word בליעל can be analyzed as בלי (without) + יעל (value/use), that is, persons without moral worth.

"**Put away the evil from Israel**" (ונבערה רעה מישראל) in verse 13 reflects the language of Deuteronomy (cf. Deut 13:5; 17:7, 12; 19:19), where the community has collective responsibility to remove evil that threatens the whole people.

**The slingers** (קלע באבן) in verse 16 describes 700 left-handed Benjamites who could hit a hair without missing. The sling was an effective military weapon in antiquity, and the tribe of Benjamin was known for its slingers (cf. 1 Chron 12:2).

## Chapter-Specific Findings

The military tactic of ambush described in verses 29–41 has remarkable parallels with the conquest of Ai in Joshua 8. Both accounts involve a feigned retreat, an ambush force, and a smoke signal. This type of coordinated tactic reflects sophisticated warfare in the Iron Age. The similarity may be due either to deliberate literary repetition or to standardized Israelite military strategies.
