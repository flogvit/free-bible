# Historical and Cultural Context for Luke 5

## People

**Simon Peter, James, and John** are introduced here as fishermen in a business partnership. The Greek word *κοινωνοί* (koinonoi) in verse 10 describes a formal commercial partnership, not merely friendship. The family of Zebedee appears to have been relatively well-off, with their own boats and hired workers. Peter's reaction, "Go away from me, Lord; I am a sinful man" (v. 8), reflects a typical Old Testament response to an encounter with the divine (cf. Isaiah 6:5).

**Levi (Matthew)** was a *τελώνης* (telones), a local tax collector working under the Roman system. The tax station (*τελώνιον*) in Capernaum was strategically located on the main road, the Via Maris, and at the border between the territories of Herod Antipas and Philip. Tax collectors were despised both for collaborating with the Romans and for widespread corruption, as they often demanded more than the fixed amount.

## Places

**The Sea of Gennesaret** (*λίμνην Γεννησαρέτ*) is Luke's designation for the Sea of Galilee – he uses the Greek word for "lake" rather than "sea," unlike Mark, probably because he was writing for readers familiar with the Mediterranean Sea. Archaeological excavations at Capernaum have uncovered remains of fishing villages and a house that early tradition identifies as Peter's home. In 1986, a first-century fishing boat was found in the mud by the Sea of Gennesaret, providing valuable insight into the type of boat mentioned in the chapter.

## Events and Customs

**Leprosy and Cleansing** (vv. 12-14) were regulated by Leviticus 13-14. The term "leper" (*λέπρας*) covered several skin diseases and resulted in ritual impurity and social isolation. Jesus instructed the healed man to follow the Law of Moses by having himself examined by a priest and bringing the prescribed offerings – a process that could take up to eight days and included offerings of two birds, a lamb, and oil.

**The Structure of the House Roof** (v. 19) is described with the Greek word *κεράμων* (keramōn), meaning roof tiles, typical for Hellenistic buildings in Galilee. Houses often had flat roofs with an outer layer of tiles over wooden beams and branches covered with clay. The fact that the men "lowered him through the roof" was a radical action that damaged someone else's property and demonstrated desperate faith.

**The Meal at Levi's House** (v. 29) was a *δοχὴν μεγάλην* (great reception/banquet). In Jewish culture, sharing a meal expressed acceptance and fellowship. That Jesus ate with "tax collectors and sinners" broke with the Pharisees' practice of avoiding ritual defilement through table fellowship with unclean persons.

## Key Terms

**Ἐπιστάτα** (Epistata, "Master") in verse 5 is a word Luke uses six times, and only he among the Gospel writers. It denotes one who has authority or oversight, and reflects Peter's respectful address before he fully understood Jesus' identity.

**Ζωγρῶν** (zōgrōn) in the expression "catch people" (v. 10) literally means "to catch alive" – unlike fish that die when caught, people are to be "caught" for life.

**Υἱὸς τοῦ ἀνθρώπου** (Son of Man) in verse 24 is Jesus' preferred self-designation, with roots in Daniel 7:13-14. By claiming that the Son of Man has authority to forgive sins, Jesus made a radical claim which the scribes correctly identified as something only God could do.

**Νυμφίος** (Bridegroom) in verses 34-35 is a loaded term. In the Old Testament, God is portrayed as Israel's bridegroom (Isaiah 62:5; Hosea 2:19-20). Jesus' use of this image for himself is an implicit claim to divinity.
