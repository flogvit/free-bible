## Places

**Valley of Jezreel** (יִזְרְעֶאל, v. 2, 24–25) is mentioned both as a declaration of judgment and as a future blessing. The name means "God sows," and this duality plays out throughout the chapter. In verse 25, the wordplay on the name is used directly: "I will sow her for myself in the land" (וּזְרַעְתִּיהָ לִּי בָּאָרֶץ).

**Valley of Achor** (עֵמֶק עָכוֹר, v. 17) lies northwest of Jericho and is connected in Joshua 7 with Achan’s sin and punishment—the name means "valley of trouble." Hosea reverses the meaning by calling it "a door of hope" (לְפֶתַח תִּקְוָה). This shows how the prophet reinterprets Israel’s historical geography from judgment to renewal.

## Events and Customs

The chapter contains references to **Canaanite fertility cults**. In verse 7, gifts such as "bread, water, wool, flax, oil, and drink" are mentioned—typical offerings to Baal. Verse 10 reveals Israel’s fundamental misunderstanding: it was YHWH, not Baal, who gave these gifts, and the silver and gold were used for Baal worship.

**Days of Baal** (יְמֵי הַבְּעָלִים, v. 15) refer to festival days in the Canaanite religious calendar. The incense offering mentioned, along with the jewelry the woman adorned herself with, reflects cultic practice. Verse 13 lists Israel’s own festivals (חַג, חֹדֶשׁ, שַׁבָּת, מוֹעֵד) which God will bring to an end.

## Key Terms

**Baali/Ishi** (בַּעְלִי/אִישִׁי, v. 18): Both words can mean "my husband" in Hebrew, but "Baal" was also the name of a god. God requires that Israel call him "Ishi" (my husband) and no longer "Baali," because the latter is associated with idolatry. This is a powerful wordplay emphasizing the break with the Baal cult.

**Espousal** (אֵרַשׂ, v. 21–22): The verb is repeated three times and describes the formal betrothal process in ancient Israel, where the bridegroom paid the bride price to the bride’s family. Here, the "bride price" is righteousness, justice, mercy, compassion, and faithfulness—qualities that characterize YHWH himself and which should mark the renewed relationship.

**Rib** (רִיב, v. 4): This legal term means "lawsuit" or "accusation" and is used in covenant contexts. The children are urged to bring a case against their mother (Israel), reflecting Old Testament judicial practice where witnesses confronted the accused.
