## People

The psalm is attributed to **Asaph** (Hebrew: אָסָף) in the heading (verse 1). Asaph was one of the leading temple musicians during David's time.

## Places

**Zion** (צִיּוֹן) is mentioned in verse 2 as the place from which God "shines forth." At this time, Zion was identified with the temple mount in Jerusalem, the center of Israel's worship. The expression "perfect in beauty" (מִכְלַל־יֹפִי) reflects the idea of the temple as the beautiful meeting place between heaven and earth.

## Events and Customs

The psalm addresses the **sacrificial system** and its proper understanding. Verse 5 mentions "those who make a covenant with me by sacrifice" (כֹּרְתֵי בְרִיתִי עֲלֵי־זָבַח), referring to the covenant ritual in which animals were slaughtered and the parties of the covenant walked between the pieces. The psalm does not criticize sacrifices themselves, but rather a mechanical attitude that assumed God depended on the offerings. This reflects a prophetic critique also found in Isaiah, Amos, and Micah.

The **thank offering** (תּוֹדָה, *todah*) in verses 14 and 23 was a special type of sacrifice that combined a slaughtered offering with confession and praise. This offering involved the giver sharing a meal with family and friends in joy before the Lord's presence, expressing personal gratitude rather than mere ritual duty.

## Key Terms

The expression **"the cattle on a thousand hills"** (בְּהֵמוֹת בְּהַרְרֵי־אָלֶף, verse 10) is poetic language emphasizing God's ownership of all creation. The point is that God does not need human gifts to survive—he already owns everything.

The word **זִיז** (*ziz*, verse 11), often translated as "wild beasts" or "that which moves," is a rare Hebrew term referring to wild animals or insects on the ground. It underscores God's intimate knowledge of all creation, from the largest creatures to the smallest.

The **theophany language** in verses 1–6 (especially verse 3), with fire, storm, and a cosmic call to heaven and earth, reflects Old Testament tradition regarding God's revelation (compare the Sinai event in Exodus 19). God is portrayed as both judge and witness in a cosmic lawsuit against his people.
