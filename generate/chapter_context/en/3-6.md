# Historical and Cultural Context for Leviticus 6

## People

The chapter focuses on Aaron and his sons in their role as priests. The expression "command Aaron and his sons" (צַו אֶת־אַהֲרֹן) indicates that these are binding service instructions for the priesthood, unlike the preceding chapters which were primarily directed at the people bringing offerings. The chapter also mentions "the anointed priest" (הַכֹּהֵן הַמָּשִׁיחַ, verse 15) who is to continue the daily grain offering.

## Places

The chapter mentions several important places connected with the tabernacle. **The altar** (הַמִּזְבֵּחַ) was the bronze burnt offering altar that stood in the courtyard. **The court of the tent of meeting** (חֲצַר אֹהֶל־מוֹעֵד) was the holy area where the priests were to eat their portion of the offerings. **Outside the camp** (מִחוּץ לַמַּחֲנֶה) was the place where the ashes were to be taken – a "clean place" (מָקוֹם טָהוֹר) that was separated from the uncleanness of the camp, yet still treated with respect because the ashes came from holy offerings.

## Events and Customs

### The Perpetual Fire
The command that the fire on the altar must never go out (אֵשׁ תָּמִיד תּוּקַד עַל־הַמִּזְבֵּחַ לֹא תִכְבֶּה, verse 6) was central to the tabernacle service. The fire symbolized God's constant presence and Israel's enduring covenant relationship with Him. The priests had to add wood to the altar every morning (בַּבֹּקֶר בַּבֹּקֶר) to maintain the flame.

### Ash Removal and Priestly Garments
The detailed procedure for ash removal (הַדֶּשֶׁן) illustrates an important principle: the priest had to change his clothes when moving from holy service at the altar to carrying the ashes outside the camp. The linen garments (מִדּוֹ בַד) were the sacred priestly vestments, while "other garments" (בְּגָדִים אֲחֵרִים) were used for this less holy task. This shows the gradual transition between holy and common space.

### Cooking Rules for Sin Offering Meat
The rules stating that clay pots had to be broken while copper pots could be scraped and rinsed (verse 21), reflect the understanding that clay absorbs liquids and cannot be thoroughly cleansed, whereas metal can be purified by scraping and washing with water.

## Key Terms

**Torah** (תּוֹרָה): The word is used here in the sense of "instruction" or "regulation" for each type of offering (תּוֹרַת הָעֹלָה, תּוֹרַת הַמִּנְחָה, תּוֹרַת הַחַטָּאת), not as a designation for the entire books of Moses.

**Mokdah** (מוֹקְדָה): Refers to the fire pit or embers on the altar where the burnt offering (עֹלָה) lay burning through the night until morning (verse 2).

**Azkarah** (אַזְכָּרָה): The "memorial portion" of the grain offering – the handful that was burned on the altar. The word comes from the root ז-כ-ר (to remember/memorialize).

**Kodesh kodashim** (קֹדֶשׁ קָדָשִׁים): "Most holy" – the designation for the most sacred offerings that only male priests could eat, and only within the holy area.

**Kalil** (כָּלִיל): "Whole/complete" – used concerning the priests' grain offering (מִנְחַת כֹּהֵן) which was to be burned completely and not eaten, in contrast to regular grain offerings where the priests were allowed to eat the remainder.
