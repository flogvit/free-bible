# Historical and Cultural Context for Revelation 5

## Key Terms

**Βιβλίον (biblion) – The Scroll:** This Greek word here describes a scroll written "inside and on the back" (ἔσωθεν καὶ ὄπισθεν). This was unusual, as papyrus scrolls were normally only written on the inside. Such a double-written scroll was called an *opisthograph* and was used when the content was very extensive. In Roman legal practice, wills were sealed with seven seals by seven witnesses—an image that could suggest the scroll represents God’s testamentary plan for creation.

**Ἀρνίον (arnion) – The Lamb:** This diminutive form of "lamb" is characteristic of Revelation (used 28 times) and differs from the common word *amnos* used elsewhere in the New Testament. The image combines two seemingly contradictory concepts: the Lamb that was "slain" (ἐσφαγμένον) simultaneously bears seven horns (a symbol of perfect power) and seven eyes (perfect insight). The perfect participle "slain" indicates a lasting state—the sacrifice has permanent effect.

**Ὁ λέων ὁ ἐκ τῆς φυλῆς Ἰούδα (the Lion from the tribe of Judah):** This title draws from Genesis 49:9-10, Jacob’s blessing over Judah. In intertestamental Judaism (4 Ezra, Testaments of the Twelve Patriarchs), the Lion of Judah was interpreted messianically. "The Root of David" (ἡ ῥίζα Δαυίδ) alludes to Isaiah 11:1,10 and reinforces the Davidic messianic expectation.

## Events and Customs

**Incense and Prayer:** Verse 8 explicitly identifies the golden bowls full of incense (θυμιαμάτων) with "the prayers of the saints." This reflects Jewish temple practice where the incense offering in the Holy Place symbolized prayers rising to God (Psalm 141:2). In the Second Temple, the incense offering was brought morning and evening on the golden altar before the veil. The image of the twenty-four elders carrying these bowls suggests a heavenly liturgy that mirrors and perfects the earthly temple worship.

**Ἠγόρασας (you have purchased):** The verb in verse 9 draws from slave market terminology and the practice of ransom in antiquity. Slaves could be bought free by another master or by a deity (sacred manumission). The phrase "with your blood" (ἐν τῷ αἵματί σου) combines this marketplace metaphor with sacrificial terminology from covenant rituals.

## Chapter-Specific Insights

Archaeological finds of sealed documents from antiquity illuminate the scroll imagery. At Wadi Murabba'at near the Dead Sea, legal documents from the Bar Kokhba period were found rolled, tied, and sealed. The Rosetta papyri from Egypt show wills sealed by multiple witnesses. Pliny the Elder (Naturalis Historia 13.80) describes how important documents were sealed to ensure authenticity and prevent forgery. Thus, the seven seals in Revelation 5 truly reflect legal practice of the time and emphasize the document’s absolute authority and inaccessibility until the right person breaks the seals.
