## Events and Customs

The psalm is a collective thanksgiving psalm, likely used in communal liturgy. The introduction "Let Israel say" (verse 1) indicates that the psalm was sung responsively, with a leader beginning and the congregation responding in chorus. The psalm's structure, starting with "lulei" (לוּלֵי, "if not"), expresses a counterfactual thought—what might have happened without God's intervention.

## Key Terms

The Hebrew word "chayyim" (חַיִּים, "alive") in verse 3 describes how the enemies would have swallowed the people alive, an image alluding to the gaping mouth of Sheol or the greed of a predator. "Hammayim hazzeidonim" (הַמַּיִם הַזֵּידוֹנִים, "the proud waters") in verse 5 combines the motif of water chaos with the concept of "zeidon" (pride, arrogance), personifying the water as an arrogant enemy. "Tereph" (טֶרֶף, "prey/loot") in verse 6 is a hunting term describing flesh torn by a predator, while "pach yoqshim" (פַּח יוֹקְשִׁים, "the snare of fowlers") in verse 7 refers to traps used in bird hunting—an image of sudden danger and captivity.

## Chapter-Specific Insights

The flood metaphor in verses 4–5 reflects a deep cultural experience in ancient Israel. Unlike Egypt's predictable Nile floods, winter wadis were known for sudden and destructive flash floods following rain. Such sudden floods were a real threat, giving the metaphor concrete resonance for its original audience.
