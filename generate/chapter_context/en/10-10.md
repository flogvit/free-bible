# Historical and Cultural Context for 2 Samuel 10

## People

**Nahash and Hanun** – Nahash was king of the Ammonites and had previously shown David kindness (verse 2). The circumstances surrounding this kindness are not specified in the text. The name Nahash (נָחָשׁ) means "snake" in Hebrew. His son Hanun succeeded him to the throne (verse 1), but lacked his father's diplomatic wisdom and was persuaded by his advisors to disgrace David’s messengers.

**Hadadezer and Shobach** – Hadadezer (הֲדַדְעֶזֶר) was king of the Aramean kingdom of Zobah. His name includes the name of the god Hadad and means "Hadad is my helper." In verse 16, he called for Aramean reinforcements from beyond the river (Euphrates). Shobach (שׁוֹבַךְ) was his commander who led the Aramean forces and was killed in the battle at Helam (verse 18).

**Joab and Abishai** – David’s commanders who led the Israelite army. Joab developed a two-part strategy: he took the elite troops against the Arameans, while his brother Abishai (אֲבִישַׁי) commanded the rest of the forces against the Ammonites (verses 9–10). They agreed to support each other if needed.

## Places

**Beth-Rehob and Zobah** – Two Aramean regions that supplied mercenaries to the Ammonites (verse 6). Beth-Rehob (בֵּית־רְחוֹב) was likely located in the Bekaa Valley, while Zobah (צוֹבָא) controlled areas northeast of Damascus.

**Maacah and Tob** – Maacah (מַעֲכָה) was a small kingdom at the foot of Mount Hermon. Tob (טוֹב) lay east of the Jordan. Both supplied troops to the Ammonite alliance (verse 6).

**Helam** – The site of the decisive battle between David and the Arameans (verses 16–17). The Hebrew text has חֵילָם (verse 16) and חֵלָאמָה (verse 17). The exact location is uncertain but was probably in the Transjordan region.

**Jericho** – The place where David told the disgraced messengers to wait until their beards had grown back (verse 5).

## Events and Customs

**The Condolence Mission** – David’s sending of officials to comfort Hanun (לְנַחֲמוֹ, verse 2) followed established diplomatic practice in the ancient Near East. Such delegations confirmed alliances between generations and were important for maintaining peaceful relations.

**The Humiliation of the Messengers** – Shaving half of their beards and cutting their garments to the buttocks (verse 4) was a calculated insult. In Semitic culture, the beard was a symbol of male dignity and honor; removing it was deeply shameful. Exposing the body by cutting the clothes intensified the offense. This act was an act of war—a deliberate provocation that demanded retaliation.

**Mercenaries in Ancient Warfare** – The Ammonites hiring Aramean troops (verse 6) was common practice. Small states could quickly raise substantial forces through payment. The numbers mentioned (20,000 foot soldiers from Beth-Rehob and Zobah, 1,000 from Maacah, 12,000 from Tob) show the scale of the military alliance against Israel.

## Key Terms

**Hesed (חֶסֶד)** – The word translated as "kindness" or "loyalty" (verse 2) describes covenantal loyalty and mutual obligations. David wanted to show hesed toward Hanun just as Nahash had shown it toward him. The term implies more than friendliness—it concerns binding commitments between parties.

**Nibʾashu (נִבְאֲשׁוּ)** – The expression in verse 6 describing that the Ammonites "had made themselves hateful" to David literally means "to stink." It indicates that the relationship was completely broken and beyond repair.

**Joab’s Statement of Confidence (verse 12)** – "Be strong, and let us be strong for our people and for the cities of our God; may the Lord do what is good in his eyes" (חֲזַק וְנִתְחַזַּק). This statement combines military courage with theological submission to God’s will—a remarkable attitude amid battle preparations.
