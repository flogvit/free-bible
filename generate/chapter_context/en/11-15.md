# Historical and Cultural Context for 1 Kings 15

## People

**Abijam/Abijah** reigned as king over Judah for only three years (vv. 1-2). His mother Maacah was the daughter of Absalom (v. 2). 2 Chronicles 13 provides a more detailed account of his reign, including a military victory over Jeroboam.

**Asa** reigned for 41 years (v. 10) and did what was right in the eyes of the Lord, like his ancestor David (v. 11). He carried out religious reforms by removing cult prostitutes and idols (v. 12), and deposed his grandmother Maacah from her position as "gebira" (queen mother) because she had made an idol for Asherah (v. 13). Toward the end of his life, he became sick in his feet (v. 23).

**Maacah** is mentioned as the mother of both Abijam (v. 2) and Asa (v. 10), which likely means she was Asa's grandmother and retained her position as gebira. She made a "mifletset" (abominable image) for the Asherah cult.

**Ben-Hadad I** of Aram-Damascus was the son of Tabrimmon, the son of Hezion (v. 18). The title means "son of (the god) Hadad." Asa sent him gold and silver to break his alliance with Baasha.

**Nadab**, Jeroboam's son, reigned over Israel for two years (vv. 25-26). He continued in the sinful ways of his father and was killed by Baasha during the siege of Gibbethon (vv. 27-28).

**Baasha** from the tribe of Issachar carried out a coup against Nadab and wiped out the entire family of Jeroboam (v. 29), fulfilling the words of the prophet Ahijah (v. 29). He reigned for 24 years from Tirzah (v. 33).

## Places

**Ramah** was strategically located north of Jerusalem. Baasha's fortification of the city (v. 17) was intended to prevent free movement to and from Judah (לְבִלְתִּי תֵּת יֹצֵא וָבָא). When Baasha had to withdraw, Asa used materials from Ramah to fortify Geba in Benjamin and Mizpah (v. 22).

**Tirzah** was the capital of Israel at this time. Baasha withdrew there after giving up the fortification of Ramah (v. 21).

**Gibbethon** was a city in Philistine territory that Israel was besieging when Baasha killed Nadab and seized power (v. 27).

**Ijon, Dan, and Abel-Beth-Maacah**, as well as the entire region of Kinneret in the land of Naphtali (v. 20), were attacked by Ben-Hadad's forces. These cities in northern Galilee were vulnerable to attacks from Aram-Damascus.

**The Kidron Valley** (v. 13) ran east of Jerusalem and was a symbolic place for the destruction of cult objects. Asa burned Maacah's Asherah image there.

## Events and Customs

**War between generations**: Verse 6 mentions that there was war between Rehoboam and Jeroboam "all his days," a retrospective reference to the conflict between Abijam's father's generation. This conflict continued between Asa and Baasha (vv. 16, 32).

**Cult prostitutes** (Hebrew: *qedeshim*, v. 12) whom Asa removed were persons associated with fertility cults that had taken root in Judah.

The **mifletset**-image (v. 13) that Maacah made for Asherah was likely an offensive cult object. The Hebrew word suggests something "grotesque" or "terrifying."

**High places** (הַבָּמוֹת) were not removed, despite Asa's reforms (v. 14). This becomes a recurring theme in the evaluation of the kings of Judah.

## Key Terms

The word **"nir"** (v. 4) is often translated as "lamp" and is used metaphorically for the continued existence of the Davidic dynasty in Jerusalem. Despite Abijam's sins, God allowed his line to continue for David's sake.

**"Gebira"** (גְּבִירָה, v. 13) was an official title for the queen mother with real power at the court. Asa's removal of Maacah from this position demonstrates the seriousness of her religious deviation.

## Chapter-Specific Findings

The **Ben-Hadad Stele**, found in northern Syria, mentions a king of Aram-Damascus and confirms the existence of this dynasty as referred to in vv. 18-20.

Archaeological excavations at **Tel Dan** have uncovered traces of destruction from the Iron Age that may possibly be linked to Ben-Hadad's attacks described in v. 20.
