# Historical and Cultural Context for 1 Samuel 27

## People

**Achish, son of Maoch, king of Gath** – A Philistine city-king over Gath. This is David’s second encounter with him (the first was in 1 Sam 21:11–15), but this time David is received as a vassal rather than as a threat. Achish’s trust in David (v. 12) proves to be completely misplaced.

**The Geshurites, the Girzites, and the Amalekites** – Semi-nomadic groups in the Negev desert and the areas south of Judah. The Geshurites here (הַגְּשׁוּרִי) are likely a different group from the Aramean Geshur in the north, where David’s father-in-law Talmai later became king (2 Sam 3:3). The Amalekites were Israel’s traditional enemies since the wilderness wanderings.

**David’s two wives** – Verse 3 notes that David brought with him Ahinoam from Jezreel and Abigail, Nabal’s widow from Carmel. He had married both of them in chapter 25.

## Places

**Gath** – One of the five major Philistine city-states, strategically located at the transition from the coastal plain to the Judean highlands. As Achish’s capital, it is called "the royal city" (עִיר הַמַּמְלָכָה) in verse 5.

**Ziklag (צִקְלָג)** – The town that Achish gave David as a fief. Its exact location is debated, with Tell esh-Sharia and Tell Halif as possible candidates in the northern Negev. The text notes that Ziklag remained the property of the kings of Judah "to this day" (v. 6), indicating its enduring political significance.

**Negev regions** – The chapter mentions three distinct "Negev" areas: the Negev of Judah, the Negev of the Jerahmeelites, and the Negev of the Kenites (v. 10). This reflects that the Negev was divided according to tribal boundaries and settlement patterns. David told Achish he had raided these Judahite and Israel-friendly areas, while in reality he attacked Israel’s enemies.

## Events and Customs

**Vassalage and deception** – David’s relationship with Achish represents a Near Eastern vassal arrangement, but the chapter emphasizes how David manipulated this relationship. By requesting "one of the towns in the country" rather than living in the capital (v. 5), David showed humility toward Achish while simultaneously gaining freedom to operate without supervision.

**Raiding and secrecy** – David’s practice of killing all witnesses (vv. 9, 11) to conceal his true targets was brutal but militarily calculated. By eliminating all potential informants, he could maintain his double role as a Philistine vassal while attacking Israel’s enemies instead of Israelite territories. The plunder (sheep, cattle, donkeys, camels, and clothing) was brought back to Achish as "proof" of loyalty.

## Key Terms

**"Towns in the country" (עָרֵי הַשָּׂדֶה)** – The expression in verse 5 distinguishes between the capital ("the royal city") and smaller provincial towns. David asked for such a secondary town to avoid living "with" (עִמָּךְ) the king.

**"To this day" (עַד הַיּוֹם הַזֶּה)** – This phrase in verse 6 is an editorial note. The plural expression "kings of Judah" (לְמַלְכֵי יְהוּדָה) suggests the text was written or edited after the kingdom’s division, when Judah had its own line of kings.

**The time reference** – Verse 7 states that David lived in Philistine territory for "a year and four months" (יָמִים וְאַרְבָּעָה חֳדָשִׁים). The word יָמִים (literally "days") is used idiomatically to mean "a year" in biblical Hebrew.
