# Historical and Cultural Context for Joshua 20

## Places

The chapter mentions six cities of refuge strategically located on both sides of the Jordan River. West of the Jordan, **Kedesh** in Galilee (in the hill country of Naphtali) is identified with Tell Qades north of Lake Merom. **Shechem** in the hill country of Ephraim is well documented archaeologically at Tell Balata, a city with a long history as a cultic and political center. **Kiriath-Arba (Hebron)** in the hill country of Judah lies near modern al-Khalil and was already an ancient city at that time.

East of the Jordan, **Bezer** in the wilderness on the plateau was designated within the territory of the tribe of Reuben, possibly identified with Umm el-Amad. **Ramoth in Gilead** in Gad's territory is likely Tell Ramith. **Golan** in Bashan, in Manasseh's eastern region, has given its name to the Golan Heights and may be located at Sahem el-Jolan. The geographical distribution—three cities west of the Jordan and three east of it—made the places of refuge accessible from throughout the land.

## Events and Customs

The chapter describes the procedure for the city-of-refuge system (*'are hammiklat*): Anyone who had killed someone unintentionally (*bishgagah*, "by mistake") was to flee to one of these cities, stand at the city gate, and present their case before the elders of that city (v. 4). The elders were to take him into the city and give him a place to live. If the avenger of blood pursued him, they were not to hand him over, since he had killed without intent and without prior hostility (v. 5). He was required to remain in the city until he stood trial before the assembly and until the death of the serving high priest (v. 6).

## Key Terms

**The avenger of blood** (*go'el haddam*) was a close male relative obligated to restore the family's honor by putting the killer to death.

**The high priest** (*hakkohen haggadol*): According to verse 6, the killer had to remain in the city of refuge until the high priest serving at that time died. Only then could he return to his own city and home.

**"Yesterday or the day before"** (*mittmol shilshom*) is an idiomatic phrase meaning "from earlier" or "previously," used in verse 5 to show that there was no long-standing hostility between the killer and the victim.

**Unintentional killing** (*bishgagah bivli-da'at*, "by mistake, without knowledge") – the expression in verses 3 and 9 emphasizes that the city-of-refuge system applied only to unintentional killings.

## Chapter-Specific Findings

Verse 9 clarifies that the arrangement applied both to Israelites and to foreigners (*ger*) living among them, demonstrating universal legal protection. Archaeological excavations at Shechem have uncovered massive city walls and gate structures from the late Bronze Age and early Iron Age, consistent with the picture of a city where elders sat at the gate to judge cases.
