## Events and Customs

Psalm 95 has a clear liturgical structure that suggests its use in temple worship. The first part of the psalm (verses 1–7a) is a hymn calling for praise, while the second part (verses 7b–11) is a prophetic oracle in which God speaks directly to the assembly. This transition from praise to divine exhortation was part of Israelite cultic tradition, where priests or prophets could speak on God’s behalf during worship. Expressions such as "come, let us bow down and kneel" (verse 6) describe concrete physical acts of worship that were common in the temple.

## Places

**Meribah and Massah** (verse 8) are place names that literally mean "quarreling" and "testing." They refer to the event described in Exodus 17:1–7, where the Israelites in the wilderness complained about lack of water and tested God at Rephidim. The place was called Massah and Meribah precisely because of the people’s rebellion. A similar incident is described in Numbers 20:1–13 at Kadesh. In Israelite tradition, these places stood as symbols of disobedience and distrust toward God.

## Key Terms

**Tsur yish'enu** (צוּר יִשְׁעֵנוּ) – "our salvation’s rock" (verse 1) – is an image of God as firm, unchanging, and reliable, in contrast to the unstable wilderness landscape. The rock metaphor also resonates with the water that came from the rock at Meribah.

**Menukhati** (מְנוּחָתִי) – "my rest" (verse 11) – refers to the promised land as God’s rest for His people. The wilderness generation was denied entry into this rest because of their disobedience. In Hebrews 3–4, this concept is expanded to include final salvation.

**Aqút** (אָקוּט) – translated "I was filled with disgust" or "I was angry" (verse 10) – describes God’s deep frustration over the people’s persistent unbelief during the forty years in the wilderness.
