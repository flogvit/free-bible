## Places

The chapter mentions several geographical regions in verse 13: "the cities of the hill country," "the cities of the lowland" (Shephelah), "the cities of the Negev," "the land of Benjamin," and "the areas around Jerusalem." These designations represent the traditional geographical divisions of the kingdom of Judah. The Hill Country (הָהָר) refers to the central highlands where Jerusalem was located. Shephelah (הַשְּׁפֵלָה) was the low hilly region between the hill country and the coastal plain, known for its fertile valleys. The Negev (הַנֶּגֶב) was the dry area in the south. This detailed geographical description emphasizes that the restoration will encompass the entire territory of the former kingdom, not just the capital city.

## Events and Customs

Verse 11 describes future wedding celebrations and thank offerings in the temple. The expression "Praise the Lord, the God of hosts, for the Lord is good; his steadfast love endures forever" (הוֹדוּ אֶת־יְהוָה צְבָאוֹת כִּי־טוֹב יְהוָה כִּי־לְעוֹלָם חַסְדּוֹ) is a liturgical formula found in the Psalms (Ps 106:1; 107:1; 118:1; 136:1) and used during temple worship. The thank offering (תּוֹדָה) was a meal offering in which the giver and his family ate the meat together, rejoicing over God's blessings. The combination of wedding sounds and temple liturgy symbolizes a community in full bloom—both civil and religious life restored.

## Key Terms

The expression "צֶמַח צְדָקָה" (tsemach tsedaqah, "the righteous Branch") in verse 15 is a messianic term that also appears in Jeremiah 23:5 and Zechariah 3:8; 6:12. The image of a "Branch" or "Shoot" from the line of David became an important messianic symbol in late biblical and intertestamental times.

The term "the two families" (שְׁתֵּי הַמִּשְׁפָּחוֹת) in verse 24 likely refers to David's royal lineage and the Levitical priestly family, or alternatively to Israel and Judah as the two branches of God's people. The people's claim that God had rejected them reflects the deep crisis during the siege—many interpreted Jerusalem's fall as proof that the covenant was broken and God had abandoned his people.

The covenant with "day and night" (verses 20, 25) uses the order of creation as a guarantee for God's promises. This connects back to Genesis 8:22 and God's promise to Noah, emphasizing that God's covenant with David and the Levites is as secure as the laws of nature.
