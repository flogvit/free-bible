# Historical and Cultural Context for Genesis 23

## People

**Sarah** is the only woman in the Old Testament whose age at death is explicitly stated—127 years (v. 1). This underscores her unique significance as the matriarch of Israel. The text uses an unusual phrasing where the age is expressed in three parts ("one hundred years and twenty years and seven years"), which rabbinic tradition has interpreted to mean that each stage of life had its own beauty and innocence.

**Ephron son of Zohar** was a Hittite and landowner in the Hebron area. His name is likely derived from the word for "dust" or "young deer/fawn" (עָפָר/עֹפֶר). He acts as a typical Near Eastern negotiator who offers generously but expects full payment. **The Hittites** (בְּנֵי־חֵת) refer to the Hittites living in Canaan, a population group distinct from the great Hittite Empire in Anatolia.

## Places

**Kiriath-arba/Hebron** is located about 30 kilometers south of Jerusalem in the Judean highlands, approximately 930 meters above sea level. The name "Kiriath-arba" means "City of Arba" or "City of Four." Hebron is one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the world, and the site holds great significance as the burial place of the patriarchs.

**The Cave of Machpelah** (מְעָרַת הַמַּכְפֵּלָה, v. 9) – The name "Machpelah" is derived from the root כפל meaning "double", possibly because the cave had two chambers or was located at a "doubling" in the terrain. Today, the Ibrahimi Mosque/Patriarchs' Tomb stands over the traditional site.

## Events and Customs

**The Negotiation Ritual** described follows a well-known pattern from the ancient Near East. Ephron appears to offer the land for free, but this was a polite gesture that both parties understood was not meant literally. Abraham insisted on paying, which secured him undisputed ownership rights. This type of negotiation with ritual politeness is well documented in Mesopotamian and Hittite texts.

**City Gate Negotiations** (בָּאֵי שַׁעַר־עִירוֹ, "all who came to the city gate", v. 10, 18) reflect that the city gate was the official place for legal transactions and witnessing in ancient cities. Elderly men and leaders gathered there to judge cases and witness contracts. That the sale took place "in the presence of" these witnesses gave it legal validity.

## Key Terms

**Ger ve-toshav** (גֵּר־וְתוֹשָׁב) – "foreigner and resident alien" – describes Abraham's legal status (v. 4). He was a resident without full citizenship rights and could not own land under normal law. This forced him to negotiate the purchase through the goodwill of the local population.

**Nesi elohim** (נְשִׂיא אֱלֹהִים) – "prince of God" or "mighty prince" – was the title the Hittites used for Abraham (v. 6). The expression shows the respect and honor Abraham had gained in the local community, which opened the door for him to negotiate a land purchase.

**Kesef over la-socher** (כֶּסֶף עֹבֵר לַסֹּחֵר) – "current currency among merchants" (v. 16) – indicates silver that was weighed and approved for trade. Coins did not yet exist; value was determined by weight. The standard ensured that the payment was of accepted quality and weight.

## Chapter-Specific Findings

Hittite legal texts from Hattusa (modern Boğazkale in Turkey), dated to approximately 1650–1180 BCE, show that purchasing an entire plot of land involved feudal obligations (such as tax liability) to the previous owner or the state. This may explain why Ephron insisted on selling the whole field, not just the cave – he simultaneously transferred any obligations to Abraham.

The price of 400 shekels of silver was significant. The Code of Hammurabi and other sources from the second millennium BCE show that land prices varied enormously depending on quality and location. Some scholars believe Ephron exploited Abraham's vulnerable situation, while others view the price as reasonable for a first-class burial site near the city.
