## Places

Chapter 3 mentions several specific places that require explanation. **Jerusalem** (referred to as "the city" in verse 1, and directly as "Zion" and "daughter of Jerusalem" in verses 14–16) is the main focus for both judgment and promises. The city is described with the title "rebellious and defiled" (מֹרְאָה וְנִגְאָלָה), which reflects its spiritual condition.

**"Rivers of Cush"** (verse 10) refers to the region south of Egypt, in present-day Sudan and Ethiopia. The Nile and its tributaries (the Blue and White Nile) marked the southern boundary of the known world in ancient Israel. The expression symbolizes that worshippers will come from the ends of the earth.

## Key Terms

The phrase **"pure lips"** (שָׂפָה בְרוּרָה) in verse 9 is theologically significant. It stands in contrast to the confusion of languages at Babel (Genesis 11) and points toward an eschatological time when peoples will be united in worship of the Lord. The term implies both purity in speech and unity in confession.

**"Evening wolves"** (זְאֵבֵי עֶרֶב) in verse 3 describe Jerusalem’s judges. The wolf was known as a nocturnal animal that hunted at twilight. The expression "they do not leave anything till morning" (לֹא גָרְמוּ לַבֹּקֶר) means either that they are so greedy that nothing is left for the next day, or that they never delay their plundering.

**"The humble and lowly people"** (עַם עָנִי וָדָל) in verse 12 represents the faithful remnant. These terms became important in later Jewish piety traditions and denote those who depend on God rather than on their own power or wealth.

## Events and Customs

The description of the city’s leaders in verses 3–4 reflects four social groups with specific responsibilities: **princes** (שָׂרֶיהָ) held political and military power, **judges** (שֹׁפְטֶיהָ) were to ensure justice, **prophets** (נְבִיאֶיהָ) conveyed God’s word, and **priests** (כֹּהֲנֶיהָ) oversaw worship and taught the law. The fact that all four groups had failed shows a total institutional collapse. The accusation that the priests "profaned what is holy and did violence to the law" points to violations of cultic regulations and incorrect interpretation of the law.

**The appointed feasts** mentioned in verse 18 (מוֹעֵד) refer to the annual pilgrimage festivals that required travel to Jerusalem. During exile and persecution, the inability to celebrate these became a source of deep sorrow and shame for the Jewish people.
