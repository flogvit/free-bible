# Historical and Cultural Context for Joshua 23

## People

The chapter presents Joshua as an elderly leader who gathers Israel’s leaders for a farewell address. The expression "old and advanced in years" (זָקֵן בָּא בַּיָּמִים) is a fixed formula in ancient Israel indicating that a person is nearing the end of their life. It is also used concerning Abraham (Gen 24:1) and David (1 Kgs 1:1). Joshua summons four groups of leaders: elders (זְקֵנִים), heads (רָאשִׁים), judges (שֹׁפְטִים), and officers (שֹׁטְרִים). This fourfold leadership reflects a developed administrative structure that combined tribal-based authority with more formalized functions.

## Places

The chapter refers to the geographical area between the Jordan (הַיַּרְדֵּן) and "the great sea toward the going down of the sun" (הַיָּם הַגָּדוֹל מְבוֹא הַשָּׁמֶשׁ), that is, the Mediterranean Sea in the west. This defines the boundaries of the promised land from east to west. "The good land" (הָאֲדָמָה הַטּוֹבָה), mentioned repeatedly, is a theologically charged expression that emphasizes the land’s character as God’s gift and the fulfillment of the promises made to the fathers.

## Events and Customs

Joshua’s warning against mentioning the names of foreign gods (לֹא־תַזְכִּירוּ), swearing by them (לֹא תַשְׁבִּיעוּ), serving them (לֹא תַעַבְדוּם), and bowing down to them (לֹא תִשְׁתַּחֲוּוּ) reflects the four main ways religion was practiced in the ancient Near East. Uttering a god’s name was itself a cultic act that acknowledged the deity’s power. The prohibition against intermarriage (הִתְחַתַּנְתֶּם) with the remaining peoples (v. 12) was not primarily ethnically motivated but religious: marriage created cultic obligations toward the gods of the other family.

## Key Terms

The verb "cling to" (דָּבַק) in verse 8 is a strong expression of loyalty and devotion, the same word used for the bond of marriage in Gen 2:24. Joshua uses this to describe the covenant relationship between God and Israel. In verse 13, four metaphors for danger are employed: "snare" (פַּח), "trap" (מוֹקֵשׁ), "whips on your sides" (לְשֹׁטֵט בְּצִדֵּיכֶם), and "thorns in your eyes" (וְלִצְנִנִים בְּעֵינֵיכֶם). These images are drawn from hunting and daily life and illustrate how the remaining peoples will become the cause of Israel’s downfall if they do not remain faithful to the covenant.
