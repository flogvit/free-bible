## People

**Pashhur son of Immer** (פַּשְׁחוּר בֶּן־אִמֵּר) was a priest holding a high administrative position in the temple. The title "overseer" (פָקִיד נָגִיד) indicates that he was responsible for maintaining order in the temple area, including the authority to punish those he regarded as rebels or false prophets. Immer was one of the priestly families (1 Chron 24:14), which confirms the family’s prominent status.

## Places

**The Upper Benjamin Gate** (שַׁעַר בִּנְיָמִן הָעֶלְיוֹן) was a gate in the temple complex, likely the northern entrance leading toward the territory of the tribe of Benjamin. This gate was a central location for public movement and trade, making it a strategic choice for public humiliation. **The stocks** (הַמַּהְפֶּכֶת), which stood at this gate, were a punishment device placed where people came and went, so that the punished person would be maximally exposed to shame.

## Key Terms

**Magor-missabib** (מָגוֹר מִסָּבִיב), meaning "terror on every side", is a wordplay that turns Pashhur’s name into a prophetic judgment. This expression is repeated in verse 10, where Jeremiah’s enemies use it against him, creating deep irony in the text. The Hebrew verb **פתה** (pata) in verse 7, translated "persuaded" or "enticed", carries strong connotations of seduction and is otherwise used to describe deceiving someone (cf. Exod 22:16). Jeremiah’s use of this word expresses his feeling of being overwhelmed by a calling he cannot escape.

## Events and Customs

**The stocks** were a punishment device that forced the body into a painful, twisted position (Hebrew מַהְפֶּכֶת comes from the root "to twist/turn"). The punishment was designed for maximum public shame and physical discomfort. Such treatment of prophets violated the respect normally shown to God’s messengers, but illustrates the intense conflict between established religious leaders and prophets who proclaimed unpopular messages. Jeremiah’s lament in verses 14–18 follows a pattern known from Job 3 and Mesopotamian laments, where cursing one’s own birth expresses extreme despair.
