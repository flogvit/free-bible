## Key Concepts

The chapter uses eight Hebrew synonyms for God’s revelation throughout: **תּוֹרָה** (*tora*, "law/instruction") denotes God’s guiding instruction; **עֵדוּת/עֵדֹת** (*edut/edot*, "testimony") refers to God’s covenant stipulations that bear witness to His will; **פִּקּוּדִים** (*piqqudim*, "precepts/commandments") emphasizes God’s authoritative oversight; **חֻקִּים** (*huqqim*, "statutes/decrees*) refers to engraved, permanent ordinances; **מִצְוֹת** (*mitsvot*, "commandments") are direct orders; **מִשְׁפָּטִים** (*mishpatim*, "judgments/rulings") indicate legal decisions; **דָּבָר/דְּבָרִים** (*davar/devarim*, "word") is God’s spoken revelation; and **אִמְרָה** (*imra*, "utterance/saying*) denotes God’s promises and declarations.

## Events and Customs

Verse 62 mentions rising at midnight (חֲצוֹת־לַיְלָה, *chatsot-layla*) to praise, reflecting a devotional practice in which the godly interrupted their sleep for prayer and meditation on Scripture. Verse 164 speaks of praising God "seven times a day" (שֶׁבַע בַּיּוֹם, *sheva bayyom*), which may have formed the basis for later Jewish and Christian liturgical prayer traditions.
