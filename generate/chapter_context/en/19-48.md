# Historical and Cultural Context for Psalm 48

## Places

Psalm 48 is a Zion psalm that praises God and celebrates his presence in Jerusalem and on the temple hill. The expression "the heights of Zaphon" (v. 3, Hebrew *yarketey tsafon*) is particularly interesting. "Zaphon" was the name of the holy mountain in Canaanite-Phoenician religion, where the god Baal was believed to dwell. By applying this terminology to Zion, the psalmist makes a theological point: the God of Israel reigns where the nations thought their gods lived. Jerusalem is actually located not in the north but in southern Israel—the language is mythological and polemical, not geographical.

"The ships of Tarshish" (v. 8, Hebrew *oniyyot Tarshish*) refer to large seagoing merchant vessels. Tarshish was possibly Tartessos in southern Spain, though other locations have also been proposed. In any case, these ships represented the farthest western horizon in the ancient worldview and symbolized wealth, power, and human pride.

## Events and Customs

The psalm describes a situation where kings gathered against Jerusalem but were struck with panic and fled (vv. 5–8). This may allude to a historical event such as Sennacherib's failed siege in 701 B.C. (2 Kings 18–19), or it could be a poetic portrayal of God's general protection of the city. The call to "walk about Zion" and "count her towers" (vv. 13–14, Hebrew *sobbu Tsiyyon... sifru migdaleyha*) may reflect a pilgrimage procession in which participants walked around the city walls as part of a religious celebration.

## Key Terms

The word *misgav* (v. 4, "stronghold" or "refuge") denotes a high, inaccessible place of safety. When God is described as *misgav*, his role as the ultimate protector is emphasized. The expression "the city of the great King" (*kiryat melekh rav*, v. 3) uses royal terminology common throughout the ancient Near East but applies it to Yahweh rather than an earthly ruler.
