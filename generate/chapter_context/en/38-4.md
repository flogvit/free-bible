## People

**Zerubbabel** is the central human figure in this chapter. He was of Davidic descent and served as governor (*peḥâ*) over the province of Judah under Persian rule. His Akkadian name (*Zēr-Bābili*, "Offspring of Babylon") testifies that he was born in exile. In this chapter, he is promised that he will complete the temple construction he has begun (v. 9), and he is presented as God's chosen instrument—not through military might, but by God's Spirit (v. 6).

## Key Terms

**Menorah** (מְנוֹרָה, "lampstand") – The vision shows a golden lampstand with seven lamps, alluding to the sacred furnishings of the tabernacle and temple (Exod 25:31-40). What is unique about Zechariah's vision is the addition of a "golden bowl" (*gullâ*, "bowl/container") at the top, pipes (*mutsaqot*) for the lamps, as well as two olive trees on either side. The system illustrates that God's work is sustained by His provision.

**Bené-hayyitzhar** (בְנֵי־הַיִּצְהָר, "sons of oil") in verse 14 is an unusual expression. The Hebrew *yitzhar* means "fresh oil" and is often used for olive oil in its purest form. The phrase refers to "the two who stand by the Lord of all the earth"—likely a reference to the dual leadership of royal and priestly nature in the restored community.

**"Not by might, nor by power"** (לֹא בְחַיִל וְלֹא בְכֹחַ, v. 6) – The words *ḥayil* (military strength) and *kōaḥ* (physical force) are contrasted with *rûaḥ* (Spirit). For powerless Judah under Persian domination, this message was both comfort and theological guidance: the temple construction would succeed by God's Spirit.

**The great mountain** (הַר־הַגָּדוֹל, v. 7) – The mountain that will become a plain before Zerubbabel represents seemingly insurmountable obstacles to the temple building. The promise that it will be leveled emphasizes God's power to remove opposition.

## Events and Customs

**The capstone** (*'even hārō'šâ*, v. 7) – The context (Zerubbabel's hands have laid the foundation of the house and shall complete it, v. 9) suggests that this is the finishing stone that crowns the structure. The shout "Grace, grace to it!" (תְּשֻׁאוֹת חֵן חֵן) likely reflects ritual acclamations at such ceremonies.

**The plumb line** (*'even habbedîl*, v. 10) – A plummet or measuring stone used by builders to ensure walls were erected straight. That Zerubbabel holds this symbolizes his active leadership of the construction work.

**"The day of small things"** (יוֹם קְטַנּוֹת, v. 10) – The expression refers to the seemingly humble beginning of the temple construction, which some despised compared to the splendor of the first temple. The verse promises that they will rejoice when they see the work completed.

## Chapter-Specific Findings

Archaeological finds of multi-lamp lampstands from the Iron Age and Persian period in Israel confirm that such objects were known in cultic and daily use. Seals from the Persian period confirm the use of governor titles (*peḥâ*) in provincial administration, which corresponds with Zerubbabel's role.
