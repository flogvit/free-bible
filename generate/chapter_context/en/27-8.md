# Historical and Cultural Context for Daniel 8

## People

**Belshazzar** is mentioned as king in the vision dated to his third year of reign (ca. 550–548 B.C.). Historically, Belshazzar was co-regent under his father Nabonidus, the last Neo-Babylonian king. This places the vision within a Babylonian exile context, even though its content points forward to the Medo-Persian and Greek empires.

**Gabriel** (v. 16) is the first angel named in the Hebrew Bible. The name means "God's mighty man" or "God is my strength." In Jewish tradition, Gabriel was later counted as one of the four archangels. His task here is to give Daniel understanding of the vision—an interpretive and revelatory function.

## Places

**Susa (Shushan)** was an ancient city in the province of Elam, by the Ulai River (in the modern Karkheh/Karun region of Iran). The city was already significant before the Persian period but later became the winter capital for the great Persian kings. That Daniel sees himself here in the vision is prophetically meaningful—Susa would become a center of power precisely for the empire represented by the ram.

**"The Beautiful Land"** (הַצֶּבִי, *ha-tsevi*, v. 9) is a poetic designation for the land of Israel, also used in Daniel 11:16, 41. The expression underscores the land's special beauty and value in God's eyes.

## Events and Customs

**The Daily Sacrifice** (הַתָּמִיד, *ha-tamid*, vv. 11–13) refers to the continuous burnt offerings brought morning and evening in the temple (Exodus 29:38–42). The abolition of this sacrifice was the most serious violation of Jewish worship. Historically, this was fulfilled under Antiochus IV Epiphanes (167–164 B.C.), who halted the temple sacrifices and desecrated the temple.

**The Desecration of the Sanctuary** is described with the expression "the abomination that makes desolate" (הַפֶּשַׁע שֹׁמֵם). This points to historical events where temple service was interrupted and the sanctuary profaned—something experienced traumatically during the Maccabean revolt.

## Key Terms

**"Evenings and mornings, 2300"** (v. 14) has been interpreted in various ways. The expression עֶרֶב בֹּקֶר (*erev boker*) can be understood as 2300 individual sacrifices (morning + evening = 1,150 days) or as 2,300 full days. This is linked to the period during which the sanctuary would remain desecrated before it was "justified" or cleansed.

**"The time of wrath"** (אַחֲרִית הַזָּעַם, v. 19) is a technical term pointing to a specific period of divine judgment and testing—not general world history, but a particular eschatological epoch.

**"The little horn"** (קֶרֶן־אַחַת מִצְּעִירָה, v. 9) describes a power that grows from humble beginnings. In ancient Near Eastern imagery, horns symbolize royal power, and the description fits historically with Antiochus IV, who came to power through intrigue rather than legitimate succession.

## Chapter-Specific Findings

Archaeological excavations in Susa (modern Shush, Iran) have uncovered significant Persian palace ruins and confirm the city's role as a royal residence, just as the chapter suggests by placing Daniel's vision there.

Historical sources, especially 1 and 2 Maccabees as well as the writings of Josephus, document in detail Antiochus IV Epiphanes' persecution of the Jews (167–164 B.C.), including the cessation of temple sacrifices, the setting up of a Zeus altar in the temple, and the subsequent Maccabean revolt that led to the rededication of the temple (Hanukkah).
