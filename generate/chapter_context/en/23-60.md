# Historical and Cultural Context for Isaiah 60

## Places

The chapter mentions several geographical areas known for their trade and wealth. **Midian** (v. 6) was a region east of the Gulf of Aqaba, inhabited by descendants of Abraham through Keturah (Gen 25:2). **Ephah** was a Midianite tribe in the same region. **Sheba** refers to the South Arabian kingdom in present-day Yemen, famous for its trade in gold, frankincense, and spices—known from the story of the Queen of Sheba. **Kedar** and **Nebajoth** (v. 7) were Arab nomadic tribes descended from Ishmael (Gen 25:13), known for their large flocks of sheep. **Tarshish** (v. 9) likely denoted a distant place on the Mediterranean, possibly in Spain or Tartessos, and "ships of Tarshish" (אֳנִיּוֹת תַּרְשִׁישׁ) was a term for large seagoing merchant vessels. **Lebanon** (v. 13) was famous for its cedar trees and other noble timber used in temple construction.

## Events and Customs

The description of camel caravans loaded with gold and frankincense (v. 6) reflects the extensive trade along the Incense Route that connected South Arabia with the Mediterranean. Frankincense (לְבוֹנָה, *levonah*) was especially valuable in temple worship. This trade was vital for the Arab tribes and brought enormous wealth to the regions it passed through. The practice of sacrifice is described in verse 7, where the sheep from Kedar and rams from Nebajoth shall "come up with acceptance on my altar"—this refers to the burnt offering ritual in which the entire animal was burned as a gift to God. That foreign peoples bring sacrificial animals to the temple in Jerusalem represented a radical vision of Gentile participation in Israel's worship.

## Key Terms

The Hebrew word **אוֹר** (*'or*, "light") is used consistently throughout the chapter as a central theological concept, especially in the opening words קוּמִי אוֹרִי ("Arise, shine!", v. 1). Light represents God's presence, salvation, and glory, in contrast to the darkness covering the peoples of the earth (v. 2). The expression **כְּבוֹד יְהוָה** (*kevod YHWH*, "the glory of the Lord") in verses 1–2 denotes God's visible manifestation, as it appeared in the tabernacle and temple. The term **תִּפְאָרָה** (*tif'arah*, "splendor/glory") and the verb לְהִתְפָּאֵר ("to glorify") are repeated several times and emphasize God's plan to beautify and exalt Zion. The expression **אֲבִיר יַעֲקֹב** (*'avir Ya'akov*, "the Mighty One of Jacob") in verse 16 is an ancient title for God that underscores His power as the protector of the patriarchs' descendants.
