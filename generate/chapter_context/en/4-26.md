## People

Eleazar, son of Aaron, now leads the census together with Moses (v. 1, 63). This marks a generational shift in priestly leadership following Aaron's death (Num 20:22-29). Eleazar had taken over his father's role as high priest, and his involvement in the census underscores the central role of the priesthood in administrative tasks.

The daughters of Zelophehad—Mahlah, Noah, Hoglah, Milcah, and Tirzah—are specifically mentioned (v. 33) because their father died without sons. This note prepares for the important legal case in chapter 27 concerning women's inheritance rights. Caleb son of Jephunneh and Joshua son of Nun are highlighted as the only survivors from the first census (v. 65), fulfilling the promise given in Num 14:30.

## Places

The plains of Moab (עַרְבֹת מוֹאָב, 'arvot mo'av) by the Jordan opposite Jericho form the location for this second census (v. 3, 63). The area lies in the flat valley east of the Jordan River, north of the Dead Sea. This was a fertile plain landscape that served as the staging ground for Israel's upcoming entry into Canaan. Geographically, the people were now at the end of their journey, in sharp contrast to the wilderness of Sinai where the first census took place 38 years earlier (v. 64).

## Events and Customs

The census (פְּקוּדִים, pekudim) was an administrative action serving several purposes: military mobilization, organization of the tribes, and now especially preparation for land distribution. The census included all men from twenty years old and upward who could "go out to war" (v. 2), the same age limit as in the first census. The system of tribal families (מִשְׁפָּחֹת, mishpahot) structured the entire census and formed the basis for how the land was to be distributed.

The principle for land distribution combined proportionality with casting lots (v. 54-56). Larger tribes were to receive larger areas, while the lot (גּוֹרָל, goral) decided where each tribe would live. This combination ensured both fairness in size and divine guidance in placement.

## Key Terms

The expression "to lift up the head" (שְׂאוּ אֶת־רֹאשׁ, se'u et-rosh, v. 2) is the Hebrew idiom for counting or conducting a census. The expression implies registering each person individually, giving the census a dignified character rather than treating the people as an anonymous mass.

The term נַחֲלָה (nahala, inheritance/heritage lot) appears repeatedly in the latter part of the chapter (v. 53-56, 62) and denotes the permanent family property that was to pass from generation to generation. This was not merely a legal concept but expressed the theological idea that the land was God's gift to His people.

## Chapter-Specific Findings

The chapter refers to the rebellion of Korah, Dathan, and Abiram (v. 9-11), described in chapter 16. The note that "the sons of Korah did not die" (v. 11) explains how the Korahites later became a significant Levitical family responsible for temple music, as several psalms testify (Ps 42, 44-49, 84-85, 87-88). This brief notice preserves an important tradition about the family's survival and rehabilitation.
