## Places

The chapter describes the **priests' chambers** (לְשָׁכוֹת, *leshakhot*) located north and south of the temple building, facing the enclosed area (*gizrah*). These chambers were three-story structures with three sections each, situated in the transition zone between the inner and outer courts. Architecturally, they are described as narrowing toward the top because the upper floors lacked columns and therefore occupied less space. Their placement near the holy area underscores their function as transitional zones between the profane and the holy.

## Events and Customs

The chapter provides important information about **the priests' practices regarding meals and changing garments**. The priests' chambers were to be used for eating the most holy offerings (קָדְשֵׁי הַקֳּדָשִׁים) – including the grain offering (*minchah*), sin offering (*chattat*), and guilt offering (*asham*). The practice of changing clothes before going out to the people (v. 14) reflects the belief that holiness could be transferred through contact. Similar requirements for changing garments are found in Leviticus 16:23-24.

## Key Concepts

**Gizrah** (גִּזְרָה) – This Hebrew term refers to the enclosed or "cut off" area west of the temple building. The word comes from the root גזר, meaning "to cut off" or "separate," and marks an area with a special status of holiness.

**To distinguish between the holy and the common** (לְהַבְדִּיל בֵּין הַקֹּדֶשׁ לְחֹל) – The chapter's concluding verse (v. 20) summarizes the theological function of the wall. This formulation is central to Israelite thought (cf. Leviticus 10:10; Ezekiel 44:23). The wall, measuring 500 by 500 cubits, enclosed the entire temple area and created a buffer zone between the sanctuary and the outside world.
