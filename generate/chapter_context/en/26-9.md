# Historical and Cultural Context for Ezekiel 9

## Places

**The upper gate facing north** (v. 2) refers to a specific entrance to the temple complex. This is likely the same gate called "the upper gate of the house of the Lord" in 2 Kings 15:35, built by King Jotham. The fact that the heavenly executioners come from the north carries double significance: North was traditionally the direction from which enemies approached Jerusalem, and in chapter 8 it was precisely at the northern gate that idolatry took place.

**The bronze altar** (v. 2) was the great burnt offering altar in the outer court of the temple. This altar, originally built by Solomon, was the center of legitimate sacrificial worship. The fact that the six men take their stand at this altar ironically marks where judgment begins—where worship should have occurred; the place meant to bring atonement now becomes the starting point for punishment.

## Key Terms

**"Tav"** (תָּו) in verse 4 is the last letter of the Hebrew alphabet. In ancient Hebrew script (before the exile), tav had the form of an X or +, that is, a cross or mark. Placing this sign on the foreheads of the righteous served as a protective mark, parallel to the blood on the doorposts in Egypt (Exodus 12:7). The term "to mark" (הִתְוִיתָ) is used only here in the Old Testament.

**"Badim"** (בַּדִּים)—the white linen garment worn by the writing man (vv. 2–3)—was the holy attire of the priests. This places the figure in a heavenly-priestly role, as an intermediary between God and humanity. The fact that he carries "the scribe’s inkhorn" (קֶסֶת הַסֹּפֵר) shows his function as the recorder of the names of the faithful.

## Events and Customs

**Judgment beginning at the sanctuary** (v. 6) reflects the principle that responsibility corresponds to closeness to God. The elders before the temple—who should have been the spiritual leaders of the people—are struck first. This principle is also found in 1 Peter 4:17. In ancient Near Eastern thought, the temple was the place of protection above all others; that judgment begins right here overturns all expectations.

**Those who "groan and sigh"** (הַנֶּאֱנָחִים וְהַנֶּאֱנָקִים) over the abominations represent a faithful remnant in the city. This concept of a preserved remnant amid judgment is central to prophetic theology and offers hope even within the darkest message.
