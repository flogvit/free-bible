# Context for Psalms 142

## Historical Background

The heading places this psalm in a specific historical situation: "When he was in the cave" (בִּהְיוֹתוֹ בַמְּעָרָה). This likely refers to one of two events from David's flight from King Saul: the cave at Adullam (1 Samuel 22:1) or the cave at En-Gedi (1 Samuel 24:1–4). At Adullam, David gathered a group of about 400 men who were in distress or burdened by debt, while the event at En-Gedi was where David spared Saul's life. Both situations represent periods when David was hunted and isolated, which aligns well with the psalm's content regarding persecution and lack of refuge.

## Locations

The heading mentions "the cave" (בַמְּעָרָה), which likely refers to either the cave at Adullam in the Shephelah region or the cave at En-Gedi on the western shore of the Dead Sea. Both are located in the rugged Judean hill country, known for its limestone caves that provided shelter for fugitives.

## Key Terms

The word **מַשְׂכִּיל** (maskil) in the heading denotes a specific type of wisdom psalm or skillfully crafted poem, possibly with an instructional purpose. Its meaning is debated, but it may indicate a psalm that calls for reflection or insight.

The expression **מַסְגֵּר** (masger) in verse 8, translated as "prison" or "enclosure," is used metaphorically to describe the cave as a prison. The word is otherwise used for actual prisons and creates a powerful contrast between the physical cave and the spiritual freedom the psalmist seeks.

**הַבֵּיט יָמִין** ("look to the right") in verse 5 reflects a cultural notion where the protector or defender stood on the right side of the one being guarded. When David looks to the right and finds no one, this expresses total abandonment by human help.
