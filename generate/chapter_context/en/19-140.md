## Key Concepts

The chapter contains several Hebrew words and expressions that require further explanation. The expression **אִישׁ חֲמָסִים** (*ish chamasim*, "man of violence") in verses 2 and 5 uses the plural form of *chamas* (violence, injustice), which intensifies the meaning and describes a person characterized by repeated and systematic violence. The word *chamas* denotes not only physical violence but also social oppression and legal injustice.

In verse 4 we find **עַכְשׁוּב** (*akshub*), a rare Hebrew word that also appears in Psalm 58:5. It likely refers to a poisonous snake, possibly a viper. The comparison of the enemies' tongue with a serpent's tongue connects to the ancient belief that snakes carried poison under their lips—a picture of deadly and destructive speech.

## Events and Customs

Verse 6 describes hunting and trapping methods well known in ancient Israel: **פַּח** (*pach*, "trap/snare"), **חֲבָלִים** (*chavalim*, "ropes/cords"), **רֶשֶׁת** (*reshet*, "net") and **מֹקְשִׁים** (*moqshim*, "traps"). These tools were used to catch birds and animals, but here they function as metaphors for the enemies' cunning plans. Such imagery was widespread throughout the ancient Near East and is also found in Egyptian and Mesopotamian texts.

The expression **בְּיוֹם נָשֶׁק** (*beyom nasheq*, "on the day of weapons/the day of battle") in verse 8 refers to a day of armed conflict. The image of God as one who **"covers the head"** (*sakkota leroshi*) alludes to the helmet or shield that protected the warrior in battle—a concrete metaphor from Israelite warfare.

## Chapter-Specific Findings

The concept of glowing coals and fire as divine judgment (verse 11) has parallels in Ugaritic texts from Ras Shamra (ca. 1400–1200 BCE), where fire is an instrument of divine justice. The term **מַהֲמֹרוֹת** (*mahamorot*, "deep pits/abyss") occurs only here in the Bible and may denote water-filled pits or marshy areas—a picture of total destruction with no possibility of rising again.
