## People

**The Prince (הַנָּשִׂיא / ha-nasi)**: The chapter introduces a prominent figure called "the prince" who receives special privileges at the east gate (v. 3). This title is deliberately distinct from "king" (מֶלֶךְ / melekh), reflecting Ezekiel's vision of a leader with limited power compared to earlier Israelite kings. The prince has religious functions but no direct access to the most holy areas.

**The Sons of Zadok (בְּנֵי צָדוֹק / bene tsadok)**: The chapter establishes the line of Zadok as the only legitimate priestly family in the future temple (v. 15). Zadok was high priest under David and Solomon, known for his faithfulness. His descendants are portrayed as pure and faithful through Israel's periods of apostasy.

## Places

**The East Gate**: The chapter begins with Ezekiel being brought back to the outer east gate, which is now shut (vv. 1–2). This gate, through which God's glory entered (43:1–4), was to remain closed because the Lord had passed through it—a unique privilege that marks the gate's holiness.

## Events and Customs

**The Role of Foreigners in the Temple**: Ezekiel condemns the practice of allowing uncircumcised foreigners to serve in the sanctuary (vv. 7–9). The text denounces the use of foreigners in temple service, which Ezekiel views as a violation of the sanctuary's purity.

**Linen Garments and Prohibition of Sweat**: The regulations concerning the priests' clothing (vv. 17–18) require linen and forbid wool because it causes sweating. In the hot climate and during the performance of physical rituals, wool would cause perspiration. In Israelite thought, bodily fluids were associated with impurity, and sweat would profane the holy space.

## Key Terms

**Uncircumcised in Heart and Flesh (עַרְלֵי־לֵב וְעַרְלֵי בָשָׂר)**: This expression in verses 7 and 9 combines physical and spiritual circumcision. "Uncircumcised heart" denotes a heart that is closed to God and His will—a metaphor known from the Torah (Deut 10:16; 30:6). The combination emphasizes that both an outward sign and an inward attitude are necessary for participation in worship.

**Mishméret (מִשְׁמֶרֶת)**: This word, which appears several times in the chapter (vv. 8, 14, 15, 16), means "watch" or "duty." It involves both responsibility for physically guarding the sanctuary and correctly performing prescribed duties. The degradation of the Levites is described precisely as their failure to keep their mishméret.
