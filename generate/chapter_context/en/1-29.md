# Historical and Cultural Context for Genesis 29

## People

**Laban** (לָבָן, "white") is Jacob's maternal uncle – the son of Bethuel and brother of Rebekah. In this chapter, he first appears as a hospitable host who runs to meet Jacob, embraces him, and kisses him (verse 13), but later proves deceitful when he substitutes Leah for Rachel on the wedding night (verses 23-25).

**Leah** (לֵאָה, possibly "weary") and **Rachel** (רָחֵל, "ewe") are Laban's two daughters who both become Jacob's wives. The description of Leah's eyes as "רַכּוֹת" (verse 17) is debated – it could mean weak, dull, or actually beautiful and gentle eyes. Rachel is described as "beautiful in form and appearance" (verse 17) and introduced as a shepherdess for her father's flock (verse 9). Laban gave servant girls to his daughters: **Zilpah** (זִלְפָּה) to Leah (verse 24), and **Bilhah** (בִּלְהָה) to Rachel (verse 29).

## Places

**The East** (אֶרֶץ בְּנֵי־קֶדֶם, verse 1) refers to the area east of Canaan, specifically the region around Haran.

**The Well in the Field** (verses 2-3) is the place where Jacob meets shepherds and Rachel. The large stone covering the well's opening normally required all flocks to gather before it could be rolled away (verses 3, 8), but Jacob rolls it alone to water Laban's flock (verse 10).

## Events and Customs

**Bride Price and Work Agreement**: Jacob's offer to work seven years for Rachel (verse 18) reflects the custom of *mohar* (bride price). Since Jacob arrived without resources, he offered his labor instead. Seven years was an unusually long period, which underscores Jacob's love (verse 20: "they seemed to him but a few days").

**The Wedding Feast** (מִשְׁתֶּה, verse 22) and **the Week of Celebration** (שְׁבֻעַ, verses 27-28): Laban's justification for the deception was that "it is not done so in our place, to give the younger before the firstborn" (verse 26). Jacob had to complete Leah's week of celebration before he could have Rachel, in exchange for a promise of seven more years of work.

**Leah's Four Sons** (verses 32-35) are born in this chapter: Reuben, Simeon, Levi, and Judah. The text explains that the Lord opened Leah's womb because she was "less loved" (שְׂנוּאָה, verse 31).

## Key Terms

The expression **"lifted his feet"** (וַיִּשָּׂא יַעֲקֹב רַגְלָיו) in verse 1 is an unusual Hebrew idiom that literally means "lifted his feet" and suggests that Jacob walked with ease and expectation.

The word **"שְׂנוּאָה"** (verses 31, 33) is often translated as "hated," but rather means "less loved" or "neglected" in comparison to Rachel. This expression is used comparatively.

**The Sons' Names** have etymological significance: **Reuben** (רְאוּבֵן) – from רָאָה (to see) and בֵּן (son), with wordplay on "the Lord has seen my affliction" (verse 32); **Simeon** (שִׁמְעוֹן) – from שָׁמַע (to hear), "because the Lord has heard" (verse 33); **Levi** (לֵוִי) – from לָוָה (to attach oneself), "now my husband will be attached to me" (verse 34); **Judah** (יְהוּדָה) – from יָדָה (to praise), "this time I will praise the Lord" (verse 35).
