# Historical and Cultural Context for Psalm 110

## People

**Melchizedek (מַלְכִּי־צֶדֶק)** is a mysterious figure mentioned only twice in the Old Testament: Genesis 14:18–20 and here in verse 4. In the Genesis account, he appears as "king of Salem" and "priest of God Most High (El Elyon)," who blesses Abraham and receives a tithe from him. His name means "my king is righteousness" or "king of righteousness." Salem is traditionally identified with Jerusalem (see Psalm 76:3). What makes Psalm 110 unique is that kingship and priesthood are combined in one person—something unusual in Israel, where these roles were normally separate (the king from the tribe of Judah, the priests from Levi).

## Places

**Zion (צִיּוֹן)** is mentioned in verse 2 as the starting point for the king’s rule. In a Davidic context, Zion specifically refers to the City of David, the oldest part of Jerusalem that David captured from the Jebusites (2 Samuel 5:7). Archaeological excavations in the City of David have uncovered structures from the Iron Age, including what may be remains of administrative buildings. Later, Zion became synonymous with the temple mount and all of Jerusalem as God’s chosen dwelling place and center of royal authority.

## Events and Customs

**The coronation ritual** reflected in this psalm has parallels in ancient Near Eastern royal ideologies. The expression "sit at my right hand" (verse 1) mirrors an ancient Near Eastern court custom where the place of honor at the ruler’s right side indicated the highest rank and authority. The image of "enemies as a footstool" (הֲדֹם לְרַגְלֶיךָ) is known from Egyptian and Assyrian iconography, where victorious kings were depicted with their foot on the necks of defeated enemies or with enemies carved into their footstools.

**The king-priest combination** in verse 4 reflects a practice known from several ancient Near Eastern cultures, especially in pre-Israelite Jerusalem under Jebusite rule. The fact that Melchizedek was both king and priest may point to a Jerusalem tradition that David inherited when he captured the city. The Melchizedek tradition became significant in later messianic interpretation.

## Key Terms

**נְאֻם יְהוָה (ne'um YHWH)** – "an utterance of the Lord" – is a prophetic formula that usually introduces divine speech among the prophets (especially Jeremiah and Ezekiel). The use of this formula in a psalm is unusual and gives the text special authority as direct divine revelation, not merely human praise.

**אֲדֹנִי (adoni)** – "my lord" – is used here in the form typically designating a human master or king, as opposed to אֲדֹנָי (Adonai), which is most often used for God. This has been central to both Jewish and Christian interpretation of whom the psalm refers to.

**עַל־דִּבְרָתִי (al-divrati)** – "according to the order of" or "because of" – this expression in verse 4 is rare and indicates that this priesthood follows a pattern or order tracing back to Melchizedek, thus older than the Levitical priesthood.
