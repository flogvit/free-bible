## Places

### Jericho (יְרִיחוֹ)

Jericho was strategically located in the Jordan Valley and controlled important trade routes and water sources, making it key to further conquest of Canaan. Verse 1 describes the city as "shut up and shut tight" (סֹגֶרֶת וּמְסֻגֶּרֶת) – an intensive expression that emphasizes the total siege condition where no one could go in or out. The city's walls and defenses presented a seemingly insurmountable obstacle, highlighting the miraculous nature of God's intervention.

## Events and Customs

### The Procession Ritual

The seven-day procession around Jericho combines military and cultic practices in a way that is unique in biblical literature. The ark of the covenant, carried by priests, served as God's visible presence among the people. The procession resembles both triumphal marches known from surrounding cultures and Israelite cultic processions around sanctuaries. The number seven – seven priests, seven trumpets, seven days, seven circuits – emphasizes wholeness and divine perfection in Old Testament symbolism.

### Herem (חֵרֶם) – The Ban

The chapter describes the practice of herem, total dedication to destruction. All living things and all property in the city were to be destroyed as an offering to the Lord (verses 17-21). This was not primarily a war booty strategy but a religious act where victory belonged to God alone. Parallels exist in the Mesha Stele from Moab (ca. 850-840 BC), where King Mesha describes how he carried out herem against Israelite cities for his god Chemosh. The practice marked that the conquest was a holy war, not ordinary territorial expansion. Verses 18-19 specify that metal objects were to go into the Lord's treasury – a detail that becomes important for understanding Achan's sin in chapter 7.

## Key Terms

### Shofar and Joblim (שׁוֹפְרוֹת הַיּוֹבְלִים)

The text specifically mentions "joblim trumpets" or "ram's horn trumpets." The expression *shofrot hayovelim* can mean either trumpets made from ram's horns or trumpets used at the Jubilee year. The shofar was not a musical instrument in the usual sense, but a signal instrument used to gather the people, proclaim holy times and – as here – mark God's presence in war. The sound was meant to evoke awe and signal divine intervention.

### The Curse on Jericho (verse 26)

Joshua's oath that whoever rebuilds Jericho will lose his firstborn at its foundation laying and his youngest at its gate construction is a special form of prophetic curse. This was not unusual in the ancient Near East, where important buildings were sometimes dedicated with human sacrifice (foundation sacrifices). The curse is fulfilled according to 1 Kings 16:34 during King Ahab's time, when Hiel from Bethel rebuilt the city.

## People

### Rahab (רָחָב)

Rahab is described as *zonah* (זוֹנָה), usually translated "prostitute" or "harlot." Some scholars have suggested that *zonah* here may mean innkeeper, since such businesses were often combined in ancient cities. Her house in the city wall placed her literally on society's edge – a position that made rescue possible when the wall collapsed. That she and her family were spared and "lived among Israel to this day" (verse 25) shows that foreigners could be incorporated into the covenant community through faith and action. The New Testament mentions her in Jesus' genealogy (Matt 1:5) and as an example of faith (Heb 11:31; James 2:25).
