# Historical and Cultural Context for Numbers 30

## Events and Customs

This chapter deals with vows (*neder*) and binding obligations (*issar*), which were central elements in Israelite society. A vow to God was a voluntary, yet extremely serious commitment—breaking such a vow was regarded as sin against the Lord Himself. Vows could involve giving gifts to the sanctuary, abstaining from certain foods or drinks, or committing oneself to specific actions.

The patriarchal family structure described here—in which a father has authority over an unmarried daughter and a husband over his wife—reflects the broader social reality of the ancient Near East. The chapter grants women a degree of religious autonomy that was unusual for the time, in that their vows were actually valid unless explicitly annulled on the same day the husband or father heard about them.

## Key Terms

The Hebrew verb *heni* in verses 6, 9, and 12 means "to hinder" or "to annul" and is used specifically regarding the cancellation of a vow. The expression *mivta sefateha* ("the utterance of her lips") in verses 7 and 9 emphasizes the oral nature of vows—in a culture without written contracts for ordinary people, the spoken word was legally binding.

The term *le'annot nafesh* ("to afflict oneself") in verse 14 refers to self-imposed vows of abstinence, likely fasting or other forms of asceticism. This is the same expression used concerning the Day of Atonement (Leviticus 16:29), showing that these vows often concerned personal piety and self-discipline.
