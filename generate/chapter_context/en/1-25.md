# Historical and Cultural Context for Genesis 25

## People

**Keturah** (קְטוּרָה) is mentioned as Abraham's wife (v. 1). Her name means "incense" or "the one who burns incense," which may suggest a connection to trading families in the incense regions. Midian (מִדְיָן), one of her sons, became the ancestor of the Midianites, who later played an important role in Israelite history.

**Esau and Jacob** are introduced as Isaac's twin sons (vv. 24–26). Esau (עֵשָׂו) is described as "reddish" (אַדְמוֹנִי) and "all over like a hairy garment" (כֻּלּוֹ כְּאַדֶּרֶת שֵׂעָר), and his name sounds similar to Seir (שֵׂעִיר), the land of the Edomites. His nickname "Edom" (אֱדוֹם) means "red" and is explained by the red stew he asked for (v. 30). Jacob (יַעֲקֹב, Ya'akov) means "he grasps the heel" or "he supplants," a wordplay based on his holding onto his brother's heel (עֲקֵב, 'aqev) at birth.

## Places

**Be'er-Lahai-Roi** (בְּאֵר לַחַי רֹאִי), where Isaac settled (v. 11), was the well where Hagar had previously met the angel of the Lord. The place lay between Kadesh and Bered, in the southern part of Canaan.

**The Cave of Machpelah** near Mamre (vv. 9–10) was the burial site Abraham had purchased from Ephron the Hittite (בֶּן־צֹחַר הַחִתִּי), where Sarah was already buried. The fact that both Isaac and Ishmael buried their father together shows that family obligations at funerals took precedence over other conflicts in ancient Near Eastern culture.

## Events and Customs

**The Birthright** (בְּכֹרָה, bekhorah) carried significant privileges in the ancient Near East. The firstborn son usually received a double portion of the inheritance and assumed leadership of the family along with religious responsibilities. Ancient Near Eastern texts document that the birthright could be sold or transferred, providing a cultural parallel to Esau's sale of his right to Jacob (vv. 29–34).

**Inheritance and Concubines**: Verses 5–6 show that Abraham gave "all he owned" to Isaac, while the sons of concubine(s) (פִּילַגְשִׁים, pilagshim) received gifts and were sent away eastward. This pattern has parallels in ancient Near Eastern inheritance laws.

## Key Terms

**"Gathered to his people"** (וַיֵּאָסֶף אֶל־עַמָּיו) is used concerning both Abraham's death (v. 8) and Ishmael's death (v. 17). This Hebrew idiom for death suggests a belief in union with the ancestors after death, and it means more than just physical burial—it implies a form of continued fellowship with one's kin.

**"The land of the East"** (אֶרֶץ קֶדֶם, 'erets qedem) in verse 6 refers to the areas east of Canaan, likely the Syrian-Arabian desert. This became the homeland of tribes that later had both trade connections and conflicts with the Israelites.

**"To seek the Lord"** (לִדְרֹשׁ אֶת־יְהוָה) in verse 22 describes Rebekah's appeal to God regarding the twins struggling within her womb. The verb דָּרַשׁ (darash) means to seek an oracle or prophetic guidance, a practice common throughout the ancient Near East when facing unusual or troubling circumstances.
