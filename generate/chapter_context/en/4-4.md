# Historical and Cultural Context for Numbers 4

## People

The chapter focuses on the three Levitical clans: the **Kohathites** (בְּנֵי קְהָת), the **Gershonites** (בְּנֵי גֵרְשׁוֹן), and the **Merarites** (בְּנֵי מְרָרִי), descendants of Levi's three sons. The Kohathites receive special attention because they are responsible for the most holy objects (קֹדֶשׁ הַקֳּדָשִׁים). **Eleazar**, Aaron's son, is given oversight of the oil for the lampstand (שֶׁמֶן הַמָּאוֹר), the fragrant incense (קְטֹרֶת הַסַּמִּים), the regular grain offering (מִנְחַת הַתָּמִיד), and the anointing oil (שֶׁמֶן הַמִּשְׁחָה), as well as supervision over the entire tabernacle and its holy items (v. 16). **Ithamar**, Aaron's son, is given supervisory responsibility (מִשְׁמֶרֶת) over the work of the Gershonites and Merarites (vv. 28, 33). This division of labor within one tribe reflects a hierarchical priestly system where proximity to the most holy determines rank and responsibility.

## Events and Customs

The chapter describes detailed procedures for **packing up and transporting the tabernacle**, as well as **the census of Levites fit for service** between the ages of 30 and 50. This age limit reflects the understanding that the work required physical strength and maturity. In surrounding cultures, temples had fixed locations, while Israel's portable sanctuary was unique to a nomadic people. Covering holy objects before transport was essential—the Kohathites had to carry the items without seeing or touching them directly, emphasizing God's holiness and the danger of approaching the holy in the wrong way (vv. 15, 19–20).

**The census results** show the number of Levites fit for service:
- Kohathites: 2,750 (v. 36)
- Gershonites: 2,630 (v. 40)
- Merarites: 3,200 (v. 44)
- **Total: 8,580** (v. 48)

## Key Terms

**עוֹר תַּחַשׁ** (*or tachash*) – "tachash skin" – often translated as "sea cow" or "dolphin skin", but the identity of the animal is uncertain. It may have been a type of marine mammal or an animal now unknown. This skin served as outer protection for most holy objects.

**תְּכֵלֶת** (*tekhelet*) – a blue color made from sea snails, an extremely costly dye in ancient times. The ark of the covenant was covered first with the curtain, then with tachash skin, and finally with a completely blue cloth (בֶּגֶד כְּלִיל תְּכֵלֶת) as the outermost layer (vv. 5–6)—the reverse order of the other objects, where tachash skin was on the outside (vv. 8–12). This marked the unique holiness of the ark, since the blue fabric remained visible during transport.

**לַצָּבָא** (*la-tsava*) – "for service/the army" – a military term used for the Levites' service, suggesting that work at the sanctuary was understood as a form of spiritual warfare.
