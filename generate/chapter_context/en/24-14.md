# Historical and Cultural Context for Jeremiah 14

## Events and Customs

Chapter 14 addresses a severe drought catastrophe (הַבַּצָּרוֹת, *habbatsarot*) that strikes Judah. Drought was one of the most feared natural disasters in ancient Israel, as agriculture depended entirely on seasonal rains. The description of empty water reservoirs (גֵּבִים, *gevim* – cisterns or water holes) in verse 3 reflects the desperate search for water. Covering the head (verses 3–4) was a sign of shame and mourning. The chapter also describes how even wild animals such as deer and onagers suffer—this emphasizes the scale of the disaster and was a well-known literary device in the ancient Near East to describe extreme drought.

Verse 12 mentions fasting, burnt offerings (עֹלָה), and grain offerings (מִנְחָה) as the people’s attempts to awaken God’s compassion. In crisis situations such as drought, it was common to proclaim a collective fast and offer sacrifices at the temple to seek divine help. The Lord’s rejection of these rituals shows that outward religious practice without genuine repentance was worthless.

## Key Terms

The expression "the hope of Israel" (מִקְוֵה יִשְׂרָאֵל, *miqveh yisra'el*) in verse 8 is a wordplay in Hebrew, since *miqveh* can also mean "collection of water" or "reservoir"—especially meaningful in the context of drought. The people appeal to God as the only source of both physical and spiritual renewal.

In verse 14, the terms "false visions" (חֲזוֹן שֶׁקֶר), "divination" (קֶסֶם), and "deceit of their heart" (תַרְמִית לִבָּם) are used to describe the activity of the false prophets. קֶסֶם refers to pagan divination techniques that were strictly forbidden in Israelite law (Deut 18:10), showing that these prophets operated outside the Lord’s will.

## Chapter-Specific Insights

The conflict between Jeremiah and the false prophets (verses 13–16) illustrates a central tension of this period. The false prophets proclaimed "שְׁלוֹם אֱמֶת" (true peace) and denied that sword and famine would come. This unconditional message of peace was popular but false. The Lord reveals that these prophets speak lies and will themselves perish by sword and famine along with those they have deceived.
