## People

**Achan (עָכָן)** is the central figure in this chapter. His name is spelled "Akar" (עכר) in 1 Chronicles 2:7, meaning "troublemaker"—possibly a wordplay variant reflecting his actions. He is presented with a full genealogy: son of Karmi, son of Zabdi, son of Zerah, from the tribe of Judah. This detailed lineage emphasizes that his sin was not merely individual but affected the entire nation. The clan of Zerah was a prominent part of the tribe of Judah, making Achan's betrayal even more serious.

## Places

**Ai (הָעַי)** is described as located near Beth-Aven, east of Bethel. The name means "ruin heap," and the city was strategically positioned in the central highlands. Archaeologically, the site is traditionally identified with et-Tell, about 3 km east of Bethel, although this identification remains debated. The city was significantly smaller than Jericho, making the defeat even more humiliating.

**Achor (הַשְּׁבָרִים)** is mentioned as the place where the Israelites were pursued—the name means "breaks" or "quarries" and likely refers to a rocky, disturbed terrain on the downhill slope from Ai.

**The Valley of Achor (עֵמֶק עָכוֹר)** was named after Achan through a wordplay: "akar" means "to bring trouble." The valley is traditionally located west of Jericho. Later, the prophet Hosea (2:17) uses this valley as an image of hope, where the "valley of trouble" will become a "doorway of hope."

## Events and Customs

**Herem (חֵרֶם)** – the principle of ban – is key to understanding this chapter. At Jericho, God had declared that all war spoils belonged to Him and were to be either destroyed or dedicated to the sanctuary. Achan's theft was therefore not merely stealing from common war booty but sacrilege against God Himself. In the ancient Near East, such ban regulations were also known in other cultures; the Mesha Stele from Moab describes similar practices.

**The lot-casting process** that reveals the sinner (vv. 14–18), where the Hebrew verb לָכַד ("to be caught/seized") is used consistently, follows a systematic narrowing: tribe → clan → family → individual. This method of discerning God's will through "holy lots" was common in Israel (cf. 1 Sam 14:41–42). The expression "be taken" (יִלָּכֵד) suggests that God Himself pointed out the guilty one.

**Mourning rituals** in verse 6 – tearing clothes, falling on one's face, and casting dust on the head – were standard expressions of deep grief and humility in the ancient world. That Joshua and the elders performed these acts before the ark of the covenant underscores that this was a national crisis threatening Israel's relationship with God.

## Key Terms

**"A robe from Shinar" (אַדֶּרֶת שִׁנְעָר)** refers to Babylonia (cf. Gen 10:10; 11:2). Babylonian textiles were renowned for their quality and value in antiquity. The choice of this particular garment may also carry symbolic meaning – Shinar was associated with the Tower of Babel and humanity's rebellion against God.

**"A bar of gold" (לְשׁוֹן זָהָב)** literally means "tongue of gold" and describes the shape of the gold – an elongated bar. The weight of 50 shekels (about 570 grams) represented considerable wealth. The amount of silver, 200 shekels (about 2.3 kg), was also a fortune for an individual.

**"Nebalah" (נְבָלָה)** in verse 15, translated "outrageous act," is a strong term used for serious moral offenses in Israel – especially actions that threaten the community's holiness and relationship with God (cf. Gen 34:7; Judg 20:6).
