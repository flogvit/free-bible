# Historical and Cultural Context for 1 Samuel 26

## People

**Abishai, son of Zeruiah**, is introduced here as David’s companion and Joab’s brother. Zeruiah was David’s sister, making Abishai David’s nephew. His offer to kill Saul with a single spear thrust (v. 8) demonstrates both his military confidence and his loyalty to David.

**Ahimelech the Hittite** is briefly mentioned as one of David’s men (v. 6). The Hittites were descendants of the ancient Hittite empire in Anatolia, and several Hittite groups lived in Canaan. That David had a Hittite among his closest men shows the diversity within his retinue.

**Abner, son of Ner**, was Saul’s army commander and closely related to the king. As supreme commander, he was responsible for the king’s security, and David’s criticism of his guard duty (vv. 15–16) was a serious accusation that touched both his honor and military competence.

## Places

**The Hill of Hachilah** (גִּבְעַת הַחֲכִילָה) is located near **the Desert of Jeshimon** (הַיְשִׁימֹן), which means "wilderness" or "desolate land"—the barren landscape sloping down toward the Dead Sea. This terrain, with its many ravines and caves, provided David with natural protection and the opportunity to observe enemy movements from a distance.

## Events and Customs

**The water jug at the head** (צַפַּחַת הַמַּיִם) was a personal item of great significance in the desert climate. Together with the spear, these objects marked the king’s resting place and his vulnerability. That David took exactly these items demonstrated how close he had come—close enough to kill—without doing so.

**The camp formation** (הַמַּעְגָּל) describes how Saul’s army slept in a circular formation with the king at the center, protected by his men around him. This was standard military practice for protecting the leader. That David could penetrate all the way to the center reveals both God’s intervention and the serious failure of the guard duty.

## Key Terms

**"The Lord’s anointed"** (מְשִׁיחַ יְהוָה) is used four times in this chapter (vv. 9, 11, 16, 23). The term emphasizes that the king was holy and set apart for his task through the anointing ritual. David’s consistent respect for this status—despite Saul’s hostility—demonstrates his understanding that God’s choice could not be overturned by humans.

**"A deep sleep from the Lord"** (תַּרְדֵּמַת יְהוָה, v. 12) is the same expression used for Adam’s sleep in Genesis 2:21. This was not ordinary sleep but a supernatural state imposed by God, explaining why no one in the camp woke up.

**"Go and serve other gods"** (v. 19) reflects the ancient belief that deities were tied to specific territories. Being driven out of Israel’s land meant being cut off from the Lord’s presence and worship—a serious spiritual consequence of persecution.
