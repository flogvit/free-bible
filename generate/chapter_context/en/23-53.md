# Chapter-Specific Context for Isaiah 53

## Characters

**The Servant of the Lord** – The main character in this chapter is the suffering servant introduced in 52:13. The chapter portrays his rejection by people, his suffering, death, and vindication. He is described as "אִישׁ מַכְאֹבוֹת" (man of sorrows, v. 3) and "עַבְדִּי" (my servant, v. 11).

**The "We" Voice** – A collective group speaking in verses 1–6. This group confesses that they wrongly rejected the servant and acknowledges that he bore their sicknesses and pains. They compare themselves to stray sheep (v. 6).

**THE LORD (יהוה)** – The one who acts through and within the servant's suffering. It was the Lord's will to crush him (v. 10), and the Lord laid the guilt of the people on him (v. 6).

## Events and Customs

**The Guilt Offering (אָשָׁם)** – In verse 10, the servant's life is described as an "asham", the guilt offering detailed in Leviticus 5:14–6:7 and 7:1–10. This offering was brought to make atonement for specific sins and required full restitution plus a fifth part. Describing the servant's life as a guilt offering suggests complete atonement and restoration.

**The Sheep Led to Slaughter** – The image in verse 7 of the sheep (שֶׂה) being led to slaughter and remaining silent before its shearers connects the servant's fate with Israel's sacrificial practices, including the Passover lamb and daily offerings.

## Key Terms

**זְרוֹעַ יְהוָה** (the arm of the Lord, v. 1) – A fixed expression for God's saving power, often used regarding the Exodus from Egypt (Exodus 6:6; Deuteronomy 4:34). Here a rhetorical question is asked: who has seen God's power revealed in this despised servant?

**מְחֹלָל** (mechollal, v. 5) – From the root חלל, it can mean "pierced," "wounded," or "desecrated." It suggests a violent and shameful death. Parallel to "מְדֻכָּא" (medukka, "crushed").

**הִפְגִּיעַ** (hifgi'a, vv. 6 and 12) – This verb has a double meaning in the chapter: In verse 6, the LORD allowed everyone's guilt to "fall upon" (פגע) the servant. In verse 12, he "intercedes" (יַפְגִּיעַ) for transgressors. The same root carries both aspects of substitutionary action.

**חֲבֻרָה** (chaburah, v. 5) – "Wound" or "stripes," referring to marks left by blows. "By his wounds (וּבַחֲבֻרָתוֹ) we are healed."

## Chapter-Specific Findings

**Textual Variant in Verse 11** – In the Dead Sea Scroll 1QIsaᵃ, verse 11 reads: "מֵעֲמַל נַפְשׁוֹ יִרְאֶה אוֹר" ("from the toil of his soul he shall see light"), whereas the Masoretic Text has only "יִרְאֶה" ("he shall see"). The Septuagint supports this reading. The addition of "light" may suggest resurrection or new life after the servant's death.

**Targum Jonathan's Interpretation** – The Aramaic translation explicitly identifies the servant as the Messiah ("עַבְדִּי מְשִׁיחָא"), but reinterprets the suffering verses so that it is Israel's enemies who suffer, not the Messiah himself.
