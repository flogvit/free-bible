## Places

The chapter mentions three specific cultic sites that Israel is warned against visiting (verse 15). **Gilgal** (הַגִּלְגָּל) had by Hosea's time become a center for syncretistic worship. **Beth-Aven** (בֵּית אָוֶן, "house of wickedness") is likely a polemical name for **Bethel** (בֵּית אֵל, "house of God"), a cultic site that had been corrupted by idolatry. By calling it "house of wickedness" instead of "house of God," the prophet reveals the spiritual decay of the place.

Verse 13 describes offerings made "on the mountaintops" and "on the hills, under oak, poplar, and terebinth." This refers to the Canaanite "high places" (במות), open-air cult sites often located on elevated ground or in groves. The trees mentioned—oak (אַלּוֹן), poplar (לִבְנֶה), and terebinth (אֵלָה)—were considered sacred in Canaanite religion and provided shade for rituals that included both sacrifices and cultic practices.

## Events and Customs

The chapter explicitly mentions **cultic prostitution** in verse 14, where Hebrew distinguishes between זֹנוֹת (zonot, common prostitutes) and קְדֵשׁוֹת (qedeshot, "holy women"). The latter were women associated with fertility cults, where sexual activity at the sanctuary was regarded as a means to ensure fertility in the land, livestock, and families. This practice was widespread in Canaanite Baal and Asherah worship.

Verse 12 describes **divination practices**: "My people consult a wooden idol, and a diviner's rod gives them an answer." This likely refers to **rhabdomancy** (divination by rods or sticks), a form of occult practice. Such techniques were common in surrounding cultures and represented a direct violation of Israel's covenant obligations.

## Key Terms

Three central terms in verse 1 define the lack present in Israel: **אֱמֶת** (emet, "truth/faithfulness"), **חֶסֶד** (chesed, "steadfast love/covenant loyalty"), and **דַּעַת אֱלֹהִים** (da'at Elohim, "knowledge of God"). Especially the word "knowledge" (דַּעַת) is repeated throughout the chapter and here means not merely intellectual information but personal, relational acquaintance with God and His will as expressed in the covenant.

The term **רִיב** (riv) in verse 1 is a technical legal term for "lawsuit" or "charge." The opening of the chapter thus presents a **covenant lawsuit** in which God acts as both accuser and judge against His people who have broken their covenant obligations.

The expression **רוּחַ זְנוּנִים** (ruach zenunim) in verse 12, "spirit of whoredom," describes a spiritual condition of unfaithfulness that drives the people away from God. The image of "whoredom" functions on two levels: literal participation in cultic prostitution and metaphorical as spiritual adultery against the covenant.

## People

The chapter directs harsh criticism toward the **priesthood** (כֹּהֲנִים). In verses 4–9, the priests are accused of rejecting knowledge and forgetting God's law (תּוֹרָה). The priesthood had evidently integrated Canaanite elements into their worship. The accusation that they "live off the sin of my people" (verse 8) may refer to the priests profiting financially from sacrifices at illegal cult sites—the more sin offerings, the greater their income.
