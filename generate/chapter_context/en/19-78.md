## Places

The psalm mentions several geographical locations central to Israel’s history. **Zoan** (צֹעַן, verses 12, 43) was one of the oldest and most important cities in the Egyptian Nile Delta. It served as a royal residence for Pharaoh and witnessed many of the plagues. **Shiloh** (verse 60) was Israel’s first religious center after entering the land, where the tabernacle stood. The psalm’s reference to God "forsaking the dwelling at Shiloh" points to the loss of the tabernacle to the Philistines (1 Sam 4). **Zion** (verse 68) refers to the hill in Jerusalem that David captured and on which the temple was later built.

## Events and Customs

The psalm presents a condensed version of Israel’s salvation history from Egypt to David. **The plagues in Egypt** (verses 44–51) are mentioned selectively: blood, flies, frogs, locusts, hail, and the death of the firstborn. The order differs from Exodus, reflecting poetic freedom within liturgical tradition. **Manna** is described as "bread of the mighty" (לֶחֶם אַבִּירִים, verse 25), an expression that can be understood as heavenly food with life-giving power. **Quails** (verses 26–31) are linked to the event in Numbers 11, where the people’s greed led to God’s judgment. The psalm’s purpose as **instruction for future generations** (verses 3–8) reflects the biblical command to pass on the faith from father to son.

## Key Terms

The word **מַשְׂכִּיל** (maskil) in the heading denotes a teaching poem or skillfully crafted psalm, possibly intended for public instruction. **חִידוֹת** (chidot, "riddles", verse 2) does not refer to mysteries but to deep truths woven into history. **קְדוֹשׁ יִשְׂרָאֵל** ("the Holy One of Israel", verse 41) is an important divine name that emphasizes God’s holiness and special relationship with Israel. **בָּמוֹת** (bamot, "high places", verse 58) were cultic heights often associated with Canaanite idolatry, to which the Israelites repeatedly turned back.

## People

**Asaph** is named as the author and was, according to Chronicles, leader of one of the Levitical singer families during David’s time. **Ephraim** (verses 9, 67) represents the leading tribe of the northern kingdom and is criticized for cowardice and apostasy. The tribe’s historical significance made God’s rejection of Ephraim in favor of Judah (verses 67–68) theologically meaningful. **David** (verses 70–72) is presented as God’s chosen shepherd, taken from the sheepfolds to care for Israel—an image that underscores continuity between his former occupation and royal calling.
