# Historical and Cultural Context for Psalms 11

## Key Concepts

The psalm contains several important Hebrew expressions that require explanation. The word **חָסִיתִי** (*chasiti*, "I have taken refuge") in verse 1 is a key term in the Psalms, describing the believer who seeks protection in God. The image of the bird (**צִפּוֹר**, *tsippor*) flying to the mountain represents fleeing from danger, a familiar motif in the Israelite highlands where mountain caves provided shelter.

In verse 3, **הַשָּׁתוֹת** (*hashatot*, "the foundations") is used, which may refer to the moral and legal foundation of society. This concept was important in ancient Near Eastern thought, where justice and law were seen as the basis for a stable community.

## Events and Customs

Verse 6 describes a judgment with **אֵשׁ וְגָפְרִית** ("fire and sulfur") and **וְרוּחַ זִלְעָפוֹת** ("a burning wind"), clearly alluding to the destruction of Sodom and Gomorrah (Genesis 19). **מְנָת כּוֹסָם** ("the portion of their cup") refers to the cup as a metaphor for fate or judgment—a common idea in the Middle East, where a host's assignment of a cup symbolized a guest’s lot.

## Places

**הֵיכַל קָדְשׁוֹ** ("his holy temple") in verse 4 refers primarily to God’s heavenly dwelling, as clarified by the parallel with "his throne in heaven" in the same verse. Ancient Near Eastern temple theology understood earthly temples as reflections of heavenly reality—meeting points between heaven and earth.
