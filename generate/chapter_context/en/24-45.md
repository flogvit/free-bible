## People

Baruch son of Neriah is the main character in this short chapter. He was Jeremiah’s scribe and faithful assistant. Baruch belonged to a prominent family in Jerusalem—his brother Seraiah was an official at court (Jer 51:59). His role as a scribe (*sofer*) meant that he was educated and held a respected position in society, which makes the warning against "seeking great things for yourself" especially relevant.

## Events and Customs

The chapter is explicitly dated to the fourth year of King Jehoiakim’s reign (605/604 B.C.). This was the same year as the battle at Carchemish (mentioned in Jer 46:2), where Babylon defeated Egypt and became the dominant power in the Middle East. It was also the year when Baruch wrote down the first scroll that was later burned by King Jehoiakim (Jer 36). Baruch’s personal crisis must be understood in light of these dramatic events: he had just written down words of judgment against his own people, and the future looked dark both nationally and personally.

## Key Terms

The expression "seek great things for yourself" (*תְּבַקֶּשׁ־לְךָ גְדֹלוֹת*) likely refers to ambitions for career advancement at court—something Baruch’s family background could have made possible. The promise of receiving "your life as plunder" (*נַפְשְׁךָ לְשָׁלָל*) is an idiom meaning to survive with one’s life intact, like a soldier who escapes battle with only his life. This is the same promise given to Ebed-Melech (Jer 39:18) and represents God’s grace in the midst of judgment.

## Chapter-Specific Findings

A seal impression (*bulla*) with the inscription "Belonging to Berechiah son of Neriah, the scribe" was discovered in 1975 and published in 1978. The bulla was purchased on the antiquities market, which makes its provenance uncertain, but most scholars consider it authentic. This is among the most direct archaeological confirmations of a person mentioned in prophetic literature.
