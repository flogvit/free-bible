# Historical and Cultural Context for 1 Chronicles 13

## People

**Uzza and Ahio** were connected to the house of Abinadab, where the ark of the covenant had stood for several decades after the Philistines returned it (1 Sam 7:1-2). The family's long association with the ark may have led to a certain familiarity with this holy object. Uzza’s death illustrates that even extensive experience with sacred things does not override God’s requirement for reverence and proper handling.

**Obed-Edom the Gittite** is mentioned as the one who received the ark into his home. The designation "the Gittite" may indicate that he was from Gath (a Philistine city) or belonged to the Levitical family from Gath-Rimmon. Chronicles later identifies him as a Levite and gatekeeper (1 Chron 15:18, 21, 24; 16:5), which explains why the ark could safely be placed with him.

## Places

**Kiriath-Jearim** (also called Baalath, see verse 6: בַּעֲלָתָה) was located about 15 kilometers west of Jerusalem on the border between Judah and Benjamin. This place had been the ark’s resting place since the Philistines returned it, making it a natural starting point for David to retrieve Israel’s most holy object. The city lies in the hill country of Judah.

**Kidon (the threshing floor)** is the place where Uzza died. In 2 Samuel 6:6, this location is called "Nakon." This variation may be due to different local traditions, the site having multiple names, or textual variations in transmission. Threshing floors were often situated on open, flat areas with good wind exposure, frequently on elevated ground—terrain that could prove challenging for oxen pulling a cart.

## Events and Customs

**Transporting the ark on a cart** was a method Israel had learned from the Philistines, who had sent the ark back on a new cart pulled by cows (1 Sam 6:7-8). However, according to the Mosaic Law, the ark was to be carried on poles by Levites from the Kohathite clan (Num 4:15; 7:9). David’s choice of transport method, though seemingly practical and respectful with a "new cart," contradicted God’s commands. The Chronicler later emphasizes that this error was corrected (1 Chron 15:13-15).

**The musical celebration** described in verse 8 includes a wide range of instruments: harps, lyres, tambourines, cymbals, and trumpets. This provides insight into Israelite cultic music and anticipates Chronicles’ strong interest in Levitical singing and music in temple service.

## Key Terms

**"The Lord who sits enthroned above the cherubim"** (יוֹשֵׁב הַכְּרוּבִים, *yoshev ha-keruvim*) is an important title that connects the ark with God’s throne. The cherubim on the cover (the mercy seat) were the place where God met with his people. This expression underscores that the ark was not merely a relic but God’s visible throne on earth.

**"Perez-Uzza"** means "breakthrough against Uzza" or "Uzza’s breach." The wordplay on the Hebrew word פֶּרֶץ (*perets* – to break through, tear down) shows how places were often named after dramatic events. The name served as a lasting reminder that God must be approached on his own terms.
