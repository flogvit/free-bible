# Historical and Cultural Context for 2 Corinthians 6

## Events and Customs

Paul’s exhortation in verses 14–18 not to be "unequally yoked" (ἑτεροζυγοῦντες) draws its imagery from Leviticus 19:19 and Deuteronomy 22:10, where Israelites are forbidden to plow with an ox and a donkey together. Separating themselves from pagan practices carried significant social and economic costs for the congregation.

The list of sufferings in verses 4–10 reflects concrete experiences from Paul’s missionary work, including references to imprisonment (φυλακαῖς), beatings (πληγαῖς), and unrest (ἀκαταστασίαις). Such "peristasis catalogs" (lists of hardships) were known in Stoic philosophy, but Paul uses this form in a distinctive way to show that apostolic legitimacy is confirmed through suffering, not worldly success.

## Key Terms

The word **Βελιάρ** (Beliar) in verse 15 is a Hebrew-Aramaic name for Satan that was widespread in Jewish apocalyptic literature, especially in the Dead Sea Scrolls and pseudepigraphal writings such as the Book of Jubilees. The name likely means "worthlessness" or "destruction." Paul’s use of this term rather than the more common "Satan" or "devil" may indicate influence from Jewish apocalyptic tradition and underscores the absolute opposition between Christ and the power of evil.

The expression **ὅπλα τῆς δικαιοσύνης τῶν δεξιῶν καὶ ἀριστερῶν** ("weapons of righteousness for the right hand and the left") in verse 7 draws imagery from Roman military practice. A fully equipped soldier carried a sword in his right hand (an offensive weapon) and a shield in his left (a defensive weapon).

The term **ναός** (temple) in verse 16 refers specifically to the holy inner sanctuary where God dwells, not the broader temple complex (ἱερόν). Paul deliberately contrasts this with the pagan temples of the city.

## Chapter-Specific Insights

Paul’s exhortation to "come out from them and be separate" (verse 17) had concrete implications for the congregation’s daily social life, where religious and social contexts were often inextricably intertwined.
