# Historical and Cultural Context for Leviticus 27

## Events and Customs

Chapter 27 deals with vows (*neder*) and valuations (*'erekh*) of persons, animals, houses, and land dedicated to the Lord. The practice of making vows to a deity was widespread throughout the ancient Near East. A person could dedicate themselves, a family member, or property to the sanctuary as thanks for answered prayer or in connection with special occasions. This chapter regulates how such vows could be fulfilled with money instead of literal fulfillment.

The system of differentiated values based on age and gender (verses 3–7) reflects the economic reality of an agricultural society where labor capacity was crucial. The values do not represent human worth, but rather the economic benefit a person could contribute to sanctuary service. Similar valuation systems are known from other ancient cultures in the Near East.

## Key Terms

**Shekel hakodesh** (שֶׁקֶל הַקֹּדֶשׁ) – "sanctuary shekel" – was a standardized unit of weight used in religious contexts, defined as 20 *gera* (גֵּרָה, verse 25). This ensured uniform valuation and prevented fraud.

**The fifth part** (חֲמִישִׁית, *chamishit*) – a recurring element in the chapter (verses 13, 15, 19, 27, 31) is that anyone wishing to redeem something dedicated must add one-fifth (20%) of its value. This served as compensation for releasing oneself from a given vow.

**Cherem** (חֵרֶם) in verses 28–29 refers to something irrevocably devoted to God. Unlike ordinary vows, *cherem* could never be redeemed or sold. Verse 29 specifies that a person under *cherem* must die – this likely refers to individuals sentenced to death or laws concerning warfare (cf. Numbers 21:2–3; Joshua 6:17–21), not voluntary human sacrifice.

**The Year of Jubilee** (יוֹבֵל, *yovel*) is mentioned repeatedly (verses 17–24) as a reference point for land values. The value of dedicated land was calculated based on the number of years until the next Jubilee, when all ancestral land would return to its original owner regardless.

**Ma'aser** (מַעֲשַׂר) – "tithe" – in verses 30–33 represents the mandatory tenth portion of crops and livestock belonging to the Lord. The practice of counting animals "under the rod" (תַּחַת הַשָּׁבֶט, verse 32) describes how the animals were driven through an opening one by one, with every tenth animal marked as holy – regardless of quality (verse 33).

## Chapter-Specific Findings

Verse 34 marks the conclusion of the entire book of Leviticus with the formulation: "These are the commandments that the Lord gave Moses for the Israelites on Mount Sinai." This closing formula links the entire law collection back to the revelation at Sinai and forms a literary frame (*inclusio*) with the beginning of the book (1:1).
