# Historical and Cultural Context for 2 Chronicles 35

## People

**Josiah** led the greatest Passover celebration since the days of the prophet Samuel, as described in this chapter. The celebration took place in his 18th regnal year (622 B.C.), after the Book of the Law had been found and reforms carried out (chapter 34). **Hilkiah, Zechariah, and Jehiel** are mentioned as leaders over God’s house who gave generous contributions to the celebration (2,600 Passover lambs and 300 cattle). **Conaniah (Kananja), Shemaiah, and Nethanel**, along with others, were Levitical leaders who organized the Passover lambs and provided 5,000 small livestock and 500 cattle.

**Pharaoh Neco II** was an Egyptian ruler who marched north to support the declining Assyrian empire against Babylon. He warned Josiah not to interfere, claiming he had a message from God. **The prophet Jeremiah** is mentioned as the one who composed laments over Josiah (v. 25) – this is the only direct reference to Jeremiah in the books of Chronicles.

## Places

**Carchemish** was a strategically important city on the Euphrates (in today’s Syria/Turkey border region), which controlled a major crossing point over the river. This was Neco’s stated objective when he marched north.

**The plain of Megiddo** (Hebrew: בִּקְעַת מְגִדּוֹ) was a large plain in the Jezreel Valley where the main road between Egypt and Mesopotamia (Via Maris) passed. Here Josiah met Neco in battle and was mortally wounded (vv. 22–24).

## Events and Customs

**The Passover celebration** followed carefully the instructions from Exodus 12 and Deuteronomy 16, with slaughter on the fourteenth day of the first month. The Levites skinned the Passover lambs while the priests sprinkled the blood (v. 11). The preparation "by fire according to the regulation" (v. 13) followed the command that the Passover lamb be roasted, while other holy offerings were boiled in pots, kettles, and pans.

**The placement of the ark** (v. 3) is remarkable: Josiah instructed the Levites to put the ark back into the temple, suggesting it had been moved, possibly during the reigns of the preceding kings Manasseh and Amon.

## Key Terms

The expression **"the Levites who taught all Israel"** (הַמְּבִינִים לְכָל־יִשְׂרָאֵל, v. 3) refers to the Levites’ role as teachers of God’s law. The word מְבִינִים can mean both "those who understand" and "those who cause others to understand."

**"Since the days of the prophet Samuel"** (v. 18) is a special dating that differs from the parallel in 2 Kings 23:22, which says "since the days of the judges." The Chronicler highlights Samuel as the one who restored proper worship.

## Chapter-Specific Findings

**Babylonian Chronicles** confirm the geopolitical background for Josiah’s death. Tablets document that the Egyptians marched north in 609 B.C. to help the Assyrians at Harran, which matches Neco’s campaign described here.

The Greek historian **Herodotus** (II.159) mentions Neco’s victory over "the Syrians" at Magdolos (likely Megiddo), providing independent confirmation of the event.
