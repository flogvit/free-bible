# Historical and Cultural Context for Numbers 36

## People

The chapter focuses on **the daughters of Zelophehad** – Mahlah, Tirzah, Hoglah, Milcah, and Noah – who were first introduced in chapter 27. They are named again here (v. 11) to show that they obeyed the command to marry within their own tribe. They married "sons of their father's brothers" (בְּנֵי דֹדֵיהֶן), meaning paternal cousins. The leaders who brought forward this matter belonged to **the clan of Gilead** from the tribe of Manasseh – the same family line to which Zelophehad belonged (Gilead – Machir – Manasseh – Joseph).

## Events and Customs

**Endogamy and tribal inheritance**: This chapter establishes a law that daughters who inherit must marry within their own tribe. This is called endogamy – marriage within a specific group. The purpose was to prevent land holdings from being transferred to other tribes through marriage. In the ancient Near East, it was common for land to follow the male line of descent, and this law balanced women's inheritance rights (from chapter 27) with the need to preserve tribal identity.

**The Jubilee Law and inheritance**: Verse 4 mentions the Year of Jubilee (הַיֹּבֵל), when all land was to return to its original owner (Lev 25). The leaders pointed out that even the Jubilee would not solve the problem of land passing to another tribe through marriage, since the woman would then permanently belong to her husband's family.

## Key Terms

The expression **"לַטּוֹב בְּעֵינֵיהֶם"** (v. 6) – "to whoever is good in their eyes" – shows that the women had freedom of choice within the boundaries of their tribe. This was a remarkable balance between individual rights and collective interests in a patriarchal culture.

The verb **"תִסֹּב"** (vv. 7, 9) means "to go around" or "to be transferred," used concerning the potential movement of inheritance from one tribe to another. It highlights concern that Israel's tribal structure might gradually break down over time.

## Places

The conclusion of the chapter (v. 13) places all this legislation **"in the plains of Moab by the Jordan, across from Jericho"** (בְּעַרְבֹת מוֹאָב עַל יַרְדֵּן יְרֵחוֹ). This marks that Israel was standing at the threshold of the promised land. The plains of Moab were a fertile area east of the Jordan River.
