## People

The chapter mentions several central figures in the early church. **James** (Ἰάκωβος) refers to Jesus' brother, who was the leader of the congregation in Jerusalem. **Cephas/Peter** (Κηφᾶς) is the apostle Peter, using his Aramaic name. **John** is likely the son of Zebedee, one of Jesus' closest disciples. These three are called "pillars" (στῦλοι, v. 9), an image drawn from the temple that marks their authoritative position in the church. **Barnabas** (Βαρναβᾶς) was Paul's travel companion to Jerusalem (v. 1) and co-worker, but he was also carried away by Peter's hypocrisy in Antioch (v. 13). **Titus** (Τίτος) was an uncircumcised Greek (Ἕλλην) who accompanied Paul to Jerusalem. The fact that he was not forced to be circumcised (v. 3) became a crucial test case. **"False brothers"** (ψευδαδέλφους, v. 4) were infiltrators who sought to restrict the freedom of believers in Christ and make them slaves under the law.

## Places

**Jerusalem** (Ἱεροσόλυμα) served as the center of the Jewish-Christian movement and the seat of church leadership. Paul's visit there, fourteen years later (διὰ δεκατεσσάρων ἐτῶν, v. 1), was decisive in gaining recognition for his mission to the Gentiles. **Antioch** (Ἀντιόχεια) was an important center for the Gentile-Christian church. Here arose the conflict between Peter and Paul over table fellowship (v. 11-14), when Peter withdrew from eating with Gentile Christians.

## Events and Customs

**Circumcision** (περιτομή) was the fundamental sign of the covenant between God and Israel. The question of whether Gentile Christians had to be circumcised was the most pressing theological conflict in the early church. That Titus was not forced to be circumcised was a principled victory for Paul. **Table fellowship** between Jews and Gentiles was problematic in Jewish tradition due to purity laws. When Peter withdrew from communal meals with Gentile Christians (v. 12), this signaled that they were second-class members of the community. **"Giving the right hand"** (δεξιὰς ἔδωκαν, v. 9) was a recognized custom in antiquity for sealing agreements and partnerships.

## Key Terms

**"Those who seemed to be something"** (οἱ δοκοῦντες) is used three times (v. 2, 6, 9) and refers to the Jerusalem apostles. The expression is not derogatory but emphasizes that human reputation is irrelevant before God. **"The gospel for the uncircumcised/circumcised"** (τὸ εὐαγγέλιον τῆς ἀκροβυστίας/περιτομῆς, v. 7) does not describe two different gospels but the same message delivered to different target groups. **"Justification"** (δικαιόω) is used four times in vv. 16-17 and means to be declared righteous. Paul insists that this happens through faith in Christ (ἐκ πίστεως Χριστοῦ), not by works of the law (ἐξ ἔργων νόμου). **"To live like a Gentile"** (ἐθνικῶς ζῆν, v. 14) means not following Jewish dietary regulations, while **"to Judaize"** (Ἰουδαΐζειν) means to adopt the Jewish way of life.
