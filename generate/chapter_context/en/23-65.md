# Historical and Cultural Context for Isaiah 65

## Events and Customs

Chapter 65 contains a detailed list of idolatry and pagan rituals that God condemns. Offering sacrifices "in the gardens" (v. 3) refers to Canaanite fertility cults where people worshipped in groves, often associated with the goddess Asherah. "Burning incense on bricks" (v. 3) describes sacrificial acts performed on altars or platforms made of brick, which violated God's command to use uncut stone (Exod 20:25). To "sit among the graves and spend the night in secret places" (v. 4) were necromancy rituals where people sought contact with the dead to receive revelations. Pork and other unclean food (v. 4) were strictly forbidden under the Mosaic Law but were used in pagan rituals, especially those connected to chthonic (underworld) deities.

The self-righteous attitude "Go your way; do not come near me, for I am holier than you" (v. 5) shows a perverted form of ritual purity where participants in pagan cults considered themselves consecrated and set apart—a grotesque inversion of Israelite thinking about holiness.

## Key Terms

The words **גַּד** (Gad) and **מְנִי** (Meni) in verse 11 are names of two pagan deities associated with luck and fate. Gad ("Luck") and Meni ("Fate" or "Portion") were worshipped in Semitic religion, and tables were set and drink offerings poured for them to secure prosperity. The wordplay is deliberate: those who fill cups for "Meni" (מְנִי) will themselves be "numbered" (מָנִיתִי) by the sword (v. 12).

The expression **אֱלֹהֵי אָמֵן** ("God of Truth" or "Amen-God," v. 16) is unique in the Old Testament. It emphasizes God as faithful and reliable, in contrast to the false gods of luck. The word "amen" means "truly" or "let it stand."

## Places

The **Plain of Sharon** (v. 10) was the fertile coastal plain between Jaffa and Carmel, known for its rich vegetation and grazing land. The **Valley of Achor** (v. 10) lay near Jericho and was the place where Achan was punished (Josh 7). The name means "Valley of Trouble," but here a transformation from curse to blessing is promised—from a place of judgment to a place of rest and fruitfulness.

"**My holy mountain**" (vv. 11, 25) refers to Zion/the temple hill in Jerusalem, the center of true worship and a contrast to the high places where idol sacrifices were offered.

## Chapter-Specific Findings

Archaeological finds from the Iron Age in Israel confirm the widespread practice of cultic rituals in gardens and on high places. Figurines of Asherah and cult installations have been discovered at several Israelite settlements. Inscriptions from Kuntillet Ajrud mention "Yahweh and his Asherah," showing the syncretism that the text condemns. Pig bones found in cultic contexts in Phoenician and Philistine areas confirm the use of swine in pagan rituals that Israelites were tempted to join.
