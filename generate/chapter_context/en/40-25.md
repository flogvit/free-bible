# Historical and Cultural Context for Matthew 25

## Events and Customs

### Wedding Customs
The parable of the ten virgins (παρθένοι, *parthenoi*) reflects Jewish wedding traditions from this period. The wedding celebration typically began with the groom going to fetch the bride from her father's house, often in the evening or at night. The group of bridesmaids was tasked with meeting the groom with torches or oil lamps (λαμπάδες, *lampades*) and escorting the procession to the wedding feast. It was not unusual for the groom to be delayed, as negotiations regarding the dowry and other formalities could take time. The closed door after the feast had begun represented a real social boundary—arriving late was a serious humiliation.

### Stewardship and Slave Economy
In the parable of the talents, a practice is described in which wealthy masters entrusted the management of their assets to trusted slaves (δοῦλοι, *douloi*) during extended journeys. This was common in Greco-Roman society, where slaves could have significant responsibility and economic freedom. Burying money in the ground (verses 18, 25) was actually an accepted way to protect valuables in a time without banks, though rabbinic tradition distinguished this from active stewardship. The reference to "money changers" (τραπεζίται, *trapezitai*) and interest (τόκος, *tokos*) points to a developed banking system within the Roman Empire.

## Key Terms

### Talent (τάλαντον)
A talent was not a coin but a unit of weight representing an enormous sum—approximately 6,000 denarii, equivalent to about 20 years' wages for a common laborer. Five talents thus constituted a fortune of royal magnitude. This extreme value underscores the immense responsibility entrusted to the servants and makes the third servant's passivity all the more striking.

### Sheep and Goats (πρόβατα / ἔριφοι)
The image of the shepherd separating sheep from goats (verses 32–33) was immediately recognizable in Palestine, where mixed flocks were common. Sheep and goats often grazed together during the day but were separated at night because goats needed more warmth. The position on the right (ἐκ δεξιῶν) was the place of honor in antiquity, while the left side was associated with lower status.

### "My Least Brothers" (τῶν ἀδελφῶν μου τῶν ἐλαχίστων)
The expression in verse 40 has been widely discussed. In Jewish tradition, caring for the poor, sick, and imprisoned were central acts of piety. Visiting prisoners was especially important because inmates in Roman prisons depended on family and friends for food and basic needs—the state did not provide these.

## Archaeological Findings

Archaeological discoveries of oil lamps from the first century in Judea and Galilee confirm details in the parable of the virgins. The so-called "Herodian lamps" had limited oil capacity and required regular refilling, making the need for extra oil stored in containers (ἀγγεῖα, *angeia*) historically credible. Several such lamps and oil vessels have been found together in household contexts, showing that prepared storage of oil was standard practice.
