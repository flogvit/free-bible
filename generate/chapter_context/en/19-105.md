## People

Psalm 105 mentions a number of central figures from Israel's history. **Abraham** (vv. 6, 9, 42) is called "God's servant" and is the one with whom the covenant was first made. **Isaac** (v. 9) and **Jacob/Israel** (vv. 6, 10, 23) represent the continuation of the covenant. **Joseph** (vv. 17-22) receives significant attention in the psalm; the description of his imprisonment with iron chains and later elevation as ruler over Egypt corresponds to the account in Genesis 39–41. **Moses and Aaron** (vv. 26-27) are mentioned as God's chosen servants who performed the signs in Egypt.

## Places

**The land of Canaan** (v. 11) is the promised land described as "the measuring line of your inheritance" (חֶבֶל נַחֲלַתְכֶם). The word "measuring line" (חֶבֶל) refers to the ancient practice of measuring out land areas with ropes during distribution. **Egypt** is poetically called "the land of Ham" (vv. 23, 27), linking the Egyptians to Noah's son Ham (Gen 10:6). This was a common poetic designation in Israelite tradition. The psalm also describes the wilderness journey with references to the **cloud** as protection and the **rock** that provided water (vv. 39-41).

## Events and Customs

The psalm retells the plagues in Egypt (vv. 28-36), but in a different order than Exodus and omits some of them. It mentions: darkness (v. 28), blood (v. 29), frogs (v. 30), flies and gnats (v. 31), hail and fire (vv. 32-33), locusts (vv. 34-35), and the death of the firstborn (v. 36). The plagues of livestock disease and boils are omitted. This selective retelling was common in Israelite liturgical practice, where the purpose was theological reflection rather than chronological accuracy. The phrase "do not touch my anointed ones" (מְשִׁיחָי, v. 15) is used here concerning the patriarchs, which is unusual—normally "anointed" refers to kings or priests. Here it emphasizes the holy status of the patriarchs and God's protection of them.

## Key Terms

The word **בְּרִית** (berit, "covenant") is central in the psalm (vv. 8, 10) and denotes the binding agreement between God and Abraham that continues to his descendants. The phrase **דָּבָר צִוָּה לְאֶלֶף דּוֹר** ("a word he commanded for a thousand generations," v. 8) emphasizes the eternal validity of the covenant. The verb **צרף** (tsaraf, "refine/purify," v. 19) is used regarding metal refining and describes how the Lord's word refined Joseph during his time in prison—suffering had a purifying and preparatory function.

## Chapter-Specific Findings

Psalm 105:1-15 appears in an almost identical version in 1 Chronicles 16:8-22, where it is quoted as part of David's song of praise when the ark of the covenant arrived in Jerusalem. This shows that the psalm, or parts of it, were already in liturgical use during the early monarchy period. Egyptian sources document that Semitic peoples migrated to Egypt, which corresponds with the psalm's description (vv. 16-23). The Beni Hasan paintings from around 1900 BC show Semitic people arriving in Egypt and illustrate the historical context for the story of Jacob and Joseph.
