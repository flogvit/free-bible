## People

**Elihu** – The speaker in this chapter. He addresses Job directly and begins his first real speech after the introduction in chapter 32. He claims to have received a living spirit from "Shaddai" (verse 4) and emphasizes that he stands on equal footing with Job as a fellow human being, "formed from clay" (verse 6).

**Job** – The listener whom Elihu addresses. Elihu quotes Job’s earlier claims of his own innocence (verses 9–11) and challenges him to respond (verses 32–33).

## Key Terms

**Mēlîṣ (מֵלִיץ)** – "Intercessor" or "mediator" in verse 23. A rare Hebrew term describing a heavenly intermediary—"one among a thousand"—who can plead the case of humanity before God and show people what is right.

**Kōfer (כֹּפֶר)** – "Ransom" in verse 24. A price paid to free someone from death or judgment. In this context, it is linked to God’s grace that rescues a person from the grave.

**Tardemâ (תַּרְדֵּמָה)** – "Deep sleep" in verse 15. The same word used for Adam’s sleep in Genesis 2:21. Here it describes the state in which God reveals Himself through dreams.

**Shaḥat (שַׁחַת)** – "The grave" or "corruption", used repeatedly throughout the chapter (verses 18, 22, 24, 28, 30). It refers to the realm of death from which a person can be rescued.

## Events and Customs

**God’s communication through dreams** – Elihu describes how God speaks to people through dreams and night visions when deep sleep falls upon them (verses 14–16). This is one of God’s ways of warning people and turning them away from pride.

**Sickness as divine discipline** – Elihu presents physical suffering as another way God communicates (verses 19–22). Through pain on the sickbed, when the flesh wastes away and the soul draws near to the grave, a person may be led to repentance.

**Intercession and restoration** – If a heavenly intercessor pleads on behalf of someone and God accepts a ransom, that person can be restored: their body becomes healthier than in youth, and the healed one publicly confesses their sin (verses 23–28).
