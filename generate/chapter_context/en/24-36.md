## People

The chapter introduces several historical figures who can be confirmed through other sources. **Baruch son of Neriah** was Jeremiah’s scribe and close associate. His existence is confirmed by an archaeological discovery of a seal impression (bulla) with the inscription "Belonging to Berechiah, son of Neriah, the scribe." **Gemariah son of Shaphan** was a high-ranking court official whose father Shaphan had served as scribe under King Josiah. The family was known for supporting Jeremiah. **Elnathan son of Achbor** is also mentioned in Jeremiah 26:22 in another context—here we see him urging the king not to burn the scroll (v. 25).

**King Jehoiakim** is the reigning monarch who receives and burns the scroll. His reaction—cutting up and burning the scroll without fear or tearing his clothes (vv. 23–24)—stands in sharp contrast to how his father Josiah reacted when the Book of the Law was found (2 Kings 22:11).

**Jehudi son of Nethaniah** serves as a messenger between the court officials and Baruch (v. 14) and later reads the scroll aloud before the king (vv. 21, 23). **Micaiah son of Gemariah** hears Baruch reading in the temple and brings the message to the court officials (vv. 11–13).

## Places

The **scribe’s chamber in the upper courtyard** near the new temple gate was an administrative room within the temple complex. Such chambers were used by the court scribes and officials and were strategically located where many people passed. The **winter house** (בֵּית הַחֹרֶף) was the part of the palace used during the cold season, equipped with a hearth (אָח) for heating. The ninth month (Kislev, November/December) was a winter month in Judah, which explains the use of fire for warmth.

## Events and Customs

The **fast day** mentioned in verse 9 was likely proclaimed in response to the Babylonian threat following the battle of Carchemish in 605 BC. Such public fasts were declared during times of crisis to seek the Lord’s favor (cf. Joel 1:14; 2:15). The fact that the fast was proclaimed "in the fifth year" (604/603 BC) may be connected to Babylonian forces operating in the region at that time.

**Writing on a scroll** (מְגִלַּת־סֵפֶר) was the standard format for longer texts in the ancient Near East. The scroll was made of sewn-together sheets of papyrus or leather. Verse 23 describes how Jehudi read "three or four columns" (דְּלָתוֹת, literally "doors/columns") at a time, and the king cut them off with a "scribe’s knife" and threw them into the fire.

## Key Terms

The Hebrew word **דְּיוֹ** (verse 18) means "ink" and occurs only here in the Old Testament. The term **תַּעַר הַסֹּפֵר** ("scribe’s knife") was a tool used to sharpen reed pens, scrape away errors, and, as here, cut papyrus or leather.

## Chapter-Specific Findings

The seal impression bearing the name Baruch son of Neriah was discovered in the 1970s and is now housed in the Israel Museum. Another seal impression from the same context bears the name "Jerahmeel, the king’s son"—possibly the same Jerahmeel who received orders in verse 26 to arrest Baruch and Jeremiah. These findings provide remarkable confirmation that the persons mentioned in this chapter were real historical figures.
