## People

Psalm 54 names a specific group: the **Ziphites** (הַזִּיפִים, *hazzifim*). These were the inhabitants of Ziph, a town in the tribe of Judah. According to the superscription, they betrayed David by reporting his hiding place to King Saul. This event is described in 1 Samuel 23:19–24 and 26:1–5. The betrayal by the Ziphites is remarkable because they belonged to David’s own tribe, Judah. This likely explains why David refers to his enemies as "strangers" (זָרִים, *zarim*) in verse 5—not ethnically foreign people, but individuals who acted like strangers by breaking tribal loyalty.

## Places

**Ziph** was located in the hill country southeast of Hebron, in the wilderness of Judah. The area is identified today with Tell Zif, about 6 kilometers south of Hebron. The region was characterized by desert terrain with caves and ravines suitable for hiding. David stayed in this inaccessible landscape while fleeing from Saul. Precisely because the Ziphites knew the area well, they were able to inform Saul of David’s exact position.

## Key Terms

The word **עָרִיצִים** (*aritsim*), translated as "violent men" or "tyrants" in verse 5, describes ruthless people who use power without regard for God. It is often used in the Psalms to refer to enemies who threaten the righteous. Combined with "strangers", this emphasizes that these people have placed themselves outside the covenant community through their behavior.

The expression **בִּנְדָבָה** (*bindavah*) in verse 8 means "with willingness" or "as a voluntary offering". This refers to an offering not required by law but given out of pure gratitude. Such voluntary offerings were highly valued in Israelite sacrificial practice because they expressed personal devotion.
