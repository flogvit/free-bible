# Historical and Cultural Context for Proverbs 10

## Key Concepts

Chapter 10 marks the beginning of the so-called "Solomonic main collection" (10:1–22:16), which differs literarily from the preceding chapters by consisting of short, individual proverbs rather than longer discourses. The Hebrew word *māshāl* (מָשָׁל, "proverb") in verse 1 denotes a concise, poetic statement that compares or contrasts two situations—a feature that characterizes the entire chapter through repeated antitheses between "the righteous" (*tsaddiq*, צַדִּיק) and "the wicked" (*rāshā'*, רָשָׁע).

The term *tsedāqāh* (צְדָקָה, "righteousness") in verse 2 carries a broader meaning than the modern Norwegian word. In wisdom literature, it encompasses both upright conduct, generosity toward the poor, and faithfulness to God's covenant. The idea that righteousness "saves from death" reflects the Old Testament understanding that moral living led to blessing and long life.

## Events and Customs

Several verses in this chapter refer to the annual rhythm of an agricultural society. Verse 10 contrasts the one who gathers during summer (*qayits*, קַיִץ) with the one who sleeps during harvest (*qātsir*, קָצִיר). In ancient Israel, grain ripened from April to June, and the grape harvest took place in August–September. Sleeping during harvest meant not only laziness but direct economic disaster for the entire household, since the crop had to be gathered quickly before the rainy season began.

The expression "winks with the eye" (*qōrēts 'ayin*, קֹרֵץ עַיִן) in verse 10 describes a form of nonverbal communication that signaled deceit or hidden motives. This was known throughout the ancient Near East as a sign of unreliability and falsehood.
