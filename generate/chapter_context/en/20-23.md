## Events and Customs

Chapter 23 contains several exhortations related to meals with rulers and powerful people (vv. 1-3, 6-8). In the ancient Near East, hospitality and shared meals were charged with political and social significance. Eating at a ruler's table could involve obligations, and the food served might be used as a means of manipulation or bribery. The expression "put a knife to your throat" (v. 2) is a hyperbolic image for self-control, indicating that the temptation at such meals was very real. The Hebrew phrase רַע עָיִן (ra' ayin, "evil eye") in v. 6 describes a stingy or envious person, in contrast to the "good eye" which denoted generosity.

The prohibition against moving ancient boundary stones and encroaching on the land of the fatherless (vv. 10-11) reflects a widespread concern in Old Testament law. Boundary stones were considered sacred throughout the ancient Near East, and moving them was viewed as both theft and a violation of divine order. The term גֹּאֵל (go'el, "redeemer" or "defender") – here used as גֹּאֲלָם (go'alam, "their redeemer") in v. 11 – refers to God as the protector of the weak, a role typically filled by a male relative in Israelite society.

Verses 29-35 contain a detailed warning against drunkenness. The Hebrew expressions אוֹי (oy) and אֲבוֹי (avoy) are cries of lament expressing deep sorrow. The word חַכְלִלוּת (chakhlilut) in v. 29 describes reddish or clouded eyes, a physical symptom of excessive drinking. The phrase מִמְסָךְ (mimsakh) in v. 30 refers to spiced or mixed wine, which was stronger than ordinary wine and considered especially seductive. In v. 32, the effect of wine is compared to a bite from a נָחָשׁ (nachash, snake) and a צִפְעֹנִי (tzif'oni, viper/adder), emphasizing the deadly danger of alcohol abuse.

## Key Terms

רַע עָיִן (ra' ayin) – "evil eye", designation for a stingy or envious person (v. 6).

גֹּאֵל (go'el) – "redeemer" or "defender", here used of God as the protector of the fatherless (v. 11).

מִמְסָךְ (mimsakh) – spiced or mixed wine (v. 30).
