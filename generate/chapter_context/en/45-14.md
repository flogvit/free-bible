# Historical and Cultural Context for Romans 14

## Events and Customs

### Food Issues in the Church
Romans 14 addresses a specific conflict in the Roman church regarding food and holy days. The background was complex: Jewish Christians brought kosher traditions from the Mosaic Law, while Gentile Christians came from a culture where meat offered to idols was common in the marketplace. In Rome, much of the meat sold at the *macellum* (meat market) had first been sacrificed to Roman gods. Some Christians therefore chose to eat only vegetables (*λάχανα*, v. 2) to avoid any involvement with idol worship or unclean meat. Others believed that idols had no real existence and ate freely.

### Holy Days and Sabbath Practice
The conflict over days (vv. 5–6) reflects the tension between Jewish observance of the Sabbath and the Gentile Christians' free attitude toward the calendar. Jewish Christians often continued to keep the Sabbath and Jewish festivals, while Gentile Christians did not have this tradition. In Roman society there were also numerous religious feast days tied to the imperial calendar, which made the question of "holy days" even more complicated.

## Key Terms

### Κοινός (koinos) – "Unclean" or "Profane"
In verse 14 Paul uses the word *κοινόν*, which in a Jewish context meant "ritually unclean" or "profane"—that is, something that was not holy or permitted. This was a charged term that distinguished between the holy and the common in Jewish purity practice. Paul’s claim that "nothing is unclean in itself" was radical in light of Jewish tradition.

### Βῆμα (bēma) – "Judgment Seat"
In verse 10 mention is made of *βήματι τοῦ θεοῦ* (the judgment seat of God). The *bēma* was a well-known institution in Roman cities—an elevated platform where officials sat to judge. There were several such structures in Rome, and readers would have been very familiar with the concept from the Roman legal system. Paul uses this well-known institution as an image of God’s final judgment.

### Πρόσκομμα and σκάνδαλον – "Stumbling Block" and "Offense"
These words in verses 13 and 21 have a concrete background. *Πρόσκομμα* means a stone over which one stumbles, while *σκάνδαλον* originally described the trigger mechanism of a trap. Both were used figuratively to refer to spiritual dangers that could lead a believer away from the faith.
