# Historical and Cultural Context for Leviticus 1

## People

**Aaron's sons, the priests (בְּנֵי אַהֲרֹן הַכֹּהֲנִים)** are mentioned repeatedly (verses 5, 7, 8, 11) as those who perform the blood ritual and arrange the pieces of the sacrifice on the altar. Their role as mediators between the people and God is central to the sacrificial act.

## Places

**The Tent of Meeting (אֹהֶל מוֹעֵד, ohel mo'ed)** is the central location in this chapter. God speaks to Moses from the tent (verse 1), and the sacrificial service takes place at its entrance. The chapter places the action "at the entrance of the Tent of Meeting" (verses 3, 5), which was the courtyard where the altar of burnt offering stood.

**The north side of the altar (יֶרֶךְ הַמִּזְבֵּחַ צָפֹנָה)** is specifically mentioned in verse 11 as the place for slaughtering small livestock. This location had ritual significance.

## Events and Customs

**The burnt offering (עֹלָה, olah)** is the only sacrifice addressed in this chapter. The word literally means "that which goes up" and refers to the smoke rising toward heaven. The entire animal was completely consumed on the altar – no part was eaten by priests or those bringing the offering. The chapter describes three variations based on the financial ability of the offerer: cattle (verses 3-9), small livestock such as sheep or goats (verses 10-13), and birds – turtledoves or young pigeons (verses 14-17).

**The laying on of hands (וְסָמַךְ יָדוֹ, vesamakh yado)** in verse 4, where the one bringing the sacrifice places their hand on the animal's head, marked an identification between the giver and the offering. This action symbolically transferred the person of the giver to the sacrificial animal, so that the animal's death and burning represented the giver's complete surrender to God.

## Key Terms

**Qorban (קָרְבָּן)** – Hebrew for "offering" or "sacrifice" (verses 2-3, 10, 14), from the root q-r-b ("to approach"). The term expresses that the purpose of the sacrifice was to bring people closer to God.

**Tamim (תָּמִים)** – "without blemish" or "perfect" (verses 3, 10). The requirement for unblemished animals was theologically motivated: the offering should represent the best that humans could give and emphasized God's holiness and perfection.

**Lekhapper (לְכַפֵּר)** – "to make atonement" (verse 4). This is the first time the term 'kipper' (atonement) is introduced in Leviticus, establishing the theology of atonement that is central to the book.

**Ishe (אִשֶּׁה)** – "fire offering" (verses 9, 13, 17). The term is used consistently together with "pleasing aroma" and emphasizes that the offering was given through fire.

**Reach nichoach (רֵיחַ נִיחֹחַ)** – "a pleasing fragrance" or "soothing aroma" (verses 9, 13, 17). In Israelite theology, this describes God's pleasure in the worshiper's devotion.

## Chapter-Specific Findings

Archaeological excavations at several Israelite cult sites have uncovered altars and bone remains that correspond to the sacrificial practices described in this chapter. At Tel Arad in the Negev desert, an Israelite cult site from the monarchy period was discovered with an altar in the courtyard. Bone analyses from this and other locations show remains of cattle, sheep, and goats – exactly the types of animals mentioned in Leviticus 1. At Lachish and other sites, pigeon bones have also been found in cultic contexts, confirming that bird offerings (verses 14-17) were an actual practice.
