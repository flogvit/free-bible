## People

**Phinehas, son of Eleazar** is mentioned as the leader of the army along with the holy articles and trumpets (v. 6). His role here follows directly from his zealous action in Numbers 25:7-13, where he killed an Israelite man and a Midianite woman during the Baal-Peor incident. The fact that Phinehas, not Joshua, leads this expedition underscores that this was a religious act of vengeance rather than an ordinary military campaign.

**The five Midianite kings**—Evi, Rekem, Sur, Hur, and Reba—are mentioned in v. 8. Joshua 13:21 calls them "chiefs under Sihon," suggesting they were vassal kings. The name "Rekem" is also known as a place name, possibly linked to the Petra area. "Sur" is mentioned in Numbers 25:15 as the father of Cozbi, the woman Phinehas killed.

**Balaam, son of Beor** was also killed in the battle (v. 8). His death here connects the narrative from chapters 22-24, where he originally blessed Israel, with v. 16, which reveals that he advised the Midianites to seduce the Israelites at Peor.

## Events and Customs

**Herem war** (devotion to destruction) was a form of holy war in which the spoils were dedicated to God. Moses' command to kill all male children and women who had had sexual relations (v. 17), while sparing virgins (v. 18), reflects ancient Near Eastern warfare practices, but with specific Israelite modifications. Assyrian and Moabite inscriptions (such as the Mesha Stele) describe similar practices where enemies were "devoted to destruction" for a deity.

**Purification rituals** in vv. 19-24 provide detailed instructions for soldiers who had killed or touched dead bodies. A seven-day purification outside the camp, with cleansing on the third and seventh days using "water of purification" (מֵי נִדָּה, *me niddah*), corresponds to the rules in Numbers 19 regarding the red heifer. Metal objects were to be purified by fire, while other materials were to be purified with water.

**The distribution of war spoils** (vv. 25-47) follows a precise system: the spoils are divided equally between the soldiers and the rest of the people. From the soldiers' half, 1/500 goes to the priests; from the people's half, 1/50 goes to the Levites. This system for war spoils has parallels in other ancient Near Eastern cultures, but the specific shares allocated to cult personnel are unique to Israel.

## Key Terms

**נִקְמַת** (*niqmat*, "vengeance/retribution") in vv. 2-3 does not refer to personal vengeance, but to divine justice that restores order after an offense. The expression alternates between "vengeance for Israel" (v. 2) and "the Lord's vengeance" (v. 3), showing that the military action is understood as the execution of divine judgment.

**טִירֹתָם** (*tirotam*, "their encampments/enclosures") in v. 10 refers to the characteristic nomadic settlements of the Midianites. The word describes guarded camps or stone enclosures for livestock, typical of semi-nomadic peoples in the region.

**כוּמָז** (*kumaz*) in v. 50, often translated as "gold ornaments" or "pendants," is a rare Hebrew word that appears only here and in Exodus 35:22. It likely refers to an ornament worn on the body, possibly with an amulet function.

## Chapter-Specific Findings

Archaeological excavations in the Midian region (northwestern Arabia, modern Tabuk Province in Saudi Arabia) have revealed settlements from the late Bronze Age and early Iron Age with characteristic "Midianite" pottery decorated with geometric patterns. These findings confirm the presence of the Midianites as a distinct cultural group during this period. The discovery of copper mines in the Timna region, traditionally associated with Midianite metallurgy, corresponds to the large quantities of metal mentioned among the war spoils (vv. 22, 50-52).
