# Historical and Cultural Context for 2 Corinthians 13

## Events and Customs

### The Rule of Witnesses (verse 1)
Paul cites the principle that "every matter must be established by the testimony of two or three witnesses" (Deuteronomy 19:15). This was a fundamental legal rule in Jewish judicial practice that ensured justice and protected against false accusations. In the Greco-Roman world, similar principles regarding witness testimony existed in court proceedings. Paul applies this legal principle to his upcoming third visit—either as an image of the three visits that serve as "witnesses", or as a warning that he will follow proper procedure when addressing the church's problems.

### The Holy Kiss (verse 12)
The greeting "ἐν ἁγίῳ φιλήματι" (with a holy kiss) refers to an established custom in early Christian communities. Kissing as a form of greeting was common in antiquity, especially in Jewish and Eastern Mediterranean cultures, but the "holy" kiss marked the distinctive character of the Christian community. It was practiced among fellow believers as an expression of spiritual unity and brotherhood in Christ, and later became a fixed part of Christian liturgy.

## Key Terms

### Dokimē and Related Words (verses 3, 5–7)
A central word cluster in this chapter is "δοκιμή" (dokimē – proof/testing), "δοκιμάζετε" (dokimazete – test/examine) and "ἀδόκιμοι" (adokimoi – those who fail the test/rejected). These words come from metallurgy and commerce, where they described the process of testing metals for authenticity and quality. Coins and precious metals were "tested" to reveal counterfeits. Paul ironically turns the Corinthians' demand to "test" his apostleship back upon themselves.

### Katartisis (verse 9)
The word "κατάρτισιν" (katartisin) is often translated as "restoration" or "making complete". The term was used in a medical context for setting broken bones, and for mending fishing nets (Matthew 4:21), and generally for restoring something to its intended function. Paul's desire is therefore that the church should be "made whole"—healed from its conflicts and brought back to spiritual integrity.

### The Trinitarian Benediction (verse 13)
The closing benediction "ἡ χάρις τοῦ κυρίου Ἰησοῦ Χριστοῦ καὶ ἡ ἀγάπη τοῦ θεοῦ καὶ ἡ κοινωνία τοῦ ἁγίου πνεύματος" is the most developed Trinitarian formula in Paul's letters. The word "κοινωνία" (koinōnia) means fellowship, participation, or communion, and expresses the deep connection believers have with one another and with God through the Spirit. This benediction has become one of the most widely used liturgical formulas in church history.
