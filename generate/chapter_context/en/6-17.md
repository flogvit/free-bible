# Historical and Cultural Context for Joshua 17

## People

**The Daughters of Zelophehad** (Mahlah, Noah, Hoglah, Milcah, and Tirzah) play a central role in this chapter. Their case was first presented to Moses in Numbers 27:1-11, where they argued for their right to inherit since their father had died without sons. The fact that they now appear before Joshua, Eleazar, and the leaders to claim their inheritance shows that Moses' legislation was upheld. This was groundbreaking in the ancient Near East, where inheritance rights belonged almost exclusively to men. Parallel texts from Nuzi (a Hurrian city from the second millennium B.C.E.) show that daughters could inherit in certain cases, confirming that this practice had cultural parallels.

**Machir** is mentioned as Manasseh's firstborn and "the father of Gilead." The expression "man of war" (אִישׁ מִלְחָמָה) explains why his descendants received the strategically important areas of Gilead and Bashan east of the Jordan—areas that required military protection against enemies from the east.

## Places

**Gilead and Bashan** were the Transjordanian territories assigned to half of the tribe of Manasseh. Gilead was known for its pastures and balsam trees, while Bashan was famous for its fertile soil, oak forests, and large cattle herds. These areas lay east of the Jordan and were among the first land regions Israel conquered.

**Beth-Shean, Ibleam, Dor, En-Dor, Taanach, and Megiddo** were strategic cities along the Jezreel Valley and the coast. Archaeological excavations at Tel Megiddo and Beth-Shean have uncovered massive Canaanite fortifications and temples from this period, explaining why the Israelites could not drive out the inhabitants (v. 12).

## Events and Customs

**Iron chariots** (רֶכֶב בַּרְזֶל) are mentioned as the reason the tribes of Joseph feared the Canaanites in the valleys. This reflects the technological transition from the Bronze Age to the Iron Age around 1200 B.C.E. The Canaanites and Philistines had access to iron technology before the Israelites, giving them a significant military advantage in open terrain. The Hittites and later peoples along the coast controlled iron production during this period.

**Forced labor (מַס)** is mentioned in verse 13 as the Israelites' solution when they became stronger. Instead of driving out the Canaanites, they imposed forced labor on them. This was a common practice in the ancient Near East, known from Egyptian and Mesopotamian sources, but it represented a compromise with God's command for complete expulsion.

## Chapter-Specific Findings

Archaeological excavations at Megiddo and Taanach have uncovered layers from the late Bronze Age and early Iron Age showing continuous Canaanite settlement throughout this period. Pottery finds and building remains suggest that Canaanite culture continued to dominate these cities well into the first millennium B.C.E., confirming the biblical account that Manasseh was unable to drive them out.
