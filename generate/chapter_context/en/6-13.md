# Historical and Cultural Context for Joshua 13

## People

The chapter mentions several historical figures. **Sihon**, the Amorite king in Heshbon, and **Og**, the king of Bashan, were two powerful rulers east of the Jordan whom Moses defeated before his death (Num 21). Og is described as "the last of the Rephaim" (v. 12), a people known for their large physical stature. **Balaam son of Beor** is referred to as "the diviner" (הַקּוֹסֵם, *haqqosem*) who was killed by the Israelites (v. 22). This refers to the events in Numbers 22–24 and 31, where Balaam was first hired to curse Israel but ended up blessing them instead. His later role in leading Israel astray led to his death. The five **Midianite chiefs** – Evi, Rekem, Sur, Hur, and Reba – are called "Sihon’s vassals" (נְסִיכֵי סִיחוֹן), suggesting they were subordinate allies or vassals under Amorite dominance in Transjordan.

## Places

The chapter provides a detailed geographical overview of both unconquered land and territory already allocated. **The region of the Philistines** included five city-states: Gaza, Ashdod, Ashkelon, Gath, and Ekron, each ruled by a *seren* (סֶרֶן) – a Phoenician-Philistine title for prince. **Shihor** (שִׁיחוֹר, v. 3) likely refers to "the Brook of Egypt" (Wadi el-Arish) or an eastern branch of the Nile Delta, marking the traditional southwestern border toward Egypt. **Geshur and Maacah** (vv. 11, 13) were small Aramean kingdoms northeast of the Sea of Galilee that remained unconquered. King David’s wife Maacah later came from this area (2 Sam 3:3). **Bashan** was known for its fertile soil and oak forests, while **Gilead** was the central highland east of the Jordan. The cities **Ashtaroth** and **Edrei** were Og’s royal residences; Ashtaroth was also a cult site for the goddess of the same name.

## Events and Customs

The land distribution described follows a specific pattern from ancient Near Eastern practice, in which a conquering king allocated land to his subjects. The term **"inheritance"** (נַחֲלָה, *naḥalah*) means more than just property – it implies a God-given, irrevocable family possession that was to remain within the clan. The special status of the Levites (vv. 14, 33) reflects their role as God’s special servants: "the Lord’s food offering" and the Lord himself are their inheritance. This meant they were to live from portions of sacrifices and tithes from the other tribes, scattered in cities throughout the land.

## Key Terms

The word **סַרְנֵי** (*sarne*), translated as "princes" (v. 3), is a non-Semitic title used exclusively for the five Philistine city rulers. The title may be related to the Greek *tyrannos* or the Luwian *tarwanis*, supporting the theory of the Philistines’ Aegean origin. The term **רְפָאִים** (*refa'im*, "Rephaim") in verse 12 refers to a prehistoric people known for their large physical size, often translated as "giants". In other contexts, the word can also mean "the dead" or "shades" in the realm of the dead, suggesting a mythological dimension. The expression **"to this day"** (עַד הַיּוֹם הַזֶּה, v. 13) is a literary formula that connects past events to the time of the author and shows that the Geshurites and Maacathites still lived in the area when the text was written.
