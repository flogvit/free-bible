# Historical and Cultural Context for Psalms 43

## Places

Psalm 43 mentions two important places: "your holy mountain" (הַר־קָדְשְׁךָ, *har qodshekha*) and "your dwelling places" (מִשְׁכְּנוֹתֶיךָ, *mishkenotekha*). The holy mountain refers to Mount Zion in Jerusalem, where the temple stood. The expression "your dwelling places" in the plural may point to various parts of the temple complex, or it could be a poetic intensification emphasizing the greatness of the sanctuary. The psalmist's longing to come to the altar (verse 4) suggests physical distance from Jerusalem. This is reinforced by the mention of "the enemy" (אוֹיֵב) in verse 2, which may indicate a concrete threat or persecution.

## Key Terms

The word **קֹדֵר** (*qoder*) in verse 2, translated as "in sorrow" or "in darkness," describes a state of deep distress and is associated with mourning garments and ritual lamentation. The expression creates a visual image of someone walking bent over and dressed in dark clothing, as at a funeral.

The word pair **אוֹר** (*'or*, "light") and **אֱמֶת** (*'emet*, "truth/faithfulness") in verse 3 are personified as guides who will lead the psalmist to God. In Old Testament thought, light represents God's presence and salvation, while truth/faithfulness stands for God's reliable character and promises. Together they express God's active intervention on behalf of the one praying.

## Events and Customs

The psalmist's desire to come to "God's altar" (מִזְבַּח אֱלֹהִים) and praise with a "harp" (כִנּוֹר, *kinnor*) in verse 4 reflects Israelite worship practices. The kinnor was a stringed instrument used by the Levites in temple service. Coming to the altar meant participating in sacrificial offerings and fellowship with God—the most central expression of Israelite piety. For someone prevented from taking part in this worship, it represented a deep spiritual crisis.
