## Key Concepts

### The Vine Tree (עֵץ־הַגֶּפֶן, *ets-hagefen*)

Israel was often portrayed as God’s vine or vineyard in the Old Testament (Isaiah 5:1–7; Psalm 80:9–17). What makes Ezekiel 15 striking is that the prophet turns this metaphor on its head: Instead of highlighting the fruit of the vine (which would have been positive), he focuses exclusively on the wood, which was known to be weak and useless.

### The Wood as Fuel (לָאֵשׁ נִתַּן לְאָכְלָה)

The expression "given to the fire for fuel" (verses 4, 6) draws on a well-known reality in ancient Israel. Grapevines that did not bear fruit were cut down and burned (cf. John 15:6). The wood of the vine was too soft and porous for craftsmanship, too weak even to make a peg (יָתֵד, *yated*) for hanging tools. Its only use was as fuel. This stands in sharp contrast to oak trees and cedar trees, which were valuable building materials.

### Unfaithfulness (מָעֲלוּ מַעַל, *ma'alu ma'al*)

The Hebrew expression in verse 8 is a legal term describing breach of contract or unfaithfulness. In the context of covenant, it denoted serious treachery against God—often linked to idolatry, which in biblical language was compared to adultery. The term was especially used regarding misuse of holy things or breaking an oath to God (cf. Leviticus 5:15; Joshua 7:1).
