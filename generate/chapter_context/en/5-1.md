# Historical and Cultural Context for Deuteronomy 1

## Places

The chapter opens with a geographical framework placing Moses’ speech east of the Jordan (בְּעֵבֶר הַיַּרְדֵּן), in the land of Moab (v. 1, 5). **Horeb** (חֹרֵב) is used as the name for the mountain where the covenant was made (v. 2, 6, 19). **Kadesh-Barnea** (קָדֵשׁ בַּרְנֵעַ), an important oasis in northeastern Sinai, served as Israel’s base and was the place where the spies were sent out and the people rebelled (v. 19, 46).

**Heshbon** (חֶשְׁבּוֹן) was the capital of Sihon, while **Ashtarot** and **Edrei** were cities in Bashan where King Og ruled (v. 4). Ashtarot was named after the goddess Astarte. The **Eshcol Valley** (נַחַל אֶשְׁכֹּל), meaning "Valley of the Cluster," became known for the large grapes the spies brought back as evidence of the land’s fertility (v. 24–25).

## People

**Sihon**, the Amorite king of Heshbon, and **Og**, the king of Bashan, are mentioned as recently defeated rulers (v. 4). Their defeat marks the end of the wilderness journey and demonstrates that the new generation will experience God’s victories.

**Caleb son of Jephunneh** (v. 36) is highlighted as one of the few from the Exodus generation who would enter the land, due to his faithfulness—he "wholly followed the Lord" (מִלֵּא אַחֲרֵי יְהוָה). **Joshua son of Nun** (v. 38) is introduced as Moses’ appointed successor who will lead Israel into the land.

## Events and Customs

The organization of leaders in groups of a thousand, a hundred, fifty, and ten (v. 15) reflects a decimalized military and administrative structure. This arrangement served both civil judicial purposes (v. 16–17) and military functions.

The spies’ mission (v. 22–25) is described with the verb **וְיַחְפְּרוּ** ("to explore"), suggesting thorough investigation. Bringing fruit as evidence was a concrete demonstration of the land’s fertility, yet despite this positive report, they also spoke of strong enemies (v. 28).

The defeat at Horma (חָרְמָה, v. 44) resulted from Israel’s disobedient attempt to enter the land after God had denied them entry. The Amorites pursued them "as bees do"—an image of relentless pursuit.

## Key Terms

The verb **בֵּאֵר** (be’er) in verse 5, translated as "to explain" or "to expound," suggests that Moses did not merely repeat the law but interpreted and clarified it for the new generation.

The expression **בְּנֵי עֲנָקִים** (the Anakim) in verse 28 refers to a people regarded as giants. The spies’ report about them contributed to the people’s fear and rebellion.

The time reference "in the fortieth year, in the eleventh month" (v. 3) places Moses’ speech at the very end of the wilderness journey, shortly before his death and Israel’s entry into the land.
