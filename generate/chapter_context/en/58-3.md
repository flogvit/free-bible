## People

### Moses (Μωϋσῆς)
Moses is mentioned as a faithful servant (θεράπων) in God's house (verses 2, 5), with reference to Numbers 12:7 where God defends Moses against the criticism of Aaron and Miriam. The author acknowledges Moses' faithfulness while arguing for Christ's superiority—not by diminishing Moses, but by highlighting the difference between a servant in the house and the Son over the house.

### The Wilderness Generation
The second half of the chapter (verses 7–19) focuses on that generation of Israelites who left Egypt under Moses' leadership but did not enter the promised land. According to Numbers 14, these were those who refused to enter Canaan after the spies' negative report and had to wander in the wilderness for forty years until the entire generation died out. This generation serves as a warning example for the letter's recipients.

## Places

### The Wilderness (ἐν τῇ ἐρήμῳ)
The wilderness mentioned in verses 8 and 17 refers to the Sinai Peninsula where the Israelites wandered for forty years. The word "παραπικρασμός" (provocation/rebellion) in verses 8 and 15 alludes to the events at Massah and Meribah, where the people murmured against God (Exodus 17:1–7; Numbers 20:1–13).

## Events and Customs

### The Rebellion at Kadesh-Barnea
The central historical reference point in the chapter's quotation from Psalm 95 is the events at Kadesh-Barnea (Numbers 13–14). There, Moses sent twelve spies into Canaan, and ten of them returned with a negative report. The people refused to trust God's promise and instead wanted to return to Egypt. As a result of this unbelief, God swore that this generation would not enter "rest"—the promised land. Hebrews uses this story typologically: just as the wilderness generation lost their rest through unbelief, the letter's recipients risk losing the heavenly rest by falling away.

## Key Terms

### Apostle and High Priest (ἀπόστολον καὶ ἀρχιερέα)
In verse 1, Jesus is called both "apostle" and "high priest"—a unique combination in the New Testament. The author presents Jesus as one who comes from God to humanity (apostle/messenger) and represents humanity before God (high priest).

### God's House (οἶκος)
The term "house" (οἶκος) in verses 2–6 has a double meaning. It refers both to God's people as a household and to the tabernacle/temple as God's dwelling place. The author develops this metaphor: Moses was faithful "in" (ἐν) the house as a servant, while Christ is faithful "over" (ἐπί) the house as Son. The recipients themselves are this house if they hold fast to their confidence and hope (verse 6).

### Hardening of Heart (σκληρύνητε)
The Greek verb "σκληρύνω" in verses 8, 13, and 15 describes a process by which the heart becomes hard and insensitive to God's voice. The term is used in the quotation from Psalm 95:7–11 and warns against the same attitude shown by the wilderness generation.

### Rest (κατάπαυσις)
The term "rest" in verses 11 and 18 refers literally to settlement in Canaan, but also carries deeper theological overtones—the final peace and fulfillment in God's presence. The author suggests that this rest has a broader meaning that is developed in the next chapter.
