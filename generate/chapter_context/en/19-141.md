## Events and Customs

Psalm 141 contains rich liturgical imagery that connects personal prayer with temple worship. Verse 2 compares prayer to *qetoret* (incense) and lifted hands to *minchat-erev* (the evening offering). The incense offering was burned on the small golden altar inside the sanctuary morning and evening (Exod 30:7–8), and its rising smoke symbolized prayer ascending to God. The evening offering (*mincha*) was presented each afternoon at sunset, marking the conclusion of the day's worship services. This comparison shows that personal prayer was viewed as a spiritual alternative to temple service, which became especially significant for those who were away from Jerusalem.

## Key Terms

The expression *shita YHWH shomra lefi* ("set, O LORD, a watch over my mouth," v. 3) uses military terminology—*shomra* denotes a guard post—applied metaphorically to self-discipline in speech. The word *dal* (door/gate) for lips is a rare expression that creates an image of the mouth as a gate needing protection.

Verse 4 contains the word *hitolel* (to practice wickedness), an intensive form describing repeated evil acts. The term *manammim* (their delicacies) refers to the luxurious feasts of the ungodly—a warning against being tempted by wealth gained through injustice.

Verse 5 uses the expression *shemen rosh* (head oil) as a metaphor for righteous correction, referencing a hospitality ritual in which guests were anointed with perfumed oil. The psalmist prefers such "wounding" kindness over the flattery of the ungodly.
