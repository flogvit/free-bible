## Places

The psalm highlights two central geographical elements: **Mount Zion** (הַר־צִיּוֹן) and **Jerusalem** surrounded by mountains. Here, Zion is used as a symbol of stability and immovability—it "does not shake" (לֹא־יִמּוֹט, v. 1). The image of Jerusalem surrounded by mountains (v. 2) serves as a concrete illustration of God’s protection: just as the hills form a natural defense around the city, so the Lord surrounds His people.

## Key Terms

The expression **שֵׁבֶט הָרֶשַׁע** ("the scepter of the wicked") in verse 3 refers to foreign or oppressive rule. The word שֵׁבֶט means both "rod", "scepter", and "tribe", and is used here for political power. **גּוֹרַל הַצַּדִּיקִים** ("the lot of the righteous") alludes to the allocation of land by casting lots (Josh 18:10), where גּוֹרַל means both the physical lot and the assigned inheritance. The psalm assures that foreign domination will not become permanent over the land God has given His people.

The expression **עַקַלְקַלּוֹתָם** ("their crooked ways") in verse 5 describes those who deviate from the right path. The word is an intensive form that emphasizes what is twisted and winding, in contrast to the "upright in heart" (יְשָׁרִים בְּלִבּוֹתָם) in verse 4.

## Events and Customs

The closing phrase **שָׁלוֹם עַל־יִשְׂרָאֵל** ("peace upon Israel") functions as a liturgical blessing that also concludes Psalm 128. This formula was likely used in communal worship. The psalm’s contrast between the righteous and those who walk crooked paths reflects the wisdom tradition of the two ways.
