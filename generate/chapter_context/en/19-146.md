## Key Concepts

Psalm 146 contains several key concepts central to its understanding. The expression **"nedivim"** (נְדִיבִים) in verse 3 is often translated as "princes" or "nobles," but literally means "the generous" or "the willing." The word describes people with power and resources, and the warning against trusting them reflects an experience that human power is unreliable and fleeting. The term **"eshtonotav"** (עֶשְׁתֹּנֹתָיו) in verse 4, translated as "his plans," is a rare word found only here in the Old Testament. It underscores how transient human projects and intentions are.

The expression **"El Yaakov"** (אֵל יַעֲקֹב) – "the God of Jacob" – in verse 5 is a covenantal title that connects Israel's God to the patriarch Jacob and the promises given to him. This name reminds the worshiper of God's faithfulness through generations.

## Events and Customs

The psalm's list of those whom God helps – the oppressed, the hungry, prisoners, the blind, the bowed down, foreigners, fatherless children, and widows (verses 7–9) – reflects Old Testament social ethics where care for vulnerable groups was a hallmark of righteous rule. In the ancient Near East, kings were often described as defenders of widows and fatherless children, but the psalm proclaims that only the LORD fully fulfills this role. The term **"ger"** (גֵּר), translated as "foreigner" or "resident alien," refers to non-Israelites living in the land who had limited legal protection – a group that the Mosaic Law repeatedly instructed Israelites to protect.

## Chapter-Specific Insights

The psalm's theme, emphasizing God's care for the weak over human power, aligns with prophetic theology, particularly from Isaiah and other prophetic texts that highlight God's defense of the oppressed. The contrast between the limitations of mortal humans (verses 3–4) and the infinite power of the eternal God (verses 6, 10) forms the theological core of the psalm.
