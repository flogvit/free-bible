# Historical and Cultural Context for Ruth 3

## Places

**The Threshing Floor (גֹּרֶן, goren)** was a central social and economic arena in Israelite society. It was usually located on an open, flat area outside the city, often on high ground where the wind could blow away the chaff during the tossing of grain. Threshing floors were frequently communal property and served as important meeting places where legal and business agreements could also be made. The fact that Boaz slept by the grain heap at night was common practice during harvest time—partly to guard the valuable grain against theft, and partly because work continued late into the night and early in the morning when the wind was favorable.

## Events and Customs

**The Kinsman-Redeemer Institution (גֹּאֵל, go'el)** that Ruth invokes in verse 9 was a system in which a close relative had responsibility for protecting the family’s property and rights. This included buying back land that had been sold out of the family, freeing relatives from slavery, and—as here—marrying a childless widow to continue the name and inheritance of the deceased. Ruth herself took the initiative to invoke this right, which was unusually bold.

**The Night Visit and "Uncovering the Feet" (וְגִלִּית מַרְגְּלֹתָיו)** has been the subject of much discussion. The action was a symbolic way of requesting protection and marriage, as Ruth’s plea that Boaz should "spread his wing over her" confirms (v. 9). The term מַרְגְּלֹת (feet) can sometimes be interpreted as a euphemism in Hebrew literature, but the context emphasizes that this was a honorable proposal of marriage, not an indecent act.

## Key Terms

**"Spread your wing over your servant" (וּפָרַשְׂתָּ כְנָפֶךָ עַל־אֲמָתְךָ)** in verse 9 is a strongly symbolic expression. The word כָּנָף (kanaf, "wing" or "corner of a garment") refers both to bird wings and to the hem or corner of a cloak. In Ruth 2:12 Boaz used the same term regarding God’s protective wings. Now Ruth asks him to become the instrument of that protection. Spreading one’s cloak over a woman was an accepted marriage gesture in the ancient Near East (cf. Ezekiel 16:8).

**"A Woman of Valor" (אֵשֶׁת חַיִל)** in verse 11 corresponds to the description of Boaz as "a mighty man" (אִישׁ גִּבּוֹר חַיִל) in 2:1. The word חַיִל denotes strength, character, and honor. That the whole town knew Ruth as such a woman shows that her reputation was established through actions, not merely words. The same expression is used for the ideal woman in Proverbs 31:10.

**"Six Measures of Barley"** in verses 15–17 was likely a generous gift (the Hebrew does not specify the exact unit of measure). The gift confirmed Boaz’s good intentions and served as a visible sign to Naomi that the negotiations had gone well.
