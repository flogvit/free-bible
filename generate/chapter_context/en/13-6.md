# Historical and Cultural Context for 1 Chronicles 6

## People

The chapter presents three main categories of Levitical servants. **Heman, Asaph, and Ethan** (vv. 18–32) were the leading singers whom David appointed to temple service. Heman, from the Kohathite branch, was Samuel’s grandson through Joel (v. 18). Asaph, from the Gershonite branch, is credited with several psalms in the Book of Psalms. Ethan from the Merarite branch is identified in parallel texts with Jeduthun. These three families formed the foundation for the temple’s singing service across generations.

**Zadok** (v. 38) is mentioned as a central figure in the line of high priests descending from Aaron through Eleazar. This line became especially important after Abiathar’s family (from the Ithamarite line) was removed during Solomon’s reign.

## Places

The chapter contains a detailed geographical overview of the Levitical cities (vv. 39–66). **Hebron** was given to Aaron’s descendants as a city of refuge, but its fields and villages belonged to Caleb (vv. 40–41). **Shechem** in the hill country of Ephraim and **Golan** in Bashan are mentioned as cities of refuge. The chapter assigns 13 cities to Aaron’s sons (v. 45), 10 to the other Kohathites (v. 46), 13 to the Gershonites (v. 47), and 12 to the Merarites (v. 48).

## Events and Customs

**Temple singing** as an institution (vv. 16–17) began, according to the text, when the ark of the covenant found rest—that is, when David brought it to Jerusalem. The musical service was organized with three singers leading three choirs, each representing one of Levi’s three main branches. This created a theological balance in which all Levitical groups participated in worship.

**Levitical cities** (vv. 39–66) represent a unique system in which the Levites received cities with pasturelands scattered throughout the land instead of a contiguous tribal territory. They did not receive ownership of the land, only the right to use the pastures surrounding the cities. This system ensured that religious guidance was available throughout Israel.

## Key Terms

The term **"overseeing the singing service"** (עַל־יְדֵי־שִׁיר, v. 16) expresses that music was a form of service and work, not merely entertainment. Singing was a liturgical function with the same dignity as the offerings.

**Cities of refuge** (עָרֵי הַמִּקְלָט, vv. 42, 52) were cities where unintentional killers could seek protection from blood vengeance. Of the Levitical cities, six were such cities of refuge—three on each side of the Jordan.

**Pasturelands** (מִגְרָשִׁים) refer to the areas around the cities reserved for the Levites’ livestock; according to Numbers 35:4–5, these extended 1,000 cubits from the city wall.
