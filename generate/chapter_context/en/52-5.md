## Key Concepts

The chapter contains several Greek terms that require cultural and theological explanation. The expression **"the Lord's day"** (ἡμέρα κυρίου, *hēmera kyriou*) in verse 2 is deeply rooted in the prophetic literature of the Old Testament, where it denotes a future day of divine judgment. Paul transfers this apocalyptic concept to Christ's return. The slogan **"peace and security"** (εἰρήνη καὶ ἀσφάλεια, *eirēnē kai asphaleia*) in verse 3 reflects Roman imperial propaganda—*Pax Romana* promised precisely peace and stability under the emperor's protection. Paul warns against trusting such human guarantees rather than God.

The contrast between **"children of light"** (υἱοὶ φωτός) and darkness in verses 4–8 reflects a dualism known from Jewish apocalyptic literature, particularly the Qumran texts. The armor imagery in verse 8 with **breastplate** (θώραξ, *thōrax*) and **helmet** (περικεφαλαία, *perikephalaia*) alludes to Isaiah 59:17 and would have been immediately recognizable in a city with Roman military presence.

## Events and Customs

The term **ἄτακτοι** (*ataktoi*, "the disorderly") in verse 14 has military origins and describes soldiers who break formation or desert. In Thessalonica, this may have referred to believers who stopped working in expectation of Christ's imminent return, a problem Paul addresses more directly in 2 Thessalonians.

**The holy kiss** (φίλημα ἅγιον, verse 26) was an established Christian greeting practice in the early congregations, likely during worship services. It marked spiritual brotherhood and fellowship across social divides—remarkable in a culture where physical contact between different classes was uncommon.

Paul's solemn command in verse 27 that the letter **be read to all the brothers** testifies that Christian gatherings already included public reading of Scripture, following the pattern of synagogue practice. The strong wording (ἐνορκίζω, "I adjure") underscores the authoritative status of the letter and suggests that the congregation met together, not only in separate house groups.
