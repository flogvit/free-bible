# Historical and Cultural Context for Psalms 99

## People

Psalm 99 mentions three central figures from Israel's history: Moses, Aaron, and Samuel (v. 6). These three represent different time periods and roles in Israel's relationship with God. Moses was the lawgiver and prophet who spoke with God face to face; Aaron was the first high priest who established the priesthood; and Samuel was the last judge and prophet who anointed Israel's first kings. By mentioning these three together, the psalmist emphasizes a tradition of intercession and closeness to God that spans generations. All three served as mediators between God and the people, and all three experienced that God answered them when they cried out to him.

## Places

Zion is explicitly mentioned in verse 2 as the place where the Lord is "great." In Israelite theology, Zion (Jerusalem with the temple mount) was the place God had chosen as his dwelling on earth. "His holy mountain" in verse 9 refers to the same thing—the temple mount where God's presence reigned. The expression "his footstool" (v. 5) may refer to the ark of the covenant in the Most Holy Place, which was understood as God's throne on earth, or to the temple and Zion as a whole.

## Events and Customs

The reference to the "cloud pillar" in verse 7 points back to the time of wandering in the wilderness when God led Israel with a cloud and fire pillar (Exodus 13:21–22). It was especially at the cloud pillar over the tabernacle that God spoke to Moses. This connection between God's speaking presence and the cloud was a central element in Israelite memory. The call to "worship at his footstool" and "at his holy mountain" reflects the practice of temple worship where the people came to bow down (Hebrew *hishtachavah*) in awe.

## Key Terms

The Hebrew expression *yoshev keruvim* ("who sits enthroned above the cherubim," v. 1) describes God's invisible throne above the ark of the covenant, where two cherubim with outstretched wings covered the mercy seat. This was the primary symbolic representation of God's kingship in Israel. The term *qadosh* ("holy") is repeated three times in the psalm (vv. 3, 5, 9), with a variation in verse 9 where it says *qadosh YHWH elohenu* ("holy is the Lord our God"). This triple emphasis on holiness may recall the seraphim's trishagion in Isaiah 6:3, although the structure here is different. Holiness (*qedushah*) denoted in Hebrew thought both God's absolute otherness and his moral purity. The combination of *mishpat* (justice/judgment) and *tsedaqah* (righteousness) in verse 4 is a word pair that often appears together in Hebrew poetry to describe God's righteous rule.
