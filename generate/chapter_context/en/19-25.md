## Key Concepts

Psalm 25 is an acrostic psalm in which each verse line begins with a successive letter of the Hebrew alphabet (aleph to tav). However, the acrostic has some irregularities: the letter *vav* (ו) is missing between *he* and *zayin*; verses 18 and 19 both begin with the letter *resh* (ר), so that *qof* (ק) is omitted from the sequence; and the closing verse (22) with *pe* (פ) falls outside the usual alphabetic pattern, expanding the focus from the individual to all Israel.

The Hebrew word *sod* (סוֹד) in verse 14, often translated as "intimacy" or "secret counsel", denotes an intimate council chamber or confidential fellowship. Here it describes the close relationship between God and the godly. The phrase *chaṭṭo'ot ne'uray* (חַטֹּאות נְעוּרַי) in verse 7, "my youthful sins", reflects an understanding that the missteps of youth should not be held against someone who has matured in faithfulness to the covenant.

## Events and Customs

The psalm's repeated use of terms such as *derek* (דֶּרֶךְ, "way") and *'orach* (אֹרַח, "path") in verses 4, 8, 9, 10, and 12 reflects the wisdom tradition in which life was understood as a journey on either the way of righteousness or the way of wickedness. The prayer that God should "teach" (*lamad*, לָמַד) and "lead" (*darak*, דָּרַךְ) the psalmist in verses 4–5 and 9 mirrors the teaching tradition in which God himself is the ultimate instructor for his people.
