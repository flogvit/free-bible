## People

The chapter introduces "Qohelet" (קֹהֶלֶת), who presents himself as "the son of David, king in Jerusalem" (verses 1, 12). This description points to King Solomon, although his name is never mentioned directly. In verse 12, Qohelet confirms his royal status: "I, Qohelet, was king over Israel in Jerusalem."

## Places

Jerusalem is mentioned twice (verses 1 and 16) as the place where Qohelet was king. In verse 16, he emphasizes that he has gathered more wisdom than all who were in Jerusalem before him, which strengthens his authority to speak on life's great questions.

## Key Terms

**הֲבֵל הֲבָלִים** (havel havalim, verse 2) – The characteristic superlative form "vanity of vanities" or "the greatest vanity" is introduced here and repeated five times in verse 1 alone. This programmatic statement establishes the foundational theme of the chapter and the book right from the opening.

**תַּחַת הַשָּׁמֶשׁ** ("under the sun") – The expression occurs three times in the chapter (verses 3, 9, 14) and denotes earthly existence viewed from a human perspective.

**יִתְרוֹן** (jitron, verse 3) – A commercial term meaning "profit" or "gain". Qohelet poses the fundamental question: "What advantage does a person have for all their toil?"

**רְעוּת רוּחַ** (re'ut ruach, verse 14) – Often translated as "chasing after wind". The image of trying to catch the wind illustrates the futility of human striving.

## Chapter-Specific Findings

The chapter has a clear two-part structure: Verses 2–11 present cosmic observations about cyclical repetition in nature (the sun, the wind, the rivers, the generations), while verses 12–18 give Qohelet's personal testimony of his search for wisdom.

The natural observations in verses 4–7 describe a world in constant motion that nevertheless never achieves anything new – the sun rises and sets, the wind blows in circles, the rivers flow to the sea which is never full. This supports the claim in verse 9: "What has been is what will be."

The chapter concludes with a paradoxical insight (verse 18): More wisdom leads to more pain, and increased knowledge brings increased sorrow. This sets the tone for Qohelet's further exploration.
