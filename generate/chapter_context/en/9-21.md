# Historical and Cultural Context for 1 Samuel 21

## People

**Ahimelech** was a priest at Nob and a descendant of the family of Eli. His name means "my brother is king" and may reflect family ties to the royal house. He served at the sanctuary and showed hospitality to David without knowing about his situation as a fugitive.

**Doeg the Edomite** is described as "the chief of Saul's shepherds" (אַבִּיר הָרֹעִים). As an Edomite, he was a foreigner serving in Israelite service. The phrase that he was "kept before the Lord" may mean that he had fulfilled a religious vow, undergone purification, or was under some form of religious obligation. His presence at the sanctuary on this day is noted in the text.

**Achish of Gath** was a Philistine king in one of the five major Philistine cities. The name Achish has parallels in Aegean languages and may confirm the Philistines' origin from the Mediterranean region. David sought refuge with him, which shows how desperate his situation had become.

## Places

**Nob** was a priestly city located north of Jerusalem, likely on the Mount of Olives or nearby. After the destruction of Shiloh, the tabernacle or parts of it had been moved there. The place held significance as a religious center, with both the showbread and the ephod kept there, along with Goliath's sword as war booty.

**Gath** was one of the five major Philistine cities (the pentapolis), traditionally located at Tell es-Safi in the Shephelah lowlands. Archaeological excavations have revealed significant Philistine settlement from the Iron Age. It is ironic that David fled to Goliath's hometown, highlighting his desperate situation as a fugitive.

## Events and Customs

**The Showbread** (לֶחֶם הַפָּנִים, "the bread of the presence") consisted of twelve loaves placed before the Lord in the sanctuary every Sabbath, representing the twelve tribes of Israel. According to Leviticus 24:5-9, only the priests were allowed to eat the old bread when it was replaced. Ahimelech's willingness to give this holy bread to David presupposes ritual purity—that the men had abstained from women.

**Feigned madness** was a known survival strategy in ancient times. David "changed his behavior" (וַיְשַׁנּוֹ אֶת־טַעְמוֹ), scratched on the gates, and let saliva run down his beard. In the ancient Near East, madness was often seen as divinely inflicted, and harming a mad person could bring misfortune. Achish's dismissive reaction—"Do I lack madmen?"—shows that such behavior was recognizable and that while such people were tolerated, they were not welcome at the court.

## Key Terms

The expression **"kept before the Lord"** (נֶעְצָר לִפְנֵי יְהוָה) regarding Doeg may indicate several things: fulfillment of a Nazirite vow, quarantine due to impurity, or a religious obligation requiring presence at the sanctuary. The exact meaning is debated, but it emphasizes that even foreigners could have religious obligations at Israelite sanctuaries.

**The Ephod** (הָאֵפוֹד) is mentioned as the place where Goliath's sword was kept. The ephod was a priestly garment used in seeking oracles, often associated with the Urim and Thummim. The fact that war booty was stored "behind the ephod" shows the sanctuary's function as a repository for important national items.
