# Historical and Cultural Context for Joshua 4

## Places

**Gilgal** (גִּלְגָּל) is mentioned here for the first time as Israel’s camp on the west side of the Jordan. The name likely means "stone circle" or "rolling," and according to the text, the place was located "at the eastern border of Jericho" (v. 19). Archaeologically, it has been difficult to identify the exact location, but several candidates exist in the area between the Jordan River and Jericho.

**The plains of Jericho** (עַרְבוֹת יְרִיחוֹ) were the fertile valley west of the Jordan, a natural gateway to the highlands of Canaan. This low-lying area (about 250 meters below sea level) was strategically important and provided access to the main roads leading into the land.

## Events and Customs

**Memorial stones** were a widespread custom in the ancient Near East for marking significant events or places. Twelve stones—one for each tribe—emphasize the unity of the community. The practice of raising stones as memorials is also found in the patriarchal narratives (Gen 28:18; 31:45-52). That the stones were to be taken from the riverbed itself where the priests stood gave them special symbolic power as witnesses to God’s action.

**The catechetical formula** in verses 6–7 and 21–22 ("when your children ask…") follows the same pattern as the Passover story (Exod 12:26-27). This shows how Israelite religion emphasized oral transmission from generation to generation. Parents were given responsibility for explaining the meaning of the memorial stones, so that God’s saving acts would not be forgotten.

## Key Terms

**"The ark of the testimony"** (אֲרוֹן הָעֵדוּת) in verse 16 is an alternative designation for the ark of the covenant, highlighting the tablets of the law (the testimony) that were kept inside it. This underscores the connection between God’s miraculous action and His covenant relationship with the people.

**"The tenth day of the first month"** (v. 19) places the crossing exactly four days before Passover was to be celebrated. This timing is theologically significant: it is the same day the Israelites were to select the Passover lamb (Exod 12:3). Thus, the entry into the land is directly linked to the deliverance from Egypt.

## Archaeological Context

Stone circles and upright stones from the Bronze Age have been found in several places in the Jordan Valley and the central highlands. Although none of these can be directly connected to Joshua’s memorial stones, they show that the custom of raising stone monuments was common in the region. The name Gilgal is preserved in several place names in the area, supporting the geographical credibility of the text.
