# Historical and Cultural Context for 1 Samuel 23

## Places

The chapter mentions several geographical locations that provide a picture of David's movements in the wilderness of Judah. **Keilah** (קְעִילָה) was a fortified city in the lowlands of Judah (the Shephelah), identified with Khirbet Qila, about 13 km northwest of Hebron. As a city with "gates and bars" (v. 7), it was a typical fortified town that could be besieged. Its location near Philistine territory made it vulnerable to attack.

The **Wilderness of Ziph** (מִדְבַּר־זִיף) lies southeast of Hebron, in the rugged desert region. The place called Horesah (חֹרְשָׁה, "the wooded area") and **the hill of Hakilah** (גִּבְעַת הַחֲכִילָה) are mentioned as David's hiding places. The **Wilderness of Maon** (מִדְבַּר מָעוֹן), further south, borders the Arabah desert. These areas, with their caves, ravines, and inaccessible terrain, were ideal for a fugitive like David. Archaeological surveys have revealed many caves in this region that could provide shelter.

## People

**Abiathar** (אֶבְיָתָר), the son of Ahimelech, is introduced as the surviving priest who fled to David with the ephod after the massacre at Nob (chapter 22). His presence gave David access to "inquire of the Lord" through the ephod, which was crucial for David's decisions in this chapter.

The **Ziphites** (זִפִים) were the inhabitants of Ziph who chose to betray David to Saul (v. 19). This shows that not everyone in Judah supported David, even though he was from their own tribe. Their loyalty lay with the reigning king.

## Events and Customs

**Inquiring of the Lord through the ephod** (שָׁאַל בַּיהוָה) was an established practice for seeking divine guidance (vv. 2, 4, 9–12). The ephod likely contained the Urim and Thummim, sacred objects used to receive yes/no answers from God. David's repeated inquiries show that this was a dialogical process in which follow-up questions could be asked.

**Raiding threshing floors** (v. 1) was a common wartime tactic. Threshing floors were often located outside city walls, and stealing grain during the harvest was both economically devastating and strategically effective for weakening the enemy.

## Key Terms

The expression **"strengthened his hand in God"** (וַיְחַזֵּק אֶת־יָדוֹ בֵּאלֹהִים, v. 16) describes Jonathan's encouragement of David. To "strengthen the hand" was an idiom for giving courage and support, and "in God" indicates that this strength was rooted in trust in God's promises.

The place name **Sela-Hammahlekoth** (סֶלַע הַמַּחְלְקוֹת, v. 28) means "Rock of Partings" or "Rock of Slipperiness." The name marks the place where Saul had to abandon his pursuit—a physical marker of the providential deliverance David experienced.
