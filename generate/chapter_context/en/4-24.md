# Historical and Cultural Context for Numbers 24

## People

**Agag** (verse 7) is a title or name used for Amalekite kings, similar to "Pharaoh" for Egyptian rulers. The prophecy that Israel's king will be "higher than Agag" points forward to conflicts between Israel and Amalek, especially known from the time of Saul (1 Samuel 15). The name also appears in extrabiblical sources from the second millennium BCE, confirming the historical presence of the Amalekites in the region.

**The Kenites** (verses 21–22) were a nomadic tribe with close ties to Israel. Moses' father-in-law Jethro was a Kenite (Judges 1:16). They were known as skilled metalworkers, and their name may be connected to the Hebrew word for "smith" (קַיִן, *qayin*).

## Places

**Kittim** (verse 24) refers to Cyprus, specifically the city of Kition (modern Larnaka). In later biblical and Jewish literature, the name was expanded to include islands and coastal areas in the western Mediterranean, including Greece and Rome. The prophecy about ships from Kittim was later interpreted as a reference to Greek and Roman conquerors.

**Seir and Edom** (verse 18) designate the mountainous region southeast of the Dead Sea. Archaeological findings show that Edomite settlement developed significantly from the late Bronze Age, and the area became an organized kingdom before Israel had kings.

## Events and Customs

**Prophetic ecstasy and divination** are clearly contrasted in this chapter. Verse 1 states that on this occasion Balaam did not seek "omens" (נְחָשִׁים, *neḥashim*), a practice involving the interpretation of natural signs common in Mesopotamian divination. Instead, God's Spirit came upon him (verse 2). This marks a transition from pagan divination to true prophecy. The expression "falling down with eyes open" (verses 4, 16) describes a state of prophetic vision in which the seer receives divine revelation.

**Clapping hands together** (verse 10) was in ancient times a gesture expressing strong displeasure, frustration, or contempt. Balak uses this gesture to show his anger over Balaam's blessings.

## Key Terms

**"In the latter days"** (בְּאַחֲרִית הַיָּמִים, *beʾaḥarit hayyamim*, verse 14) is an important prophetic expression denoting a future, often eschatological time. This is the first time the expression is used in a prophecy about a coming ruler.

**"Star" and "scepter"** (כּוֹכָב, *kokhav* and שֵׁבֶט, *shevet*, verse 17) are royal symbols. The star as a symbol of kingship was widespread throughout the ancient Near East. The scepter represents royal authority and dominion. This prophecy was later interpreted messianically in Jewish tradition, and the rebel leader Bar Kokhba ("Son of the Star," 132–135 CE) took his name from this verse.

**"Shaddai"** (שַׁדַּי, verses 4, 16) is an ancient divine name, often translated "Almighty." The name appears frequently in the patriarchal narratives and in the book of Job, representing an archaic tradition that gives Balaam's oracles a solemn, ancient tone.
