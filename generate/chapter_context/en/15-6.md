# Historical and Cultural Context for Ezra 6

## People

**Darius I (522–486 B.C.)** is the Persian king who ordered a search of the archives and then confirmed and expanded Cyrus's original decree regarding temple construction (verses 1–12). His decree provides detailed instructions about funding from the province's treasury and daily provisions for the offerings.

**Tattenai and Shethar-Bozenai** (verses 6, 13) were the Persian governor over the province "Beyond the River" (*Abar-nahara*) and his official. They had previously challenged the Jews' building project (chapter 5), but now they obeyed the king's decree "exactly" (Aramaic: *osparna*).

**Haggai and Zechariah** (verse 14) are mentioned as the prophets whose preaching inspired and guided the temple construction to completion. Zechariah is identified here as "son of Iddo" (Aramaic: *bar-Iddo*).

**Artaxerxes** is mentioned in verse 14 alongside Cyrus and Darius. Since Artaxerxes reigned later (465–424 B.C.), this verse may summarize the entire rebuilding period or refer to later work on the temple grounds.

## Places

**Ecbatana** (Aramaic: *Aḥmeta* [אַחְמְתָא], verse 2) was the summer capital of the Persian Empire, located in the province of Media (*Maday*, modern-day Hamadan in Iran). The city lay in the mountains and was an ideal location for royal archives. The fact that Cyrus's decree was found here, rather than in Babylon, suggests it was issued while Cyrus was staying in this city.

## Events and Customs

**Cyrus's Decree** (verses 3–5) is reproduced in its archival form and specifies: temple dimensions of 60 cubits high and 60 cubits wide; a construction method using three layers of cut stone and one layer of timber; funding from the king's treasury; and the return of the temple vessels that Nebuchadnezzar had taken to Babylon.

**Completion of the Temple** (verse 15) is dated to the third day of Adar in the sixth year of King Darius, which corresponds to February/March 516 B.C. This was approximately 70 years after the destruction in 586 B.C.

**Temple Dedication** (Aramaic: *ḥanukkah* [חֲנֻכַּת], verses 16–17) followed ancient Israelite tradition for consecrating sanctuaries to God. The offerings included 100 bulls, 200 rams, 400 lambs, and 12 male goats as a sin offering—one for each of the tribes of Israel. The number twelve emphasizes that this was a joint project for all Israel, even though only part of the people had returned.

**Passover Celebration** (verses 19–22) marked that temple worship had been fully restored. The language of the chapter shifts here from Aramaic to Hebrew. The text's emphasis on the priests and Levites purifying themselves "all as one" shows renewed attention to ritual purity. The fact that those who had "separated themselves from the uncleanness of the peoples of the land" were also allowed to participate suggests that some had joined the Jewish community.

## Key Terms

**"God of Heaven"** (Aramaic: *Elah shemayya* [אֱלָהּ שְׁמַיָּא], verses 9–10) is a title appearing in Darius's decree. This expression was understandable to Persians and served as a bridge between Jewish and Persian religious terminology.

**"King of Assyria"** (Hebrew: *melek Aššur* [מֶלֶךְ־אַשּׁוּר], verse 22) is a surprising designation for the Persian king in connection with the Passover celebration. This may reflect that Persians ruled over territories formerly belonging to Assyria, or it could be a theological reference to God's sovereign rule over world empires.

**"Exactly"** (Aramaic: *osparna* [אָסְפַּרְנָא], verses 8, 12, 13) is a Persian loanword emphasizing that the king's command must be carried out precisely and without delay.
