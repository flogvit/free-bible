## People

**Rahab** (רָחָב) is a central figure in this chapter. She is described as a זוֹנָה (*zonah*), which is traditionally translated as "prostitute." Some scholars have suggested that the word could also mean "innkeeper," since such businesses often overlapped in ancient times. Her house, located within the city wall, was a natural place for travelers to seek shelter without arousing suspicion.

**The King of Jericho** appears as the city's authority who receives intelligence about Israelite spies and sends orders to Rahab to hand them over (vv. 2–3).

## Places

**Jericho** (יְרִיחוֹ) was strategically located at fords across the Jordan River and at the entrance to mountain passes, making it key to the conquest of Canaan. Rahab's house was built into the city wall itself (v. 15), which allowed the spies to escape through her window.

**Shittim** (הַשִּׁטִּים), the Israelites' camp east of the Jordan, was the starting point for the spies' mission (v. 1). The name means "acacia trees" and refers to the vegetation in that area.

**The Hill** (הָהָר, vv. 16, 22–23) was the place where the spies hid for three days on Rahab's advice before returning to Joshua.

## Events and Customs

**Spies and military reconnaissance** were common practice in ancient warfare. The expression מְרַגְּלִים חֶרֶשׁ (*meraglim cheresh*) – "spying secretly" (v. 1) – emphasizes the hidden nature of the mission.

**Drying flax on the roof** (פִשְׁתֵּי הָעֵץ, v. 6) reflects agricultural practices in the region. Flax stalks were harvested in spring and laid out to dry on flat roofs before the fibers could be processed into textiles. This provided Rahab with a natural hiding place for the spies.

**Lowering them by rope** (v. 15) was possible because Rahab's house was built into the city wall, with a window facing outward. This was a known form of housing in Canaanite cities.

## Key Terms

**חֶסֶד** (*chesed*) in verses 12 and 14 is a central covenant term meaning faithful love or loyal kindness. Rahab asks for *chesed* because she has shown *chesed* – this establishes a mutual obligation similar to covenant language.

**אוֹת אֱמֶת** (*ot emet*) – "a reliable sign" (v. 12) – refers to the scarlet cord (תִּקְוַת חוּט הַשָּׁנִי, vv. 18, 21) that was to mark Rahab's house. Such visible signs of protection have parallels with the blood on the doorposts at Passover (Exod 12).

**הֶחֱרַמְתֶּם** (*hecharamtem*) in verse 10 refers to *cherem* – the total destruction devoted to God that was practiced in holy war. Rahab specifically mentions the victory over Sihon and Og, and how the report of this had reached Jericho, explaining the inhabitants' fear (vv. 9, 11, 24).
