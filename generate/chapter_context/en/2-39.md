# Historical and Cultural Context for Exodus 39

## Events and Customs

This chapter describes the actual making of the priestly garments and the completion of the tabernacle—a detailed report that mirrors the instructions given earlier. The repeated phrase "just as the Lord had commanded Moses" (which appears eight times) emphasizes faithful obedience to divine commands. This repetitive structure has parallels in other ancient Near Eastern building accounts, where the completion of a temple or sanctuary was reported with exact correspondence to the gods' original command. Moses' blessing of the workers (v. 43) reflects the custom whereby a leader or priest gave formal approval upon the completion of a sacred project.

The gold-beating technique described in verse 3—where gold was hammered into thin sheets and cut into threads for weaving—is confirmed by finds of ancient textiles with gold inlays from Egyptian tombs. This advanced technique demonstrates the high level of craftsmanship required for the tabernacle's furnishings.

## Key Terms

**Ephod** (אֵפֹד, *efod*): A high priestly garment that bore the names of the twelve tribes on its shoulders. Archaeological finds from surrounding cultures show that similar ritual garments were used by priests in Egypt and Mesopotamia, but the Israelite ephod was unique in its symbolic representation of the entire people.

**Breastpiece** (חֹשֶׁן, *choshen*): Contained twelve precious stones with the names of the tribes, engraved like seals. The technique "engraved like a seal" (פִּתּוּחֵי חוֹתָם) refers to the professional engraving art that was highly developed in the ancient Near East.

**Gold plate** (צִיץ, *tsits*): Also called "rosette" or "diadem"—the holy headpiece with the inscription "Holy to the Lord" (קֹדֶשׁ לַיהוָה, v. 30). The word can also mean "flower," suggesting a decorative form.
