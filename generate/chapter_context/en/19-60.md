## Events and Customs

Psalm 60 is linked to a specific military campaign described in the superscription. "Aram-Naharajim" (Aram between the two rivers) refers to the Mesopotamian region between the Euphrates and Tigris, while "Aram-Soba" was an Aramean kingdom north of Damascus. According to 2 Samuel 8 and 1 Chronicles 18, David waged wars against these Aramean states to secure Israel's northern borders. Joab's victory over Edom in the Valley of Salt, where 12,000 were killed, marked a significant expansion of David's kingdom to the south. This combination of wars in the north and south demonstrates the scope of David's military operations.

## Places

The psalm contains a poetic listing of geographical areas that made up David's kingdom. **Shechem** in the central highlands and the **Valley of Succoth** east of the Jordan represent the core regions. **Gilead** and **Manasseh** were territories on the east side of the Jordan, while **Ephraim** and **Judah** constituted the two dominant tribes west of the river – Ephraim as a military force ("my helmet") and Judah as the royal seat ("scepter"). The hostile nations **Moab**, **Edom**, and **Philistia** are mentioned with derogatory imagery: Moab as a washbasin and Edom as a place to cast sandals (possibly a sign of ownership or contempt). The "fortified city" in verse 11 likely refers to Sela (later Petra), the capital of Edom.

## Key Terms

The expression "שׁוּשַׁן עֵדוּת" (shushan edut, "lily-witness") in the superscription is a musical or liturgical direction whose exact meaning is uncertain. The word "מִכְתָּם" (miktam) appears in six psalm superscriptions and may mean "golden psalm" or refer to a special poetic form. The expression "יַיִן תַּרְעֵלָה" (wine that makes one stagger) in verse 5 is a powerful image of God's judgment – an intoxicating wine that causes dizziness and confusion, a well-known prophetic motif (cf. Isaiah 51:17).
