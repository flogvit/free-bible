# Historical and Cultural Context for Proverbs 2

## Key Concepts

Chapter 2 contains several Hebrew terms that are central to understanding the wisdom tradition. The word **תּוּשִׁיָּה** (*tushiyyah*, verse 7), translated as "wisdom" or "sound judgment," refers to practical, successful insight that leads to good outcomes. This term is found mainly in wisdom literature and describes an ability to navigate life wisely.

The expression **יִרְאַת יְהוָה** (*yir'at YHWH*, "fear of the Lord," verse 5) is not primarily emotional fear, but reverence combined with obedience and trust. In Israelite thought, this was the starting point for all true wisdom—a fundamental attitude toward God that influenced one's entire way of life.

The reference to **רְפָאִים** (*refa'im*, verse 18), often translated as "the dead" or "shades," points to the Israelite conception of the realm of the dead. *Refa'im* denoted the weak shadowy existence of the deceased in *Sheol*. Similar ideas appear in Ugaritic literature, where *rpu/rpum* occurs as a designation for the dead.

## Events and Customs

The chapter warns against **אִשָּׁה זָרָה** (*ishah zarah*, "the foreign woman," verse 16). This expression has been interpreted in several ways: either as a literal reference to adultery and sexual immorality, or as a metaphor for foreign wisdom and idolatry. In the context of ancient Israel, the term may be linked to concerns about foreign religious influence. The phrase "forsakes the companion of her youth" (verse 17) refers to marriage as a **בְּרִית** (*berit*, covenant)—a legally binding agreement established with God as witness.
