# Historical and Cultural Context for Job 40

## Events and Customs

### Job’s Acknowledgment (vv. 3–5)  
Job’s brief reply marks a turning point. The gesture of placing the hand over the mouth (v. 4: יָדִי שַׂמְתִּי לְמוֹ־פִי) was in the ancient Near East a sign of reverence and silence before a superior. Job acknowledges that he is *qallōtî* (רינג/insignificant) and refrains from further argument.

### Hunting and Trading Wild Animals  
Verses 25–32 describe various methods for capturing and taming animals: hooks, snares, piercing the jaw, and trade among merchants (*kenaʿanim*, v. 30). In the ancient Near East, hunting large animals such as hippos and crocodiles was a royal and prestigious activity. Egyptian reliefs depict pharaohs engaged in hippo hunts. The point of the text is that what humans might accomplish with ordinary animals is impossible with these creatures.

## Key Terms

### Behemoth (בְּהֵמוֹת)  
The word *behemot* (v. 15) is the plural form of the Hebrew *behemah* (animal/cattle), but here it functions as a proper name for a single, mighty creature. This is so-called an "intensive plural" that emphasizes the overwhelming greatness of the animal. The description—with strength in its loins, sinews like ropes, bones like tubes of bronze and bars of iron—points to a massive land animal. Many scholars identify it with the hippopotamus (*Hippopotamus amphibius*), which was known in Egypt and possibly in the Jordan region during biblical times. Others view it as a mythological representative of chaotic forces that God has created and controls.

### Leviathan (לִוְיָתָן)  
*Leviathan* (vv. 25–32) is described through a series of rhetorical questions about humanity’s ability to capture, tame, or make it a slave or plaything. The name is related to the Ugaritic *Lotan*, a sea monster from Canaanite mythology. The description continues and is expanded in chapter 41.

### Jordan River (יַרְדֵּן)  
The reference to the Jordan (v. 23) is the only geographical location mentioned in God’s speeches. This anchors Behemoth in a concrete, familiar reality for the original audience, even though the description transcends the purely natural.
