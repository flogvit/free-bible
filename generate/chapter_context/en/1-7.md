## People

Noah is described as "righteous" (צַדִּיק, *tsaddiq*) in verse 1 – the only one of his generation to find favor with God. In verse 13, Noah’s three sons are named: Shem, Ham, and Japheth, along with their wives. A total of eight people entered the ark (verses 7, 13).

## Events and Customs

The chapter introduces the distinction between "clean" (טְהוֹרָה, *tehorah*) and "unclean" animals in verses 2 and 8. This is the first time this distinction is mentioned in the Bible – long before the Mosaic Law formally defined these categories. The text assumes that Noah already knew this distinction, suggesting a pre-Mosaic understanding of ritual categories. The clean animals were to be taken "seven and seven" (שִׁבְעָה שִׁבְעָה, verse 2) – either seven pairs or seven individuals, interpretations vary – while the unclean animals were to be taken two by two (שְׁנַיִם, one pair). The higher number of clean animals can be explained by the need for sacrificial animals after the flood (cf. 8:20) and as food once eating meat was permitted (9:3).

The number forty – "forty days and forty nights" (אַרְבָּעִים יוֹם וְאַרְבָּעִים לָיְלָה, verses 4, 12, 17) – indicates the duration of the rain. The water remained on the earth for 150 days (verse 24).

## Key Terms

The word **מַבּוּל** (*mabbul*, "flood") in verses 6–7 is used in the Bible almost exclusively to refer to Noah’s flood. It describes a cosmic catastrophe, not an ordinary flood.

The expression in verse 11 – "all the fountains of the great deep burst forth, and the windows of the heavens were opened" – reflects the Old Testament worldview where water existed both beneath the earth (תְּהוֹם רַבָּה, *tehom rabbah*, "the great deep") and above the firmament. The flood is thus described as a reverse creation, in which the waters that God separated in Genesis 1 are released again.

**וַיִּסְגֹּר יְהוָה בַּעֲדוֹ** – "the Lord shut him in" (verse 16) – indicates that God Himself sealed the ark, a personal intervention that underscores divine care and protection.
