## Key Concepts

The psalm uses several Hebrew expressions related to secret evil and hidden persecution. The word **סוֹד** (*sôd*, "secret counsel") in verse 3 denotes a conspiratorial gathering where enemies plot in secret. The expression **רִגְשַׁת פֹּעֲלֵי אָוֶן** ("the noisy throng of evildoers") suggests an organized group working together to do harm.

The imagery of a **tongue like a sword** and **bitter words like arrows** (verse 4) is common in wisdom literature and the Psalms to describe slander and false accusations. This reflects a culture where rumors and false testimony could destroy a person’s honor, livelihood, and even life. In Israelite legal practice, false witnesses could lead to a death sentence, giving such "weapons of words" literally deadly power.

## Events and Customs

The psalm’s description of enemies setting **snare** (*môqshîm*) and plotting in secret reflects the legal and social realities of ancient Israel. False accusations and conspiracies were real threats, especially for those in leading positions. Corrupt witnesses could collaborate to destroy an innocent person, giving the psalmist’s fear of secret plans a concrete legal background.

The turning point in verses 8–9, where God shoots his arrow at the enemies and their own tongue causes them to stumble, expresses a well-known biblical principle: the wicked are caught in their own trap. This "retribution motif" reflects the belief that God upholds a moral order of the world in which evil ultimately returns upon the doer.
