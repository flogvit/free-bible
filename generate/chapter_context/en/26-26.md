## Places

### Tyre (צֹר)

Tyre was the target of the judgment prophecy in this chapter. The dating in verse 1 ("in the eleventh year, on the first day of the month") places the prophecy around 587–586 BC, the same period as the siege and fall of Jerusalem. Tyre's mocking reaction to Jerusalem's downfall (v. 2) – "The gate of the nations is broken; it has turned to me; I shall be filled now that she lies in ruins" – reveals the city's commercial interest in Jerusalem's destruction.

## Events and Customs

### Nebuchadnezzar's Siege

The prophecy names Nebuchadnezzar (נְבוּכַדְרֶאצַּר) as God's instrument against Tyre (v. 7), called "king of kings" (מֶ֣לֶךְ מְלָכִ֑ים). The description of siege equipment – siegeworks (דָּיֵק), ramps (סֹֽלְלָה), and shields (צִנָּה) in verses 8–9 – reflects standard Babylonian siege techniques. Historically, Nebuchadnezzar carried out a prolonged siege of Tyre (approximately 586–573 BC).

### Mourning Rituals Among Coastal Rulers

Verses 15–18 describe how the "princes of the sea" (נְשִׂיאֵי הַיָּם) descend from their thrones, remove their robes of splendor and embroidered garments (בִּגְדֵ֥י רִקְמָתָ֖ם), and sit on the ground trembling (חֲרָד֤וֹת יִלְבָּ֙שׁוּ֙). These were established mourning rituals in the ancient Near East – sitting on the ground symbolized deep grief and humiliation.

## Key Terms

### "The Gate of the Nations" (דַּלְת֥וֹת הָעַמִּ֖ים)

Tyre's declaration that "the gates of the nations" are broken (v. 2) refers to Jerusalem as a hub for trade routes. With Jerusalem destroyed, Tyre expected to profit from the situation – a cynical motive behind their gloating.

### "A Bare Rock" (לִצְחִ֣יחַ סֶ֗לַע)

The expression "bare rock" or "naked cliff" (vv. 4, 14) describes total destruction – the city was to be so completely wiped out that only the bare rock would remain, fit only for spreading fishing nets on (מִשְׁטַ֤ח חֲרָמִים֙).

### The Grave and the Abyss (בּוֹר / תְּהוֹם)

Verses 19–21 use the motif of the underworld: Tyre will sink down to "the pit" (בּוֹר) and "the abyss" (תְּהוֹם), dwelling in "the depths of the earth" (אֶ֨רֶץ תַּחְתִּיּ֜וֹת) together with "the people of old" (עַ֣ם עוֹלָ֗ם). This picture of cosmic downfall underscores the total and final character of the judgment.
