## Events and Customs

Psalm 92 is the only psalm in the entire Book of Psalms that is explicitly linked to the Sabbath through its superscription, "A song for the Sabbath day" (לְיוֹם הַשַׁבָּת, v. 1). According to Jewish tradition, attested in the Mishnah (Tamid 7:4), this psalm was sung by the Levites in the temple every Sabbath at the morning offering. The Sabbath was not merely a day of rest but a day dedicated to celebrating creation and God's sovereignty—themes that permeate the entire psalm. The psalm's focus on God's "works" (פָּעֳלֶךָ) and "the works of Your hands" (מַעֲשֵׂי יָדֶיךָ) in verse 5 connects it directly to the creation account, where God rested on the seventh day.

## Key Terms

The expressions "ten-stringed instrument" (עָשׂוֹר), "harp" (נָבֶל), and "lyre" (כִּנּוֹר) in verse 4 refer to instruments used in temple worship. The word "Higgayon" (הִגָּיוֹן) is a musical term that likely denotes a thoughtful, meditative style of playing. The image of the righteous person who "flourishes like a palm tree" (כַּתָּמָר יִפְרָח) and "grows like a cedar in Lebanon" (כְּאֶרֶז בַּלְּבָנוֹן) in verse 13 uses two trees that were well-known symbols in ancient Israel. The palm tree represented endurance and fruitfulness in dry regions, while the cedar of Lebanon was the foremost symbol of strength, majesty, and long life throughout the Middle East.

## Places

The reference to "the house of the Lord" and "the courts of our God" (v. 14) points to the temple in Jerusalem as the central place of worship. The righteous are described as being "planted" in this holy space, suggesting a permanent belonging to the community of worship. Lebanon is mentioned (v. 13) as the origin of the famous cedar trees, which were an export symbol for the region and were used in the construction of Solomon's temple.
