## Places

**Samaria (שֹׁמְרוֹן)** is the main focus of this chapter and is explicitly mentioned in verses 9 and 12. The city was the capital of the northern kingdom of Israel. Archaeological excavations have uncovered impressive palaces and the famous Samarian ivory carvings, which confirm Amos’s references to a luxurious lifestyle. The city’s location on the mountains made it a natural amphitheater where surrounding nations could "look down upon" the injustice taking place there (v. 9).

**Ashdod (אַשְׁדּוֹד)** and **Egypt (מִצְרָיִם)** in verse 9 are striking witnesses. Ashdod was a Philistine city-state. The fact that God calls these pagan nations to testify against Israel underscores how scandalous Israel’s behavior was—even pagans would be shocked by the oppression in Samaria.

**Bethel (בֵּֽית־אֵל)** in verse 14 was the central worship site of the northern kingdom. The horns on the altar that were to be cut off were normally places of refuge for those seeking asylum (cf. Exod 21:14)—the destruction of these symbolizes that no protection will be available.

## Key Terms

The verb **יָדַע (jada)** in verse 2 ("only you have I known") means more than intellectual knowledge. In Hebrew usage, it implies intimate fellowship, covenant relationship, and election. The same word is used for marital intimacy. God is saying that Israel has had a unique, close covenant relationship with him—something that paradoxically increases their responsibility and makes the judgment stricter, not milder.

The expression **פָּקַד (paqad)** is often translated as "visit" or "punish" (vv. 2, 14), but it means that God "takes notice of" or "calls to account" his people. It is a covenant term describing how God holds the people accountable for breaking the covenant.

## Events and Customs

**The shepherd’s duty to provide evidence** in verse 12 reflects ancient Near Eastern legal practice. According to the laws in Exodus 22:10–13, a shepherd who lost an animal to a predator had to present proof (remains such as bones or ear fragments) to avoid liability for compensation. The image Amos uses is therefore legally precise: what remains of Israel will be only enough to prove that the nation once existed, not enough for real survival.

**Winter and summer houses** (בֵית־הַחֹרֶף og בֵּית הַקָּיִץ) in verse 15 testify to the wealth of the elite. The wealthy had separate homes adapted to the seasons—a known luxury feature also attested in other Near Eastern texts. **Ivory houses** (בָּתֵּי הַשֵּׁן) are confirmed by the numerous ivory inlays found in Samaria: over 500 fragments with Phoenician and Egyptian styles dated to the 8th century BC, showing furniture and walls decorated with imported ivory.
