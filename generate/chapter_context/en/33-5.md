# Historical and Cultural Context for Micah 5

## Places

**Bethlehem Ephrathah** (v. 1) was a small village about 8 kilometers south of Jerusalem. The epithet "Ephrathah" distinguishes this Bethlehem from another city with the same name in Zebulun (Josh 19:10, 15). The name "Ephrathah" means "fruitful" and was the older name for the region (Gen 35:19; Ruth 4:11). The town was known as David's hometown (1 Sam 16:1-13), and the expression "too little to be among the clans of Judah" refers to the fact that the town was insignificant in the tribe's military and administrative divisions, where "thousand" (אֶלֶף) could mean both a number and a clan or military unit.

**The land of Nimrod** (v. 5) is a poetic designation for the Assyria/Babylonia region. Nimrod is mentioned in Genesis 10:8-12 as the founder of several cities in Mesopotamia, including Nineveh. The expression "in its gates" refers to the city gate as a symbol of the seat of power, where judicial decisions and trade took place.

## Events and Customs

The chapter mentions several religious practices that were widespread in Judah and surrounding cultures, which God promises to eradicate from His people. **Sorcery** (כְשָׁפִים) **and divination** (מְעוֹנְנִים, v. 11) were common throughout the ancient Near East. Assyrian texts show extensive use of omen interpretation, and this had influenced Israelite practice despite the prohibition in the Mosaic Law (Deut 18:10-14). **Sacred pillars** (מַצֵּבוֹת, v. 12) were upright stones used in Canaanite worship, often placed near shrines for fertility gods. **Asherah poles** (אֲשֵׁרִים, v. 13) were wooden posts or stylized trees associated with the goddess Asherah. Archaeological finds from Kuntillet Ajrud and Khirbet el-Qom show inscriptions mentioning "YHWH and his Asherah," confirming the syncretism that Micah warns against.

## Key Terms

The expression **"his origin is from of old, from ancient days"** (מִקֶּדֶם מִימֵי עוֹלָם, v. 1) uses two Hebrew words for the distant past. This can be understood as a reference to the Davidic dynasty promise, but it also points to something deeper than human origin. These words are otherwise used of God's eternal existence (Ps 93:2).

**"Seven shepherds and eight princes"** (שִׁבְעָה רֹעִים וּשְׁמֹנָה נְסִיכֵי אָדָם, v. 4) is an example of numerical poetry common in wisdom literature (cf. Prov 30:15-31; Amos 1:3). The pattern "x and x+1" expresses completeness and abundance, not an exact number.

## Historical Background

Assyrian royal annals from Sennacherib (ca. 701 BC) describe the invasion of Judah as the background for the verses about Assyria. Sennacherib boasts of having besieged 46 fortified cities and countless villages in Judah. Micah's prophecies about Assyria's intrusion into "our land" and "our palaces" (vv. 4-5) reflect this historical threat, which the people experienced or feared during the prophet's time.
