# Historical and Cultural Context for 2 Thessalonians 1

## People

**Paul, Silas, and Timothy** (v. 1): The three co-authors of the letter. Silas (Silvanus) and Timothy accompanied Paul during the founding of the church in Thessalonica (Acts 17:1–9).

## Events

The chapter mentions ongoing "persecutions" (διωγμοί, *diōgmoi*) and "troubles" (θλίψεις, *thlipseis*) that the church is enduring (v. 4). This opposition forms the background for Paul’s thanksgiving prayer and eschatological teaching. Acts 17:5–9 provides context for the church’s history of persecution.

## Key Terms

**ὑπεραυξάνει** (*hyperauxanei*, v. 3): This rare Greek verb, meaning "grows exceedingly", appears nowhere else in the New Testament. It underscores the extraordinary nature of the church’s growth in faith amid opposition.

**ἀποκάλυψις** (*apokalypsis*, v. 7): The term means "unveiling" or "revelation" and is used here to refer to Christ’s visible return. In Jewish apocalyptic tradition, this denoted the final divine intervention.

**ὄλεθρος αἰώνιος** (*olethros aiōnios*, v. 9): The phrase "eternal destruction" describes a lasting state of separation from God’s presence. The term *olethros* was used in Greek literature to denote total ruin, while *aiōnios* (age-long/eternal) points toward the coming age.
