## Key Concepts

### The Storm (הַסְּעָרָה, *hasseʿarah*)
God’s speech comes "out of the storm" (v. 1). In the ancient Near East, thunderstorms were often associated with theophanies—divine revelations. The storm represents God’s majesty and overwhelming power, serving as a fitting backdrop for the rhetorical confrontation that follows. Similar storm theophanies appear in connection with revelations at Sinai and in prophetic literature.

### "Sons of God" (בְּנֵי אֱלֹהִים, *benê ʾelohim*)
The expression in verse 7 that "all the sons of God shouted for joy" refers to heavenly beings or angelic hosts who were present at creation. This phrase also occurs in Job 1:6 and 2:1, reflecting an ancient Near Eastern concept of a divine council or court around God.

### Constellation Names
The chapter mentions several star configurations: כִּימָה (*Kimah*, the Pleiades/Seven Stars), כְּסִיל (*Kesil*, Orion), מַזָּרוֹת (*Mazzarot*, possibly the zodiac or other constellations), and עַיִשׁ (*ʿAyish*, the Great Bear with its "children"/stars). This shows that the Israelites shared the ancient world’s interest in astronomy, while the text emphasizes that only God governs the movements of celestial bodies.

## Events and Customs

### Creation as a Building Project
Verses 4–6 describe the creation of the earth using construction terminology: foundation, measuring line, cornerstone, and pillars. This imagery was well known in antiquity, where temples and palaces were founded with carefully measured rituals. The metaphor underscores God’s role as the sovereign architect and builder of the cosmos.

### The Sea as a Chaotic Force
Verses 8–11 portray the sea as a newborn that must be wrapped and restrained. In Mesopotamian and Canaanite mythology, the sea was often a chaotic force that had to be defeated. Here the image is transformed: God sets boundaries for the sea with authority—not through battle, but through royal decree and care.
