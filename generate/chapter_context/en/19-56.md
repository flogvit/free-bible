# Historical and Cultural Context for Psalm 56

## Historical Background

The superscription links this psalm to a specific event in David's life: "when the Philistines seized him in Gath." This refers to the episode described in 1 Samuel 21:11–16, where David fled from King Saul and sought refuge with Achish, king of Gath. Gath was one of the five major Philistine cities and the hometown of the giant Goliath, whom David had previously defeated. When David's identity was recognized by Achish's servants, he feared for his life and pretended to be insane in order to escape. This desperate situation—caught between Saul's persecution and the hostility of the Philistines—forms the historical framework for the psalm's prayers for deliverance from enemies.

## Key Terms

The expression "yonat elem rechokim" (יוֹנַת אֵלֶם רְחֹקִים) in the superscription is enigmatic. "Yonat" means "dove," "elem" can mean "mute" or "silence," and "rechokim" means "far off." This yields translations such as "the silent dove far away." The phrase was likely a known musical direction, possibly the title of a folk song indicating the tune to which the psalm should be sung.

The word "miktam" (מִכְתָּם) in the superscription is a technical term appearing in six psalms (16 and 56–60). Its meaning is uncertain but may be connected to the Akkadian "katamu" (to cover or hide), possibly suggesting a prayer for protection.

In verse 9, there is a wordplay between two similar Hebrew words: "nodi" (נֹדִי) from the root "nod" (נֹד), meaning wandering or roaming, and "beno'dekha" (בְנֹאדֶךָ) from "no'd" (נֹאד), meaning wineskin. David first asks God to count his "nodi"—his wandering existence as a fugitive—and then to place his tears in God's "no'd"—a leather container for storing liquid. This poetic wordplay connects David's suffering as a refugee with the image of God collecting and preserving his tears. Parallel to this, "sifratekha" (your book or record) is mentioned, suggesting that God keeps a detailed account of the trials of His servants.
