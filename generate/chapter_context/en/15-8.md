## People

The chapter contains a detailed genealogy of family heads who followed Ezra from Babylon. The list opens with three individuals of special significance: Gershom from the line of Phinehas, Daniel from the line of Ithamar, and Hashub from the lineage of David (v. 2). These represent respectively the high priestly, the priestly, and the royal heritage. Then leaders for twelve additional families are named (vv. 3-14), which may symbolically represent the twelve tribes of Israel.

Sheariah is mentioned as a prominent Levite, described as "a man of understanding" (אִישׁ שֶׂכֶל), from the family of Merari (v. 18). He and Hashabiah (v. 19) became central figures in the transport of the temple gifts. Iddo is described as "the head" (הָרֹאשׁ) over the temple servants at Casiphia (v. 17), and clearly served as leader of a Jewish community there.

## Places

**Ahava** (אַהֲוָא) was a river or canal where Ezra gathered the caravan before departure (vv. 15, 21, 31). The place has not been securely identified but was likely one of the many canals in the Babylonian river system between the Euphrates and Tigris. Here the group stayed for three days for organization and fasting.

**Casiphia** (כָסִפְיָא הַמָּקוֹם) is described as "the place" (v. 17), an expression that may suggest a holy site or an organized Jewish community in exile. The name may mean "place of silver" and has been interpreted as a center for Levites and temple servants waiting to return. The place is unknown from other sources.

## Events and Customs

**The Fast at Ahava** (vv. 21-23) shows an established Jewish practice of fasting and humility before important undertakings. Ezra declared a fast to seek God's protection on the dangerous journey. His shame in asking the king for military escort (v. 22) reflects his earlier testimony to the king that God's hand is upon all who seek Him. The journey of about 1400-1500 kilometers along trade routes through the desert was genuinely dangerous due to robbers.

**The Delivery of the Temple Gifts** (vv. 24-30) followed a careful system of counting, weighing, and accountability. Twelve leading priests were set apart, together with Sheariah, Hashabiah, and ten of their brothers (v. 24). The expression "you are holy to the Lord, and the vessels are holy" (v. 28) emphasizes that both the persons and the objects were consecrated for holy service. The items were to be weighed again upon arrival in Jerusalem (vv. 29, 33-34), ensuring full accountability.

## Key Terms

**"God's good hand over us"** (כְּיַד־אֱלֹהֵינוּ הַטּוֹבָה עָלֵינוּ, v. 18) is a recurring expression in the book of Ezra that is especially highlighted in this chapter (also vv. 22, 31). It expresses a theology of God's providence and guidance.

**Nethinim** (נְתִינִים, vv. 17, 20) were temple servants who performed subordinate tasks in the sanctuary. According to the text, they were originally given by David and the leaders for the service of the Levites.

**Darics/adarkonim** (אֲדַרְכֹנִים, v. 27) were Persian gold coins, named after Darius I. One daric weighed about 8.4 grams of gold. The coin is also mentioned in 1 Chron 29:7, Ezra 2:69 and Neh 7:70-72.
