# Historical and Cultural Context for 2 Chronicles 5

## Places

**City of David (Zion)** was the original Jebusite fortress that David conquered and made his capital (2 Sam 5:6-9). It was located on the southeastern hill, south of where the temple was later built. The ark had been kept here in a tent David had pitched for it, for about 30 years before it was now moved the short distance up to the temple platform on Mount Moriah.

**The Debir (the Most Holy Place)** was the innermost room of the temple, a perfect cube measuring twenty cubits in every direction. The Hebrew word *debir* (דְּבִיר) can mean "the rear chamber" or possibly "place of speaking" (related to the root דבר, "to speak"). This was God's throne room on earth, where his presence rested between the cherubim above the ark of the covenant.

## Events and Customs

**The Feast in the Seventh Month** refers to the Festival of Tabernacles (Sukkot), which was celebrated from the 15th to the 22nd day of the month Tishri (September-October). This was the greatest of the three pilgrimage festivals and marked the harvest. The choice of this feast for the temple dedication was strategic—the people were already gathered in Jerusalem, and it was a time of joy and thanksgiving.

**The Carrying of the Ark** followed strict protocols from the Mosaic Law. The Levites carried the ark with poles through rings on its sides, without touching the chest itself. After the incident with Uzzah (1 Chron 13:9-10), care was taken to follow the prescribed rules. Chronicles emphasizes the role of the Levites more than the parallel account in 1 Kings.

## Key Terms

**"David's Holy Gifts"** (v. 1) refers to the treasures and objects David had dedicated to the Lord throughout his life, including war spoils and gold and silver he received from subjugated peoples. These were not used in the actual construction of the temple but were stored in the temple treasuries as a holy reserve.

**"The Glory of the Lord"** (כְּבוֹד יְהוָה, *kevod YHWH*) describes the visible manifestation of God's presence, often associated with a cloud or radiant light. The cloud that filled the temple (vv. 13-14) was the same phenomenon that led Israel through the wilderness and that filled the tabernacle at Sinai (Exod 40:34-35). This marked God's acceptance of the temple as his dwelling place.

## People

**Asaph, Heman, and Jeduthun** were the three leaders of temple music appointed by David. Asaph was a Levite from the Gershon clan and the author of several psalms. Heman was a Levite from the Kohath clan and known as "the king's seer" (1 Chron 25:5). Jeduthun (also called Ethan) led a third group of musicians. These three families formed the core of temple music for generations to come.

## Chapter-Specific Findings

Archaeologists have discovered several silver trumpets from antiquity in the Middle East, including examples from Tutankhamun's tomb in Egypt. The biblical *chatsotsrot* (חֲצֹצְרוֹת, silver trumpets) were straight tubes, different from the curved ram's horns (shofar). That 120 priests blew trumpets simultaneously (v. 12) illustrates the enormous scale of this ceremony—a soundscape that must have been overwhelming in the temple courtyard.
