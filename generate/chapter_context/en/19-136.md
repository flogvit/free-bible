## Events and Customs

Psalm 136 is traditionally called "The Great Hallel" (הַלֵּל הַגָּדוֹל) in Jewish liturgy, distinct from the Egyptian Hallel (Psalms 113–118). It was sung at the Passover meal, especially in connection with the fourth cup. The psalm’s responsorial structure – where the refrain "for his steadfast love endures forever" is repeated 26 times – points to antiphonal temple worship in which a leader sang and the congregation or a Levitical choir responded. This form is documented in 2 Chronicles 7:3 and Ezra 3:11.

The psalm follows a recognizable structure found in ancient Near Eastern hymns: first, God is praised (vv. 1–3), then his acts in creation are listed (vv. 4–9), followed by his saving deeds in history (vv. 10–22), and finally his enduring care (vv. 23–26).

## Places

**The Sea of Reeds** (יַם־סוּף, vv. 13, 15) traditionally refers to the Red Sea or one of the lakes in the Egyptian border region. **Bashan** (v. 20) was a fertile highland east of the Sea of Galilee, known for its oak trees and fat cattle.

## People

**Sihon, king of the Amorites** (v. 19) ruled from Heshbon over the regions east of the Jordan. **Og, king of Bashan** (v. 20) reigned with capitals at Ashtaroth and Edrei. These two powerful kings were defeated by the Israelites before they crossed the Jordan (Numbers 21). Their victories became paradigmatic examples of God’s power and are mentioned repeatedly in the Old Testament.

## Key Terms

The Hebrew word **חֶסֶד** (ḥesed), repeated in every verse, is one of the richest theological terms in the Old Testament. It is variously translated as "steadfast love", "faithfulness", "grace", or "lovingkindness". The term denotes God’s covenant loyalty – an active, loyal love that sustains his relationship with his people through all circumstances. The phrase **לְעוֹלָם חַסְדּוֹ** ("his steadfast love endures forever") serves as a liturgical confession of God’s unchanging character.
