# Historical and Cultural Context for 1 Corinthians 5

## Events and Customs

### Sexual Norms in Greco-Roman Culture
The specific sin Paul addresses—that a man "has his father’s wife" (γυναῖκά τινα τοῦ πατρὸς ἔχειν)—refers to a sexual relationship with a stepmother. Paul emphasizes that this was forbidden "even among the Gentiles" (οὐδὲ ἐν τοῖς ἔθνεσιν). Roman law explicitly prohibited marriage between a stepson and stepmother, and the renowned jurist Gaius classified such acts as incest. Cicero condemned similar cases in his speeches. Mosaic law also categorically forbade this (Leviticus 18:8). The fact that the church tolerated something both Jewish and Gentile morality rejected shows how deeply the Corinthian mindset of freedom had taken root.

### Passover Language
Paul uses Passover terminology: "clean out the old leaven" (ἐκκαθάρατε τὴν παλαιὰν ζύμην) and "our Passover lamb has been sacrificed" (τὸ πάσχα ἡμῶν ἐτύθη). In Jewish practice, all homes had to be cleansed of leavened bread before the Passover celebration (Exodus 12:15–19). This "bedikat chametz" (search for leaven) was a thorough ritual cleansing. Paul transfers this image to the moral cleansing of the church, indicating that the Corinthian Christians were familiar with Jewish Passover traditions.

## Key Terms

### Πορνεία (porneia) – Sexual Immorality
This Greek word covers a broad range of sexual sins outside marriage. Its use here specifically denotes the incestuous relationship that the church tolerated.

### Παραδοῦναι τῷ Σατανᾷ (paradounai tō Satana) – Hand Over to Satan
The phrase "hand over to Satan for the destruction of the flesh" (v. 5) describes a formal exclusion from the fellowship of the church. Outside the church—the body of Christ—one was in Satan’s domain. "Destruction of the flesh" (ὄλεθρον τῆς σαρκός) may refer either to physical suffering or to the destruction of the sinful nature. The goal was the salvation of the spirit (ἵνα τὸ πνεῦμα σωθῇ), showing that this discipline was restorative, not merely punitive.

### Ζύμη (zymē) – Leaven
Leaven functioned as yeast in antiquity and was often used as a metaphor for something that spreads invisibly and permeates everything. In Jewish tradition, leaven frequently symbolized sin or corruption. Paul’s point is that tolerating obvious sin "leavens" the entire church—it affects the character and standards of the community.

## Chapter-Specific Insights

### "The Previous Letter"
In verse 9, Paul refers to a previous letter he wrote to the Corinthians (Ἔγραψα ὑμῖν ἐν τῇ ἐπιστολῇ). This indicates that what we call "1 Corinthians" was not actually his first letter. This "lost letter" apparently dealt with sexual immorality but was misunderstood by the church. They thought Paul meant they should avoid all contact with sinners in general, whereas he clarifies that it concerns internal church discipline toward professing believers living in open sin.
