# Historical and Cultural Context for Numbers 1

## People

The chapter names the twelve tribal leaders (*nesi'ei*) who were to assist Moses and Aaron with the census (verses 5–15):

- **Reuben**: Elizur, son of Shedeur
- **Simeon**: Shelumiel, son of Zurishaddai
- **Judah**: Nahshon, son of Amminadab
- **Issachar**: Nethanel, son of Zuar
- **Zebulun**: Eliab, son of Helon
- **Ephraim** (son of Joseph): Elishama, son of Ammihud
- **Manasseh** (son of Joseph): Gamaliel, son of Pedahzur
- **Benjamin**: Abidan, son of Gideoni
- **Dan**: Ahiezer, son of Ammishaddai
- **Asher**: Pagiel, son of Ochran
- **Gad**: Eliasaph, son of Deuel
- **Naphtali**: Ahira, son of Enan

These men are described as "the called out from the congregation" (*qeru'e ha'edah*), "leaders of their ancestral tribes" and "heads of the thousands of Israel" (verse 16).

## Places

The chapter opens with a precise geographical and temporal indication: God speaks to Moses in the "Wilderness of Sinai" (*midbar Sinai*) at the "Tent of Meeting" (*ohel mo'ed*), on the first day of the second month in the second year after the exodus from Egypt (verse 1). The Tent of Meeting served as the place of divine revelation and was the starting point for the command to conduct the census.

## Events and Customs

The census (*paqad*) described has a specific military character. All men twenty years old and upward who could "go out to war" (*yotse tsava*) were to be counted. The age limit of twenty for military service was standard in Israelite tradition.

Organization by "families" (*mishpachot*) and "father's houses" (*beit avot*) reflects the patrilineal clan structure typical of Semitic societies in the Bronze Age.

The total from the census was **603,550** men fit for war (verse 46). The largest tribes were Judah (74,600), Dan (62,700) and Simeon (59,300), while the smallest were Manasseh (32,200), Benjamin (35,400) and Ephraim (40,500).

## Key Terms

The expression "lift up the head" (*se'u et-rosh*) in verse 2 is a Hebrew idiom meaning to count or muster. The word *legulgolotam* ("according to their skulls/heads") emphasizes that this was an individual count of each person.

The term *nasi*, translated as "leader" or "chief" in verse 16, designates the tribal heads. The title indicates a person who is "lifted up" or exalted among the people and represents the highest civil authority within each tribe.

The exemption of the Levites from the census (verses 47–53) and their special role as keepers of the "Tabernacle of the Testimony" (*mishkan ha'edut*) establish an important principle of separation between the holy and the common. Their position around the tabernacle functioned as a protective buffer to prevent "wrath" (*qetsef*) against the congregation. Any "stranger" (*zar*) – that is, anyone who was not a Levite – who approached the tabernacle was to die (verse 51).
