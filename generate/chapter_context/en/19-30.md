## Events and Customs

The heading in verse 1 mentions "the dedication of the house" (חֲנֻכַּת הַבַּיִת, *chanukat habajit*). This expression may refer to the dedication of David's palace (2 Sam 5:11; cf. 2 Sam 7) or to the temple, although the temple was not built until Solomon's time. Later Jewish tradition associated this psalm with the celebration of Hanukkah and the rededication of the temple in 164 B.C.E. during the Maccabean revolt.

The psalm describes a dramatic shift from sorrow to joy. Verse 12 notes that the psalmist exchanged "sackcloth" (שַׂק, *sak*) for dancing. Sackcloth was a coarse, dark fabric made of goat hair worn next to the skin as a sign of mourning, repentance, or humility. Removing the sackcloth and putting on festive garments marked the end of a period of grief and served as a publicly visible sign of a changed life situation.

## Key Terms

The Hebrew verb דָּלָה (*dalah*) in verse 2, translated "you drew me up," uses imagery drawn from drawing water out of a well. This creates a powerful visual metaphor in which the psalmist was like someone about to drown in a deep well but was rescued by God pulling him up.

The terms "Sheol" (שְׁאוֹל) and "the pit" (בּוֹר) in verse 4 represent the realm of the dead and the grave in Old Testament thought. Sheol was not a place of punishment but rather a shadowy existence for the dead beneath the earth. The expression "those who go down to the pit" describes people on their way toward death, and the psalmist's deliverance from this state meant a return to life among the living.

The phrase "the memory of his holiness" (זֵכֶר קָדְשׁוֹ) in verse 5 refers to God's holy name and his character as revealed to humanity. To praise God's "memory" means to proclaim and remember his saving acts throughout history.
