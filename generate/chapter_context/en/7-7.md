# Historical and Cultural Context for Judges 7

## People

**Gideon/Jerubba'al** bears both names in this chapter. "Jerubba'al" ("Let Ba'al contend") refers back to the event in chapter 6 where he tore down the altar of Ba'al. **Pura** is Gideon's servant who accompanies him on reconnaissance of the Midianite camp. Servants in this role often served as witnesses and companions during dangerous missions. **Oreb and Ze'eb** are Midianite chieftains whose names mean "raven" and "wolf" – animal names common among semi-nomadic peoples, often indicating warrior traits.

## Places

**En-Harod** ("trembling spring") lies at the foot of Mount Gilboa, traditionally identified with 'Ain Jalud. The name may refer to the soldiers' fear or to the movement of the water. **Moreh Hill** in the north of the valley gave the Midianites a strategic vantage point. **Beth-Shittah**, **Zererah**, **Abel-Meholah**, and **Tabbath** mark escape routes eastward toward the Jordan. Abel-Melah is later known as the hometown of the prophet Elisha (1 Kings 19:16). **Beth-Barah** by the Jordan was an important ford that the tribe of Ephraim could block.

## Events and Customs

**The reduction of the army** through the water test has parallels in ancient military selection processes. Those who lapped water from their hands remained alert and ready, unlike those who knelt down and lost situational awareness. **The middle watch** (Hebrew: *ha'ashmoret hattikhona*) refers to the three-part watch system – the attack came right after the change of guard when the new sentries had just been posted (v. 19).

**Dream interpretation** was highly regarded in the ancient Near East. The fact that a Midianite soldier interpreted the dream about the barley loaf as Gideon's victory shows widespread knowledge of Israel's leader and underscores psychological warfare. Barley bread was food for the poor compared to wheat, symbolizing Israel's apparent inferiority.

## Key Terms

**Shofar** (שׁוֹפָר) was a ram's horn trumpet used for religious and military signals. Three hundred horns sounding simultaneously would create enormous chaos. **Kaddim rekim** (כַּדִּים רֵקִים) were empty clay jars that concealed the torches until the dramatic moment. Breaking them created sudden light and sound that confused the enemy. The cry **"For the Lord and for Gideon!"** (v. 18) expands to **"A sword for the Lord and for Gideon!"** (v. 20) – a combination of divine and human leadership, serving as a theological reminder that victory belongs to God.

## Chapter-Specific Findings

Archaeological investigations in the Jezreel Valley confirm the strategic importance of this area as a thoroughfare for trade and invasion armies. Discoveries of Midianite pottery from the late Bronze Age/early Iron Age in the Jordan Valley support accounts of their presence in the region.
