# Historical and Cultural Context for James 5

## People

**Job (Ἰώβ)** is mentioned in verse 11 as an example of endurance. Job was a central figure in Jewish wisdom tradition, known from the Old Testament book bearing his name. For James’s Jewish-Christian readers, Job was the foremost example of a righteous man who held fast to his faith through extreme suffering. The expression "the Lord's outcome" (τὸ τέλος κυρίου) refers to how God ultimately restored and blessed Job.

**Elijah (Ἠλίας)** is highlighted in verses 17–18 as an example of the power of prayer. James emphasizes that Elijah was "a human being with a nature like ours" (ὁμοιοπαθὴς ἡμῖν) – an important clarification showing that powerful prayer is not reserved for superhuman heroes. The time reference "three years and six months" also appears in Luke 4:25 and reflects Jewish tradition, since 1 Kings 18:1 mentions only "the third year".

## Events and Customs

**Anointing with oil** (verse 14) was a widespread practice in antiquity with both medical and religious significance. Olive oil was used for treating wounds (cf. Luke 10:34), but here the physical act is combined with prayer "in the name of the Lord", giving it a sacramental character. The elders (πρεσβύτεροι) held an established leadership role in early Christian assemblies, inherited from synagogue structure.

**Harvesting and day labor** (verses 4, 7) reflect agricultural conditions in Palestine. Workers were typically paid daily (cf. Leviticus 19:13; Deuteronomy 24:15). Withholding wages was a serious sin. "The early rain and the late rain" (πρόϊμον καὶ ὄψιμον) in verse 7 refer to the autumn rains (October–November), which made plowing and sowing possible, and the spring rains (March–April), which ensured the ripening of the grain.

## Key Terms

**Κύριος Σαβαώθ** (verse 4) – "Lord Sabaoth" or "the Lord of hosts" – is a transliteration of the Hebrew צְבָאוֹת (tsevaot). This divine name, common among the prophets (especially Isaiah), emphasizes God’s power as ruler over heavenly armies and as judge over oppressors. Its use signals that the workers’ cry has reached Almighty God Himself.

**Παρουσία** (verses 7–8) – "coming" or "presence" – was a technical term for the official arrival of a king or emperor in a city. In early Christian usage, it referred to Christ’s return. This concept gave suffering believers hope that the righteous Judge would come soon.

**Πολύσπλαγχνος** (verse 11) – "full of compassion" – is a rare word that literally means "having many inward parts". In Hebrew thought, the inward parts were regarded as the seat of deep emotions. This word appears only here in the New Testament and underscores God’s intense, heartfelt compassion.
