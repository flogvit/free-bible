# Historical and Cultural Context for Hosea 9

## Places

**Gilgal** (v. 15) was an important cult site in the Northern Kingdom, located in the Jordan Valley near Jericho. The place originally had positive significance as Israel's first campsite after crossing the Jordan (Josh 4:19-20), but by Hosea's time it had become a center for syncretistic worship. Hosea mentions Gilgal several times as a symbol of Israel's apostasy (4:15; 12:12).

**Gibeah** (v. 9) refers to the event described in Judges 19-21, where a Levite's concubine was gang-raped and killed by men from the town of Gibeah in the tribe of Benjamin. This incident is regarded as a low point in Israel's moral history and is used here as a measure of the people's current corruption.

**Baal-Peor** (v. 10) refers to the event in Numbers 25, where the Israelites participated in sexual and religious idolatry with Moabite women at Mount Peor. This became a paradigmatic example of Israel's tendency toward apostasy already from the wilderness period.

**Moph** (מֹף, v. 6) is the Hebrew name for Memphis, the ancient Egyptian capital south of the Nile Delta. The city was known for its enormous graveyards and necropolises, which gives special force to the prophecy's words that "Moph shall bury them."

## Events and Customs

**The Feast of Tabernacles** is central in this chapter. Verses 1-2 refer to the celebration on the threshing floors after the harvest, where the people rejoiced and gave "harlot's wages"—likely taxes or offerings to Baal as a fertility god. The threshing floor (גֹּרֶן, *goren*) was not only a workplace but also a place for religious rituals and festivals.

**"Mourning bread"** (לֶחֶם אוֹנִים, *lechem onim*) in verse 4 refers to food consumed in connection with burial and mourning. Such food made all who ate it ritually unclean (Num 19:14; Deut 26:14). Hosea says that in exile all food will be like this—unclean and unfit as an offering to the Lord.

## Key Terms

**"Harlot's wages"** (אֶתְנָן, *etnan*) in verse 1 is a technical term for payment to a prostitute. Its use here is metaphorical and describes the payments Israel made to Baal cults in the belief that fertility came from him, not from Yahweh. The same term is used in Deuteronomy 23:19 regarding the prohibition against bringing such payment to the temple.

**"Folly"** and **"madness"** (אֱוִיל, *evil* and מְשֻׁגָּע, *meshugga*) in verse 7 are expressions that likely reflect the people's mockery of the prophet. These words were used derogatorily about Hosea and other prophets who proclaimed judgment. Similar language is found in 2 Kings 9:11 regarding Elisha's disciple.

## Chapter-Specific Findings

Archaeological excavations in the Gilgal areas have uncovered traces of cult activity from the Iron Age, which may confirm the site's religious significance during that period. The findings indicate that the place was actively used for religious purposes at the time of Hosea, supporting the prophet's criticism of the cultic practices there.
