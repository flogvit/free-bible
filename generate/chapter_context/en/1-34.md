## People

**Dinah** (דִּינָה) is Jacob’s daughter born to Leah, as mentioned in verse 1 (בַּת־לֵאָה אֲשֶׁר יָלְדָה לְיַעֲקֹב). Her name comes from the Hebrew word for "judgment" or "justice" (דין). In this chapter, she appears as a young woman of marriageable age.

**Shechem** (שְׁכֶם) was the son of Hamor the Hivite. His name is identical to the city name Shechem. He is described as the "most honored" in his father’s house (v. 19), indicating that he was the heir to his father’s leadership position.

**Hamor** (חֲמוֹר) was the chief of the Hivites (נְשִׂיא הָאָרֶץ, v. 2) in the Shechem area. His name means "donkey" in Hebrew.

**Simeon and Levi** (שִׁמְעוֹן וְלֵוִי) were Dinah’s full brothers (born to Leah), specifically called "Dinah’s brothers" (אֲחֵי דִּנָה) in verse 25. They led the revenge attack against Shechem and killed all the men in the city.

## Places

**Shechem** (שְׁכֶם) was the city where the events of this chapter took place. Jacob had previously purchased a plot of land outside the city (Gen 33:18–20), which established his family as neighbors to the Shechemites.

**The city gate** (שַׁעַר עִירָם) is mentioned as the place where Hamor and Shechem presented their proposal concerning marriage and circumcision to the men of the city (vv. 20–24). In ancient cities, the city gate was the central location for legal proceedings, business agreements, and public assemblies. The fact that "all who went out at the gate" agreed indicates a formal city assembly.

## Events and Customs

**Bride price** (מֹהַר) and **dowry** (מַתָּן) were central elements in marriage arrangements in the ancient Near East. Shechem’s offer to pay "whatever you ask" (v. 12) shows his desperate desire to legitimize the relationship. The bride price was a payment from the groom’s family to the bride’s family, while the dowry consisted of gifts given directly to the bride.

**Marriage alliances between ethnic groups** (vv. 9–10) were a common diplomatic tool in ancient times. Hamor’s proposal for mutual intermarriage and the right to settle and trade represented a political alliance, not merely a single marriage. For Jacob’s family, this posed a real danger of assimilation with the Canaanites.

**Rape and disgrace** are described using three verbs in verse 2: "he took her" (וַיִּקַּח), "he lay with her" (וַיִּשְׁכַּב), and "he humiliated her" (וַיְעַנֶּהָ). The last verb (ענה) is consistently used in the Old Testament to refer to sexual violence and coercion. The act is described as "a disgraceful thing" (נְבָלָה) done against Israel (v. 7), an expression that in the OT denotes serious violations of society’s moral order.

**Circumcision** (הִמּוֹל) was for the Israelites a sign of the covenant with God. That Jacob’s sons required circumcision from the Shechemites (v. 15) served a dual purpose: it gave an appearance of religious legitimacy to the agreement, while simultaneously making the men physically vulnerable. On the third day after circumcision (v. 25), the pain was at its worst, leaving the Shechemites defenseless.

## Key Terms

**"A disgraceful thing in Israel"** (נְבָלָה עָשָׂה בְיִשְׂרָאֵל, v. 7) is an expression used several times in the OT to describe actions that violate the holy character of the community (cf. Deut 22:21; Josh 7:15; Judg 20:6). The phrase "in Israel" here refers to Jacob/Israel’s family as a distinct community with its own norms, since Jacob had recently received the name Israel (Gen 32:28).

**"Made us stink"** (לְהַבְאִישֵׁנִי, v. 30) is Jacob’s troubled reaction. The expression literally means "to make smell bad" and describes how the family’s reputation was now ruined among neighboring peoples. Jacob’s main concern seems to be practical consequences rather than moral questions regarding the revenge action itself.

**"Should he treat our sister like a prostitute?"** (הַכְזוֹנָה יַעֲשֶׂה אֶת־אֲחוֹתֵנוּ, v. 31) is the sons’ rhetorical closing statement. The word זוֹנָה (prostitute) emphasizes that without action, Dinah’s violation would remain unaddressed, which in their eyes was unacceptable.
