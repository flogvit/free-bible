## Places

The psalm mentions "your holy temple" (הֵיכַל קָדְשְׁךָ, *hejkhal qodshekha*) in verse 2, where the psalmist bows down in worship. This may refer to the temple in Jerusalem, but the Hebrew word *hejkhal* can also denote God’s heavenly dwelling. The phrase "before gods" (נֶגֶד אֱלֹהִים, *neged elohim*) in verse 1 is debated—it could mean "before angels", "before rulers/judges", or be a polemical declaration of praising Israel’s God in contrast to the gods of other nations. The Septuagint translated this as "angels", reflecting one early interpretive tradition.

## Key Concepts

The phrase "your faithful love and your truth" (חַסְדְּךָ וְעַל־אֲמִתֶּךָ, *chasdekha ve'al-amittekha*) in verse 2 combines two central covenant concepts: *chesed* (faithful love, covenant loyalty) and *emet* (truth, reliability). This word pair appears frequently in the Psalms and highlights God’s character as both loving and trustworthy in his promises.

Verse 2 also contains the theologically charged phrase that God has "exalted your word above all your name" (הִגְדַּלְתָּ עַל־כָּל־שִׁמְךָ אִמְרָתֶךָ). This difficult sentence has been interpreted in several ways, but it likely points to the fact that God’s revealed word (his promises and speech) confirms and manifests his character and reputation.

## Themes

The vision that "all the kings of the earth" will praise the Lord (verses 4–5) reflects an Israelite expectation of universal recognition of Yahweh. This theme appears in several royal psalms and prophetic texts, and may reflect a known motif from the ancient Near East where subordinate kings acknowledged a supreme king. In the Israelite context, this image is transferred to God’s cosmic rule.
