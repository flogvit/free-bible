## Events and Customs

The psalm mentions "righteous offerings" (זִבְחֵי־צֶדֶק, *zivḥe-ṣedeq*) in verse 6, referring to sacrifices brought with a right heart attitude and in accordance with the regulations of the Torah. In Israelite sacrificial worship, it was not only the ritual act that mattered, but also the inner disposition of the one offering. The prophets repeatedly criticized offerings made without sincere devotion (cf. Isaiah 1:11–17; Amos 5:21–24). The phrase "say in your heart, on your bed" (verse 5) points to a practice of quiet meditation and self-examination in the evening, which was common in Israelite piety.

## Key Terms

The expression "the light of your face" (א֨וֹר פָּנֶ֬יךָ, *'or panekha*) in verse 7 is a central theological image in the Psalms and in the Aaronic blessing (Numbers 6:25). It expresses God's grace, favor, and goodwill, where an enlightened face symbolizes goodness and acceptance. The phrase "in distress you gave me room" (בַּ֭צָּר הִרְחַ֣בְתָּ לִּ֑י, *baṣṣar hirḥavta li*) in verse 2 uses a spatial metaphor where distress represents trouble and wide space represents deliverance—a common image in Hebrew poetry.

The word "selah" (סֶֽלָה) appears twice in the psalm (verses 3 and 5) and is likely a liturgical marker, possibly indicating a musical interlude or a pause for reflection. Its exact meaning remains debated among scholars.
