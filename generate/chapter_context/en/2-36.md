# Historical and Cultural Context for Exodus 36

## People

The chapter highlights **Bezalel** (בְּצַלְאֵל) and **Oholiab** (אָהֳלִיאָב) as the leaders of the tabernacle's construction. Bezalel is mentioned first and held primary responsibility, while Oholiab served as his assistant. **Moses** (מֹשֶׁה) played a coordinating role by summoning the craftsmen (v. 2) and issuing a proclamation to stop the offerings (v. 6). The Hebrew expression "חֲכַם־לֵב" (chakam-lev, "wise of heart") describes the craftsmen and shows that in Hebrew thought, the heart was the seat of both intellect and skill, not merely emotions. These were men with God-given technical expertise.

## Events and Customs

The chapter describes a remarkable situation where the people gave so generously that Moses had to issue a proclamation to stop the offerings (vv. 5–7). The expression "וַיַּעֲבִירוּ קוֹל בַּמַּחֲנֶה" (they caused a voice to pass through the camp) describes a public announcement, a common practice in ancient societies without written mass communication. The terms "תְּרוּמָה" (terumah, contribution/offering) and "נְדָבָה" (nedavah, freewill offering) both appear in the chapter (vv. 3, 6) and emphasize the voluntary nature of the people's contributions to the sanctuary.

## Key Terms

The chapter contains several textile technical terms. **"שֵׁשׁ מָשְׁזָר"** (shesh mashzar) means "twined fine linen" – a high-quality fabric where linen threads were spun together for extra strength and luster. **"תְּכֵלֶת"** (tekhelet) was a blue-violet dye extracted from murex snails, extremely costly. **"אַרְגָּמָן"** (argaman) was reddish-purple from the same type of snail, while **"תוֹלַעַת שָׁנִי"** (tola'at shani) was scarlet dye from cochineal insects. The word **"פָּרֹכֶת"** (parokhet, v. 35) refers to the curtain that separated the Holy Place from the Most Holy Place, made with "מַעֲשֵׂה חֹשֵׁב" (ma'aseh choshev) – skillful weaving with embroidered cherubim.

## Chapter-Specific Findings

The construction details in this chapter show a hierarchy of materials: gold hooks (קַרְסֵי זָהָב) for the linen fabrics (v. 13), bronze hooks (קַרְסֵי נְחֹשֶׁת) for the goat-hair curtains (v. 18), silver sockets (אַדְנֵי־כֶסֶף) under the boards (vv. 24, 26), and bronze sockets at the entrance (v. 38). The precise measurements – curtains measuring 28 by 4 cubits (v. 9), goat-hair curtains measuring 30 by 4 cubits (v. 15), and boards measuring 10 by 1.5 cubits (v. 21) – show a carefully planned structure. The chapter systematically describes the layered construction: linen curtains with cherubim (innermost), then goat-hair curtains, and finally red-dyed ram skins and tahash skins (תְּחָשִׁים, v. 19) as the outer covering – the latter being a debated term where the exact animal species is uncertain.
