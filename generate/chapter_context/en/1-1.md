# Chapter Context for Genesis 1

## Key Concepts

**אֱלֹהִים (Elohim)** – The name of God used consistently throughout chapter 1. The form is grammatically plural but is consistently paired with singular verbs, emphasizing the majesty and fullness of the one God.

**תֹהוּ וָבֹהוּ (tohu vavohu)** – "Formless and empty" (v. 2). A word pair describing the state of the earth before creation was completed. This expression appears elsewhere only in Jer 4:23 and Isa 34:11.

**רָקִיעַ (raqia)** – "Expanse" or "firmament" (vv. 6–8). Describes what God placed between the waters, which He called "heaven." The word comes from a root meaning to hammer out or stretch out.

**צֶלֶם (tselem)** – "Image" (vv. 26–27). Humanity is created in God’s צֶלֶם and דְּמוּת (likeness). In the ancient Near East, kings were often described as the image of a god on earth; here this concept is extended to all humanity.

**לְמִינוֹ/לְמִינָהּ (lemino/leminah)** – "According to its kind" (vv. 11–12, 21, 24–25). A recurring expression that emphasizes order and distinction in creation.

## Events and Customs

**Creation through God’s Word:**
The distinctive feature of the chapter is that God creates by speaking: "God said… and it was" (וַיֹּאמֶר אֱלֹהִים... וַיְהִי). This pattern repeats throughout the chapter, which later Jewish tradition associated with the "ten creative words."

**Seven-Day Structure:**
The chapter describes the six days of creation. The seventh day, with God’s rest, is described in 2:1–3. The structure follows a pattern:
- Days 1–3: Formation of "realms" (light/darkness, expanse, dry land)
- Days 4–6: Filling of these realms (heavenly bodies, sea creatures/birds, land animals/humans)

**Blessing:**
God blesses twice in the chapter: sea creatures and birds (v. 22), and humanity (v. 28). Both blessings include the command to be fruitful and multiply.

**Human Dominion:**
Humans receive a specific mandate: to "subdue" the earth and "rule over" the animals (v. 28). This distinguishes humanity’s role from the rest of creation.

## Chapter-Specific Observations

**Literary Structure:**
The chapter has a clear refrain: "And God saw that it was good" (וַיַּרְא אֱלֹהִים כִּי־טוֹב) appears six times (vv. 4, 10, 12, 18, 21, 25), with an intensified version "very good" (טוֹב מְאֹד) appearing a seventh time on the sixth day (v. 31).

**Day-Ending Formula:**
"And there was evening, and there was morning" (וַיְהִי־עֶרֶב וַיְהִי־בֹקֶר) marks each day. The mention of evening first forms the basis for the Jewish practice that a day begins at sunset.

**Heavenly Bodies:**
The sun and moon are not named but referred to simply as "the greater light" and "the lesser light" (v. 16). This may be deliberate polemic against the worship of heavenly bodies, which was widespread in surrounding cultures.
