## People

**Joshua (יְהוֹשֻׁעַ)** stands central in this chapter as Moses' appointed successor. His name means "The Lord saves" or "The Lord is salvation." The chapter describes a formal public installation where Moses calls Joshua forward "before all Israel's eyes" (v. 7) and gives him the task of leading the people into the promised land. This public ceremony was important to establish Joshua's authority as a legitimate leader. In verses 14-15 and verse 23, Joshua also receives direct divine confirmation at the tent of meeting, where God Himself gives him the promise of His presence.

**The Levites and priests** are mentioned as guardians of the ark of the covenant and recipients of the written law (v. 9, 25). They were responsible for carrying "the Lord's ark of the covenant" and for keeping the book of the law beside the ark as a witness against the people.

**Sihon and Og**, the Amorite kings, are mentioned in verse 4 as examples of God's previous victories. These victories serve as assurance to Israel that God will act in the same way toward the peoples in Canaan.

## Events and Customs

**Moses' farewell speech** begins with his declaration that he is 120 years old (v. 2). This marks not physical weakness, but the end of his leadership and the transition to Joshua.

**Public reading of the law** (v. 10-13) was to take place every seventh year during the Feast of Tabernacles in the year of release (שְׁמִטָּה, *shemittah*). This practice, called *hakhel* (הַקְהֵל, "assemble"), ensured that all the people—men, women, children, and foreigners—heard the law read aloud. This was an educational institution designed to preserve the covenant relationship across generations. Later Jewish tradition describes how the king performed this reading.

**Storing covenant texts at the sanctuary** (v. 26) reflects a widespread practice in the ancient Near East. Treaties and important documents were often placed at the feet of divine statues or in temples as holy witnesses. In Israel's case, the book of the law was laid "beside" the ark of the covenant—not inside it—but as a witness against the people.

## Key Terms

**"Be strong and courageous" (חֲזַק וֶאֱמָץ)** is repeated three times in the chapter (v. 6, 7, 23)—first to all Israel, then to Joshua from Moses, and finally to Joshua from God Himself. This repetition underscores the chapter's central message of trust in God's presence during the transition to new leadership.

**"To go out and come in" (לָצֵאת וְלָבוֹא)** in verse 2 is a Hebrew idiom describing a leader's full activity, especially military leadership and public duties. Moses does not say that he is physically weak, but that his time as leader is over.

**"Lie down with your fathers" (שֹׁכֵב עִם־אֲבֹתֶיךָ)** in verse 16 is a common euphemism for death in the Old Testament, expressing connection to the ancestors in death.

**"The song" (הַשִּׁירָה)** mentioned in verses 19-22 and 30 refers to Moses' song in chapter 32. Songs were important mnemonic tools in oral cultures, and this one was to function as a "witness" (לְעֵד) against Israel in case of future apostasy.

**"Hide my face" (הִסְתַּרְתִּי פָנַי)** in verses 17-18 describes God's withdrawal of protection and blessing. In contrast to the blessing "light of His face" (Num 6:25-26), the hidden face represents the consequences of covenant breaking.

## Places

**The tent of meeting (אֹהֶל מוֹעֵד)** in verses 14-15 was the meeting place between God and Moses. There the Lord appeared in the pillar of cloud at the entrance to the tent to install Joshua. This tent functioned as God's "throne room" in the wilderness and the place of divine revelation.
