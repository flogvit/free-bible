# Historical and Cultural Context for 2 Kings 10

## People

**Jehu** carries out two main actions in this chapter: the extermination of Ahab’s descendants (verses 1–17) and the destruction of the Baal cult in Israel (verses 18–28). He presents himself as the Lord’s instrument to fulfill the prophecy against Ahab’s house (verse 10), referring to Elijah’s prophecy.

**Ahab’s seventy sons** (verses 1–8) were royal descendants raised by the city elders in Samaria. The term "sons" (בָּנִים) can include grandsons and other male descendants. They were killed by their own foster fathers at Jehu’s command.

**Jonadab, son of Rechab** (verses 15–17, 23) was the ancestor of the Rechabites. In this chapter, he meets Jehu, confirms his loyalty with a handshake, and is invited to ride in Jehu’s chariot to witness his "zeal for the Lord" (verse 16). He also participates in inspecting the temple of Baal to ensure that no servants of the Lord were present (verse 23).

**Ahaziah’s brothers** (verses 13–14) were relatives of Judah’s king Ahaziah. The Hebrew word אֲחֵי (achei) can mean both "brothers" and "relatives in a broader sense." They were on their way to greet "the sons of the kings and the sons of the queen mother" when Jehu met them and killed all 42 at Beth Eked.

## Places

**Beth Eked Haroim** (בֵּית־עֵקֶד הָרֹעִים, verses 12–14), literally "the gathering place of the shepherds" or "the rock well of the shepherds," was a location along the main road between Jezreel and Samaria. Here the massacre of Ahaziah’s relatives took place at a well (בּוֹר, verse 14).

**The temple of Baal in Samaria** (verses 21–27) was the cult site built by Ahab (1 Kings 16:32), which Jehu destroys in this chapter. The text suggests that the temple was large enough to hold all the worshipers of Baal in the kingdom—it was filled "from wall to wall" (פֶּה לָפֶה, verse 21).

**Bethel and Dan** (verse 29) were the places where the golden calves set up by Jeroboam still stood. Although Jehu eradicated the Baal cult, he did not remove these idols.

## Events and Customs

**Display of severed heads** (verses 7–8) was a common practice in the ancient Near East to demonstrate military victory. Jehu ordered the seventy heads placed in "two piles" (שְׁנֵי צִבֻּרִים) at the city gate of Jezreel until the next morning. This served as public proof that Ahab’s lineage had been exterminated.

**The cult assembly** (עֲצָרָה, verse 20) was a solemn religious gathering. Jehu used deception by declaring a "great sacrifice" to Baal in order to gather all the worshipers in one place. The Hebrew word בְּעָקְבָּה (verse 19) means "with cunning" or "by deceit."

**Special garments** (verse 22): Jehu ordered that all worshipers of Baal be given their own clothes from the "wardrobe" (הַמֶּלְתָּחָה). This ensured they could be identified and that no worshipers of Yahweh would accidentally be killed.

**Destruction to latrines** (verse 27): The Hebrew text has two readings—כְּתִיב (written) מַחֲרָאוֹת means "latrines," while קְרֵי (read) מוֹצָאוֹת means "dumping ground." Both expressions convey the total desecration of the former cult site.

## Key Terms

**Runners and officers** (הָרָצִים וְהַשָּׁלִשִׁים, verse 25) made up the king’s elite guard who carried out the massacre in the temple of Baal. The "runners" were bodyguards, while the "officers" (שָׁלִישׁ) were high-ranking commanders.

**Sacred pillars/cult stones** (מַצֵּבוֹת, verses 26–27) were standing stones used in Canaanite religion as representations of deities. These were burned and torn down as signs of the complete destruction of the Baal cult.

**"Cut off" Israel** (לְקַצּוֹת, verse 32): The Lord began to reduce Israel’s territory through Hazael’s attacks. All land east of the Jordan was lost: Gilead, and the tribal territories of Gad, Reuben, and Manasseh, from Aroer by the Arnon River to Bashan.

## Chapter-Specific Observations

**Jehu’s double standard** (verses 28–31): The chapter highlights a paradoxical evaluation of Jehu. He is praised for destroying the Baal cult and fulfilling the Lord’s word against Ahab’s house—with the promise of four generations on the throne (verse 30). However, he is criticized for keeping Jeroboam’s golden calves in Bethel and Dan (verses 29, 31).

**The Lord’s word fulfilled** (verses 10, 17): The chapter repeatedly emphasizes that these events fulfill the prophecy given through Elijah (1 Kings 21:21–24), which legitimizes Jehu’s brutal actions as divine judgment.
