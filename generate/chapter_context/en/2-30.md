# Historical and Cultural Context for Exodus 30

## Events and Customs

The chapter describes several ritual practices with parallels in the ancient Near East. **Incense offering** was widespread in temples throughout the ancient Near East. The Hebrew word *qeṭoret* (קְטֹרֶת) refers to incense that rises up and is associated with prayer and God's presence. The burning of incense morning and evening (vv. 7–8) reflected common practice for daily temple rituals at sunrise and sunset.

**Ritual washing** in the bronze basin (vv. 17–21) reflects a widespread understanding in antiquity that purity was necessary to approach the divine. Egyptian priests washed themselves several times each day, and similar cleansing rituals are documented in Mesopotamian cults. The **half-shekel** as ransom (vv. 12–16) represents an early form of "cultic tax" also known from other ancient societies, where a census was considered potentially dangerous without divine protection.

## Key Terms

**Sheqel haqqodesh** (שֶׁקֶל הַקֹּדֶשׁ) – "sanctuary shekel" – was a standardized unit of weight used in cultic contexts. One shekel corresponded to approximately 11.4 grams of silver, making the half-shekel about 5.7 grams. Twenty *gera* made up one shekel (v. 13). This specification was necessary because units of weight varied between different trading regions.

**Kofer** (כֹּפֶר) in verse 12 means "ransom" or "atonement price" and is related to *kippur* (atonement). The concept implies that a human life has value that must be "covered" before God. The fact that both rich and poor paid the same amount (v. 15) emphasized equality before God – an unusual idea in a time of strict social hierarchies.

## Ingredients and Materials

The ingredients in the anointing oil and incense (vv. 23–25, 34) are well documented in archaeological and botanical studies. **Mor-deror** (מָר־דְּרוֹר) is liquid myrrh from *Commiphora* trees in South Arabia and East Africa. **Qinnamon** (קִנָּמוֹן) is traditionally identified as cinnamon, possibly imported via trade networks through Arabia, although its exact identification remains debated. **Qiddah** (קִדָּה) is likely cassia, a cinnamon-like spice. These exotic ingredients testify to extensive trade networks during the Bronze Age.

**Incense ingredients** include *nataph* (balsam resin), *sheḥelet* (possibly onyx-shell or a plant resin), *ḥelbenah* (galbanum, a resin from the *Ferula* plant), and *levonah* (pure frankincense from *Boswellia* trees). Archaeological finds of incense altars from ancient Israel show residues of similar mixtures, and trade connections for frankincense from South Arabia are documented in Assyrian sources.
