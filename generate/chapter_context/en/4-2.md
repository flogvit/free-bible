# Historical and Cultural Context for Numbers 2

## People

The chapter mentions twelve tribal leaders (נָשִׂיא, *nasi*), one for each tribe. These leaders were responsible for the placement and organization of their respective tribes:

- **Camp of Judah (east):** Nahshon son of Amminadab (Judah); Nethanel son of Zuar (Issachar); Eliab son of Helon (Zebulun)
- **Camp of Reuben (south):** Elizur son of Shedeur (Reuben); Shelumiel son of Zurishaddai (Simeon); Eliasaph son of Deuel (Gad)
- **Camp of Ephraim (west):** Elishama son of Ammihud (Ephraim); Gamaliel son of Pedahzur (Manasseh); Abidan son of Gideoni (Benjamin)
- **Camp of Dan (north):** Ahiezer son of Ammishaddai (Dan); Pagiel son of Ocran (Asher); Ahira son of Enan (Naphtali)

## Events and Customs

The chapter describes the organization of the Israelite camp around the tabernacle, with three tribes on each of the four sides (east, south, west, and north). Each tribal group had its own banner and a specific marching order: Judah first (v. 9), then Reuben (v. 16), followed by the Levites carrying the tabernacle in the center (v. 17), then Ephraim (v. 24), and finally Dan bringing up the rear (v. 31).

The use of "flag" or "banner" (דֶּגֶל, *degel*) for each tribal group served both practical and symbolic purposes: they helped identify units during marches and represented each tribe's identity.

## Key Terms

The Hebrew word **דֶּגֶל** (*degel*), translated as "banner" or "flag", denotes a military standard or tribal emblem. In the ancient context, this was likely a pole with an emblem or symbol at the top. Jewish tradition (Midrash Bamidbar Rabbah) assigns each banner a specific color and symbol.

The expression **אֹהֶל מוֹעֵד** (*ohel mo'ed*), "Tent of Meeting" or "tabernacle", refers to the tabernacle itself. In this chapter, its central placement is crucial for the entire camp structure (vv. 2, 17).

The word **צָבָא** (*tsava*), "army" or "host", emphasizes that the organization has a military character—Israel is structured as an army on the march.
