# Historical and Cultural Context for Exodus 37

## People

**Bezalel** (בְּצַלְאֵל) is the craftsman who carries out the actual work of building the sacred objects of the tabernacle in this chapter. His name means "in God's shadow" or "under God's protection." The chapter presents him as the master who translates the divine instructions from chapter 25 into concrete objects.

## Events and Customs

The chapter describes the making of the tabernacle's sacred objects: **the ark of the covenant** (vv. 1–5) with **the mercy seat and the cherubim** (vv. 6–9), **the table for the bread of the Presence** with its utensils (vv. 10–16), **the lampstand or menorah** (vv. 17–24), **the altar of incense** (vv. 25–28), as well as **the holy anointing oil** and **the pure incense** (v. 29). These objects have parallels in surrounding cultures; Egyptian temples had sacred chests, ceremonial tables, and lampstands. The cherubim (כְּרֻבִים) on the mercy seat share similarities with winged guardian figures known from both Egyptian and Mesopotamian art. Incense was widespread throughout the ancient Near East as a means of approaching the gods.

## Key Terms

**Acacia wood** (עֲצֵי שִׁטִּים, *atsei shittim*) was the primary building material, a hard and durable tree that grew in the Sinai desert and was resistant to decay and insects—ideal for sacred objects meant to last. **The mercy seat** (כַּפֹּרֶת), often translated "mercy seat" or "place of atonement," was the lid of the ark. The word comes from the root *kpr*, meaning "to cover over" or "to make atonement." **Mikshah** (מִקְשָׁה), used for both the cherubim and the lampstand, means "hammered work"—that is, beaten out of a single piece of gold without soldering or joining. This required exceptional craftsmanship.

## Chapter-Specific Findings

Archaeological discoveries from the ancient Near East shed light on several elements in this chapter. Ivory plaques from Nimrud and other sites show cherubim with outstretched wings protecting thrones and sacred objects, providing context for the cherubim on the ark of the covenant. Carrying poles inserted through rings, as described for the ark and the table, are also known from Egyptian portable shrines.
