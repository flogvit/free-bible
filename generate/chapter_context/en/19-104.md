# Historical and Cultural Context for Psalm 104

## Events and Customs

Psalm 104 is a creation hymn that follows a structure similar to Genesis 1, but unfolds it poetically. The psalm describes God's care for the created world through natural processes: the water cycle, the changing of seasons, and the relationship between day and night. This reflects the Israelite worldview in which nature was not independent, but entirely dependent on God's continuous sustenance.

Verse 19 states that the moon was made "for appointed times" (לְמוֹעֲדִים, *lemoadim*). This points to the Jewish calendar, which was lunar-based, where the moon determined the timing of festivals. In contrast to surrounding cultures that worshipped celestial bodies, this psalm presents them as created tools in God's service.

## Key Terms

The word **רוּחַ** (*ruach*) appears with several meanings in this psalm: "wind" (v. 4), "breath of life" (v. 29), and God's "Spirit" (v. 30). This ambiguity underscores the connection between God's creative power and the sustenance of life.

**לִוְיָתָן** (*Livjatan*, v. 26) was in Canaanite mythology a terrifying chaos monster. Here it is presented as a playful sea creature, created by God for his delight—a radical demythologization that demonstrates the Lord's sovereign power over all forces.

## Chapter-Specific Findings

Psalm 104 shows striking similarities with the Egyptian "Hymn to Aton" from the time of Akhenaten (c. 1350 BCE), particularly in the description of darkness as the time for wild animals, the blessings of sunrise, and all creatures' dependence on the deity. The hymn was found inscribed in tombs at Tell el-Amarna. Scholars debate whether there is direct literary dependence or if both reflect a common Near Eastern genre of creation hymns. The Israelite psalm stands out due to its monotheistic focus and absence of sun worship.
