## Key Concepts

Psalm 139 contains several Hebrew words with deep theological significance. The verb **חָקַר** (*ḥāqar*, "to search/examine") in verses 1 and 23 forms a frame around the psalm and describes a thorough, exhaustive investigation of something hidden. The word **כִלְיֹתָי** (*kilyōtāy*, "my kidneys") in verse 13 refers to the kidneys as the seat of human deepest feelings and motives in Hebrew thought—parallel to how we today speak of "the heart."

The expression **גָּלְמִי** (*gālĕmî*, "my unformed mass") in verse 16 is unique in the Bible and denotes an unshaped substance or embryo. This word later gained great significance in Jewish mysticism and folklore. The phrase **בְּתַחְתִּיּוֹת אָרֶץ** ("the depths of the earth") in verse 15 is poetic imagery for the womb, linking human creation to God's original creative work from the dust of the earth.

## Events and Customs

The psalm's description of fetal development (verses 13–16) reflects ancient understanding of conception as a divine mystery. The verb **סָכַךְ** (*sākak*, "to cover/protect") in verse 13, often translated as "woven together," uses the image of God's protective care. In the ancient world, textile work was frequently used as a metaphor for creation, and Israelites viewed the coming into being of life as God's artistic craftsmanship.

The psalm's concluding prayer for examination (verses 23–24) has parallels to declarations of innocence known from both Israelite and Egyptian religious practice. The expression **דֶּרֶךְ־עֹצֶב** in verse 24 is disputed. The word עֹצֶב can mean "pain" or be related to עָצָב (idol), giving two possible interpretations: "the way of pain" or "the way of idols." The psalmist asks to be led away from this and onto **דֶּרֶךְ עוֹלָם** ("the everlasting way").
